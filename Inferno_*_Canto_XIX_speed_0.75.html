Inferno * Canto XIX

Dante and Virgil arrive at the third bolgia of the eighth circle. The rocky ground is filled with circular holes, from which the legs of sinners protrude from the calf down. The rest of their bodies are trapped headfirst inside the apertures. The soles of their feet are set on fire, causing them to writhe in torment. Dante’s attention is drawn to one soul whose legs kick more violently than the others. Virgil carries Dante down the embankment to the hole. The sinner, Pope Nicholas III, initially mistakes Dante for Pope Boniface VIII, whom he is expecting. After the misunderstanding is cleared, Nicholas III identifies himself and explains that other popes are buried beneath him. He prophesies the arrival of future popes who will be thrust into the same hole. After Dante delivers a harsh speech, a pleased Virgil carries him back to the bridge, and they proceed to the next chasm.

Inferno • Canto XIX

O Simon mago, o miseri seguaci
che le cose di Dio, che di bontate
deon essere spose, e voi rapaci

Oh, Simon Magus, and you wretched followers,who treat the things of God, meant for goodnessas a bride, with you, the rapacious ones.

per oro e per argento avolterate,
or convien che per voi suoni la tromba,
però che ne la terza bolgia state.

Through gold and silver you have prostituted yourselves;now it is fitting that the trumpet sound for you,because you are in the third pouch.

Già eravamo, a la seguente tomba,
montati de lo scoglio in quella parte
ch’a punto sovra mezzo ’l fosso piomba.

We had already climbed to the next tomb, mounting the cliff face on that side that hangs directly over the middle of the ditch.

O somma sapïenza, quanta è l’arte
che mostri in cielo, in terra e nel mal mondo,
e quanto giusto tua virtù comparte!

Oh, highest wisdom, what artistry you displayin heaven, on earth, and in the wicked world,and how justly your power is apportioned!

Io vidi per le coste e per lo fondo
piena la pietra livida di fóri,
d’un largo tutti e ciascun era tondo.

I saw, along the slopes and the bottom,the livid stone filled with holes,all of a generous size, each one round.

Non mi parean men ampi né maggiori
che que’ che son nel mio bel San Giovanni,
fatti per loco d’i battezzatori;

They seemed to me neither smaller nor largerthan those in my beautiful San Giovanni,made for the place of the baptizers.

rupp’ io per un che dentro v’annegava:
e questo sia suggel ch’ogn’ omo sganni.

One of them, not many years ago, I broke to save a man who was drowning inside. Let this be a seal that disillusions every man.

Fuor de la bocca a ciascun soperchiava
d’un peccator li piedi e de le gambe
infino al grosso, e l’altro dentro stava.

From the mouth of each one, the feet and legs of a sinner protruded, up to the thickest part, while the rest of him was inside.

Le piante erano a tutti accese intrambe;
per che sì forte guizzavan le giunte,
che spezzate averien ritorte e strambe.

The soles of both feet were ablaze in every instance; because of this, they twitched so violently at the joints that they would have shattered ropes, both twisted and distorted.

Qual suole il fiammeggiar de le cose unte
muoversi pur su per la strema buccia,
tal era lì dai calcagni a le punte.

As fire on oily things will move, still burning, along the surface, so it was there, from heels to toes.

«Chi è colui, maestro, che si cruccia
guizzando più che li altri suoi consorti»,
diss’ io, «e cui più roggia fiamma succia?».

"Who is that, master, who torments himself," I said, "writhing more than the others there, and who is sucked by a redder flame?"

Ed elli a me: «Se tu vuo’ ch’i’ ti porti
là giù per quella ripa che più giace,
da lui saprai di sé e de’ suoi torti».

And he to me: "If you wish me to carry you down there by that bank which lies lowest, you shall learn from him of himself and of his wrongs."

E io: «Tanto m’è bel, quanto a te piace:
tu se’ segnore, e sai ch’i’ non mi parto
dal tuo volere, e sai quel che si tace».

And I: "It pleases me as much as it pleases you; you are the master, and you know that I do not depart from your will, and you know what is left unsaid."

Allor venimmo in su l’argine quarto;
volgemmo e discendemmo a mano stanca
là giù nel fondo foracchiato e arto.

Then we arrived at the fourth embankment; we turned and we descended, weary, down into the constricted, pitted depths.

Lo buon maestro ancor de la sua anca
non mi dipuose, sì mi giunse al rotto
di quel che si piangeva con la zanca.

The good master still did not leave my side, but brought me to the fissure where one of the damned was weeping, his leg severed.

«O qual che se’ che ’l di sù tien di sotto,
anima trista come pal commessa»,
comincia’ io a dir, «se puoi, fa motto».

“Oh, you, whatever you are, held by the upper world beneath, a wretched soul, like a post embedded,” I began to say, “if you can, speak.”

Io stava come ’l frate che confessa
lo perfido assessin, che, poi ch’è fitto,
richiama lui per che la morte cessa.

I stood there, much like a friar hearing the confessionof a treacherous assassin who, once captured,calls the friar back to learn how to avoid death.

Ed el gridò: «Se’ tu già costì ritto,
se’ tu già costì ritto, Bonifazio?
Di parecchi anni mi mentì lo scritto.

And he cried out: "Are you already standing there,are you already standing there, Boniface?The written record lied to me by many years."

Se’ tu sì tosto di quell’ aver sazio
per lo qual non temesti tòrre a ’nganno
la bella donna, e poi di farne strazio?».

"Are you so quickly sated with the wealthfor which you did not fear to deceivethe lovely woman, and then to destroy her?"

Tal mi fec’ io, quai son color che stanno,
per non intender ciò ch’è lor risposto,
quasi scornati, e risponder non sanno.

Thus did I become, like those who stand,unable to understand what is answered to them,almost scorned, and unable to respond.

Allor Virgilio disse: «Dilli tosto:
“Non son colui, non son colui che credi”»;
e io rispuosi come a me fu imposto.

Then Virgil said: "Tell him quickly: 'I am not he, I am not he that you believe.'" And I replied as I was told.

Per che lo spirto tutti storse i piedi;
poi, sospirando e con voce di pianto,
mi disse: «Dunque che a me richiedi?

Whereat the spirit twisted his feet away, then, sighing and with a voice of weeping, said to me: “So then, what do you ask of me?”

Se di saper ch’i’ sia ti cal cotanto,
che tu abbi però la ripa corsa,
sappi ch’i’ fui vestito del gran manto;

If you are so concerned to know what I am, that you have thus run along the bank, know that I was once clothed in the great mantle.

e veramente fui figliuol de l’orsa,
cupido sì per avanzar li orsatti,
che sù l’avere e qui me misi in borsa.

And in truth, I was a son of the she-bear, so greedy to advance my cubs that I set my sights upon wealth, and here I put myself in the purse.

Di sotto al capo mio son li altri tratti
che precedetter me simoneggiando,
per le fessure de la pietra piatti.

Beneath my head, the others are drawn forth, those who simoniacally preceded me, flattened into the fissures of the stone.

Là giù cascherò io altresì quando
verrà colui ch’i’ credea che tu fossi,
allor ch’i’ feci ’l sùbito dimando.

Down there I too shall fall, when he arrives, the one I thought you were, when I made my sudden demand.

Ma più è ’l tempo già che i piè mi cossi
e ch’i’ son stato così sottosopra,
ch’el non starà piantato coi piè rossi:

But more time has passed since my feet were scorched, and since I’ve been upside down, than he will stand there with his red feet planted.

ché dopo lui verrà di più laida opra,
di ver’ ponente, un pastor sanza legge,
tal che convien che lui e me ricuopra.

For after him, a more hideous deed will come, from the west, a shepherd without law, such that he must conceal both him and me.

Nuovo Iasón sarà, di cui si legge
ne’ Maccabei; e come a quel fu molle
suo re, così fia lui chi Francia regge».

A new Jason will arise, of whom it is written in Maccabees; and just as his king was compliant, so will be he who rules France.

Ah, Constantine, of how much evil you were the mother, not your conversion, but that dowry which the first wealthy father received from you!

E mentr’ io li cantava cotai note,

And while I sang these verses, whether from wrath or conscience that gnawed him, he kicked strongly with both his feet.

I’ credo ben ch’al mio duca piacesse,

I believe my guide was pleased, for with such a contented expression, he attended to the sound of the true words I had spoken.

Però con ambo le braccia mi prese;

But with both his arms he embraced me; and once he had lifted me to his chest, he climbed back up the path he had descended.

Né si stancò d’avermi a sé distretto,

Nor did he tire of holding me close,so he carried me over the crest of the archthat bridges the fourth to the fifth dike.

Quivi soavemente spuose il carco,

There he gently set down his burden, gently upon the rough and steep rock, a passage that would be difficult for goats.

Indi un altro vallon mi fu scoperto.

Then another valley was revealed to me.

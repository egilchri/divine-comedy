Purgatorio * Canto X

After passing through the Gate of Purgatory, Dante and Virgil navigate a narrow, winding path. They emerge onto the first terrace, a level ledge encircling the mountain. The inner cliff face is made of white marble and features intricately carved reliefs that appear to be alive. Dante examines three scenes. The first depicts the Annunciation, featuring the Angel Gabriel and the Virgin Mary. The second shows King David dancing before the Ark of the Covenant while his wife Michal looks on. The third relief portrays the Roman Emperor Trajan on horseback, pausing to grant justice to a poor widow. As Dante is absorbed by the lifelike sculptures, Virgil directs his attention to a group of souls approaching. They are bent low to the ground, moving with great difficulty as they carry massive, heavy stones on their backs.

Purgatorio • Canto X

Poi fummo dentro al soglio de la porta
che ’l mal amor de l’anime disusa,
perché fa parer dritta la via torta,

Then we were within the threshold of the gate,which evil love makes souls disuse,because it makes the crooked way seem straight.

sonando la senti’ esser richiusa;
e s’io avesse li occhi vòlti ad essa,
qual fora stata al fallo degna scusa?

Hearing it, I knew it had shut once more. And if I had turned my eyes toward it, what excuse would have been worthy for that fault?

Noi salavam per una pietra fessa,
che si moveva e d’una e d’altra parte,
sì come l’onda che fugge e s’appressa.

We sailed along a fissure in the stone, which shifted back and forth, like a wave that retreats and then returns.

«Qui si conviene usare un poco d’arte»,
cominciò ’l duca mio, «in accostarsi
or quinci, or quindi al lato che si parte».

"Here, we must employ a bit of craft," my guide began, "in maneuvering, now to this side, now to that, along the separating wall."

E questo fece i nostri passi scarsi,
tanto che pria lo scemo de la luna
rigiunse al letto suo per ricorcarsi,

And this made our steps slow,so slow that before the waning moonreturned to its bed to rest,

che noi fossimo fuor di quella cruna;
ma quando fummo liberi e aperti
sù dove il monte in dietro si rauna,

that we were out of that hole;but when we were free and in the open,up where the mountain gathers itself back,

ïo stancato e amendue incerti
di nostra via, restammo in su un piano
solingo più che strade per diserti.

We, exhausted and uncertain bothof our path, stopped on a plain,more solitary than roads through deserts.

Da la sua sponda, ove confina il vano,
al piè de l’alta ripa che pur sale,
misurrebbe in tre volte un corpo umano;

From its bank, where it borders emptiness,at the foot of the high cliff that still ascends,it would measure a human body three times over.

e quanto l’occhio mio potea trar d’ale,
or dal sinistro e or dal destro fianco,
questa cornice mi parea cotale.

And as far as my eye could take flight, now from the left and now from the right side, this ledge appeared to me like that.

Là sù non eran mossi i piè nostri anco,
quand’ io conobbi quella ripa intorno
che dritto di salita aveva manco,

We hadn't even moved our feet up there yet, when I recognized that the ledge around us had less of a direct upward climb.

esser di marmo candido e addorno
d’intagli sì, che non pur Policleto,
ma la natura lì avrebbe scorno.

being of pure white marble, adornedwith carvings so fine that not only Praxiteles,but nature itself would be shamed there.

L’angel che venne in terra col decreto
de la molt’ anni lagrimata pace,
ch’aperse il ciel del suo lungo divieto,

The angel, who descended to Earth with the decree of the peace so long lamented, which opened the heavens, previously forbidden,

dinanzi a noi pareva sì verace
quivi intagliato in un atto soave,
che non sembiava imagine che tace.

Before us, it seemed so real,there engraved in a gentle act,that it did not seem an image that was silent.

Giurato si saria ch’el dicesse ‘Ave!’;
perché iv’ era imaginata quella
ch’ad aprir l’alto amor volse la chiave;

One would have sworn he was saying, "Hail!";because there was depicted the onewho turned the key to open supreme love.

e avea in atto impressa esta favella
‘Ecce ancilla Deï’, propriamente
come figura in cera si suggella.

And in his face was imprinted this speech:"Behold the handmaid of God," preciselyas a figure is sealed in wax.

«Non tener pur ad un loco la mente»,
disse ’l dolce maestro, che m’avea
da quella parte onde ’l cuore ha la gente.

"Don't just keep your mind fixed on one place,"said my sweet master, who had meon that side where people have their hearts.

Per ch’i’ mi mossi col viso, e vedea
di retro da Maria, da quella costa
onde m’era colui che mi movea,

Because I turned with my face, and I sawbehind Mary, from that sidewhere he who moved me was.

un’altra storia ne la roccia imposta;
per ch’io varcai Virgilio, e fe’mi presso,
acciò che fosse a li occhi miei disposta.

Another story set into the unyielding rock; because I stepped past Virgil and moved close to it, so that it might be arranged for my eyes.

Era intagliato lì nel marmo stesso
lo carro e ’ buoi, traendo l’arca santa,
per che si teme officio non commesso.

There, carved into the very marble itself, were the cart and oxen, pulling the sacred ark, for which an unbidden task is feared.

Dinanzi parea gente; e tutta quanta,
partita in sette cori, a’ due mie’ sensi
faceva dir l’un ‘No’, l’altro ‘Sì, canta’.

Before me seemed a people; and all of them, divided into seven choirs, presented to my two senses an experience that caused one to say, "No," and the other, "Yes, sing."

Similemente al fummo de li ’ncensi
che v’era imaginato, li occhi e ’l naso
e al sì e al no discordi fensi.

Similarly to the imagined smoke of incense, the eyes and the nose found themselves in disagreement, one saying yes and the other no.

Lì precedeva al benedetto vaso,
trescando alzato, l’umile salmista,
e più e men che re era in quel caso.

He went before the blessed vessel,leaping and dancing, the humble psalmist,and more and less than a king was he in that instance.

Di contra, effigïata ad una vista
d’un gran palazzo, Micòl ammirava
sì come donna dispettosa e trista.

Instead, depicted in a single viewof a great palace, Michal observed,a woman spiteful and sad.

I’ mossi i piè del loco dov’ io stava,
per avvisar da presso un’altra istoria,
che di dietro a Micòl mi biancheggiava.

I shifted my feet from where I stood, to examine closely another scene, which gleamed white behind Michal.

Quiv’ era storïata l’alta gloria
del roman principato, il cui valore
mosse Gregorio a la sua gran vittoria;

Which once contained the history of the high gloryof the Roman principality, whose valormoved Gregory to his great victory;

i’ dico di Traiano imperadore;
e una vedovella li era al freno,
di lagrime atteggiata e di dolore.

I speak of the Emperor Trajan, and a widow was at his bridle, shaped by tears and sorrow.

Intorno a lui parea calcato e pieno
di cavalieri, e l’aguglie ne l’oro
sovr’ essi in vista al vento si movieno.

Around him seemed a throng, tightly packedwith knights, and eagles in goldabove them moved in view of the wind.

La miserella intra tutti costoro
pareva dir: «Segnor, fammi vendetta
di mio figliuol ch’è morto, ond’ io m’accoro»;

The wretched woman among them allseemed to say, "Lord, avenge mefor my son, who is dead, and for whom I grieve;"

ed elli a lei rispondere: «Or aspetta
tanto ch’i’ torni»; e quella: «Segnor mio»,
come persona in cui dolor s’affretta,

and he to her: "Now you waituntil I return"; and she: "My Lord,"like someone in whom grief hastens,

«se tu non torni?»; ed ei: «Chi fia dov’ io,
la ti farà»; ed ella: «L’altrui bene
a te che fia, se ’l tuo metti in oblio?»;

"What if you don't return?" And he: "Whoever I am with, will be with you." And she: "What good will other people's well-being be to you, if you forget your own?"

ond’ elli: «Or ti conforta; ch’ei convene
ch’i’ solva il mio dovere anzi ch’i’ mova:
giustizia vuole e pietà mi ritene».

And he said: "Now, be comforted; for I must fulfill my duty before I depart: justice demands it, and compassion holds me here."

Colui che mai non vide cosa nova
produsse esto visibile parlare,
novello a noi perché qui non si trova.

He who never saw a new thing produced this visible speech, new to us because it is not found here.

Mentr’ io mi dilettava di guardare
l’imagini di tante umilitadi,
e per lo fabbro loro a veder care,

While I delighted in gazing upon the images of so many acts of humility, and found them dear to see because of their maker,

«Ecco di qua, ma fanno i passi radi»,
mormorava il poeta, «molte genti:
questi ne ’nvïeranno a li alti gradi».

"Behold, from here," the poet murmured, "but they walk slowly, many people: these will send us to the high steps."

Li occhi miei, ch’a mirare eran contenti
per veder novitadi ond’ e’ son vaghi,
volgendosi ver’ lui non furon lenti.

My eyes, which had been pleased to gaze,eager to see new things, of which they are fond,turning toward him, were not slow.

Non vo’ però, lettor, che tu ti smaghi
di buon proponimento per udire
come Dio vuol che ’l debito si paghi.

Reader, I don’t want you to abandonyour good intentions upon hearinghow God wants debts to be paid.

Non attender la forma del martìre:
pensa la succession; pensa ch’al peggio
oltre la gran sentenza non può ire.

Do not expect the form of martyrdom: consider the succession; consider that the worst that can happen cannot go beyond the final judgment.

Io cominciai: «Maestro, quel ch’io veggio
muovere a noi, non mi sembian persone,
e non so che, sì nel veder vaneggio».

I began, "Master, what I seemoving toward us does not seem like people,and I do not know what it is; my sight deceives me."

Ed elli a me: «La grave condizione
di lor tormento a terra li rannicchia,
sì che ’ miei occhi pria n’ebber tencione.

And he to me: "The grievous state of their tormentcrouches them to the ground,so that my eyes first contended with them."

Ma guarda fiso là, e disviticchia
col viso quel che vien sotto a quei sassi:
già scorger puoi come ciascun si picchia».

But look closely there, and unbindwith your gaze what lies beneath those rocks:you can already discern how each one strikes himself.

O superbi cristian, miseri lassi,
che, de la vista de la mente infermi,
fidanza avete ne’ retrosi passi,

Oh, proud Christians, miserable and weary,who, with minds afflicted in their sight,have faith in backward steps, so contrary.

non v’accorgete voi che noi siam vermi
nati a formar l’angelica farfalla,
che vola a la giustizia sanza schermi?

Do you not realize that we are worms,born to form the angelic butterflythat flies to justice without defenses?

Di che l’animo vostro in alto galla,
poi siete quasi antomata in difetto,
sì come vermo in cui formazion falla?

Of what then does your spirit float on high, since you are almost defective as an automaton, like a worm in which formation fails?

Come per sostentar solaio o tetto,
per mensola talvolta una figura
si vede giugner le ginocchia al petto,

As if to support a ceiling or a roof, sometimes a figure is seen, its knees drawn up to its chest, acting as a bracket.

la qual fa del non ver vera rancura
nascere ’n chi la vede; così fatti
vid’ io color, quando puosi ben cura.

which makes true rancor of what is not truearise in those who see her; such were theywhen I paid close attention to them.

Vero è che più e meno eran contratti
secondo ch’avien più e meno a dosso;
e qual più pazïenza avea ne li atti,

It is true that they were more or less contracteddepending on how much they had on their backs;and the one who had the most patience in their actions,

piangendo parea dicer: ‘Più non posso’.

weeping, seemed to say: ‘I can't go on.’

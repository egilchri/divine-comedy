Purgatorio * Canto XXXI

Beatrice continues her sharp reprimand of Dante, detailing his unfaithfulness to her memory after her death. Overcome with remorse, Dante faints. He awakens in the arms of Matelda, who pulls him into the river Lethe. She submerges him up to his throat and makes him drink the water. Afterward, Matelda leads the cleansed Dante to four dancing women. They position him before the griffin-drawn chariot where Beatrice stands and petition her to unveil her face. Beatrice removes her veil, revealing her eyes and her smile. Dante becomes transfixed by the sight of her eyes, which reflect the dual nature of the griffin. His senses are completely absorbed by her unveiled beauty as the canto concludes.

Purgatorio • Canto XXXI

«O tu che se’ di là dal fiume sacro»,
volgendo suo parlare a me per punta,
che pur per taglio m’era paruto acro,

"O you who are beyond the sacred river," turning his speech towards me like a point, which had appeared sharp to me as if by a blade,

ricominciò, seguendo sanza cunta,
«dì, dì se questo è vero: a tanta accusa
tua confession conviene esser congiunta».

He began again, without hesitation, "Tell me, tell me if this is true: your confession must be joined to so great an accusation."

Era la mia virtù tanto confusa,
che la voce si mosse, e pria si spense
che da li organi suoi fosse dischiusa.

My virtue was so confoundedthat my voice arose, but died awaybefore it could be released from its source.

Poco sofferse; poi disse: «Che pense?
Rispondi a me; ché le memorie triste
in te non sono ancor da l’acqua offense».

He suffered a little, then said: "What do you think?Answer me; for the sad memoriesin you have not yet been harmed by the water."

Confusione e paura insieme miste
mi pinsero un tal «sì» fuor de la bocca,
al quale intender fuor mestier le viste.

Confusion and fear, mixed together,pushed such a “yes” out of my mouth,for which sight alone was insufficient to understand.

Come balestro frange, quando scocca
da troppa tesa, la sua corda e l’arco,
e con men foga l’asta il segno tocca,

As a crossbow breaks, when its string and boware overstretched,and the arrow strikes the target with less force,

sì scoppia’ io sottesso grave carco,
fuori sgorgando lagrime e sospiri,
e la voce allentò per lo suo varco.

So I broke beneath that heavy weight,pouring forth tears and sighs,and my voice weakened in its passage.

Ond’ ella a me: «Per entro i mie’ disiri,
che ti menavano ad amar lo bene
di là dal qual non è a che s’aspiri,

Then she to me: "Within my desires, which guided you to love the good beyond which nothing is to be yearned for,

quai fossi attraversati o quai catene
trovasti, per che del passare innanzi
dovessiti così spogliar la spene?

"What obstacles or what chains did you find, that you should have stripped yourself of hope, preventing you from advancing?"

E quali agevolezze o quali avanzi
ne la fronte de li altri si mostraro,
per che dovessi lor passeggiare anzi?».

And what advantages, or what gains,presented themselves on the faces of the others,that you should have walked before them?

Dopo la tratta d’un sospiro amaro,
a pena ebbi la voce che rispuose,
e le labbra a fatica la formaro.

After the drawing of a bitter sigh,I barely had the voice to respond,and with effort my lips formed it.

Piangendo dissi: «Le presenti cose
col falso lor piacer volser miei passi,
tosto che ’l vostro viso si nascose».

Weeping, I said: "These earthly things,with their false pleasures, turned my steps,as soon as your face was hidden."

Ed ella: «Se tacessi o se negassi
ciò che confessi, non fora men nota
la colpa tua: da tal giudice sassi!

And she said: "Whether you were silent or deniedwhat you confess, your fault would be no less known:such a judge sees all!"

Ma quando scoppia de la propria gota
l’accusa del peccato, in nostra corte
rivolge sé contra ’l taglio la rota.

But when the accusation of sin bursts forth from one’s own cheek, in our court, the wheel turns against the cut.

Tuttavia, perché mo vergogna porte
del tuo errore, e perché altra volta,
udendo le serene, sie più forte,

However, so that the shame of your mistake doesn’t torment you, and so that another time, hearing the Sirens, you might be stronger,

pon giù il seme del piangere e ascolta:
sì udirai come in contraria parte
mover dovieti mia carne sepolta.

Lay down the seed of weeping and listen:Yes, you will hear how, in the opposite direction,my buried flesh must move.

Mai non t’appresentò natura o arte
piacer, quanto le belle membra in ch’io
rinchiusa fui, e che so’ ’n terra sparte;

Never did nature or art present youpleasure equal to the beautiful limbs in which Iwas enclosed, and which are scattered on earth now;

e se ’l sommo piacer sì ti fallio
per la mia morte, qual cosa mortale
dovea poi trarre te nel suo disio?

and if the highest pleasure failed you sobecause of my death, what mortal thingcould then have drawn you into its desire?

Ben ti dovevi, per lo primo strale
de le cose fallaci, levar suso
di retro a me che non era più tale.

Because of that first arrow of deceptive things, you ought to have risen up after me, who was no longer the same.

Non ti dovea gravar le penne in giuso,
ad aspettar più colpo, o pargoletta
o altra novità con sì breve uso.

You shouldn't have weighed your wings down, waiting for a further blow, little girl, or some other new thing so quickly used.

Novo augelletto due o tre aspetta;
ma dinanzi da li occhi d’i pennuti
rete si spiega indarno o si saetta».

The little bird waits for one or two more; but before the eyes of the feathered ones, nets are spread in vain, or arrows are shot in vain.

Quali fanciulli, vergognando, muti
con li occhi a terra stannosi, ascoltando
e sé riconoscendo e ripentuti,

Like children, ashamed, silent, with eyes to the ground, they stand listening, recognizing themselves, and repentant.

tal mi stav’ io; ed ella disse: «Quando
per udir se’ dolente, alza la barba,
e prenderai più doglia riguardando».

Such was my posture; and she said: "When you are pained to hear, lift your beard, and you will find greater sorrow in seeing."

Con men di resistenza si dibarba
robusto cerro, o vero al nostral vento
o vero a quel de la terra di Iarba,

As if in resistance, a sturdy oak uproots itself, whether by the wind of our own land, or by the wind from the land of Iarbas.

ch’io non levai al suo comando il mento;
e quando per la barba il viso chiese,
ben conobbi il velen de l’argomento.

Because I did not lift my chin at his command; and when he grabbed my face by the beard, I knew well the poison of his words.

E come la mia faccia si distese,
posarsi quelle prime creature
da loro aspersïon l’occhio comprese;

And as my face relaxed, those first creatures, settling, their sprinkling veiled my sight;

e le mie luci, ancor poco sicure,
vider Beatrice volta in su la fiera
ch’è sola una persona in due nature.

And my eyes, still unsteady, saw Beatrice turn toward the beast, which is one person in two natures.

Sotto ’l suo velo e oltre la rivera
vincer pariemi più sé stessa antica,
vincer che l’altre qui, quand’ ella c’era.

Beneath her veil and beyond the river, she seemed to me to conquer herself more than before, to conquer herself more than the others here, when she was present.

Di penter sì mi punse ivi l’ortica,
che di tutte altre cose qual mi torse
più nel suo amor, più mi si fé nemica.

There, the nettle stung me so sharply that it twisted me more than all other things, into its love; it became my greatest enemy.

Tanta riconoscenza il cor mi morse,
ch’io caddi vinto; e quale allora femmi,
salsi colei che la cagion mi porse.

So much gratitude pierced my heart, I fell defeated; and only she who gave me the cause knows what I became then.

Poi, quando il cor virtù di fuor rendemmi,
la donna ch’io avea trovata sola
sopra me vidi, e dicea: «Tiemmi, tiemmi!».

Then, when my heart restored my outward strength, I saw the woman I had found alone above me, and she said: "Hold me, hold me!"

Tratto m’avea nel fiume infin la gola,
e tirandosi me dietro sen giva
sovresso l’acqua lieve come scola.

She had drawn me into the river up to my throat, and pulling me behind her, she moved across the water, light as a boat.

Quando fui presso a la beata riva,
‘Asperges me’ sì dolcemente udissi,
che nol so rimembrar, non ch’io lo scriva.

When I drew near the blessed shore, I heard "Asperges me" so sweetly that I cannot recall it, much less write it down.

La bella donna ne le braccia aprissi;
abbracciommi la testa e mi sommerse
ove convenne ch’io l’acqua inghiottissi.

The lovely woman opened her arms to me; she embraced my head and submerged me where I was forced to swallow the water.

Indi mi tolse, e bagnato m’offerse
dentro a la danza de le quattro belle;
e ciascuna del braccio mi coperse.

Then she took me, and, soaked, offered me within the dance of the four beauties; and each covered me with her arm.

«Noi siam qui ninfe e nel ciel siamo stelle;
pria che Beatrice discendesse al mondo,
fummo ordinate a lei per sue ancelle.

We are nymphs, and in the heavens, we are stars; before Beatrice descended into the world, we were ordained to be her handmaidens.

Merrenti a li occhi suoi; ma nel giocondo
lume ch’è dentro aguzzeranno i tuoi
le tre di là, che miran più profondo».

"You will bring them to her eyes; but in the joyful light within, yours will sharpen, as will the three over there, who see more profoundly."

Così cantando cominciaro; e poi
al petto del grifon seco menarmi,
ove Beatrice stava volta a noi.

Thus singing, they began; and then,they led me to the griffin's breast,where Beatrice stood turned toward us.

Disser: «Fa che le viste non risparmi;
posto t’avem dinanzi a li smeraldi
ond’ Amor già ti trasse le sue armi».

He said, "Don't hold back your gaze; we've placed you before the emeralds from which Love once drew his weapons."

Mille disiri più che fiamma caldi
strinsermi li occhi a li occhi rilucenti,
che pur sopra ’l grifone stavan saldi.

A thousand desires, hotter than flame,constrained my eyes to those shining eyes,which remained steady, even above the griffin.

Come in lo specchio il sol, non altrimenti
la doppia fiera dentro vi raggiava,
or con altri, or con altri reggimenti.

As the sun in a mirror, the double beast within them shone, now with one expression, now with another.

Pensa, lettor, s’io mi maravigliava,
quando vedea la cosa in sé star queta,
e ne l’idolo suo si trasmutava.

Reader, consider if I marveled, when I saw the thing itself remain still, and in its reflection, it was transformed.

Mentre che piena di stupore e lieta
l’anima mia gustava di quel cibo
che, saziando di sé, di sé asseta,

While my soul, full of wonder and joy, savored that food which, sating itself, also thirsts for itself,

sé dimostrando di più alto tribo
ne li atti, l’altre tre si fero avanti,
danzando al loro angelico caribo.

showing themselves of a loftier stock,the other three advanced in the dance,swaying to their angelic carib.

«Volgi, Beatrice, volgi li occhi santi»,
era la sua canzone, «al tuo fedele
che, per vederti, ha mossi passi tanti!

“Turn, Beatrice, turn your holy eyes,”was his song, “to your faithful onewho, to see you, has taken so many steps!”

Per grazia fa noi grazia che disvele
a lui la bocca tua, sì che discerna
la seconda bellezza che tu cele».

"By grace, bestow on us the grace that you unveilyour mouth to him, so that he may discernthe second beauty that you conceal."

O isplendor di viva luce etterna,
chi palido si fece sotto l’ombra
sì di Parnaso, o bevve in sua cisterna,

O splendor of living, eternal light,who grew pale beneath the shade, so muchof Parnassus, or drank from its cistern,

che non paresse aver la mente ingombra,
tentando a render te qual tu paresti
là dove armonizzando il ciel t’adombra,

that the mind not seem encumbered,trying to render you as you appearedthere where, harmonizing, the heavens shadow you.

quando ne l’aere aperto ti solvesti?

When did you disrobe in the open air?

Paradiso * Canto XII

As the song of the first circle of twelve souls ceases, a second, identical circle of twelve spirits materializes, encircling the first. The two concentric rings of light rotate in unison and sing together. A new voice, identified as Saint Bonaventure of the Franciscan order, addresses Dante from the second circle. Bonaventure proceeds to deliver a eulogy for Saint Dominic, founder of the Dominican order. He recounts Dominic’s life, his devoutness from birth, and his fervent battle against heresy. After praising Dominic, Bonaventure laments the state of his own Franciscan order, stating that many of its members have deviated from the path established by their founder, Saint Francis. He concludes by identifying the eleven other souls who comprise his circle, including the figures Illuminato and Augustine, after which the two rings of spirits fall silent.

Paradiso • Canto XII

Sì tosto come l’ultima parola
la benedetta fiamma per dir tolse,
a rotar cominciò la santa mola;

As soon as the blessed flame finished speaking its final word, the holy mill began to turn.

e nel suo giro tutta non si volse
prima ch’un’altra di cerchio la chiuse,
e moto a moto e canto a canto colse;

and in its turning it did not complete itselfbefore another closed it off with a circle,and caught motion to motion and song to song;

canto che tanto vince nostre muse,
nostre serene in quelle dolci tube,
quanto primo splendor quel ch’e’ refuse.

a song that so surpasses our muses, our sirens in those sweet pipes, as the first light surpasses that which it refused.

Come si volgon per tenera nube
due archi paralelli e concolori,
quando Iunone a sua ancella iube,

As in a tender cloud there curvetwo parallel and like-colored bows,when Juno bids her handmaid to serve,

nascendo di quel d’entro quel di fori,
a guisa del parlar di quella vaga
ch’amor consunse come sol vapori,

arising from the inner to the outer, like the speech of that lovely one whom love consumed as the sun does vapors,

e fanno qui la gente esser presaga,
per lo patto che Dio con Noè puose,
del mondo che già mai più non s’allaga:

and here make people prescient, through the covenant that God made with Noah, of the world that will never again be flooded:

così di quelle sempiterne rose
volgiensi circa noi le due ghirlande,
e sì l’estrema a l’intima rispuose.

Thus, from those everlasting rosesthe two garlands turned toward us,and the outermost responded to the innermost.

Poi che ’l tripudio e l’altra festa grande,
sì del cantare e sì del fiammeggiarsi
luce con luce gaudïose e blande,

Once the rejoicing and the other great celebration,of song and of flashing,light with light, joyous and gentle,

insieme a punto e a voler quetarsi,
pur come li occhi ch’al piacer che i move
conviene insieme chiudere e levarsi;

together with the moment and the desire to be still,even as the eyes, which pleasure stirs,must close and rise together.

del cor de l’una de le luci nove
si mosse voce, che l’ago a la stella
parer mi fece in volgermi al suo dove;

From the heart of one of the new lights, a voice arose, which made me turn as if toward a star, like a needle.

e cominciò: «L’amor che mi fa bella
mi tragge a ragionar de l’altro duca
per cui del mio sì ben ci si favella.

And he began: "The love that makes me beautiful compels me to speak of the other leader, through whom my own is so well spoken of."

Degno è che, dov’ è l’un, l’altro s’induca:
sì che, com’ elli ad una militaro,
così la gloria loro insieme luca.

It is fitting that where one is, the other is drawn: so that, as they fought together, their glory might shine together.

L’essercito di Cristo, che sì caro
costò a rïarmar, dietro a la ’nsegna
si movea tardo, sospeccioso e raro,

The army of Christ, so dearly bought for rearmament, moved slowly, suspiciously, and sparsely behind the banner.

quando lo ’mperador che sempre regna
provide a la milizia, ch’era in forse,
per sola grazia, non per esser degna;

When the Emperor, who always reigns, provided for the militia, which was faltering, by grace alone, not because it deserved it.

e, come è detto, a sua sposa soccorse
con due campioni, al cui fare, al cui dire
lo popol disvïato si raccorse.

And, as it's told, he aided his bridewith two champions, through whose deeds, whose wordsthe straying populace turned back to the fold.

In quella parte ove surge ad aprire
Zefiro dolce le novelle fronde
di che si vede Europa rivestire,

In that place where Zephyr rises to openthe sweet new leaves, by which Europe is seento be clothed,

non molto lungi al percuoter de l’onde
dietro a le quali, per la lunga foga,
lo sol talvolta ad ogne uom si nasconde,

not far from where the waves crash,behind which, through their long rush,the sun sometimes hides itself from everyone,

siede la fortunata Calaroga
sotto la protezion del grande scudo
in che soggiace il leone e soggioga:

sits fortunate Calaroga,under the protection of the great shieldin which the lion lies and is subdued:

dentro vi nacque l’amoroso drudo
de la fede cristiana, il santo atleta
benigno a’ suoi e a’ nemici crudo;

Within was born the loving championof the Christian faith, the holy athlete,kind to his own, and to his enemies cruel;

e come fu creata, fu repleta
sì la sua mente di viva vertute
che, ne la madre, lei fece profeta.

And as it was created, so was it filled,her mind so full of living virtuethat, in her mother, she was made a prophet.

Poi che le sponsalizie fuor compiute
al sacro fonte intra lui e la Fede,
u’ si dotar di mutüa salute,

After the marriage was fulfilledat the sacred font between him and Faith,where they endowed each other with mutual health,

la donna che per lui l’assenso diede,
vide nel sonno il mirabile frutto
ch’uscir dovea di lui e de le rede;

The woman who gave her consent for him,saw in a dream the marvelous fruitthat was to come forth from him and his heirs;

e perché fosse qual era in costrutto,
quinci si mosse spirito a nomarlo
del possessivo di cui era tutto.

And so that he would be as he was in his structure, a spirit moved to name him with the possessive pronoun of which he was entirely comprised.

Domenico fu detto; e io ne parlo
sì come de l’agricola che Cristo
elesse a l’orto suo per aiutarlo.

Domenic was his name; and I speak of himas of the farmer whom Christ chose for his garden,to help him tend it.

Ben parve messo e famigliar di Cristo:
che ’l primo amor che ’n lui fu manifesto,
fu al primo consiglio che diè Cristo.

He seemed a messenger and intimate of Christ, for the first love that was made manifest in him was shown at Christ’s first counsel.

Spesse fïate fu tacito e desto
trovato in terra da la sua nutrice,
come dicesse: ‘Io son venuto a questo’.

Often, he was found silent and awake on the ground by his nurse, as if he were saying, “I have come for this.”

Oh padre suo veramente Felice!
oh madre sua veramente Giovanna,
se, interpretata, val come si dice!

Oh, truly blessed father!Oh, truly Joanna, his mother,if she is understood as they say!

Non per lo mondo, per cui mo s’affanna
di retro ad Ostïense e a Taddeo,
ma per amor de la verace manna

Not for the world, for which it now labors,following Ostiense and Thaddeus,but for love of the true manna.

in picciol tempo gran dottor si feo;
tal che si mise a circüir la vigna
che tosto imbianca, se ’l vignaio è reo.

In a short while, he became a great doctor, so much so that he set about circling the vineyard, which quickly whitens if the vinedresser is wicked.

E a la sedia che fu già benigna
più a’ poveri giusti, non per lei,
ma per colui che siede, che traligna,

And to the chair that once was kindestto the poor and just, not for its own sake,but for him who sits there, who goes astray,

non dispensare o due o tre per sei,
non la fortuna di prima vacante,
non decimas, quae sunt pauperum Dei,

Not to give out two or three for six,Not the vacant fortune of the first,Not tithes, which are the God of the poor.

addimandò, ma contro al mondo errante
licenza di combatter per lo seme
del qual ti fascian ventiquattro piante.

he asked, but against the wandering world,for permission to fight for the seedaround which twenty-four plants entwine you.

Poi, con dottrina e con volere insieme,
con l’officio appostolico si mosse
quasi torrente ch’alta vena preme;

Then, with learning and will combined,with apostolic duty he movedlike a torrent that a high vein constrains;

e ne li sterpi eretici percosse
l’impeto suo, più vivamente quivi
dove le resistenze eran più grosse.

And he struck the heretical thicketswith his force, most vividly therewhere the resistances were strongest.

Di lui si fecer poi diversi rivi
onde l’orto catolico si riga,
sì che i suoi arbuscelli stan più vivi.

From him, then, various streams arose,with which the Catholic garden is irrigated,so that its shrubs are more alive.

Se tal fu l’una rota de la biga
in che la Santa Chiesa si difese
e vinse in campo la sua civil briga,

If such was one wheel of the chariotin which the Holy Church defended herselfand won in the field her civil strife,

ben ti dovrebbe assai esser palese
l’eccellenza de l’altra, di cui Tomma
dinanzi al mio venir fu sì cortese.

Then it should be quite clear to youthe excellence of the other, of which Thomaswas so courteous before my arrival.

Ma l’orbita che fé la parte somma
di sua circunferenza, è derelitta,
sì ch’è la muffa dov’ era la gromma.

But the orbit that once formed the highest part of its circumference is deserted, so that mold now exists where once was the core.

La sua famiglia, che si mosse dritta
coi piedi a le sue orme, è tanto volta,
che quel dinanzi a quel di retro gitta;

His family, which moved directlyin his footsteps, has turned so muchthat the one in front pushes the one behind;

e tosto si vedrà de la ricolta
de la mala coltura, quando il loglio
si lagnerà che l’arca li sia tolta.

And soon will be seen the harvestof the bad cultivation, when the tareswill lament that the barn is denied them.

Ben dico, chi cercasse a foglio a foglio
nostro volume, ancor troveria carta
u’ leggerebbe “I’ mi son quel ch’i’ soglio”;

Well I say, whoever sought, page by page,through our volume, would still find a placewhere they could read, “I am what I have always been”;

ma non fia da Casal né d’Acquasparta,
là onde vegnon tali a la scrittura,
ch’uno la fugge e altro la coarta.

But not from Casal nor Acquasparta will come those who, in their writings,either flee from it or force it to their will.

Io son la vita di Bonaventura
da Bagnoregio, che ne’ grandi offici
sempre pospuosi la sinistra cura.

I am the life of Bonaventure,from Bagnoregio, who in the great officesalways set aside the care for the left.

Illuminato e Augustin son quici,
che fuor de’ primi scalzi poverelli
che nel capestro a Dio si fero amici.

Illuminato and Augustine are here, who were among the first barefoot paupers to become friends with God through the noose.

Ugo da San Vittore è qui con elli,
e Pietro Mangiadore e Pietro Spano,
lo qual giù luce in dodici libelli;

Hugh of Saint Victor is here with him, and Peter Lombard, and Peter of Spain, who sheds light below in twelve volumes.

Natàn profeta e ’l metropolitano
Crisostomo e Anselmo e quel Donato
ch’a la prim’ arte degnò porre mano.

Nathan the prophet, and the metropolitan Chrysostom, and Anselm, and that Donatus who deigned to lay a hand upon the first art.

Rabano è qui, e lucemi dallato
il calavrese abate Giovacchino
di spirito profetico dotato.

Rabanus is here, and at my side shinesthe Calabrian Abbot Joachim,endowed with the prophetic spirit.

Ad inveggiar cotanto paladino
mi mosse l’infiammata cortesia
di fra Tommaso e ’l discreto latino;

To urge on such a champion, I was movedby the inflamed courtesy of Friar Thomasand his discerning Latin.

e mosse meco questa compagnia».

and this company moved with me."

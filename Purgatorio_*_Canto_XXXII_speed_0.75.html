Purgatorio * Canto XXXII

Dante watches as the procession halts at a tall, bare tree. The Griffin ties the chariot to the trunk, causing the tree to instantly burst into leaf and flower. A hymn is sung, and Dante falls into a deep sleep. He is awakened by Matelda to find that the procession has departed, leaving only Beatrice seated on the roots of the tree with seven nymphs. Beatrice instructs Dante to watch closely. An eagle swoops down, strikes the tree, and batters the chariot, leaving it covered in feathers. A lean fox appears and attempts to climb into the chariot but is driven away by Beatrice. The chariot then sprouts seven monstrous, horned heads. An unchaste woman appears seated inside, attended by a giant. When the woman glances at Dante, the giant becomes enraged, beats her, and then detaches the monstrous chariot from the tree, dragging it and the woman into the forest until they are out of sight.

Purgatorio • Canto XXXII

Tant’ eran li occhi miei fissi e attenti
a disbramarsi la decenne sete,
che li altri sensi m’eran tutti spenti.

So fixed and intent were my eyes,in their thirst to slake a decade's drought,that all my other senses were extinct.

Ed essi quinci e quindi avien parete
di non caler—così lo santo riso
a sé traéli con l’antica rete!—;

And they on this side and that made a wall,of indifference—so did the holy smiledraw them to itself with the ancient snare!—

quando per forza mi fu vòlto il viso
ver’ la sinistra mia da quelle dee,
perch’ io udi’ da loro un «Troppo fiso!»;

when by force my face was turnedto my left by those goddesses,because I heard them say, "You stare too much!";

e la disposizion ch’a veder èe
ne li occhi pur testé dal sol percossi,
sanza la vista alquanto esser mi fée.

And the state of things that is seenin the eyes, just now struck by the sun,caused me to be for a time without sight.

Ma poi ch’al poco il viso riformossi
(e dico ‘al poco’ per rispetto al molto
sensibile onde a forza mi rimossi),

But after my sight had somewhat recovered—and I say ‘somewhat’ in deference to the intensesensitivity that forced me to turn away—

vidi ’n sul braccio destro esser rivolto
lo glorïoso essercito, e tornarsi
col sole e con le sette fiamme al volto.

I saw on the right arm the glorious army turn, and return, facing the sun and the seven flames.

Come sotto li scudi per salvarsi
volgesi schiera, e sé gira col segno,
prima che possa tutta in sé mutarsi;

As under shields to save themselves,the company turns, circling with its banner,before it can wholly transform.

quella milizia del celeste regno
che procedeva, tutta trapassonne
pria che piegasse il carro il primo legno.

That militia of the celestial kingdom,which was proceeding, passed through them allbefore the first wood bent the chariot.

Indi a le rote si tornar le donne,
e ’l grifon mosse il benedetto carco
sì, che però nulla penna crollonne.

India turned the wheels toward the women, and the griffin moved the blessed burden such that not a single feather was disturbed.

La bella donna che mi trasse al varco
e Stazio e io seguitavam la rota
che fé l’orbita sua con minore arco.

The beautiful woman who had drawn me to the opening, Stazio and I followed the wheel, which made its orbit in a smaller arc.

Sì passeggiando l’alta selva vòta,
colpa di quella ch’al serpente crese,
temprava i passi un’angelica nota.

While we walked through the high, empty wood, a fault of her who believed the serpent, a song from an angel tempered our steps.

Forse in tre voli tanto spazio prese
disfrenata saetta, quanto eramo
rimossi, quando Bëatrice scese.

Perhaps in three swift flights, that unbridled arrow traveled a distance equal to how far we had moved when Beatrice descended.

Io senti’ mormorare a tutti «Adamo»;
poi cerchiaro una pianta dispogliata
di foglie e d’altra fronda in ciascun ramo.

Then I heard everyone murmur "Adam"; they circled a plant stripped bare of leaves and every other branch.

La coma sua, che tanto si dilata
più quanto più è sù, fora da l’Indi
ne’ boschi lor per altezza ammirata.

Its coma, which expands so,the higher up it grows, is found beyond the Indies,in their forests, admired for its height.

«Beato se’, grifon, che non discindi
col becco d’esto legno dolce al gusto,
poscia che mal si torce il ventre quindi».

“Blessed are you, Griffin, who does not rendthis wood with your beak, so sweet to taste,since it badly twists the belly from this point.”

Così dintorno a l’albero robusto
gridaron li altri; e l’animal binato:
«Sì si conserva il seme d’ogne giusto».

Thus around the sturdy tree the others cried out, and the twofold beast replied: “Thus is preserved the seed of every just one.”

E vòlto al temo ch’elli avea tirato,
trasselo al piè de la vedova frasca,
e quel di lei a lei lasciò legato.

And turning to the stem it had drawn, it pulled it to the foot of the barren branch, and left that of it bound to her.

Come le nostre piante, quando casca
giù la gran luce mischiata con quella
che raggia dietro a la celeste lasca,

As our plants, when the great light descends,mixed with that which radiates behindthe celestial constellation,

turgide fansi, e poi si rinovella
di suo color ciascuna, pria che ’l sole
giunga li suoi corsier sotto altra stella;

become swollen, and then each one is renewedin its hue, before the sunyokes its steeds beneath another star.

men che di rose e più che di vïole
colore aprendo, s’innovò la pianta,
che prima avea le ramora sì sole.

But displaying colors more vibrant than roses or violets, the plant was renewed, which before had only bare branches.

Io non lo ’ntesi, né qui non si canta
l’inno che quella gente allor cantaro,
né la nota soffersi tutta quanta.

I did not understand it, nor is the hymn sung here that those people sang then, nor did I endure the whole note.

S’io potessi ritrar come assonnaro
li occhi spietati udendo di Siringa,
li occhi a cui pur vegghiar costò sì caro;

If I could depict how those pitiless eyes, hearing of Syrinx, grew drowsy, those eyes for whom wakefulness had cost so dear;

come pintor che con essempro pinga,
disegnerei com’ io m’addormentai;
ma qual vuol sia che l’assonnar ben finga.

Like a painter who with an example paints, I would sketch how I fell asleep; but he would be a fool who feigned sleep well.

Però trascorro a quando mi svegliai,
e dico ch’un splendor mi squarciò ’l velo
del sonno, e un chiamar: «Surgi: che fai?».

But I'll skip ahead to when I woke, and I say that a brightness tore the veil of sleep from me, and a calling: "Arise: what are you doing?"

Quali a veder de’ fioretti del melo
che del suo pome li angeli fa ghiotti
e perpetüe nozze fa nel cielo,

As if beholding the blossoms of the apple tree,whose fruit makes angels crave,and forever celebrates a wedding in heaven,

Pietro e Giovanni e Iacopo condotti
e vinti, ritornaro a la parola
da la qual furon maggior sonni rotti,

Peter and John and James, having been led and overcome, returned to the word that had shattered their deepest slumbers.

e videro scemata loro scuola
così di Moïsè come d’Elia,
e al maestro suo cangiata stola;

and saw diminished their company,of both Moses and Elijah,and their master's changed raiment;

tal torna’ io, e vidi quella pia
sovra me starsi che conducitrice
fu de’ miei passi lungo ’l fiume pria.

Then I turned back, and saw that holy one,above me, who had been the guideof my steps beside the river.

E tutto in dubbio dissi: «Ov’ è Beatrice?».
Ond’ ella: «Vedi lei sotto la fronda
nova sedere in su la sua radice.

And I, in doubt, said: “Where is Beatrice?”Then she replied: “You see her beneath the freshfoliage, seated upon its root.”

Vedi la compagnia che la circonda:
li altri dopo ’l grifon sen vanno suso
con più dolce canzone e più profonda».

See the company that surrounds her; the others go upward after the griffin, with a sweeter and more profound song.

E se più fu lo suo parlar diffuso,
non so, però che già ne li occhi m’era
quella ch’ad altro intender m’avea chiuso.

And if her words were more expansive, I do not know, because already in my eyes was she who had closed me to all other understanding.

Sola sedeasi in su la terra vera,
come guardia lasciata lì del plaustro
che legar vidi a la biforme fera.

Alone you sat upon the solid ground,like a guard left there from the wagonthat I saw bound to the two-formed beast.

In cerchio le facevan di sé claustro
le sette ninfe, con quei lumi in mano
che son sicuri d’Aquilone e d’Austro.

The seven nymphs formed a cloister around you, with those lamps in their hands, secure from the North Wind and the South.

«Qui sarai tu poco tempo silvano;
e sarai meco sanza fine cive
di quella Roma onde Cristo è romano.

"Here you shall be a woodland creature no longer;and with me you will be a citizen without endof that Rome for which Christ is Roman."

Però, in pro del mondo che mal vive,
al carro tieni or li occhi, e quel che vedi,
ritornato di là, fa che tu scrive».

But, for the sake of the world that lives in sin, now fix your eyes on the chariot, and what you see, when you return, be sure to write it down.”

Così Beatrice; e io, che tutto ai piedi
d’i suoi comandamenti era divoto,
la mente e li occhi ov’ ella volle diedi.

Thus Beatrice; and I, who was wholly devoted to her commands, gave my mind and eyes to where she wished.

Non scese mai con sì veloce moto
foco di spessa nube, quando piove
da quel confine che più va remoto,

Never descended fire with such swift motion from a thick cloud, when it rains from that boundary which is most distant,

com’ io vidi calar l’uccel di Giove
per l’alber giù, rompendo de la scorza,
non che d’i fiori e de le foglie nove;

as I saw Jove's bird descend,breaking the bark as it fell,not just flowers and new leaves.

e ferì ’l carro di tutta sua forza;
ond’ el piegò come nave in fortuna,
vinta da l’onda, or da poggia, or da orza.

and struck the chariot with all its might;whereupon it bent like a ship in a storm,overcome by the wave, now by the wind, now by the helm.

Poscia vidi avventarsi ne la cuna
del trïunfal veiculo una volpe
che d’ogne pasto buon parea digiuna;

Then I saw a fox leap into the triumphal cradle, appearing starved of every good meal.

ma, riprendendo lei di laide colpe,
la donna mia la volse in tanta futa
quanto sofferser l’ossa sanza polpe.

Then, taking up her foul accusations, my lady turned her into such a repulsive heap as to leave her bones without flesh.

Poscia per indi ond’ era pria venuta,
l’aguglia vidi scender giù ne l’arca
del carro e lasciar lei di sé pennuta;

Then, from where she had first appeared, I saw the eagle descend into the cart's ark and leave it feathered with her plumes.

e qual esce di cuor che si rammarca,
tal voce uscì del cielo e cotal disse:
«O navicella mia, com’ mal se’ carca!».

And as a voice escapes a heart in grief, so that voice issued forth from the heavens, saying thus: "Oh, my little ship, how wretchedly burdened you are!"

Poi parve a me che la terra s’aprisse
tr’ambo le ruote, e vidi uscirne un drago
che per lo carro sù la coda fisse;

Then it seemed to me that the earth opened up between the two wheels, and I saw a dragon emerge, fixing its tail upward through the chariot.

e come vespa che ritragge l’ago,
a sé traendo la coda maligna,
trasse del fondo, e gissen vago vago.

And like a wasp that withdraws its stinger, drawing its malicious tail to itself, it pulled from the depths, and went away, vaguely, vaguely.

Quel che rimase, come da gramigna
vivace terra, da la piuma, offerta
forse con intenzion sana e benigna,

What remained, like a patch of tenacious weedsfrom lively earth, from the feather, offeredperhaps with sound and benevolent intention,

si ricoperse, e funne ricoperta
e l’una e l’altra rota e ’l temo, in tanto
che più tiene un sospir la bocca aperta.

They covered themselves, and became covered,both wheels and the wagon's shaft, in a timeshorter than a sigh holds the mouth open.

Trasformato così ’l dificio santo
mise fuor teste per le parti sue,
tre sovra ’l temo e una in ciascun canto.

Thus transformed, the holy edificethrust out heads in every direction:three above the shaft, and one at each side.

Le prime eran cornute come bue,
ma le quattro un sol corno avean per fronte:
simile mostro visto ancor non fue.

The first were horned like oxen,but the other four had a single horn on their foreheads:such a monster had never been seen.

Sicura, quasi rocca in alto monte,
seder sovresso una puttana sciolta
m’apparve con le ciglia intorno pronte;

Secure, like a fortress on a high mountain, sat a loose whore above me, her eyes darting about.

e come perché non li fosse tolta,
vidi di costa a lei dritto un gigante;
e basciavansi insieme alcuna volta.

And as if she would not be taken from him,I saw a giant standing beside her;And they kissed each other from time to time.

Ma perché l’occhio cupido e vagante
a me rivolse, quel feroce drudo
la flagellò dal capo infin le piante;

But because her roving, covetous eye turned to me, that fierce bully whipped her from head to foot.

poi, di sospetto pieno e d’ira crudo,
disciolse il mostro, e trassel per la selva,
tanto che sol di lei mi fece scudo

Then, full of suspicion and cruel anger, he released the monster, and dragged it through the wood, until it made me its only shield.

a la puttana e a la nova belva.

To the whore and to the new beast.

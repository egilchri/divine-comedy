Purgatorio * Canto XXXIII

The canto opens with the seven virtues singing a psalm. Beatrice, visibly sorrowful, rises and leads a procession away from the Tree of Knowledge. Dante, Matelda, and Statius follow her. After a short walk, Beatrice stops and addresses Dante directly. She delivers an obscure prophecy concerning an avenger, a "Five Hundred, Ten, and Five," who will kill the giant and the thief who damaged the Chariot. She instructs Dante to record her words for the living. When Dante states that he has no memory of ever being estranged from her, Beatrice explains that his amnesia is proof of his sin. The group arrives at the source of two rivers: Lethe and Eunoë. Matelda clarifies that Dante has already passed through the Lethe, which erases the memory of sin. At Beatrice’s command, Matelda leads Dante to the Eunoë. Dante drinks from its waters and feels completely renewed, pure, and ready to ascend to the stars.

Purgatorio • Canto XXXIII

‘Deus, venerunt gentes’, alternando
or tre or quattro dolce salmodia,
le donne incominciaro, e lagrimando;

"God, the heathen have come," alternating,three or four sweet psalms,the women began, and weeping;

e Bëatrice, sospirosa e pia,
quelle ascoltava sì fatta, che poco
più a la croce si cambiò Maria.

And Beatrice, sighing and pious, listened to them, so moved that she resembled Mary at the foot of the cross.

Ma poi che l’altre vergini dier loco
a lei di dir, levata dritta in pè,
rispuose, colorata come foco:

But after the other virgins gave her leave to speak, she stood up straight, and, colored like fire, responded:

‘Modicum, et non videbitis me;
et iterum, sorelle mie dilette,
modicum, et vos videbitis me’.

‘A little while, and you will not see me;and again, my beloved sisters,a little while, and you will see me.’

Poi le si mise innanzi tutte e sette,
e dopo sé, solo accennando, mosse
me e la donna e ’l savio che ristette.

Then he placed them all seven before him, and behind him, gesturing only, he moved me, the woman, and the wise one who remained behind.

Così sen giva; e non credo che fosse
lo decimo suo passo in terra posto,
quando con li occhi li occhi mi percosse;

Thus he went; and I don't believe his tenth stephad yet been placed upon the ground,when his eyes struck mine.

e con tranquillo aspetto «Vien più tosto»,
mi disse, «tanto che, s’io parlo teco,
ad ascoltarmi tu sie ben disposto».

And with a tranquil air, he said to me, "Come closer, so that, if I speak with you, you will be well-disposed to listen."

Sì com’ io fui, com’ io dovëa, seco,
dissemi: «Frate, perché non t’attenti
a domandarmi omai venendo meco?».

As soon as I was near, as I was meant to be, he said to me, "Brother, why don't you make an effort to ask questions, now that you're coming with me?"

Come a color che troppo reverenti
dinanzi a suo maggior parlando sono,
che non traggon la voce viva ai denti,

Like one who, overly reverent, stands before a superior, speaking, and cannot force a lively voice from his teeth,

avvenne a me, che sanza intero suono
incominciai: «Madonna, mia bisogna
voi conoscete, e ciò ch’ad essa è buono».

It happened to me that, without a clear sound, I began: "My Lady, you know my need, and what is good for it."

Ed ella a me: «Da tema e da vergogna
voglio che tu omai ti disviluppe,
sì che non parli più com’ om che sogna.

And she to me: "From fear and from shameI want you now to cast off,so that you speak no longer like a man who dreams."

Sappi che ’l vaso che ’l serpente ruppe,
fu e non è; ma chi n’ha colpa, creda
che vendetta di Dio non teme suppe.

Know that the vessel which the serpent shattered,existed and exists no more; but let he who is at fault believethat God's vengeance fears no offerings of supplication.

Non sarà tutto tempo sanza reda
l’aguglia che lasciò le penne al carro,
per che divenne mostro e poscia preda;

It won't be long before the eagle that abandoned its feathers to the chariot gets its revenge, becoming a monster and then prey.

ch’io veggio certamente, e però il narro,
a darne tempo già stelle propinque,
secure d’ogn’ intoppo e d’ogne sbarro,

for I see it clearly, and so I tell it, that the stars are already close, giving us the time, free of any obstacle and any barrier.

nel quale un cinquecento diece e cinque,
messo di Dio, anciderà la fuia
con quel gigante che con lei delinque.

In which a five-hundred-and-fifteen,sent by God, will slay the whore,along with the giant who sins with her.

E forse che la mia narrazion buia,
qual Temi e Sfinge, men ti persuade,
perch’ a lor modo lo ’ntelletto attuia;

And perhaps my obscure narration, like Themis and the Sphinx, convinces you less, because it dulls the intellect in their manner.

ma tosto fier li fatti le Naiade,
che solveranno questo enigma forte
sanza danno di pecore o di biade.

But soon the Naiads will make the facts clear,and they will solve this difficult riddlewithout harm to sheep or to grain.

Tu nota; e sì come da me son porte,
così queste parole segna a’ vivi
del viver ch’è un correre a la morte.

Note this, and as they come from me,so mark these words for those alive,of a life that is a running to death.

E aggi a mente, quando tu le scrivi,
di non celar qual hai vista la pianta
ch’è or due volte dirubata quivi.

And add this to your notes when you write,do not conceal how you saw the plant,which has now twice been torn up here.

Qualunque ruba quella o quella schianta,
con bestemmia di fatto offende a Dio,
che solo a l’uso suo la creò santa.

Whoever steals that or tears that down,in fact, with blasphemy, offends God,who created it holy only for His use.

Per morder quella, in pena e in disio
cinquemilia anni e più l’anima prima
bramò colui che ’l morso in sé punio.

For murdering her, in torment and desire,five thousand years and more, the primal soullonged for Him who punished the bite within itself.

Dorme lo ’ngegno tuo, se non estima
per singular cagione esser eccelsa
lei tanto e sì travolta ne la cima.

Your intellect slumbers if it doesn’t believe, for a singular reason, that she is so exalted and so transformed at the summit.

E se stati non fossero acqua d’Elsa
li pensier vani intorno a la tua mente,
e ’l piacer loro un Piramo a la gelsa,

And if your thoughts, surrounding your mind, were not like the waters of the Elsa, and their pleasure a Pyramus to the mulberry tree,

per tante circostanze solamente
la giustizia di Dio, ne l’interdetto,
conosceresti a l’arbor moralmente.

Through so many circumstances alone,God’s justice, not the interdict,you would know morally in the tree.

Ma perch’ io veggio te ne lo ’ntelletto
fatto di pietra e, impetrato, tinto,
sì che t’abbaglia il lume del mio detto,

But because I see within your intellecta heart of stone, turned to stone, and stained,so that the light of my words blinds you,

voglio anco, e se non scritto, almen dipinto,
che ’l te ne porti dentro a te per quello
che si reca il bordon di palma cinto».

I want it also, if not written, then painted at least,that you carry it within yourself, by that meansthat the palm-cinctured pilgrim brings back with him.

E io: «Sì come cera da suggello,
che la figura impressa non trasmuta,
segnato è or da voi lo mio cervello.

And I: "Just as wax for a seal,that does not alter the imprint it receives,so now is my mind marked by you."

Ma perché tanto sovra mia veduta
vostra parola disïata vola,
che più la perde quanto più s’aiuta?».

And I: "Why does your desired speech fly so far above my vision, so that I lose it the more it helps me?"

«Perché conoschi», disse, «quella scuola
c’hai seguitata, e veggi sua dottrina
come può seguitar la mia parola;

“Because you understand,” he said, “that school you've followed, and you see how its teachings can keep pace with my words;

e veggi vostra via da la divina
distar cotanto, quanto si discorda
da terra il ciel che più alto festina».

and you see how far your path diverges from the divine, as much as the heavens, which hasten highest, are distant from the earth.”

Ond’ io rispuosi lei: «Non mi ricorda
ch’i’ stranïasse me già mai da voi,
né honne coscïenza che rimorda».

Then I answered her: "I don't recall ever having strayed from you, nor do I have any sense of guilt to weigh on me."

«E se tu ricordar non te ne puoi»,
sorridendo rispuose, «or ti rammenta
come bevesti di Letè ancoi;

"And if you can't recall," she replied, smiling, "now remember how you drank from Lethe today."

e se dal fummo foco s’argomenta,
cotesta oblivïon chiaro conchiude
colpa ne la tua voglia altrove attenta.

And if fire is inferred from smoke, this oblivion clearly concludes that guilt lies in your desire, focused elsewhere.

Veramente oramai saranno nude
le mie parole, quanto converrassi
quelle scovrire a la tua vista rude».

Truly, by now my words will be bare, as much as they must be revealed to your rough gaze.

E più corusco e con più lenti passi
teneva il sole il cerchio di merigge,
che qua e là, come li aspetti, fassi,

And brighter still, with slower, measured tread,the sun now walked the circle of the day,where, here and there, the shapes are being made.

quando s’affisser, sì come s’affigge
chi va dinanzi a gente per iscorta
se trova novitate o sue vestigge,

when they became fixed, just as one is fixedwho goes before a people as their guideif he finds something new or the trace of it,

le sette donne al fin d’un’ombra smorta,
qual sotto foglie verdi e rami nigri
sovra suoi freddi rivi l’alpe porta.

The seven women, at the edge of a faded shade,like the Alps, which bear over their cold streamsshadows under green leaves and dark branches.

Dinanzi ad esse Ëufratès e Tigri
veder mi parve uscir d’una fontana,
e, quasi amici, dipartirsi pigri.

Before them, Euphrates and TigrisI seemed to see issuing from a fountain,and, like friends, parting slowly.

«O luce, o gloria de la gente umana,
che acqua è questa che qui si dispiega
da un principio e sé da sé lontana?».

"O light, o glory of humankind, what water is this that unfurls here from a source and then departs from itself?"

Per cotal priego detto mi fu: «Priega
Matelda che ’l ti dica». E qui rispuose,
come fa chi da colpa si dislega,

At this request, I was told: "Ask Matelda to tell you." And here she answered, like one who frees herself from blame,

la bella donna: «Questo e altre cose
dette li son per me; e son sicura
che l’acqua di Letè non gliel nascose».

The beautiful woman said: "This and other thingswere told to him by me; and I am surethe water of Lethe did not hide them from him."

E Bëatrice: «Forse maggior cura,
che spesse volte la memoria priva,
fatt’ ha la mente sua ne li occhi oscura.

Beatrice said: "Perhaps a greater concern,which often robs memory,has made his mind obscure in his eyes."

Ma vedi Eünoè che là diriva:
menalo ad esso, e come tu se’ usa,
la tramortita sua virtù ravviva».

But see Eunoe flowing there:lead him to it, and as you are accustomed,revive his weakened virtue.

Come anima gentil, che non fa scusa,
ma fa sua voglia de la voglia altrui
tosto che è per segno fuor dischiusa;

Come, gentle spirit, who makes no excuse,but makes their will from another's will,as soon as it is openly revealed as a sign;

così, poi che da essa preso fui,
la bella donna mossesi, e a Stazio
donnescamente disse: «Vien con lui».

Thus, once I was taken by her,the lovely woman moved, and to Statiussaid in a ladylike manner: "Come with him."

S’io avessi, lettor, più lungo spazio
da scrivere, i’ pur cantere’ in parte
lo dolce ber che mai non m’avria sazio;

If I had, reader, more spaceto write, I still would sing, in part,of the sweet drink that never would sate me;

ma perché piene son tutte le carte
ordite a questa cantica seconda,
non mi lascia più ir lo fren de l’arte.

But because all the pages are full,woven for this second canticle,the bridle of art no longer allows me to go on.

Io ritornai da la santissima onda
rifatto sì come piante novelle
rinovellate di novella fronda,

I returned from the holiest wave, remade, like new plants renewed with fresh foliage.

puro e disposto a salire a le stelle.

Pure and ready to ascend to the stars.

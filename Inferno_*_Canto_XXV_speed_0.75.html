Inferno * Canto XXV

The canto begins as the thief Vanni Fucci makes a blasphemous gesture at God. In immediate response, serpents wrap around his throat and arms, and he flees. A centaur named Cacus, his back covered in snakes and a dragon, pursues him. Dante and Virgil then witness three more souls, identified as Florentine nobles. A six-footed serpent attacks one of the men, Agnello. The two figures fuse together, their bodies and limbs melding into a single, monstrous new form that then departs. Next, a small, dark reptile bites another soul, Buoso, in the navel. The man and the serpent gaze at each other as they begin to exchange forms. Through a cloud of smoke, the man’s features soften and his legs join into a tail, while the serpent sprouts human limbs and adopts the man's face. The transformation completes, and the newly formed man walks away while the newly formed snake slithers off.

Inferno • Canto XXV

Al fine de le sue parole il ladro
le mani alzò con amendue le fiche,
gridando: «Togli, Dio, ch’a te le squadro!».

At the end of his words, the thief raised both hands with figs, shouting, "Take that, God, for I throw them at you!"

Da indi in qua mi fuor le serpi amiche,
perch’ una li s’avvolse allora al collo,
come dicesse ‘Non vo’ che più diche’;

From then on, the serpents became friendly toward me, because one wrapped itself around my neck, as if saying, "I don't want you to say anything more."

e un’altra a le braccia, e rilegollo,
ribadendo sé stessa sì dinanzi,
che non potea con esse dare un crollo.

and another to my arms, and bound them,knotting itself so tightly in front,that I could not even move them.

Ahi Pistoia, Pistoia, ché non stanzi
d’incenerarti sì che più non duri,
poi che ’n mal fare il seme tuo avanzi?

Ah, Pistoia, Pistoia, why do you refuseto be burnt to ash, so that you might no longer endure,since you excel in wickedness?

Per tutt’ i cerchi de lo ’nferno scuri
non vidi spirto in Dio tanto superbo,
non quel che cadde a Tebe giù da’ muri.

Through all the circles of dark HellI did not see a spirit so proud toward God,not even the one who fell from Thebes' walls.

El si fuggì che non parlò più verbo;
e io vidi un centauro pien di rabbia
venir chiamando: «Ov’ è, ov’ è l’acerbo?».

He fled, no longer uttering a word; and I saw a centaur, full of rage, come forth, crying, "Where is he, where is the harsh one?"

Maremma non cred’ io che tante n’abbia,
quante bisce elli avea su per la groppa
infin ove comincia nostra labbia.

Maremma doesn't, I believe, hold so many,as the snakes he had upon his backup to where our mouths begin.

Sovra le spalle, dietro da la coppa,
con l’ali aperte li giacea un draco;
e quello affuoca qualunque s’intoppa.

Upon his shoulders, behind the neck,a dragon lay with open wings;and it sets afire whomever it strikes.

Lo mio maestro disse: «Questi è Caco,
che, sotto ’l sasso di monte Aventino,
di sangue fece spesse volte laco.

My master said: “This is Cacus, who, beneath the rock of Mount Aventine, often made a lake of blood.”

Non va co’ suoi fratei per un cammino,
per lo furto che frodolente fece
del grande armento ch’elli ebbe a vicino;

He does not walk with his brothers along the same path, for the theft that he deceitfully committed, of the great herd that he had near him.

onde cessar le sue opere biece
sotto la mazza d’Ercule, che forse
gliene diè cento, e non sentì le diece».

where his wicked deeds ceasedbeneath the club of Hercules, who perhapsgave him a hundred blows, and he felt not ten.”

Mentre che sì parlava, ed el trascorse,
e tre spiriti venner sotto noi,
de’ quai né io né ’l duca mio s’accorse,

While he spoke thus, and moved on, three spirits came beneath us, of whom neither I nor my guide took notice.

se non quando gridar: «Chi siete voi?»;
per che nostra novella si ristette,
e intendemmo pur ad essi poi.

until they cried out: "Who are you?";at which our progress halted,and we turned our attention fully toward them.

Io non li conoscea; ma ei seguette,
come suol seguitar per alcun caso,
che l’un nomar un altro convenette,

I did not know them, but it happened, as it often does by chance, that one had to call out the other's name.

dicendo: «Cianfa dove fia rimaso?»;
per ch’io, acciò che ’l duca stesse attento,
mi puosi ’l dito su dal mento al naso.

saying, "Cianfa, where has he remained?";so that the guide would pay attention,I put my finger up from my chin to my nose.

Se tu se’ or, lettore, a creder lento
ciò ch’io dirò, non sarà maraviglia,
ché io che ’l vidi, a pena il mi consento.

If you, reader, are slow to believe what I will say, it will be no surprise, for I who saw it barely consent to it myself.

Com’ io tenea levate in lor le ciglia,
e un serpente con sei piè si lancia
dinanzi a l’uno, e tutto a lui s’appiglia.

As I kept my eyes fixed on them, a serpent with six feet launched itself forward, striking the first, and clung to him.

Co’ piè di mezzo li avvinse la pancia
e con li anterïor le braccia prese;
poi li addentò e l’una e l’altra guancia;

With its middle feet, it clasped his belly, and with the front feet seized his arms; then it bit both his cheeks.

li diretani a le cosce distese,
e miseli la coda tra ’mbedue
e dietro per le ren sù la ritese.

Then it directed its hind legs to his outstretched thighs, and thrust its tail between them both, and raised it up behind along his loins.

Ellera abbarbicata mai non fue
ad alber sì, come l’orribil fiera
per l’altrui membra avviticchiò le sue.

Never did ivy cling so tightly to a tree as the horrifying beast entwined its limbs with his.

Poi s’appiccar, come di calda cera
fossero stati, e mischiar lor colore,
né l’un né l’altro già parea quel ch’era:

Then they stuck, as if they were hot wax, and mixed their colors, and neither of them looked like what they had been before.

come procede innanzi da l’ardore,
per lo papiro suso, un color bruno
che non è nero ancora e ’l bianco more.

as they advance from the heat,a brown color rises across the papyrus,not yet black, but the white dies.

Li altri due ’l riguardavano, e ciascuno
gridava: «Omè, Agnel, come ti muti!
Vedi che già non se’ né due né uno».

The other two watched him, and each onecried out: "Alas, Lamb, how you change!See how you are no longer two nor one."

Già eran li due capi un divenuti,
quando n’apparver due figure miste
in una faccia, ov’ eran due perduti.

Already the two heads had become one, when there appeared to us two forms mixed together in a single face, where two were lost.

Fersi le braccia due di quattro liste;
le cosce con le gambe e ’l ventre e ’l casso
divenner membra che non fuor mai viste.

The arms morphed from two into four limbs; the thighs with the legs and the belly and the chest became a body never before seen.

Ogne primaio aspetto ivi era casso:
due e nessun l’imagine perversa
parea; e tal sen gio con lento passo.

The initial fiery visage was now gone: two and none, the perverse image appeared; and thus it departed with slow steps.

Come ’l ramarro sotto la gran fersa
dei dì canicular, cangiando sepe,
folgore par se la via attraversa,

Come a green lizard beneath the scorching lashof dog-day sun, changing its hue,a lightning bolt seems to cross the path.

sì pareva, venendo verso l’epe
de li altri due, un serpentello acceso,
livido e nero come gran di pepe;

So it seemed, coming toward the other two, a little snake aflame, livid and black as a peppercorn.

e quella parte onde prima è preso
nostro alimento, a l’un di lor trafisse;
poi cadde giuso innanzi lui disteso.

and pierced one of them through that part where our food first is taken; then fell, stretched out before him.

Lo trafitto ’l mirò, ma nulla disse;
anzi, co’ piè fermati, sbadigliava
pur come sonno o febbre l’assalisse.

He stared at the one he'd pierced, but said nothing; instead, with his feet planted, he yawned as though sleep or fever had overtaken him.

Elli ’l serpente e quei lui riguardava;
l’un per la piaga e l’altro per la bocca
fummavan forte, e ’l fummo si scontrava.

Elli looked upon the serpent, and the other man; from the wound, one spewed forth, and from the mouth of the other, smoke poured out, and the fumes collided.

Taccia Lucano ormai là dov’ e’ tocca
del misero Sabello e di Nasidio,
e attenda a udir quel ch’or si scocca.

Tacia Lucan now, where he meetsthe wretched Sabellius and Nasidius,and he waits to hear what is unleashed.

Taccia di Cadmo e d’Aretusa Ovidio,
ché se quello in serpente e quella in fonte
converte poetando, io non lo ’nvidio;

Ovid writes of Cadmus and Arethusa,for if he transforms the one to a serpent, the other to a spring,I do not envy him that skill;

ché due nature mai a fronte a fronte
non trasmutò sì ch’amendue le forme
a cambiar lor matera fosser pronte.

For he never transformed two natures, face to face, so that both forms were ready to change their substance.

Insieme si rispuosero a tai norme,
che ’l serpente la coda in forca fesse,
e ’l feruto ristrinse insieme l’orme.

Together they responded to those terms, so that the serpent split its tail in two, and the wounded man drew his legs back together.

Le gambe con le cosce seco stesse
s’appiccar sì, che ’n poco la giuntura
non facea segno alcun che si paresse.

The legs and thighs then rejoined each other, so that in a moment the healed spot showed no sign of where they’d been joined.

Togliea la coda fessa la figura
che si perdeva là, e la sua pelle
si facea molle, e quella di là dura.

The figure then shed the split tail, disappearing from view. Its skin became soft, and the other's hardened.

Io vidi intrar le braccia per l’ascelle,
e i due piè de la fiera, ch’eran corti,
tanto allungar quanto accorciavan quelle.

I saw the arms enter through the armpits, and the beast's two feet, which were short, lengthen as the others shrank.

Poscia li piè di rietro, insieme attorti,
diventaron lo membro che l’uom cela,
e ’l misero del suo n’avea due porti.

Then the hind feet, twisted together, became the member that a man conceals, and the wretched one had two of his own.

Mentre che ’l fummo l’uno e l’altro vela
di color novo, e genera ’l pel suso
per l’una parte e da l’altra il dipela,

While the smoke veils both of themwith a new color, and grows hair upwardon one side, and strips it away on the other,

l’un si levò e l’altro cadde giuso,
non torcendo però le lucerne empie,
sotto le quai ciascun cambiava muso.

One rose, and the other fell down,yet without twisting the impious lanterns,beneath which each of them changed their face.

Quel ch’era dritto, il trasse ver’ le tempie,
e di troppa matera ch’in là venne
uscir li orecchi de le gote scempie;

That which was straight he drew towards his temples, and from too much matter that came there, the ears emerged from the simple cheeks.

ciò che non corse in dietro e si ritenne
di quel soverchio, fé naso a la faccia
e le labbra ingrossò quanto convenne.

That which did not recede and remainedof that excess, formed the nose upon the face,and thickened the lips as was fitting.

Quel che giacëa, il muso innanzi caccia,
e li orecchi ritira per la testa
come face le corna la lumaccia;

That which lay still, thrust forward its snout,and drew its ears back over its headas the slug does its horns;

e la lingua, ch’avëa unita e presta
prima a parlar, si fende, e la forcuta
ne l’altro si richiude; e ’l fummo resta.

And the tongue, once nimble and quickin speech, now split, and the forked partreclosed in the other; and the smoke remained.

L’anima ch’era fiera divenuta,
suffolando si fugge per la valle,
e l’altro dietro a lui parlando sputa.

The soul, which had grown proud,hissing, fled through the valley,and the other spat as he spoke behind it.

Poscia li volse le novelle spalle,
e disse a l’altro: «I’ vo’ che Buoso corra,
com’ ho fatt’ io, carpon per questo calle».

Then he turned his new shoulders, and said to the other, "I want Buoso to crawl, as I have, on all fours along this path."

Così vid’ io la settima zavorra
mutare e trasmutare; e qui mi scusi
la novità se fior la penna abborra.

Thus I saw the seventh weightshift and change; and here, may novelty excusemy pen if it should falter.

E avvegna che li occhi miei confusi
fossero alquanto e l’animo smagato,
non poter quei fuggirsi tanto chiusi,

And although my eyes were clouded somewhat, and my mind weakened, they could not close themselves so tightly to avoid it,

ch’i’ non scorgessi ben Puccio Sciancato;
ed era quel che sol, di tre compagni
che venner prima, non era mutato;

that I didn't see Puccio Sciancato clearly; and he, alone of the three companions who came first, had not changed.

l’altr’ era quel che tu, Gaville, piagni.

The other was he for whom you weep, Gaville.

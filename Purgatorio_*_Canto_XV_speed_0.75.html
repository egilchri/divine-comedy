Purgatorio * Canto XV

As Dante and Virgil walk on the Terrace of Envy, the setting sun’s light is reflected toward them with blinding intensity. Virgil explains that an angel is approaching to guide them to the next terrace. The Angel of Mercy removes the second "P" from Dante's brow with its wing and recites the beatitude, “Blessed are the merciful,” before directing the poets up the stairway. As they ascend, Virgil begins a discourse on the nature of spiritual good, explaining that unlike material possessions which diminish when divided, divine grace increases the more it is shared. Upon reaching the Third Terrace, the Terrace of Wrath, Dante is overcome by an ecstatic trance. He witnesses three successive visions of meekness: the Virgin Mary gently questioning the young Jesus in the temple, the Athenian tyrant Pisistratus forgiving his daughter’s suitor, and Saint Stephen praying for the forgiveness of his executioners. As the visions end, a thick, dark smoke envelops the terrace.

Purgatorio • Canto XV

Quanto tra l’ultimar de l’ora terza
e ’l principio del dì par de la spera
che sempre a guisa di fanciullo scherza,

As much time as passes between the end of the third hour and the start of the day, as appears to the sphere that always plays like a child.

tanto pareva già inver’ la sera
essere al sol del suo corso rimaso;
vespero là, e qui mezza notte era.

So much of its course did the sun seem to have left, heading toward evening; there, it was dusk, and here, it was midnight.

E i raggi ne ferien per mezzo ’l naso,
perché per noi girato era sì ’l monte,
che già dritti andavamo inver’ l’occaso,

And the rays struck us through the nose, because the mountain had turned so for us, that now we were walking straight toward the west.

quand’ io senti’ a me gravar la fronte
a lo splendore assai più che di prima,
e stupor m’eran le cose non conte;

When I felt the light press upon my brow, more intensely than before, and the unfamiliar things filled me with wonder.

ond’ io levai le mani inver’ la cima
de le mie ciglia, e fecimi ’l solecchio,
che del soverchio visibile lima.

And I raised my hands toward the crest of my brow, making a shade, which lessened the excessive light.

Come quando da l’acqua o da lo specchio
salta lo raggio a l’opposita parte,
salendo su per lo modo parecchio

As when a ray leaps from water or a mirror to the opposite side, climbing upward in a manner similar to its descent.

a quel che scende, e tanto si diparte
dal cader de la pietra in igual tratta,
sì come mostra esperïenza e arte;

to that which falls, and it veers as far awayfrom the stone's descent in an equal measure,as experience and skill both demonstrate;

così mi parve da luce rifratta
quivi dinanzi a me esser percosso;
per che a fuggir la mia vista fu ratta.

So it seemed to me that I was struck by reflected light, there before me; because of this, my sight was quick to flee.

«Che è quel, dolce padre, a che non posso
schermar lo viso tanto che mi vaglia»,
diss’ io, «e pare inver’ noi esser mosso?».

"What is that, sweet father, that I can't protect my eyes from, however I try," I said, "and which seems to be moving toward us?"

«Non ti maravigliar s’ancor t’abbaglia
la famiglia del cielo», a me rispuose:
«messo è che viene ad invitar ch’om saglia.

"Don't be surprised if the family of Heaven still dazzles you," he answered me, "that one has come to invite someone to ascend."

Tosto sarà ch’a veder queste cose
non ti fia grave, ma fieti diletto
quanto natura a sentir ti dispuose».

Soon it will be that seeing these thingswill not be grievous to you, but will be delightful,in as much measure as nature has disposed you to feel.

Poi giunti fummo a l’angel benedetto,
con lieta voce disse: «Intrate quinci
ad un scaleo vie men che li altri eretto».

Then, we arrived at the blessed angel, who with a joyful voice said, "Enter here, to a stairway that is less steep than the others."

Noi montavam, già partiti di linci,
e ‘Beati misericordes!’ fue
cantato retro, e ‘Godi tu che vinci!’.

We climbed, already departed from the lynxes, and "Blessed are the merciful!" was sung behind us, and "Rejoice, you who overcome!".

Lo mio maestro e io soli amendue
suso andavamo; e io pensai, andando,
prode acquistar ne le parole sue;

My master and I alone, the two of us, went upward; and I thought, as we walked, to gain profit from his words.

e dirizza’mi a lui sì dimandando:
«Che volse dir lo spirto di Romagna,
e ‘divieto’ e ‘consorte’ menzionando?».

And I turned to him, thus asking: "What did the spirit of Romagna mean, mentioning 'prohibition' and 'companion'?"

Per ch’elli a me: «Di sua maggior magagna
conosce il danno; e però non s’ammiri
se ne riprende perché men si piagna.

He replied to me: "He recognizes the harm of his greatest failing, and therefore it is no wonder if he reproaches himself, so that he might suffer less."

Perché s’appuntano i vostri disiri
dove per compagnia parte si scema,
invidia move il mantaco a’ sospiri.

Because your desires are fixedwhere companionship diminishes,envy moves the heart to sighs.

Ma se l’amor de la spera supprema
torcesse in suso il disiderio vostro,
non vi sarebbe al petto quella tema;

But if the love of the highest sphereshould turn your desire upward,that fear would not exist within your breast.

ché, per quanti si dice più lì ‘nostro’,
tanto possiede più di ben ciascuno,
e più di caritate arde in quel chiostro».

For the more they say "ours" up there,the more good each one possesses,and the more charity burns in that cloister."

«Io son d’esser contento più digiuno»,
diss’ io, «che se mi fosse pria taciuto,
e più di dubbio ne la mente aduno.

"I am more eager to be content,"I said, "than if I had remained silent,and I gather more doubt in my mind."

Com’ esser puote ch’un ben, distributo
in più posseditor, faccia più ricchi
di sé che se da pochi è posseduto?».

"How can it be that a good, when sharedby many owners, makes them richer with itselfthan if it were possessed by only a few?"

Ed elli a me: «Però che tu rificchi
la mente pur a le cose terrene,
di vera luce tenebre dispicchi.

And he to me: "Because you still set your mind on earthly things, you will thus distinguish darkness from true light."

Quello infinito e ineffabil bene
che là sù è, così corre ad amore
com’ a lucido corpo raggio vene.

That infinite and ineffable goodthat is on high, flows toward loveas a ray of light to a shining body.

Tanto si dà quanto trova d’ardore;
sì che, quantunque carità si stende,
cresce sovr’ essa l’etterno valore.

It gives as much as it finds ardor; thus, as much as charity extends, eternal value grows over it.

E quanta gente più là sù s’intende,
più v’è da bene amare, e più vi s’ama,
e come specchio l’uno a l’altro rende.

And the more people understood up there, the more there is to love and the more they love, each reflecting the other as in a mirror.

E se la mia ragion non ti disfama,
vedrai Beatrice, ed ella pienamente
ti torrà questa e ciascun’ altra brama.

And if my reasoning does not satisfy you, you will see Beatrice, and she will fully relieve you of this and every other desire.

Procaccia pur che tosto sieno spente,
come son già le due, le cinque piaghe,
che si richiudon per esser dolente».

Strive to hurry them extinguished, like the five wounds, already closed, which reopen to cause pain.

Com’ io voleva dicer ‘Tu m’appaghe’,
vidimi giunto in su l’altro girone,
sì che tacer mi fer le luci vaghe.

As I was about to say, “You satisfy me,” I found myself arrived on the next terrace, so that my wandering eyes silenced me.

Ivi mi parve in una visïone
estatica di sùbito esser tratto,
e vedere in un tempio più persone;

There, it seemed to me, I was suddenly drawn into an ecstatic vision, and saw many people in a temple;

e una donna, in su l’entrar, con atto
dolce di madre dicer: «Figliuol mio,
perché hai tu così verso noi fatto?

and a woman, at the entrance, with a gentle, maternal gesture, saying: “My son, why have you treated us this way?”

Ecco, dolenti, lo tuo padre e io
ti cercavamo». E come qui si tacque,
ciò che pareva prima, dispario.

Behold, sorrowful one, your father and Isearched for you.” And as she fell silent,what had appeared before vanished.

Indi m’apparve un’altra con quell’ acque
giù per le gote che ’l dolor distilla
quando di gran dispetto in altrui nacque,

Then another woman appeared to me, with those watersstreaming down her cheeks, distilled by sorrowwhen great disdain for another is born.

e dir: «Se tu se’ sire de la villa
del cui nome ne’ dèi fu tanta lite,
e onde ogne scïenza disfavilla,

and say: "If you are the lord of the villagewhose name caused so much strife among the gods,and from which all knowledge sparks,"

vendica te di quelle braccia ardite
ch’abbracciar nostra figlia, o Pisistràto».
E ’l segnor mi parea, benigno e mite,

"Avenge yourself with those bold arms,which embraced our daughter, O Pisistratus."And the lord appeared to me, kind and gentle,

risponder lei con viso temperato:
«Che farem noi a chi mal ne disira,
se quei che ci ama è per noi condannato?»,

she answered with a tempered face:“What shall we do to those who wish us ill,if he who loves us is condemned for us?”

Poi vidi genti accese in foco d’ira
con pietre un giovinetto ancider, forte
gridando a sé pur: «Martira, martira!».

Then I saw people consumed by the fire of rage, killing a young man with stones, fiercely shouting to themselves: "Kill him, kill him!"

E lui vedea chinarsi, per la morte
che l’aggravava già, inver’ la terra,
ma de li occhi facea sempre al ciel porte,

And he saw himself stooping, under the weightof death which already bore him down, toward the earth,but still his eyes made portals to the sky,

orando a l’alto Sire, in tanta guerra,
che perdonasse a’ suoi persecutori,
con quello aspetto che pietà diserra.

praying to the high Lord, in such great war,that he would forgive his persecutors,with that expression which unlocks pity.

Quando l’anima mia tornò di fori
a le cose che son fuor di lei vere,
io riconobbi i miei non falsi errori.

When my soul returned outside, back to the things that are truly outside itself, I recognized my own, genuine errors.

Lo duca mio, che mi potea vedere
far sì com’ om che dal sonno si slega,
disse: «Che hai che non ti puoi tenere,

My guide, who could see how I was behaving, like a man roused from sleep, said: "What's wrong? Why can't you stand still?

ma se’ venuto più che mezza lega
velando li occhi e con le gambe avvolte,
a guisa di cui vino o sonno piega?».

but have you come more than half a league,veiling your eyes and with legs entwined,in the manner of one whom wine or sleep subdues?

«O dolce padre mio, se tu m’ascolte,
io ti dirò», diss’ io, «ciò che m’apparve
quando le gambe mi furon sì tolte».

"Oh, my sweet father, if you are listening to me," I said, "I will tell you what appeared to me when my legs were so taken from me."

Ed ei: «Se tu avessi cento larve
sovra la faccia, non mi sarian chiuse
le tue cogitazion, quantunque parve.

And he said, "If you had a hundred masks upon your face, your thoughts would not be hidden from me, no matter how they seemed."

Ciò che vedesti fu perché non scuse
d’aprir lo core a l’acque de la pace
che da l’etterno fonte son diffuse.

What you saw was because you did not excuse yourself from opening your heart to the waters of peace, which spread from the eternal source.

Non dimandai “Che hai?” per quel che face
chi guarda pur con l’occhio che non vede,
quando disanimato il corpo giace;

I did not ask, "What troubles you?" like onewho stares, yet sees with sight that comprehends not,when the lifeless body lies, its spirit gone.

ma dimandai per darti forza al piede:
così frugar conviensi i pigri, lenti
ad usar lor vigilia quando riede».

But I inquired, to strengthen your pace:so must the lazy be prodded, the slow,to use their vigilance when it returns."

Noi andavam per lo vespero, attenti
oltre quanto potean li occhi allungarsi
contra i raggi serotini e lucenti.

We walked along in the evening, attentive, straining our eyesto see as far as possibleagainst the late-burning, bright rays.

Ed ecco a poco a poco un fummo farsi
verso di noi come la notte oscuro;
né da quello era loco da cansarsi.

And then, little by little, a smoke began to risetoward us, as dark as night;and there was no place to flee from it.

Questo ne tolse li occhi e l’aere puro.

This stole away the eyes and the pure air.

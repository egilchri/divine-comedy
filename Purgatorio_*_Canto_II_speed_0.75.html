Purgatorio * Canto II

At dawn on the shore of Mount Purgatory, Dante and Virgil see a brilliant light approaching rapidly over the water. It resolves into a boat piloted by a divine angel, carrying more than a hundred souls destined for Purgatory. The souls are singing Psalm 114. After the angel blesses them, they disembark, and the vessel departs. The new arrivals, unfamiliar with the area, ask Dante and Virgil for the way. They are startled to discover that Dante is a living man, evidenced by his shadow and breathing. One soul steps forward, whom Dante recognizes as his friend, the musician Casella. Dante tries and fails three times to embrace the insubstantial shade. At Dante’s request, Casella sings a song that captivates all who are present, including Virgil. Their reverie is broken by the sudden appearance of Cato, who sternly rebukes them for their idleness and orders them to hurry to the mountain to begin their penance. Chastened, the souls, along with Dante and Virgil, immediately scatter and run toward the slope.

Purgatorio • Canto II

Già era ’l sole a l’orizzonte giunto
lo cui meridïan cerchio coverchia
Ierusalèm col suo più alto punto;

The sun had already reached the horizon, its highest point now covering Jerusalem's meridian circle.

e la notte, che opposita a lui cerchia,
uscia di Gange fuor con le Bilance,
che le caggion di man quando soverchia;

And night, which circles opposite him, emerged from the Ganges with the Scales, which it drops from its hands when it prevails.

sì che le bianche e le vermiglie guance,
là dov’ i’ era, de la bella Aurora
per troppa etate divenivan rance.

So that the white and crimson cheeks, where I was, of the fair Dawn, became orange with too much age.

Noi eravam lunghesso mare ancora,
come gente che pensa a suo cammino,
che va col cuore e col corpo dimora.

We were still beside the sea,like people pondering their journey,who travel with their heart while their body lingers.

Ed ecco, qual, sorpreso dal mattino,
per li grossi vapor Marte rosseggia
giù nel ponente sovra ’l suol marino,

And lo, like Mars, reddening in the morning,through thick vapors, glows reddown in the west above the sea's surface,

cotal m’apparve, s’io ancor lo veggia,
un lume per lo mar venir sì ratto,
che ’l muover suo nessun volar pareggia.

It seemed to me, if I still see it,a light through the sea coming so swiftly,that its motion outstripped any flight.

Dal qual com’ io un poco ebbi ritratto
l’occhio per domandar lo duca mio,
rividil più lucente e maggior fatto.

From which, as soon as I had drawn backmy eye a bit, to question my guide,I saw it again, brighter and larger.

Poi d’ogne lato ad esso m’appario
un non sapeva che bianco, e di sotto
a poco a poco un altro a lui uscìo.

Then on each side of it appeared to mesomething I did not know was white, and from beneathlittle by little another emerged toward it.

Lo mio maestro ancor non facea motto,
mentre che i primi bianchi apparver ali;
allor che ben conobbe il galeotto,

My master still uttered no word, while the first white wings appeared. When he clearly recognized the boatman,

gridò: «Fa, fa che le ginocchia cali.
Ecco l’angel di Dio: piega le mani;
omai vedrai di sì fatti officiali.

he cried: "Fall, fall, let your knees bend low.Behold God's angel: fold your hands;now you will see such officials."

Vedi che sdegna li argomenti umani,
sì che remo non vuol, né altro velo
che l’ali sue, tra liti sì lontani.

You see that he scorns human arguments, so that he wants neither oar nor any sail but his own wings between such distant shores.

Vedi come l’ha dritte verso ’l cielo,
trattando l’aere con l’etterne penne,
che non si mutan come mortal pelo».

You see how he directs them toward the sky, treating the air with his eternal plumes, which do not change like mortal hair."

Poi, come più e più verso noi venne
l’uccel divino, più chiaro appariva:
per che l’occhio da presso nol sostenne,

Then, as the divine bird came closer and closer to us, it became more luminous: thus, the eye could not endure it close at hand.

ma chinail giuso; e quei sen venne a riva
con un vasello snelletto e leggero,
tanto che l’acqua nulla ne ’nghiottiva.

But it bent downwards; and it came to shorewith a vessel so swift and light,that the water swallowed none of it.

Da poppa stava il celestial nocchiero,
tal che faria beato pur descripto;
e più di cento spirti entro sediero.

At the stern stood the celestial boatman,a sight that would bring bliss to those who described him,and more than a hundred spirits sat within.

‘In exitu Isräel de Aegypto’
cantavan tutti insieme ad una voce
con quanto di quel salmo è poscia scripto.

‘In exitu Israel de Aegypto’ they all sang together in one voice, along with the rest of that psalm that is written.

Poi fece il segno lor di santa croce;
ond’ ei si gittar tutti in su la piaggia:
ed el sen gì, come venne, veloce.

Then he made the sign of the holy cross for them; at which they all flung themselves down upon the shore; and he departed swiftly, as he had come.

La turba che rimase lì, selvaggia
parea del loco, rimirando intorno
come colui che nove cose assaggia.

The crowd that remained there looked wild, eyeing the surroundings like someone sampling new things.

Da tutte parti saettava il giorno
lo sol, ch’avea con le saette conte
di mezzo ’l ciel cacciato Capricorno,

From every side, day was shooting forth,the sun, which, with its counted arrows,had chased Capricorn from mid-heaven.

quando la nova gente alzò la fronte
ver’ noi, dicendo a noi: «Se voi sapete,
mostratene la via di gire al monte».

When the new souls lifted their facestoward us, saying to us: "If you know,show us the way to climb the mountain."

E Virgilio rispuose: «Voi credete
forse che siamo esperti d’esto loco;
ma noi siam peregrin come voi siete.

And Virgil answered: "You perhaps believethat we are experts in this place;but we are pilgrims, even as you are."

Dianzi venimmo, innanzi a voi un poco,
per altra via, che fu sì aspra e forte,
che lo salire omai ne parrà gioco».

"We arrived here before you, by another path, a path so harsh and difficult that climbing will now seem like child's play."

L’anime, che si fuor di me accorte,
per lo spirare, ch’i’ era ancor vivo,
maravigliando diventaro smorte.

The souls, having become aware of me,because of the fact that I was still breathing,grew pale with wonder.

E come a messagger che porta ulivo
tragge la gente per udir novelle,
e di calcar nessun si mostra schivo,

And as a messenger bearing an olive branchdraws the people near to hear the news,and none are shy to press close,

così al viso mio s’affisar quelle
anime fortunate tutte quante,
quasi oblïando d’ire a farsi belle.

So upon my face, those blessed souls did fix their gaze, almost forgetting to attend to their own beauty.

Io vidi una di lor trarresi avante
per abbracciarmi con sì grande affetto,
che mosse me a far lo somigliante.

I saw one of them come forward,to embrace me with such great affectionthat it moved me to do the same.

Ohi ombre vane, fuor che ne l’aspetto!
tre volte dietro a lei le mani avvinsi,
e tante mi tornai con esse al petto.

Oh, empty shadows, nothing but appearances! Three times I clasped my hands behind her, and three times I brought them back to my chest.

Di maraviglia, credo, mi dipinsi;
per che l’ombra sorrise e si ritrasse,
e io, seguendo lei, oltre mi pinsi.

In amazement, I believe, I painted myself; because the shade smiled and drew back, and I, following her, painted myself beyond.

Soavemente disse ch’io posasse;
allor conobbi chi era, e pregai
che, per parlarmi, un poco s’arrestasse.

Gently, she told me to rest. Then I knew who she was, and I begged her to pause a moment, so she could speak with me.

Rispuosemi: «Così com’ io t’amai
nel mortal corpo, così t’amo sciolta:
però m’arresto; ma tu perché vai?».

She answered me: "Just as I loved you in your mortal form, I love you now, unbound. Therefore, I will stop; but why do you go?"

«Casella mio, per tornar altra volta
là dov’ io son, fo io questo vïaggio»,
diss’ io; «ma a te com’ è tanta ora tolta?».

"My Casella, it is to return another timeto where I am that I make this journey,"I said, "but how has so much time been taken from you?"

Ed elli a me: «Nessun m’è fatto oltraggio,
se quei che leva quando e cui li piace,
più volte m’ha negato esto passaggio;

And he to me: "No offense has been done to me, save that He who takes when and whom He pleases, has often denied me this passage;

ché di giusto voler lo suo si face:
veramente da tre mesi elli ha tolto
chi ha voluto intrar, con tutta pace.

For His will is made of just desire: truly, for three months He has taken away, in perfect peace, whoever wished to enter.

Ond’ io, ch’era ora a la marina vòlto
dove l’acqua di Tevero s’insala,
benignamente fu’ da lui ricolto.

And I, who had turned toward the shore where the water of the Tiber mingles with the sea, was received by him with kindness.

A quella foce ha elli or dritta l’ala,
però che sempre quivi si ricoglie
qual verso Acheronte non si cala».

To that mouth he has now turned his wing, because there always gathers whoever doesn’t descend towards Acheron.

E io: «Se nuova legge non ti toglie
memoria o uso a l’amoroso canto
che mi solea quetar tutte mie doglie,

io prego te, perché di lui favantoun poco; e qual cagion li fece tantotristo, che ancora duol ne sia cotanto».

di ciò ti piaccia consolare alquanto
l’anima mia, che, con la sua persona
venendo qui, è affannata tanto!».

Of this, I pray you, console my soul a little, for in coming here with his person, it is so afflicted!

‘Amor che ne la mente mi ragiona’
cominciò elli allor sì dolcemente,
che la dolcezza ancor dentro mi suona.

“Love, which in my mind discourses,” he began then, so sweetly, that its sweetness still resounds within me.

Lo mio maestro e io e quella gente
ch’eran con lui parevan sì contenti,
come a nessun toccasse altro la mente.

My guide, I, and those people with him, seemed so content, as if nothing else occupied their minds.

Noi eravam tutti fissi e attenti
a le sue note; ed ecco il veglio onesto
gridando: «Che è ciò, spiriti lenti?

We were all fixed and attentive to his notes; and then the honest elder cried out: "What is this, slow spirits?"

qual negligenza, quale stare è questo?
Correte al monte a spogliarvi lo scoglio
ch’esser non lascia a voi Dio manifesto».

What negligence, what lingering is this? Hurry to the mountain to strip away the stone that prevents you from seeing God revealed.

Come quando, cogliendo biado o loglio,
li colombi adunati a la pastura,
queti, sanza mostrar l’usato orgoglio,

Like when, gathering weed or tares,the doves, assembled at their feeding,are quiet, without showing their usual pride,

se cosa appare ond’ elli abbian paura,
subitamente lasciano star l’esca,
perch’ assaliti son da maggior cura;

but if something appears that causes them fear, they suddenly leave their bait, because they are assailed by a greater concern.

così vid’ io quella masnada fresca
lasciar lo canto, e fuggir ver’ la costa,
com’ om che va, né sa dove rïesca;

Thus I saw that fresh throng abandon their song and flee toward the shore, like a man who goes, and knows not where he'll end up.

né la nostra partita fu men tosta.

Nor was our departure less swift.

Paradiso * Canto VIII

Dante and Beatrice ascend to the third heaven, the sphere of Venus. Dante perceives their ascent by witnessing an increase in Beatrice's beauty. In this sphere, they are surrounded by joyful, swiftly-circling lights, which are the souls of the blessed. One of these souls approaches Dante, identifying himself as Charles Martel of Anjou. He expresses his past affection for Dante and speaks of the kingdoms he would have ruled had he not died young. Dante then asks him how a good parent can produce a wicked child. Charles Martel explains that divine providence assigns different innate talents and dispositions to individuals to serve the diverse needs of society. He states that forcing a person into a role contrary to their nature, such as making a warrior a priest, disrupts this natural order and leads to negative outcomes. After concluding his explanation, the soul of Charles Martel rejoins the other spirits.

Paradiso • Canto VIII

Solea creder lo mondo in suo periclo
che la bella Ciprigna il folle amore
raggiasse, volta nel terzo epiciclo;

They used to believe the world was in peril, that beautiful Cypris, turning in the third epicycle, radiated mad love.

per che non pur a lei faceano onore
di sacrificio e di votivo grido
le genti antiche ne l’antico errore;

for not only did ancient peoples, in their old delusion, honor her with sacrifice and votive cry;

ma Dïone onoravano e Cupido,
quella per madre sua, questo per figlio,
e dicean ch’el sedette in grembo a Dido;

but they honored Dione and Cupid, she as his mother, he as her son, and they said he sat in Dido's lap.

e da costei ond’ io principio piglio
pigliavano il vocabol de la stella
che ’l sol vagheggia or da coppa or da ciglio.

and from her, from whom I take my beginning, they took the name of the star that the sun woos, now from below, now from above.

Io non m’accorsi del salire in ella;
ma d’esservi entro mi fé assai fede
la donna mia ch’i’ vidi far più bella.

I did not realize I had ascended into it; but that I was within it, my lady, whom I saw become more beautiful, gave me sufficient proof.

E come in fiamma favilla si vede,
e come in voce voce si discerne,
quand’ una è ferma e altra va e riede,

And as a spark is seen in a flame, and as a voice is discerned within a voice, when one is still while the other comes and goes,

vid’ io in essa luce altre lucerne
muoversi in giro più e men correnti,
al modo, credo, di lor viste interne.

I saw within that light other lanterns, moving in a circle, some swifter, some slower, in the manner, I believe, of their inner visions.

Di fredda nube non disceser venti,
o visibili o no, tanto festini,
che non paressero impediti e lenti

From no cold cloud did winds descend,visible or not, in such a rush,that they did not seem impeded and slow.

a chi avesse quei lumi divini
veduti a noi venir, lasciando il giro
pria cominciato in li alti Serafini;

to whomever saw those divine lights,as they came toward us, forsaking the coursethey had begun among the high Seraphim;

e dentro a quei che più innanzi appariro
sonava ‘Osanna’ sì, che unque poi
di rïudir non fui sanza disiro.

and within those that appeared before us,"Hosanna" sang out so, that I never sincehave been without the desire to hear it again.

Indi si fece l’un più presso a noi
e solo incominciò: «Tutti sem presti
al tuo piacer, perché di noi ti gioi.

Then one drew closer to us,and alone began, "We are all preparedfor your pleasure, because you find joy in us."

Noi ci volgiam coi principi celesti
d’un giro e d’un girare e d’una sete,
ai quali tu del mondo già dicesti:

We turn with the celestial princes,in a circling, a turning, and a yearning,to those of whom you once spoke to the world:

‘Voi che ’ntendendo il terzo ciel movete’;
e sem sì pien d’amor, che, per piacerti,
non fia men dolce un poco di quïete».

"You who, understanding, move the third heaven,"and we are so full of love that, to please you,a little rest will be no less sweet."

Poscia che li occhi miei si fuoro offerti
a la mia donna reverenti, ed essa
fatti li avea di sé contenti e certi,

After my eyes were offeredreverently to my lady, and shehad made them content and certain of herself,

rivolsersi a la luce che promessa
tanto s’avea, e «Deh, chi siete?» fue
la voce mia di grande affetto impressa.

I turned toward the light that had promised so much, and, deeply moved, I asked, "Who are you?"

E quanta e quale vid’ io lei far piùe
per allegrezza nova che s’accrebbe,
quando parlai, a l’allegrezze sue!

And how much, and of what kind, I saw her do even moredue to the new joy that increased,when I spoke, to her joys!

Così fatta, mi disse: «Il mondo m’ebbe
giù poco tempo; e se più fosse stato,
molto sarà di mal, che non sarebbe.

In that state, she said to me: “The world had mefor only a short time; and if it had been longer,much evil would be here that is not now.”

La mia letizia mi ti tien celato
che mi raggia dintorno e mi nasconde
quasi animal di sua seta fasciato.

My joy keeps you hidden from me,radiating all around and concealing you,like an animal wrapped in its own silk.

Assai m’amasti, e avesti ben onde;
che s’io fossi giù stato, io ti mostrava
di mio amor più oltre che le fronde.

You loved me so much, and you had good reason;for if I had been below, I would have shown youmore of my love than the leaves.

Quella sinistra riva che si lava
di Rodano poi ch’è misto con Sorga,
per suo segnore a tempo m’aspettava,

That left bank that Rodano washes,after it is mixed with the Sorgue,awaited me, in due time, as its lord.

e quel corno d’Ausonia che s’imborga
di Bari e di Gaeta e di Catona,
da ove Tronto e Verde in mare sgorga.

And that horn of Ausonia that is enclosedby Bari and Gaeta and Catona,from where the Tronto and the Verde flow into the sea.

Fulgeami già in fronte la corona
di quella terra che ’l Danubio riga
poi che le ripe tedesche abbandona.

Already on my brow, the crown shone forthof that land the Danube irrigates,after it leaves the German shores.

E la bella Trinacria, che caliga
tra Pachino e Peloro, sopra ’l golfo
che riceve da Euro maggior briga,

And fair Trinacria, which lies in shadowbetween Pachino and Peloro, above the gulfthat takes the greater struggle from the East Wind,

non per Tifeo ma per nascente solfo,
attesi avrebbe li suoi regi ancora,
nati per me di Carlo e di Ridolfo,

not for Typhoeus, but for nascent sulfur,would have awaited its rulers again,born for me from Charles and Rudolph,

se mala segnoria, che sempre accora
li popoli suggetti, non avesse
mosso Palermo a gridar: “Mora, mora!”.

If evil lordship, which always afflictsthe subject peoples, had not moved Palermoto cry out: “Death, death!”.

E se mio frate questo antivedesse,
l’avara povertà di Catalogna
già fuggeria, perché non li offendesse;

And if my brother had foreseen this,the stingy poverty of Cataloniawould have fled already, lest it offend him.

ché veramente proveder bisogna
per lui, o per altrui, sì ch’a sua barca
carcata più d’incarco non si pogna.

For truly, one must provide for him, or for another, so that his boat is not burdened with more cargo than it can bear.

La sua natura, che di larga parca
discese, avria mestier di tal milizia
che non curasse di mettere in arca».

His nature, descended from a generous lineage, would require a defense that cared not to hoard in a chest.

«Però ch’i’ credo che l’alta letizia
che ’l tuo parlar m’infonde, segnor mio,
là ’ve ogne ben si termina e s’inizia,

«Yet I believe that the high joythat your speech instills in me, my lord,where every good finds its end and beginning,

per te si veggia come la vegg’ io,
grata m’è più; e anco quest’ ho caro
perché ’l discerni rimirando in Dio.

May it be seen through you as I see it;It is more pleasing to me; and I also hold this dearBecause you perceive it by gazing upon God.

Fatto m’hai lieto, e così mi fa chiaro,
poi che, parlando, a dubitar m’hai mosso
com’ esser può, di dolce seme, amaro».

You have made me joyful, and in the same way, made it clear to me, since, by speaking, you have moved me to doubt how something bitter can come from sweet seed.”

Questo io a lui; ed elli a me: «S’io posso
mostrarti un vero, a quel che tu dimandi
terrai lo viso come tien lo dosso.

I to him; and he to me: "If I can show you the truth, you will regard what you ask as you regard your back."

Lo ben che tutto il regno che tu scandi
volge e contenta, fa esser virtute
sua provedenza in questi corpi grandi.

Behold, though all the realm you traverse turns and is content, its providence makes its virtue reside in these great bodies.

E non pur le nature provedute
sono in la mente ch’è da sé perfetta,
ma esse insieme con la lor salute:

And not only are the proven natures heldwithin the mind, which is perfect in itself,but they, together, with their own well-being.

per che quantunque quest’ arco saetta
disposto cade a proveduto fine,
sì come cosa in suo segno diretta.

Because whatever this bow shootsfalls purposefully to a preordained end,like an arrow aimed at its mark.

Se ciò non fosse, il ciel che tu cammine
producerebbe sì li suoi effetti,
che non sarebbero arti, ma ruine;

If it were not so, then the heavens in which you walk would produce such effects that they would not be works of art, but rather ruin.

e ciò esser non può, se li ’ntelletti
che muovon queste stelle non son manchi,
e manco il primo, che non li ha perfetti.

And this cannot be, if the Intelligences that move these stars are not flawed, and even more so the Prime Mover, who did not make them perfect.

Vuo’ tu che questo ver più ti s’imbianchi?».
E io: «Non già; ché impossibil veggio
che la natura, in quel ch’è uopo, stanchi».

"Do you wish this truth to become even clearer to you?"And I: "No, for I see it is impossible that nature should fail in that which is necessary."

Ond’ elli ancora: «Or dì: sarebbe il peggio
per l’omo in terra, se non fosse cive?».
«Sì», rispuos’ io; «e qui ragion non cheggio».

Then he again: "Now tell me: would things be worse for humanity on Earth if it were not civic?""Yes," I answered; "and I ask no further reason."

«E puot’ elli esser, se giù non si vive
diversamente per diversi offici?
Non, se ’l maestro vostro ben vi scrive».

"Could it be otherwise if life below weren't lived differently through diverse functions?""No, if your teacher writes well."

Sì venne deducendo infino a quici;
poscia conchiuse: «Dunque esser diverse
convien di vostri effetti le radici:

Thus it came to be deduced up to this point; then he concluded: "Therefore the roots of your effects must needs be different:

per ch’un nasce Solone e altro Serse,
altro Melchisedèch e altro quello
che, volando per l’aere, il figlio perse.

because one is born a Solon, another a Xerxes, another Melchizedek, and another the one who, flying through the air, lost his son.

La circular natura, ch’è suggello
a la cera mortal, fa ben sua arte,
ma non distingue l’un da l’altro ostello.

The circular nature, which is a sealupon mortal wax, performs its art well,but does not distinguish one dwelling from another.

Quinci addivien ch’Esaù si diparte
per seme da Iacòb; e vien Quirino
da sì vil padre, che si rende a Marte.

From here it follows that Esau separates himself by seed from Jacob; and Quirinus comes from such a base father that he gives himself to Mars.

Natura generata il suo cammino
simil farebbe sempre a’ generanti,
se non vincesse il proveder divino.

Nature generates her course,would always make it like the generators,if divine providence did not conquer.

Or quel che t’era dietro t’è davanti:
ma perché sappi che di te mi giova,
un corollario voglio che t’ammanti.

And now what was behind you lies before: but so that you know I benefit you, I want a corollary to enfold you.

Sempre natura, se fortuna trova
discorde a sé, com’ ogne altra semente
fuor di sua regïon, fa mala prova.

Nature always, if she finds fortuneat odds with her, as any seedoutside its region, makes a poor showing.

E se ’l mondo là giù ponesse mente
al fondamento che natura pone,
seguendo lui, avria buona la gente.

And if the world down there would only pay attentionto the foundation that Nature lays,following it, humanity would be good.

Ma voi torcete a la religïone
tal che fia nato a cignersi la spada,
e fate re di tal ch’è da sermone;

But you distort religion itself,so that those born to bear arms are forced into it,and make a king of one suited only for sermons.

onde la traccia vostra è fuor di strada».

where your path is astray.”

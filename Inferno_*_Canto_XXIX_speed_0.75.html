Inferno * Canto XXIX

Virgil urges Dante away from the ninth chasm of the eighth circle, where Dante has been looking for the spirit of his kinsman, Geri del Bello. Dante explains that he saw Geri’s shade making threatening gestures. Virgil confirms the sighting but instructs Dante to leave the sower of discord to his punishment. The two poets proceed to the bridge over the tenth and final chasm. From this vantage point, they are met with a foul stench and the sounds of lamentation. The floor of the pit is filled with heaps of sick souls, afflicted with diseases. These are the falsifiers. Dante and Virgil descend to observe two spirits leaning against each other, covered from head to toe in scabs, which they scratch incessantly. Upon being addressed by Virgil, one of the spirits identifies himself as Griffolino of Arezzo and his companion as Capocchio of Siena, both of whom were alchemists.

La molta gente e le diverse piaghe
avean le luci mie sì inebrïate,
che de lo stare a piangere eran vaghe.

The multitude and the various woundshad so inebriated my eyesthat they longed to stand and weep.

Ma Virgilio mi disse: «Che pur guate?
perché la vista tua pur si soffolge
là giù tra l’ombre triste smozzicate?

Ma Virgil said to me: "Why do you stare? Why does your gaze linger so, fixed down there among the wretched, mangled shades?"

Tu non hai fatto sì a l’altre bolge;
pensa, se tu annoverar le credi,
che miglia ventidue la valle volge.

You have not yet gone through the other pouches; consider, if you wish to count them, that the valley extends for twenty-two miles.

E già la luna è sotto i nostri piedi;
lo tempo è poco omai che n’è concesso,
e altro è da veder che tu non vedi».

And the moon is already beneath our feet;little time remains that is granted to us,and there is more to see than what you see."

«Se tu avessi», rispuos’ io appresso,
«atteso a la cagion per ch’io guardava,
forse m’avresti ancor lo star dimesso».

"If you had," I responded, "considered the reason I was looking, perhaps you would have excused my lingering."

Parte sen giva, e io retro li andava,
lo duca, già faccendo la risposta,
e soggiugnendo: «Dentro a quella cava

"If you had seen," I responded, and I followed after him, the guide, who was already finishing his reply, adding, "Inside that hollow..."

dov’ io tenea or li occhi sì a posta,
credo ch’un spirto del mio sangue pianga
la colpa che là giù cotanto costa».

Where I had been holding my eyes so intently, I believe a spirit of my blood weeps for the sin that costs so much down there.”

Allor disse ’l maestro: «Non si franga
lo tuo pensier da qui innanzi sovr’ ello.
Attendi ad altro, ed ei là si rimanga;

Then the master said: "Do not let your thoughts dwell on him from now on. Attend to other things, and let him remain there;

ch’io vidi lui a piè del ponticello
mostrarti e minacciar forte col dito,
e udi’ ’l nominar Geri del Bello.

that I saw him at the foot of the little bridge, pointing and fiercely threatening you with his finger, and I heard him call out Geri del Bello’s name.

Tu eri allor sì del tutto impedito
sovra colui che già tenne Altaforte,
che non guardasti in là, sì fu partito».

You were so occupied then,with the one who once held Altaforte,that you didn't look over there, and he was gone.»

«O duca mio, la vïolenta morte
che non li è vendicata ancor», diss’ io,
«per alcun che de l’onta sia consorte,

«Oh, my guide,» I said, «the violent death that he suffered is still unavenged by anyone who shares in the shame,

fece lui disdegnoso; ond’ el sen gio
sanza parlarmi, sì com’ ïo estimo:
e in ciò m’ha el fatto a sé più pio».

He turned away in disdain, and departedwithout speaking to me, I believe;and in this he has shown himself more merciful to me.

Così parlammo infino al loco primo
che de lo scoglio l’altra valle mostra,
se più lume vi fosse, tutto ad imo.

Thus we spoke until the first place, which showed the other valley from the cliff, down to the bottom, if there were more light.

Quando noi fummo sor l’ultima chiostra
di Malebolge, sì che i suoi conversi
potean parere a la veduta nostra,

When we were at the final enclosure of Malebolge, so that its inhabitants appeared before our sight,

lamenti saettaron me diversi,
che di pietà ferrati avean li strali;
ond’ io li orecchi con le man copersi.

Diverse laments assailed me, their arrows forged with pity; thus I covered my ears with my hands.

Qual dolor fora, se de li spedali
di Valdichiana tra ’l luglio e ’l settembre
e di Maremma e di Sardigna i mali

What pain it would be, to witness the suffering in the hospitalsof Valdichiana between July and September,and the sickness of Maremma and Sardinia.

fossero in una fossa tutti ’nsembre,

If they were all together in a ditch,that is what it was like, and such a stench came forthas usually rises from rotting limbs.

Noi discendemmo in su l’ultima riva
del lungo scoglio, pur da man sinistra;
e allor fu la mia vista più viva

We descended onto the final bankof the long cliff, still on the left side;and then my vision became more keen.

giù ver’ lo fondo, la ’ve la ministra
de l’alto Sire infallibil giustizia
punisce i falsador che qui registra.

Down toward the depths, where the ministerof the high Lord’s infallible justicepunishes the falsifiers he registers here.

Non credo ch’a veder maggior tristizia
fosse in Egina il popol tutto infermo,
quando fu l’aere sì pien di malizia,

I do not believe that, to witness greater misery,the entire population of Aegina was afflicted,when the air was so full of malice,

che li animali, infino al picciol vermo,
cascaron tutti, e poi le genti antiche,
secondo che i poeti hanno per fermo,

that the animals, even the smallest worm,all fell, and then the ancient people,according to what the poets hold as certain,

si ristorar di seme di formiche;
ch’era a veder per quella oscura valle
languir li spirti per diverse biche.

to refresh themselves with ant seeds; for in that dark valley, it was to see the spirits languish in diverse heaps.

Qual sovra ’l ventre e qual sovra le spalle
l’un de l’altro giacea, e qual carpone
si trasmutava per lo tristo calle.

Some lay upon their bellies, others upon their shoulders, one on top of the other, and some crawled, shuffling along the wretched path.

Passo passo andavam sanza sermone,
guardando e ascoltando li ammalati,
che non potean levar le lor persone.

Step by step they walked, without a word,watching and listening to the afflicted,who could not lift their bodies.

Io vidi due sedere a sé poggiati,
com’ a scaldar si poggia tegghia a tegghia,
dal capo al piè di schianze macolati;

I saw two souls, pressed close together, just as one pot leans against another to warm itself, their bodies stained from head to foot with scabs.

e non vidi già mai menare stregghia
a ragazzo aspettato dal segnorso,
né a colui che mal volontier vegghia,

che ciascun d'essi affannava con l'orsole unghie, per isfellar le scabbie.

come ciascun menava spesso il morso
de l’unghie sopra sé per la gran rabbia
del pizzicor, che non ha più soccorso;

as each one often used the biteof their nails upon themselves, in great rageat the itch, which has no further relief;

e sì traevan giù l’unghie la scabbia,
come coltel di scardova le scaglie
o d’altro pesce che più larghe l’abbia.

And thus they scraped away their scabs with their nails,as one might use a knife to scale a carp,or any fish that has scales more broad.

«O tu che con le dita ti dismaglie»,
cominciò ’l duca mio a l’un di loro,
«e che fai d’esse talvolta tanaglie,

"Oh, you there, who with your fingers are unmaking yourselves,"my guide began, addressing one of them,"and who sometimes turn those fingers into pincers,"

dinne s’alcun Latino è tra costoro
che son quinc’ entro, se l’unghia ti basti
etternalmente a cotesto lavoro».

"Tell me, is there a Latin speaker among these, here within, if your nail is sufficient for that eternal task?"

«Latin siam noi, che tu vedi sì guasti
qui ambedue», rispuose l’un piangendo;
«ma tu chi se’ che di noi dimandasti?».

"We are Latin, as you see, so ruined, both of us here," one replied, weeping. "But who are you, that you have asked about us?"

E ’l duca disse: «I’ son un che discendo
con questo vivo giù di balzo in balzo,
e di mostrar lo ’nferno a lui intendo».

And the guide said: "I am one who descends with this living man from cliff to cliff, and I intend to show him Hell."

Allor si ruppe lo comun rincalzo;
e tremando ciascuno a me si volse
con altri che l’udiron di rimbalzo.

Then the common bond shattered; and trembling, each turned toward me, along with others who heard it echoing back.

Lo buon maestro a me tutto s’accolse,
dicendo: «Dì a lor ciò che tu vuoli»;
e io incominciai, poscia ch’ei volse:

My good master then gathered himself fully toward me, saying, "Tell them what you wish"; and I began, after he willed it so:

«Se la vostra memoria non s’imboli
nel primo mondo da l’umane menti,
ma s’ella viva sotto molti soli,

«If your memory does not take flightin the first world, from human minds,but lives on under many suns,

ditemi chi voi siete e di che genti;
la vostra sconcia e fastidiosa pena
di palesarvi a me non vi spaventi».

Tell me who you are and of what people. Let not the shameful and disgusting suffering you endure frighten you from revealing yourselves to me.

«Io fui d’Arezzo, e Albero da Siena»,
rispuose l’un, «mi fé mettere al foco;
ma quel per ch’io mori’ qui non mi mena.

"I was from Arezzo, and Albero of Siena," the one replied, "had me burned at the stake; but the cause of my death does not bring me here."

Vero è ch’i’ dissi lui, parlando a gioco:
“I’ mi saprei levar per l’aere a volo”;
e quei, ch’avea vaghezza e senno poco,

He did speak truly when I said to him, jokingly: "I know how to lift myself into flight through the air"; and that one, who had desire but little sense,

volle ch’i’ li mostrassi l’arte; e solo
perch’ io nol feci Dedalo, mi fece
ardere a tal che l’avea per figliuolo.

he wanted me to show him the art; and only because I did not make him a Daedalus, he made me burn at the stake for the one he had as his son.

Ma ne l’ultima bolgia de le diece

But in the last circle of the ten, Minos condemned me for the alchemy I practiced in the world, a judgment he is not allowed to err.

E io dissi al poeta: «Or fu già mai

And I said to the poet, "Has there ever been a people as vain as the Sienese? Certainly not the French, not by much!"

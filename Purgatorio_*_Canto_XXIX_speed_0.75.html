Purgatorio * Canto XXIX

As Dante, Statius, and Matelda walk along the river Lethe in the Earthly Paradise, a brilliant light and a sweet melody fill the air. The light resolves into seven golden candlesticks, which leave seven parallel bands of color in the sky like banners. A procession follows. First appear twenty-four elders, walking two by two and crowned with white lilies. They are followed by four living creatures, each with six wings covered in eyes and crowned with green leaves. Between these creatures is a two-wheeled chariot drawn by a griffin whose wings stretch up to the heavens. To the chariot’s right wheel, three ladies dance—one red, one green, and one white. To the left, four ladies in purple dance, led by one with three eyes. Behind the chariot walk two old men, then four of humble appearance, and finally a single, sleeping elder.

Purgatorio • Canto XXIX

Cantando come donna innamorata,
continüò col fin di sue parole:
‘Beati quorum tecta sunt peccata!’.

Singing like a woman in love,she continued to the end of her words:“Blessed are those whose sins are forgiven!”

E come ninfe che si givan sole
per le salvatiche ombre, disïando
qual di veder, qual di fuggir lo sole,

And like nymphs who wander alonethrough the wild shadows, desiringsome to see, some to flee the sun,

allor si mosse contra ’l fiume, andando
su per la riva; e io pari di lei,
picciol passo con picciol seguitando.

Then she moved toward the river, walkingalong the bank; and I, beside her,followed with a small step matching hers.

Non eran cento tra ’ suoi passi e ’ miei,
quando le ripe igualmente dier volta,
per modo ch’a levante mi rendei.

Not a hundred paces lay between her steps and minewhen the banks equally turned,so that I found myself facing east.

Né ancor fu così nostra via molta,
quando la donna tutta a me si torse,
dicendo: «Frate mio, guarda e ascolta».

We hadn't gone much further on our path,when the woman turned completely toward me,saying: "My brother, look and listen."

Ed ecco un lustro sùbito trascorse
da tutte parti per la gran foresta,
tal che di balenar mi mise in forse.

And lo, a flash immediately crossed overfrom every direction through the great forest,so that it made me doubt if it was lightning.

Ma perché ’l balenar, come vien, resta,
e quel, durando, più e più splendeva,
nel mio pensier dicea: ‘Che cosa è questa?’.

But because the flashing, as it comes, ceases, while that, enduring, shone more and more, I said in my thought: 'What is this?'

E una melodia dolce correva
per l’aere luminoso; onde buon zelo
mi fé riprender l’ardimento d’Eva,

And a sweet melody ranthrough the luminous air; wherefore good zealmade me retake the daring of Eve,

che là dove ubidia la terra e ’l cielo,
femmina, sola e pur testé formata,
non sofferse di star sotto alcun velo;

that where earth and heaven obeyed,a woman, alone and just then formed,did not suffer to stay beneath any veil;

sotto ’l qual se divota fosse stata,
avrei quelle ineffabili delizie
sentite prima e più lunga fïata.

beneath which, had she been devout,I would have felt those ineffable delightssooner and for a longer time.

Mentr’ io m’andava tra tante primizie
de l’etterno piacer tutto sospeso,
e disïoso ancora a più letizie,

While I went amongst so many first fruits of eternal pleasure, entirely suspended, and desirous still of greater joys,

dinanzi a noi, tal quale un foco acceso,
ci si fé l’aere sotto i verdi rami;
e ’l dolce suon per canti era già inteso.

Before us, like a fire alight,the air within the green branches became so;and the sweet sound, in songs, was already heard.

O sacrosante Vergini, se fami,
freddi o vigilie mai per voi soffersi,
cagion mi sprona ch’io mercé vi chiami.

Oh, sacred Virgins, if for you I ever suffered hunger, cold, or sleepless nights, a cause now spurs me to implore your mercy.

Or convien che Elicona per me versi,
e Uranìe m’aiuti col suo coro
forti cose a pensar mettere in versi.

Now it is fitting that Helicon pour forth for me, and Urania aid me with her chorus, so that I may set strong things to thought in verse.

Poco più oltre, sette alberi d’oro
falsava nel parere il lungo tratto
del mezzo ch’era ancor tra noi e loro;

A little further on, seven golden treeswere made false to the eye by the long reachof the space that was still between us and them.

ma quand’ i’ fui sì presso di lor fatto,
che l’obietto comun, che ’l senso inganna,
non perdea per distanza alcun suo atto,

but when I came so close to them that the shared appearance, which deceives the senses, lost none of its form due to the distance,

la virtù ch’a ragion discorso ammanna,
sì com’ elli eran candelabri apprese,
e ne le voci del cantare ‘Osanna’.

The virtue which orders rational speech, understood them to be like candelabras, and in the voices singing 'Hosanna'.

Di sopra fiammeggiava il bello arnese
più chiaro assai che luna per sereno
di mezza notte nel suo mezzo mese.

Above, the beautiful instrument blazed,far brighter than the moon in a clear midnight skyduring its waxing phase.

Io mi rivolsi d’ammirazion pieno
al buon Virgilio, ed esso mi rispuose
con vista carca di stupor non meno.

I turned, full of admiration,to good Virgil, and he answered mewith a look no less laden with wonder.

Indi rendei l’aspetto a l’alte cose
che si movieno incontr’ a noi sì tardi,
che foran vinte da novelle spose.

Then I turned my gaze back to those exalted forms, that moved towards us so slowly, that they would be outpaced by a bride's new steps.

La donna mi sgridò: «Perché pur ardi
sì ne l’affetto de le vive luci,
e ciò che vien di retro a lor non guardi?».

The woman scolded me: "Why are you still so consumedby the affection for the living lights,and do not look at what comes behind them?".

Genti vid’ io allor, come a lor duci,
venire appresso, vestite di bianco;
e tal candor di qua già mai non fuci.

Then I saw people coming behind them, as if they were their leaders, dressed in white; and such brightness never was on earth.

L’acqua imprendëa dal sinistro fianco,
e rendea me la mia sinistra costa,
s’io riguardava in lei, come specchio anco.

The water yielded to my left side, and returned to me my left rib, if I looked into it, like a mirror.

Quand’ io da la mia riva ebbi tal posta,
che solo il fiume mi facea distante,
per veder meglio ai passi diedi sosta,

When I had taken my place upon the shore, so that only the river separated me, I paused in my steps to see better.

e vidi le fiammelle andar davante,
lasciando dietro a sé l’aere dipinto,
e di tratti pennelli avean sembiante;

And I saw the little flames go ahead, leaving the painted air behind them, and they had the look of brushstrokes.

sì che lì sopra rimanea distinto
di sette liste, tutte in quei colori
onde fa l’arco il Sole e Delia il cinto.

so that above, distinct, remainedseven bands, all in those colorsfrom which the Sun makes the bow and Delia the belt.

Questi ostendali in dietro eran maggiori
che la mia vista; e, quanto a mio avviso,
diece passi distavan quei di fori.

These banners behind were largerthan my sight; and, as far as I could judge,ten paces separated those outside.

Sotto così bel ciel com’ io diviso,
ventiquattro seniori, a due a due,
coronati venien di fiordaliso.

Beneath so fair a heaven, where I was sundered, twenty-four elders, two by two, came crowned with fleur-de-lis.

Tutti cantavan: «Benedicta tue
ne le figlie d’Adamo, e benedette
sieno in etterno le bellezze tue!».

All were singing: "Blessed are you amongthe daughters of Adam, and blessedforever be your beauties!"

Poscia che i fiori e l’altre fresche erbette
a rimpetto di me da l’altra sponda
libere fuor da quelle genti elette,

After the flowers and the other fresh grasses, opposite me on the other bank, were free from those chosen people,

sì come luce luce in ciel seconda,
vennero appresso lor quattro animali,
coronati ciascun di verde fronda.

As light follows light in the heavens, four animals followed, each crowned with verdant foliage.

Ognuno era pennuto di sei ali;
le penne piene d’occhi; e li occhi d’Argo,
se fosser vivi, sarebber cotali.

Each was feathered with six wings, the plumes full of eyes. And the eyes were like Argus', had they been alive.

A descriver lor forme più non spargo
rime, lettor; ch’altra spesa mi strigne,
tanto ch’a questa non posso esser largo;

I will not waste any more rhymes describing their forms, reader, for another expense constrains me, so that I cannot be generous with this one.

ma leggi Ezechïel, che li dipigne
come li vide da la fredda parte
venir con vento e con nube e con igne;

But read Ezekiel, who depicts themas he saw them coming from the cold side,with wind, cloud, and fire.

e quali i troverai ne le sue carte,
tali eran quivi, salvo ch’a le penne
Giovanni è meco e da lui si diparte.

And you will find them in his writings, just as they were here, except that John is with me and departs from him.

Lo spazio dentro a lor quattro contenne
un carro, in su due rote, trïunfale,
ch’al collo d’un grifon tirato venne.

The space within them held a triumphal chariot, upon two wheels, drawn by a griffin at its neck.

Esso tendeva in sù l’una e l’altra ale
tra la mezzana e le tre e tre liste,
sì ch’a nulla, fendendo, facea male.

It raised up one wing and then the otherbetween the middle stripe and the three stripes,so that, as it sliced through, it harmed nothing.

Tanto salivan che non eran viste;
le membra d’oro avea quant’ era uccello,
e bianche l’altre, di vermiglio miste.

So much saliva did they spew that they were unseen; the golden limbs were all a bird’s, and the others, white, were mixed with vermillion.

Non che Roma di carro così bello
rallegrasse Affricano, o vero Augusto,
ma quel del Sol saria pover con ello;

Not even Rome with its most splendid chariotcould have pleased Africanus, or even Augustus,but that of the Sun would be poor compared to it.

quel del Sol che, svïando, fu combusto
per l’orazion de la Terra devota,
quando fu Giove arcanamente giusto.

that of the Sun, veering, was consumedby the prayer of the devout Earth,when Jove was secretly just.

Tre donne in giro da la destra rota
venian danzando; l’una tanto rossa
ch’a pena fora dentro al foco nota;

Three women, circling from the right-hand wheel,came dancing; one so crimson-hued,she’d scarcely be remarked within a fire.

l’altr’ era come se le carni e l’ossa
fossero state di smeraldo fatte;
la terza parea neve testé mossa;

The other was as if her flesh and boneswere fashioned out of emerald;the third appeared like freshly fallen snow.

e or parëan da la bianca tratte,
or da la rossa; e dal canto di questa
l’altre toglien l’andare e tarde e ratte.

And one seemed drawn from white,another from red; and from the song of this one,the others took their movement, slow or swift.

Da la sinistra quattro facean festa,
in porpore vestite, dietro al modo
d’una di lor ch’avea tre occhi in testa.

From the left, four made merry, in robes of purple, following the manner of one of them who had three eyes in her head.

Appresso tutto il pertrattato nodo
vidi due vecchi in abito dispari,
ma pari in atto e onesto e sodo.

Following that intricate knot, I saw two old men, their clothing different, but their demeanor matched, both honest and steadfast.

L’un si mostrava alcun de’ famigliari
di quel sommo Ipocràte che natura
a li animali fé ch’ell’ ha più cari;

One appeared to be among the kinof that great Hippocrates, whom naturefavored in its creatures, holding them dear.

mostrava l’altro la contraria cura
con una spada lucida e aguta,
tal che di qua dal rio mi fé paura.

The other, however, displayed the opposing care,with a shining, sharp sword,so that on this side of the river, he made me afraid.

Poi vidi quattro in umile paruta;
e di retro da tutti un vecchio solo
venir, dormendo, con la faccia arguta.

Then I saw four in humble guise; and behind them all, an old man came alone, sleeping, with a clever face.

E questi sette col primaio stuolo
erano abitüati, ma di gigli
dintorno al capo non facëan brolo,

And these seven, with the first group, were accustomed to the ways of old, but of lilies around their heads they made no garland.

anzi di rose e d’altri fior vermigli;
giurato avria poco lontano aspetto
che tutti ardesser di sopra da’ cigli.

rather, of roses and other crimson flowers;one would have sworn from their near-distant facesthat they all burned above their brows.

E quando il carro a me fu a rimpetto,
un tuon s’udì, e quelle genti degne
parvero aver l’andar più interdetto,

And when the chariot was before me,a thunderclap was heard, and those worthy peopleseemed to have their movement more impeded,

fermandosi ivi con le prime insegne.

Remaining there with their leading standards.

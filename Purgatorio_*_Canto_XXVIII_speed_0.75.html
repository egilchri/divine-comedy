Purgatorio * Canto XXVIII

Dante enters a dense, ancient forest, the Earthly Paradise. He walks through it, noting a gentle, constant breeze and the sound of birdsong. His path is halted by a clear stream. On the opposite bank, he sees a solitary woman, Matelda, who is gathering flowers and singing as she walks. Dante, with Virgil and Statius, calls to her across the water. The woman approaches the bank and listens to Dante’s question: he asks her to explain the source of the wind and the stream in a place said to be free of atmospheric disturbance. She explains that the breeze is not weather but the result of the motion of the celestial spheres. The water flows from a constant, divine fount, not from rain. This source splits into two rivers: Lethe, which removes the memory of sin, and Eunoë, which restores the memory of good deeds.

Purgatorio • Canto XXVIII

Vago già di cercar dentro e dintorno
la divina foresta spessa e viva,
ch’a li occhi temperava il novo giorno,

I have already begun to search within and around the divine forest, dense and alive, which tempered the new day to my eyes.

sanza più aspettar, lasciai la riva,
prendendo la campagna lento lento
su per lo suol che d’ogne parte auliva.

Without waiting further, I left the shore, and slowly began to traverse the countryside, climbing over ground that bloomed on every side.

Un’aura dolce, sanza mutamento
avere in sé, mi feria per la fronte
non di più colpo che soave vento;

A gentle breeze, unwavering, struck my brow, no more forceful than a tender wind.

per cui le fronde, tremolando, pronte
tutte quante piegavano a la parte
u’ la prim’ ombra gitta il santo monte;

Because of this, the leaves, trembling, eager, all bent themselves towards the side where the first shadow is cast by the holy mountain.

non però dal loro esser dritto sparte
tanto, che li augelletti per le cime
lasciasser d’operare ogne lor arte;

but not so much did they swerve from their upright stance that the little birds amongst the highest branches ceased all their artistry.

ma con piena letizia l’ore prime,
cantando, ricevieno intra le foglie,
che tenevan bordone a le sue rime,

but with full joy the early hours, singing, received them among the leaves, which kept chorus to their rhymes,

tal qual di ramo in ramo si raccoglie
per la pineta in su ’l lito di Chiassi,
quand’ Ëolo scilocco fuor discioglie.

Just as from branch to branch they gather,through the pine forest on Chiassi’s shore,when Aeolus releases the scirocco wind.

Già m’avean trasportato i lenti passi
dentro a la selva antica tanto, ch’io
non potea rivedere ond’ io mi ’ntrassi;

My slow steps had already carried me so far into the ancient wood that I could no longer see the place where I had entered.

ed ecco più andar mi tolse un rio,
che ’nver’ sinistra con sue picciole onde
piegava l’erba che ’n sua ripa uscìo.

And then a stream stopped me from going further, a stream that, with its small waves, bent the grass that grew along its bank towards the left.

Tutte l’acque che son di qua più monde,
parrieno avere in sé mistura alcuna
verso di quella, che nulla nasconde,

All the waters on this side, however pure, would seem to have some impurity when compared to that stream, which hides nothing.

avvegna che si mova bruna bruna
sotto l’ombra perpetüa, che mai
raggiar non lascia sole ivi né luna.

Although it moves, dark and dark, under the perpetual shadow, which never lets sun or moon shine there.

Coi piè ristetti e con li occhi passai
di là dal fiumicello, per mirare
la gran varïazion d’i freschi mai;

I paused with my feet and let my eyes cross over the little river, to observe the great variety of the fresh meadows.

e là m’apparve, sì com’ elli appare
subitamente cosa che disvia
per maraviglia tutto altro pensare,

and there he appeared to me, even as he appears, suddenly, a thing that distracts all thought through wonder.

una donna soletta che si gia
e cantando e scegliendo fior da fiore
ond’ era pinta tutta la sua via.

A solitary woman went along, singing as she went, gathering flowers, blossom by blossom, that painted the path behind her.

«Deh, bella donna, che a’ raggi d’amore
ti scaldi, s’i’ vo’ credere a’ sembianti
che soglion esser testimon del core,

"Oh, beautiful woman, warmed by love's rays, if I am to trust the signs, which often bear witness to the heart,

vegnati in voglia di trarreti avanti»,
diss’ io a lei, «verso questa rivera,
tanto ch’io possa intender che tu canti.

"come into my desire to bring you forward," I said to her, "towards this river, so that I may understand what you sing."

Tu mi fai rimembrar dove e qual era
Proserpina nel tempo che perdette
la madre lei, ed ella primavera».

You make me remember where and what she was,Proserpina, in the time when she losther mother, and she was spring.

Come si volge, con le piante strette
a terra e intra sé, donna che balli,
e piede innanzi piede a pena mette,

As a woman dancing turns, with her feet pressed close to the ground and together, barely putting one foot in front of the other.

volsesi in su i vermigli e in su i gialli
fioretti verso me, non altrimenti
che vergine che li occhi onesti avvalli;

turned toward me, the crimson and the yellow little flowers, just as a virgin lowers her modest eyes.

e fece i prieghi miei esser contenti,
sì appressando sé, che ’l dolce suono
veniva a me co’ suoi intendimenti.

And granted my prayers, drawing so near that the sweet sound reached me along with its meanings.

Tosto che fu là dove l’erbe sono
bagnate già da l’onde del bel fiume,
di levar li occhi suoi mi fece dono.

As soon as he was where the grass was already soaked by the beautiful river's waves, he gave me the gift of lifting his eyes.

Non credo che splendesse tanto lume
sotto le ciglia a Venere, trafitta
dal figlio fuor di tutto suo costume.

I don't believe so much light ever shone beneath the eyelids of Venus, struck by her son, contrary to all his custom.

Ella ridea da l’altra riva dritta,
trattando più color con le sue mani,
che l’alta terra sanza seme gitta.

She laughed from the opposite bank, upright, manipulating more colors with her hands than the high earth casts forth without seed.

Tre passi ci facea il fiume lontani;
ma Elesponto, là ’ve passò Serse,
ancora freno a tutti orgogli umani,

Three paces away from us the river made;but the Hellespont, where Xerxes passed,still a bridle on all human pride,

più odio da Leandro non sofferse
per mareggiare intra Sesto e Abido,
che quel da me perch’ allor non s’aperse.

More hatred Leandro never sufferedfrom the sea’s surge between Sestos and Abydosthan I did because it did not then open.

«Voi siete nuovi, e forse perch’ io rido»,
cominciò ella, «in questo luogo eletto
a l’umana natura per suo nido,

"You are newcomers, and perhaps because I laugh," she began, "in this place chosen as a nest for human nature,

maravigliando tienvi alcun sospetto;
ma luce rende il salmo Delectasti,
che puote disnebbiar vostro intelletto.

wondering, some suspicion holds you; but the psalm *Delectasti* gives light, which can clear your intellect.

E tu che se’ dinanzi e mi pregasti,
dì s’altro vuoli udir; ch’i’ venni presta
ad ogne tua question tanto che basti».

And you, who are before me and have questioned me, tell me if you wish to hear more; for I have come quickly to answer any of your questions, as much as necessary.”

«L’acqua», diss’ io, «e ’l suon de la foresta
impugnan dentro a me novella fede
di cosa ch’io udi’ contraria a questa».

“The water,” I said, “and the sound of the foreststir in me a new faithin something I heard that contradicted this.”

Ond’ ella: «Io dicerò come procede
per sua cagion ciò ch’ammirar ti face,
e purgherò la nebbia che ti fiede.

And she said: "I will tell you how it proceeds,for its cause, that which makes you marvel,and I will clear the fog that wounds you.

Lo sommo Ben, che solo esso a sé piace,
fé l’uom buono e a bene, e questo loco
diede per arr’ a lui d’etterna pace.

The highest Good, which delights only in itself, made man good and for good, and gave him this place as a pledge of eternal peace.

Per sua difalta qui dimorò poco;
per sua difalta in pianto e in affanno
cambiò onesto riso e dolce gioco.

Because of his fault, he did not remain here long; because of his fault, he exchanged honest laughter and sweet games for weeping and suffering.

Perché ’l turbar che sotto da sé fanno
l’essalazion de l’acqua e de la terra,
che quanto posson dietro al calor vanno,

Because the disturbance they create below—the exhalation of the water and the earth—which follows heat as far as it can go,

a l’uomo non facesse alcuna guerra,
questo monte salìo verso ’l ciel tanto,
e libero n’è d’indi ove si serra.

so that it made no war on man, this mountain ascended toward the sky so much, and is free of it from where it closes.

Or perché in circuito tutto quanto
l’aere si volge con la prima volta,
se non li è rotto il cerchio d’alcun canto,

Or because the whole air circles in its first rotation, if the circle is not broken at any point,

in questa altezza ch’è tutta disciolta
ne l’aere vivo, tal moto percuote,
e fa sonar la selva perch’ è folta;

in this height, which is entirely dissolvedin the living air, such motion strikes,and makes the dense forest resound;

e la percossa pianta tanto puote,
che de la sua virtute l’aura impregna
e quella poi, girando, intorno scuote;

and the tree, once struck, has such powerthat it imbues the air with its essence,and the air then, revolving, shakes it loose;

e l’altra terra, secondo ch’è degna
per sé e per suo ciel, concepe e figlia
di diverse virtù diverse legna.

And the other earth, according to its worth,by itself and by its heaven, conceives and bearsof diverse virtues, diverse woods.

Non parrebbe di là poi maraviglia,
udito questo, quando alcuna pianta
sanza seme palese vi s’appiglia.

It would not seem a marvel there, once this is heard, when any plant takes hold without an obvious seed.

E saper dei che la campagna santa
dove tu se’, d’ogne semenza è piena,
e frutto ha in sé che di là non si schianta.

And know that the sacred land where you are is full of every seed, and bears fruit within itself that is not plucked from there.

L’acqua che vedi non surge di vena
che ristori vapor che gel converta,
come fiume ch’acquista e perde lena;

The water you see doesn't spring from a veinthat replenishes vapor turned to ice,like a river that gains and loses strength;

ma esce di fontana salda e certa,
che tanto dal voler di Dio riprende,
quant’ ella versa da due parti aperta.

But it emerges from a fountain, solid and sure,which receives as much from the will of Godas it pours out, open on two sides.

Da questa parte con virtù discende
che toglie altrui memoria del peccato;
da l’altra d’ogne ben fatto la rende.

From this side, virtue descends,erasing others' memory of sin;from the other, it restores the memory of every good deed.

Quinci Letè; così da l’altro lato
Eünoè si chiama, e non adopra
se quinci e quindi pria non è gustato:

From this side is Lethe; so, on the other,Eunoë is named, and it does not workunless first tasted from this side and that.

a tutti altri sapori esto è di sopra.
E avvegna ch’assai possa esser sazia
la sete tua perch’ io più non ti scuopra,

above all other tastes, it is supreme.And though your thirst may well be quenchedbecause I will reveal no more to you,

darotti un corollario ancor per grazia;
né credo che ’l mio dir ti sia men caro,
se oltre promession teco si spazia.

I will further bestow a corollary upon you as a kindness; nor do I believe my words will be less pleasing to you if they extend beyond what was promised.

Quelli ch’anticamente poetaro
l’età de l’oro e suo stato felice,
forse in Parnaso esto loco sognaro.

Those who of old did write in verse of the golden age and its happy state, perhaps in Parnassus they dreamt of this place.

Qui fu innocente l’umana radice;
qui primavera sempre e ogne frutto;
nettare è questo di che ciascun dice».

Here was the innocent root of humankind; here is perpetual springtime and every fruit; this is the nectar that everyone speaks of.”

Io mi rivolsi ’n dietro allora tutto
a’ miei poeti, e vidi che con riso
udito avëan l’ultimo costrutto;

I turned then fully back to my poets, and saw that with a smile they had heard the final statement.

poi a la bella donna torna’ il viso.

Then I turned my face again to the beautiful woman.

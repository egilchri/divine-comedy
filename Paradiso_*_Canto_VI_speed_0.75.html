Paradiso * Canto VI

In the Second Sphere of Heaven, the soul of the Emperor Justinian continues his speech to Dante. He identifies himself and describes his work codifying Roman law. He then recounts an extensive history of the Roman Empire by tracing the path of its imperial standard, the Eagle, from its origins with Pallas through the eras of the Republic, Julius Caesar, Augustus, and Charlemagne. Justinian then condemns the contemporary Guelph and Ghibelline political factions for their misuse of this standard. He explains that the souls in this sphere are those who acted virtuously on Earth but were motivated by a desire for worldly honor and fame. He points out another soul, that of the exiled courtier Romeo of Villeneuve. After Justinian concludes his speech, the host of souls in the sphere of Mercury sing "Hosanna" in unison and then ascend swiftly out of sight.

Paradiso • Canto VI

«Poscia che Costantin l’aquila volse
contr’ al corso del ciel, ch’ella seguio
dietro a l’antico che Lavina tolse,

After Constantine turned the eagleagainst the course of the heavens, which it followedafter the ancient one who took Lavinia,

cento e cent’ anni e più l’uccel di Dio
ne lo stremo d’Europa si ritenne,
vicino a’ monti de’ quai prima uscìo;

A hundred years, a hundred years and more, God’s birdremained in Europe’s farthest reach,near the mountains from which it first had flown;

e sotto l’ombra de le sacre penne
governò ’l mondo lì di mano in mano,
e, sì cangiando, in su la mia pervenne.

And under the shadow of its sacred wings,it governed the world there, hand to hand,and, thus changing, it came upon mine.

Cesare fui e son Iustinïano,
che, per voler del primo amor ch’i’ sento,
d’entro le leggi trassi il troppo e ’l vano.

I was Caesar, and I am Justinian, who, by the will of the primal love I feel, removed the excess and the vanity from within the laws.

E prima ch’io a l’ovra fossi attento,
una natura in Cristo esser, non piùe,
credea, e di tal fede era contento;

And before I turned my attention to the task, I believed that Christ had but one nature, and no more, and with this faith I was content.

ma ’l benedetto Agapito, che fue
sommo pastore, a la fede sincera
mi dirizzò con le parole sue.

But the blessed Agapetus, who was the supreme pastor, directed me to the sincere faith with his words.

Io li credetti; e ciò che ’n sua fede era,
vegg’ io or chiaro sì, come tu vedi
ogni contradizione e falsa e vera.

I believed him; and what was in his faith, I now see so clearly, just as you see every contradiction, false and true.

Tosto che con la Chiesa mossi i piedi,
a Dio per grazia piacque di spirarmi
l’alto lavoro, e tutto ’n lui mi diedi;

As soon as I set foot with the Church, it pleased God to inspire in me, by His grace, the lofty task, and I gave myself wholly to it.

e al mio Belisar commendai l’armi,
cui la destra del ciel fu sì congiunta,
che segno fu ch’i’ dovessi posarmi.

And to my Belisarius, I entrusted the arms, whose right hand of Heaven was so joined, that it was a sign that I should rest.

Or qui a la question prima s’appunta
la mia risposta; ma sua condizione
mi stringe a seguitare alcuna giunta,

Now, who first aimed his questionat my response, but whose conditioncompels me to add a little more,

perché tu veggi con quanta ragione
si move contr’ al sacrosanto segno
e chi ’l s’appropria e chi a lui s’oppone.

so you may see with what good causeit wars against the sacred sign,both those who seize it and those who oppose.

Vedi quanta virtù l’ha fatto degno
di reverenza; e cominciò da l’ora
che Pallante morì per darli regno.

See how much virtue made it worthy of reverence, and it began from the hour that Pallas died to give it reign.

Tu sai ch’el fece in Alba sua dimora
per trecento anni e oltre, infino al fine
che i tre a’ tre pugnar per lui ancora.

You know he made his dwelling in Albafor three hundred years and more, until the endwhen the three fought the three for him again.

E sai ch’el fé dal mal de le Sabine
al dolor di Lucrezia in sette regi,
vincendo intorno le genti vicine.

And you know that from the Sabine’s wrong,to Lucretia’s suffering, he reigned in seven,conquering the neighboring peoples all around.

Sai quel ch’el fé portato da li egregi
Romani incontro a Brenno, incontro a Pirro,
incontro a li altri principi e collegi;

You know what he did, carried by the noble Romans, against Brennus, against Pyrrhus, against the other princes and their alliances;

onde Torquato e Quinzio, che dal cirro
negletto fu nomato, i Deci e ’ Fabi
ebber la fama che volontier mirro.

Where Torquato and Quinzio, who was called by the neglected lock, the Decii and the Fabii, had the fame that I gladly admire.

Esso atterrò l’orgoglio de li Aràbi
che di retro ad Anibale passaro
l’alpestre rocce, Po, di che tu labi.

He humbled the pride of the Arabs, who, after Hannibal, passed the alpine rocks, O Po, from which you flow.

Sott’ esso giovanetti trïunfaro
Scipïone e Pompeo; e a quel colle
sotto ’l qual tu nascesti parve amaro.

Beneath it, the young Scipio and Pompey triumphed; and that hill, under which you were born, seemed bitter.

Poi, presso al tempo che tutto ’l ciel volle
redur lo mondo a suo modo sereno,
Cesare per voler di Roma il tolle.

Then, near the time when all Heaven wished to restore the world to its serene state, Caesar took Rome at its will.

E quel che fé da Varo infino a Reno,
Isara vide ed Era e vide Senna
e ogne valle onde Rodano è pieno.

And what he did from the Var to the Rhine, the Isère saw and the Er and saw the Seine and every valley from which the Rhône is filled.

Quel che fé poi ch’elli uscì di Ravenna
e saltò Rubicon, fu di tal volo,
che nol seguiteria lingua né penna.

What he did after he left Ravenna and crossed the Rubicon was of such a flight that neither tongue nor pen could follow.

Inver’ la Spagna rivolse lo stuolo,
poi ver’ Durazzo, e Farsalia percosse
sì ch’al Nil caldo si sentì del duolo.

Then to Spain he turned his legion's course,Then towards Durazzo, and Pharsalia struck,So that the warm Nile felt the sting of grief.

Antandro e Simeonta, onde si mosse,
rivide e là dov’ Ettore si cuba;
e mal per Tolomeo poscia si scosse.

He saw again Antandros and the Simois, whence he departed, and where Hector lies; then he stirred himself to ill for Ptolemy.

Da indi scese folgorando a Iuba;
onde si volse nel vostro occidente,
ove sentia la pompeana tuba.

Then he descended like lightning to Juba;from there he turned toward your west,where he heard the Pompeian trumpet.

Di quel che fé col baiulo seguente,
Bruto con Cassio ne l’inferno latra,
e Modena e Perugia fu dolente.

Of what he did with the next carrier,Brutus with Cassius howls in Hell,and Modena and Perugia suffered.

Piangene ancor la trista Cleopatra,
che, fuggendoli innanzi, dal colubro
la morte prese subitana e atra.

Let Cleopatra still weep her sorrows,who, fleeing before them, from the serpenttook death, sudden and grim.

Con costui corse infino al lito rubro;
con costui puose il mondo in tanta pace,
che fu serrato a Giano il suo delubro.

With this one, he ran as far as the Red Sea's shore;with this one, he placed the world in such peacethat Janus's temple was shut.

Ma ciò che ’l segno che parlar mi face
fatto avea prima e poi era fatturo
per lo regno mortal ch’a lui soggiace,

But what the sign that prompts my speech had done before, and then was destined to do, for the mortal realm that is subject to him,

diventa in apparenza poco e scuro,
se in mano al terzo Cesare si mira
con occhio chiaro e con affetto puro;

appears small and dim,if viewed in the hand of the third Caesarwith clear eye and pure affection;

ché la viva giustizia che mi spira,
li concedette, in mano a quel ch’i’ dico,
gloria di far vendetta a la sua ira.

for the living justice that inspires me granted to him, in the hand of the one I speak of, glory of exacting vengeance for its wrath.

Or qui t’ammira in ciò ch’io ti replìco:
poscia con Tito a far vendetta corse
de la vendetta del peccato antico.

Now admire what I tell you: then he ran with Titus to avenge the vengeance of the ancient sin.

E quando il dente longobardo morse
la Santa Chiesa, sotto le sue ali
Carlo Magno, vincendo, la soccorse.

And when the Lombard tooth bitThe Holy Church, beneath its wings,Charlemagne, victorious, came to her aid.

Omai puoi giudicar di quei cotali
ch’io accusai di sopra e di lor falli,
che son cagion di tutti vostri mali.

Now you may judge those I accused above and their faults, which are the cause of all your evils.

L’uno al pubblico segno i gigli gialli
oppone, e l’altro appropria quello a parte,
sì ch’è forte a veder chi più si falli.

One flaunts the yellow lilies on the public banner,the other claims them for his private gain,so it is hard to see who errs the more.

Faccian li Ghibellin, faccian lor arte
sott’ altro segno, ché mal segue quello
sempre chi la giustizia e lui diparte;

Let the Ghibellines practice their craftunder another banner, for whoever partsjustice from themselves always errs.

e non l’abbatta esto Carlo novello
coi Guelfi suoi, ma tema de li artigli
ch’a più alto leon trasser lo vello.

And may this new Charles not bring it down,along with his Guelphs, but fear the talonsthat stripped the hide from the loftier lion.

Molte fïate già pianser li figli
per la colpa del padre, e non si creda
che Dio trasmuti l’armi per suoi gigli!

Many times before, sons have weptfor their fathers' sins, and let no one believethat God alters his weapons for their offspring!

Questa picciola stella si correda
d’i buoni spirti che son stati attivi
perché onore e fama li succeda:

This little star is adornedwith the good spirits who were activeso that honor and fame would succeed them.

e quando li disiri poggian quivi,
sì disvïando, pur convien che i raggi
del vero amore in sù poggin men vivi.

and when desires settle there, thus straying, it is inevitable that the rays of true love rise less brightly.

Ma nel commensurar d’i nostri gaggi
col merto è parte di nostra letizia,
perché non li vedem minor né maggi.

But in the measuring of our rewards against merit, lies a portion of our joy, because we see them neither diminished nor increased.

Quindi addolcisce la viva giustizia
in noi l’affetto sì, che non si puote
torcer già mai ad alcuna nequizia.

Thus affection sweetens living justice in us, so that it can never be twisted toward any wickedness.

Diverse voci fanno dolci note;
così diversi scanni in nostra vita
rendon dolce armonia tra queste rote.

Diverse voices create sweet notes;thus diverse stations in our liferender sweet harmony among these spheres.

E dentro a la presente margarita
luce la luce di Romeo, di cui
fu l’ovra grande e bella mal gradita.

And within this present pearlshines the light of Romeo, whose deedswere great and beautiful, though ill-regarded.

Ma i Provenzai che fecer contra lui
non hanno riso; e però mal cammina
qual si fa danno del ben fare altrui.

But the Provençals who warred against himhave not laughed; and thus it fares ill for onewho harms himself by doing good for others.

Quattro figlie ebbe, e ciascuna reina,
Ramondo Beringhiere, e ciò li fece
Romeo, persona umìle e peregrina.

Ramond Berenguer had four daughters, each a queen. Romeo, a humble and wandering soul, accomplished this for him.

E poi il mosser le parole biece
a dimandar ragione a questo giusto,
che li assegnò sette e cinque per diece,

And then the crooked words spurred him onto demand of this just man his reckoning,who assigned him seven and five for ten.

indi partissi povero e vetusto;
e se ’l mondo sapesse il cor ch’elli ebbe
mendicando sua vita a frusto a frusto,

then he departed, poor and worn with age;and if the world knew the heart that he bore,begging his life in bits, upon life's stage,

assai lo loda, e più lo loderebbe».

"He praises him greatly, and would praise him even more."

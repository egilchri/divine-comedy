Inferno * Canto XVI

As Dante and Virgil continue across the burning sand of the Seventh Circle, walking along a stone embankment that shields them from the raining fire, a group of three naked, scorched shades breaks away from a larger company and runs toward them. The shades, notable Florentines named Iacopo Rusticucci, Guido Guerra, and Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, cannot stop moving due to their punishment. They form a rotating wheel, running in a circle to keep their faces turned toward Dante as they speak. Rusticucci asks Dante for news of Florence. Dante responds that new wealth and pride have caused the city's moral decline. The souls accept his grim report, ask to be remembered in the world above, and then speed away. Reaching the edge of a great precipice where the river Phlegethon plunges into the next circle, Virgil instructs Dante to hand over the cord he wears as a belt. Virgil throws the coiled cord into the abyss, and in response, a strange, monstrous form begins swimming up from the dark depths.

Inferno • Canto XVI

Già era in loco onde s’udia ’l rimbombo
de l’acqua che cadea ne l’altro giro,
simile a quel che l’arnie fanno rombo,

We were already in the place where the echoing soundof the water falling into the next circle was heard,similar to the buzzing made by beehives.

quando tre ombre insieme si partiro,
correndo, d’una torma che passava
sotto la pioggia de l’aspro martiro.

When three shades departed together, running, from a company that was passing under the rain of harsh torment.

Venian ver’ noi, e ciascuna gridava:
«Sòstati tu ch’a l’abito ne sembri
esser alcun di nostra terra prava».

They came toward us, and each one cried: "Stop, you, who in your garb appear to be one of our wicked land."

Ahimè, che piaghe vidi ne’ lor membri,
ricenti e vecchie, da le fiamme incese!
Ancor men duol pur ch’i’ me ne rimembri.

Alas, what wounds I saw upon their limbs, fresh and old, seared by the flames! Recalling them still brings me no less pain.

A le lor grida il mio dottor s’attese;
volse ’l viso ver’ me, e «Or aspetta»,
disse, «a costor si vuole esser cortese.

My guide then paused, listening to their cries. He turned his face toward me and said, "Now, you must be courteous to them."

E se non fosse il foco che saetta
la natura del loco, i’ dicerei
che meglio stesse a te che a lor la fretta».

And if the fire that blazes throughthe nature of this place weren’t here, I would saythe hurry is better suited to you than to them.”

Ricominciar, come noi restammo, ei
l’antico verso; e quando a noi fuor giunti,
fenno una rota di sé tutti e trei.

He began again, as we stood, the ancient verse; and when they arrived before us, the three of them made a circle.

Qual sogliono i campion far nudi e unti,
avvisando lor presa e lor vantaggio,
prima che sien tra lor battuti e punti,

As champions, naked and oiled, are wont to do, sizing up their grip and their advantage, before they are struck and punctured.

così rotando, ciascuno il visaggio
drizzava a me, sì che ’n contraro il collo
faceva ai piè continüo vïaggio.

Thus spinning, each one lifted their face towards me, so that, with their necks twisted, they moved in a continuous backward march.

E «Se miseria d’esto loco sollo
rende in dispetto noi e nostri prieghi»,
cominciò l’uno, «e ’l tinto aspetto e brollo,

"If the wretchedness of this place alonecauses us to scorn our prayers and our suffering,"one began, "and our stained and ruined appearance,

la fama nostra il tuo animo pieghi
a dirne chi tu se’, che i vivi piedi
così sicuro per lo ’nferno freghi.

May our story bend your spiritto tell us who you are, who treads with such confidencethrough Hell with living feet.

Questi, l’orme di cui pestar mi vedi,
tutto che nudo e dipelato vada,
fu di grado maggior che tu non credi:

This one, whose footprints you see me treading upon, though he goes naked and shorn of skin, was of a higher rank than you believe:

nepote fu de la buona Gualdrada;
Guido Guerra ebbe nome, e in sua vita
fece col senno assai e con la spada.

He was the grandson of the good Gualdrada; Guido Guerra was his name, and in his life he accomplished much with his wisdom and his sword.

L’altro, ch’appresso me la rena trita,
è Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, la cui voce
nel mondo sù dovria esser gradita.

The other, who treads the sand beside me, is Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, whose voice should have been esteemed in the world above.

E io, che posto son con loro in croce,
Iacopo Rusticucci fui, e certo
la fiera moglie più ch’altro mi nuoce».

And I, who am crucified here with them, was Jacopo Rusticucci, and truly, my savage wife has harmed me more than anything else.

S’i’ fossi stato dal foco coperto,
gittato mi sarei tra lor di sotto,
e credo che ’l dottor l’avria sofferto;

If I had been covered by the fire, I would have thrown myself beneath them, and I believe the doctor would have allowed it.

ma perch’ io mi sarei brusciato e cotto,
vinse paura la mia buona voglia
che di loro abbracciar mi facea ghiotto.

But because I would have been scorched and roasted, fear overcame my good will, which made me eager to embrace them.

Poi cominciai: «Non dispetto, ma doglia
la vostra condizion dentro mi fisse,
tanta che tardi tutta si dispoglia,

Then I began: "Not disdain, but grief, your condition fixed within me, so much that it departs only slowly.

tosto che questo mio segnor mi disse
parole per le quali i’ mi pensai
che qual voi siete, tal gente venisse.

As soon as this lord of mine spoke words to me, through which I understood that people like you might come.

Di vostra terra sono, e sempre mai
l’ovra di voi e li onorati nomi
con affezion ritrassi e ascoltai.

I am from your land, and I have always, with affection, observed and listened to your works and honored names.

Lascio lo fele e vo per dolci pomi
promessi a me per lo verace duca;
ma ’nfino al centro pria convien ch’i’ tomi».

I leave the gall and go for sweet fruitpromised to me by the truthful guide;but first I must descend to the center.

«Se lungamente l’anima conduca
le membra tue», rispuose quelli ancora,
«e se la fama tua dopo te luca,

«If your soul guides your limbs for long,» he answered again, «and if your fame shines after you,

cortesia e valor dì se dimora
ne la nostra città sì come suole,
o se del tutto se n’è gita fora;

courtesy and valor, say if they still dwellwithin our city, as they once did,or if they’ve utterly departed hence.

ché Guiglielmo Borsiere, il qual si duole
con noi per poco e va là coi compagni,
assai ne cruccia con le sue parole».

for William Borsiere, who grieveswith us a while, and goes with his companions there,annoys us greatly with his words.”

«La gente nuova e i sùbiti guadagni
orgoglio e dismisura han generata,
Fiorenza, in te, sì che tu già ten piagni».

"New wealth and sudden profits have bred in you, Florence, pride and excess, so that you already weep for it."

Così gridai con la faccia levata;
e i tre, che ciò inteser per risposta,
guardar l’un l’altro com’ al ver si guata.

Thus I cried out, my face lifted high. And the three, who understood this as a reply, looked at each other as one does when seeing the truth.

«Se l’altre volte sì poco ti costa»,
rispuoser tutti, «il satisfare altrui,
felice te se sì parli a tua posta!

"If at other times it costs you so little," they all responded, "to satisfy others, how fortunate you are if you speak so readily!"

Però, se campi d’esti luoghi bui
e torni a riveder le belle stelle,
quando ti gioverà dicere “I’ fui”,

But if you escape these shadowed landsand see the lovely stars once more,when will you find it worthwhile to say, "I was"?

fa che di noi a la gente favelle».
Indi rupper la rota, e a fuggirsi
ali sembiar le gambe loro isnelle.

so that the people might speak of us."Then they broke the circle, and their swift legs seemed like wings in flight.

Un amen non saria possuto dirsi
tosto così com’ e’ fuoro spariti;
per ch’al maestro parve di partirsi.

A single "amen" could not have been spokenas quickly as they vanished from sight;therefore, it seemed right for the master to depart.

Io lo seguiva, e poco eravam iti,
che ’l suon de l’acqua n’era sì vicino,
che per parlar saremmo a pena uditi.

I followed him, and we had gone only a short distance when the sound of the water was so close that we would scarcely be heard speaking.

Come quel fiume c’ha proprio cammino
prima dal Monte Viso ’nver’ levante,
da la sinistra costa d’Apennino,

Like that river which has its own path,first from Mount Viso toward the east,from the left side of the Apennines,

che si chiama Acquacheta suso, avante
che si divalli giù nel basso letto,
e a Forlì di quel nome è vacante,

which is called Acquacheta, up above,before it plunges down into its lower bed,and at Forlì its name is no longer heard,

rimbomba là sovra San Benedetto
de l’Alpe per cadere ad una scesa
ove dovea per mille esser recetto;

It echoes there above San Benedettoof the Apennines, before its dropwhere it was to be received by a thousand;

così, giù d’una ripa discoscesa,
trovammo risonar quell’ acqua tinta,
sì che ’n poc’ ora avria l’orecchia offesa.

Thus, down a sloping bank,we found that tainted water roaring,so that in a short time it would have offended the ear.

Io avea una corda intorno cinta,
e con essa pensai alcuna volta
prender la lonza a la pelle dipinta.

I had a cord girded around me, and with it, I once thought I could capture the leopard with the painted hide.

Poscia ch’io l’ebbi tutta da me sciolta,
sì come ’l duca m’avea comandato,
porsila a lui aggroppata e ravvolta.

After I had untied it completely, as the guide had commanded me, I presented it to him, knotted and coiled.

Ond’ ei si volse inver’ lo destro lato,
e alquanto di lunge da la sponda
la gittò giuso in quell’ alto burrato.

Then he turned toward the right side, and, a little distance from the bank, he cast it down into the high ravine.

‘E’ pur convien che novità risponda’,
dicea fra me medesmo, ‘al novo cenno
che ’l maestro con l’occhio sì seconda’.

"And yet it's necessary that a new thing respond,"I said to myself, "to the new signthat the master seconds with his eye."

Ahi quanto cauti li uomini esser dienno
presso a color che non veggion pur l’ovra,
ma per entro i pensier miran col senno!

Ah, how cautiously men must behavearound those who don't just see the work,but gaze into thoughts with their understanding!

El disse a me: «Tosto verrà di sovra
ciò ch’io attendo e che il tuo pensier sogna;
tosto convien ch’al tuo viso si scovra».

He said to me: "Soon, above, will arrivethat which I await and which your thought dreams of;soon it must be revealed to your face."

Sempre a quel ver c’ha faccia di menzogna
de’ l’uom chiuder le labbra fin ch’el puote,
però che sanza colpa fa vergogna;

Always to that truth which has the face of a liea man must keep his lips closed as long as he is able,because without fault it brings shame.

ma qui tacer nol posso; e per le note
di questa comedìa, lettor, ti giuro,
s’elle non sien di lunga grazia vòte,

But here I cannot be silent; and by the notesof this comedy, reader, I swear to you,if they are not empty of long-lived grace,

ch’i’ vidi per quell’ aere grosso e scuro
venir notando una figura in suso,
maravigliosa ad ogne cor sicuro,

that I saw through that thick, dark aira figure coming upward, swimming,marvellous to any fearless heart.

sì come torna colui che va giuso
talora a solver l’àncora ch’aggrappa
o scoglio o altro che nel mare è chiuso,

so as one returns who goes below,at times to loose the anchor that has grippeda rock or something else held in the sea.

che ’n sù si stende e da piè si rattrappa.

that stretches upward and shrinks at its base.

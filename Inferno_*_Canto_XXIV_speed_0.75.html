Inferno * Canto XXIV

After his initial frustration, Virgil assists Dante in a difficult climb up the ruins of a collapsed bridge into the eighth circle's seventh ditch (*bolgia*). Looking down, they see a dark pit filled with a terrifying mass of serpents. Among the snakes, the naked shades of thieves run in terror. Their hands are bound behind their backs by serpents that knot themselves through the sinners' bodies. Dante witnesses one sinner being bitten in the neck by a snake. The man instantly combusts, turns to ash, and then slowly and painfully re-forms into his original shape. Questioned by Virgil, the sinner identifies himself as Vanni Fucci of Pistoia, punished for stealing holy relics. To spite Dante, Fucci delivers a dark political prophecy. The canto ends as Fucci makes an obscene gesture toward God, prompting several snakes to swarm and silence him.

In quella parte del giovanetto anno
che ’l sole i crin sotto l’Aquario tempra
e già le notti al mezzo dì sen vanno,

In that part of the young year when the sun tempers its rays beneath Aquarius and the nights already wane towards noon,

quando la brina in su la terra assempra
l’imagine di sua sorella bianca,
ma poco dura a la sua penna tempra,

when frost upon the earth assemblesthe image of its white sister,but its pen endures but briefly,

lo villanello a cui la roba manca,
si leva, e guarda, e vede la campagna
biancheggiar tutta; ond’ ei si batte l’anca,

the peasant, whose goods are wanting,arises, looks, and sees the countrysideall whitening; at which he slaps his hip,

ritorna in casa, e qua e là si lagna,
come ’l tapin che non sa che si faccia;
poi riede, e la speranza ringavagna,

returns home, and complains this way and that,like the poor wretch who knows not what to do;then goes back out, and hope regains its hold,

veggendo ’l mondo aver cangiata faccia
in poco d’ora, e prende suo vincastro
e fuor le pecorelle a pascer caccia.

seeing the world change its facein a short time, he takes up his staffand drives his little sheep out to pasture.

Così mi fece sbigottir lo mastro
quand’ io li vidi sì turbar la fronte,
e così tosto al mal giunse lo ’mpiastro;

The master startled me so,when I saw his brow so troubled,and then so quickly, the cure arrived for the malady;

ché, come noi venimmo al guasto ponte,
lo duca a me si volse con quel piglio
dolce ch’io vidi prima a piè del monte.

For, when we came upon the ruined bridge, the guide turned to me with that sweet look I first saw at the mountain's foot.

Le braccia aperse, dopo alcun consiglio
eletto seco riguardando prima
ben la ruina, e diedemi di piglio.

He opened his arms, after some deliberation and careful consideration of the wreckage, and took hold of me.

E come quei ch’adopera ed estima,
che sempre par che ’nnanzi si proveggia,
così, levando me sù ver’ la cima

And like one who works and calculates, who always seems to anticipate, so, lifting me towards the summit,

d’un ronchione, avvisava un’altra scheggia
dicendo: «Sovra quella poi t’aggrappa;
ma tenta pria s’è tal ch’ella ti reggia».

with a grumpy tone, he warned another ledge, saying, “Then, grab onto that one; but first, try to see if it’ll hold you.”

Non era via da vestito di cappa,
ché noi a pena, ei lieve e io sospinto,
potavam sù montar di chiappa in chiappa.

He wasn't wearing a cloak, sowe could barely, he nimble and I pushed,climb up from ledge to ledge.

E se non fosse che da quel precinto
più che da l’altro era la costa corta,
non so di lui, ma io sarei ben vinto.

And if it weren't that the climbwas shorter from that beltthan from the other, I don't know about him, but I'd be defeated.

Ma perché Malebolge inver’ la porta
del bassissimo pozzo tutta pende,
lo sito di ciascuna valle porta

But because Malebolge leans towards the gateof the lowest pit,the location of each valley it carries.

che l’una costa surge e l’altra scende;
noi pur venimmo al fine in su la punta
onde l’ultima pietra si scoscende.

that one bank rises and the other descends;we arrived eventually at the crestwhere the final stone breaks off.

La lena m’era del polmon sì munta
quand’ io fui sù, ch’i’ non potea più oltre,
anzi m’assisi ne la prima giunta.

My breath was so spent from my lungswhen I was at the top that I couldn’t go on,instead, I sat down at the first ledge.

«Omai convien che tu così ti spoltre»,
disse ’l maestro; «ché, seggendo in piuma,
in fama non si vien, né sotto coltre;

"Now you must rouse yourself," the master said, "because you cannot gain renown by sitting in feathers, nor under a blanket.

sanza la qual chi sua vita consuma,
cotal vestigio in terra di sé lascia,
qual fummo in aere e in acqua la schiuma.

Without which, whoever spends their life, leaves behind such a trace of themselves on earth, as smoke in the air and foam on the water.

E però leva sù; vinci l’ambascia
con l’animo che vince ogne battaglia,
se col suo grave corpo non s’accascia.

And yet, rise up; conquer your anguishwith the spirit that wins every battle,if it does not succumb to the weight of its own body.

Più lunga scala convien che si saglia;
non basta da costoro esser partito.
Se tu mi ’ntendi, or fa sì che ti vaglia».

A longer climb remains;it is not enough to have departed from them.If you understand me, now act so that it benefits you."

Leva’mi allor, mostrandomi fornito
meglio di lena ch’i’ non mi sentia,
e dissi: «Va, ch’i’ son forte e ardito».

Then he raised me, demonstrating a strengthgreater than I felt within myself,and I said, "Go, for I am strong and brave."

Su per lo scoglio prendemmo la via,
ch’era ronchioso, stretto e malagevole,
ed erto più assai che quel di pria.

Up on the crag, we took the path,which was rough, narrow, and difficult,and much steeper than the first.

Parlando andava per non parer fievole;
onde una voce uscì de l’altro fosso,
a parole formar disconvenevole.

Speaking, he continued, so as not to seem faint; then a voice issued from the other ravine, forming words, but unsuitable.

Non so che disse, ancor che sovra ’l dosso
fossi de l’arco già che varca quivi;
ma chi parlava ad ire parea mosso.

I don't know what he said, though I was already on the ridge, the arch that crosses there; but the one who spoke seemed moved to anger.

Io era vòlto in giù, ma li occhi vivi
non poteano ire al fondo per lo scuro;
per ch’io: «Maestro, fa che tu arrivi

I was turned downward, but my living eyes couldn't reach the bottom because of the darkness; so I said: "Master, see that you make it."

da l’altro cinghio e dismontiam lo muro;
ché, com’ i’ odo quinci e non intendo,
così giù veggio e neente affiguro».

From the other barrier, let us descend the wall; for as I hear here, yet I do not understand, so below I see, but I discern nothing.

«Altra risposta», disse, «non ti rendo
se non lo far; ché la dimanda onesta
si de’ seguir con l’opera tacendo».

"I'll give you no other answer," he said, "unless I do it; for a just request must be followed by action, not words."

Noi discendemmo il ponte da la testa
dove s’aggiugne con l’ottava ripa,
e poi mi fu la bolgia manifesta:

We descended the bridge from the head, where it joins with the eighth bank, and then the ditch was revealed to me.

e vidivi entro terribile stipa
di serpenti, e di sì diversa mena
che la memoria il sangue ancor mi scipa.

I saw myself then within a terrible heap of serpents, a brood so varied that the memory of it still chills my blood.

Più non si vanti Libia con sua rena;
ché se chelidri, iaculi e faree
produce, e cencri con anfisibena,

Let Libya no longer boast of her sands; for if she produces chelidri, jaculi, and faree, and cencri with amphisbaena,

né tante pestilenzie né sì ree
mostrò già mai con tutta l’Etïopia
né con ciò che di sopra al Mar Rosso èe.

Nor did any land ever show so many plagues, nor such wicked ones, with all of Ethiopia, nor with all that lies above the Red Sea.

Tra questa cruda e tristissima copia
corrëan genti nude e spaventate,
sanza sperar pertugio o elitropia:

Through this cruel and sorrowful throngran naked and terrified people,without hope of escape or solace:

con serpi le man dietro avean legate;
quelle ficcavan per le ren la coda
e ’l capo, ed eran dinanzi aggroppate.

Their hands were bound behind them with serpents; these snakes thrust their tails and heads through the loins, knotting together in front.

Ed ecco a un ch’era da nostra proda,
s’avventò un serpente che ’l trafisse
là dove ’l collo a le spalle s’annoda.

And then, at one who was upon our shore, a serpent lunged, piercing him where the neck joins the shoulders.

Né O sì tosto mai né I si scrisse,
com’ el s’accese e arse, e cener tutto
convenne che cascando divenisse;

Nor ever was it written, no, not so quickly,as he ignited and burned, and as ashes,he had to become, falling to the ground;

e poi che fu a terra sì distrutto,
la polver si raccolse per sé stessa
e ’n quel medesmo ritornò di butto.

And after he was so destroyed upon the ground,the dust gathered itself together,and in that same instant, it returned to its original form.

Così per li gran savi si confessa
che la fenice more e poi rinasce,
quando al cinquecentesimo anno appressa;

Thus it is confessed by the great sagesthat the phoenix dies and then is reborn,when it approaches the five-hundredth year;

erba né biado in sua vita non pasce,
ma sol d’incenso lagrime e d’amomo,
e nardo e mirra son l’ultime fasce.

It feeds not on grass or grain in its life, but only on tears of incense and balsam, and its final wrappings are nard and myrrh.

E qual è quel che cade, e non sa como,
per forza di demon ch’a terra il tira,
o d’altra oppilazion che lega l’omo,

And as one falls, unaware how, by the force of a demon that pulls him earthward, or by some other binding obstruction.

quando si leva, che ’ntorno si mira
tutto smarrito de la grande angoscia
ch’elli ha sofferta, e guardando sospira:

when he rises, looking around him,utterly lost in the great anguishhe has suffered, and gazing sighs:

tal era ’l peccator levato poscia.
Oh potenza di Dio, quant’ è severa,
che cotai colpi per vendetta croscia!

Then the sinner arose. Oh, how severe is the power of God, that it rains down such blows in vengeance!

Lo duca il domandò poi chi ello era;
per ch’ei rispuose: «Io piovvi di Toscana,
poco tempo è, in questa gola fiera.

Then the guide asked him who he was, and he replied, "I came from Tuscany, and it was not long ago that I rained into this savage gorge."

Vita bestial mi piacque e non umana,
sì come a mul ch’i’ fui; son Vanni Fucci
bestia, e Pistoia mi fu degna tana».

Bestial life pleased me, not human, like a mule I was. I am Vanni Fucci, beast, and Pistoia was a worthy lair for me.

E ïo al duca: «Dilli che non mucci,
e domanda che colpa qua giù ’l pinse;
ch’io ’l vidi uomo di sangue e di crucci».

And I to the Duke: "Tell him not to hide himself, and ask what guilt has brought him down here; for I saw him a man of blood and of wrath."

E ’l peccator, che ’ntese, non s’infinse,
ma drizzò verso me l’animo e ’l volto,
e di trista vergogna si dipinse;

And the sinner, who understood, did not feign, but turned his mind and face toward me, and was painted with sorrowful shame.

poi disse: «Più mi duol che tu m’hai colto
ne la miseria dove tu mi vedi,
che quando fui de l’altra vita tolto.

Then he said: "It pains me more that you have found mein this wretched state that you now see,than when I was taken from my other life."

Io non posso negar quel che tu chiedi;
in giù son messo tanto perch’ io fui
ladro a la sagrestia d’i belli arredi,

I can't deny what you're asking; I've been placed so low because I stole from the sacristy the beautiful furnishings.

e falsamente già fu apposto altrui.
Ma perché di tal vista tu non godi,
se mai sarai di fuor da’ luoghi bui,

and falsely accused someone else. But because you don't enjoy that sight, if you ever get outside of the dark places,

apri li orecchi al mio annunzio, e odi.
Pistoia in pria d’i Neri si dimagra;
poi Fiorenza rinova gente e modi.

Open your ears to my announcement, and listen. Pistoia first shrinks in the grip of the Black faction; then Florence renews its people and its ways.

Tragge Marte vapor di Val di Magra
ch’è di torbidi nuvoli involuto;
e con tempesta impetüosa e agra

Mars draws vapors from the Magra Valley, wrapped in dark clouds. With a raging and bitter storm...

sovra Campo Picen fia combattuto;
ond’ ei repente spezzerà la nebbia,
sì ch’ogne Bianco ne sarà feruto.

Above the Piceno field, the battle will be joined;from there, he will suddenly break the fog,so that every White will be wounded.

E detto l’ho perché doler ti debbia!».

And I have said it so that you will grieve!

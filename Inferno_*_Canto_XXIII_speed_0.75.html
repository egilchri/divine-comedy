Inferno * Canto XXIII

Fleeing the pursuing Malebranche demons from the previous circle, Virgil carries Dante and slides down a steep bank into the sixth bolgia. Here they observe the hypocrites, souls walking in a slow, endless procession. They are burdened by heavy cloaks that are beautifully gilded on the outside but are lined with lead, forcing them into a state of weary, labored movement. Two of the sinners, the Bolognese friars Catalano and Loderingo, identify themselves and describe their punishment. Dante then sees a man crucified on the ground, positioned so that every passing sinner must walk over him. Catalano explains this is Caiaphas, the high priest who counseled the Pharisees. When Virgil asks how to leave the bolgia, Catalano points to a collapsed bridge, revealing that the demon Malacoda had lied to them about the path. Angered by this deception, Virgil strides away, with Dante following him.

Inferno • Canto XXIII

Taciti, soli, sanza compagnia
n’andavam l’un dinanzi e l’altro dopo,
come frati minor vanno per via.

Silent, alone, without company,we went, one before and the other behind,like minor friars on their way.

Vòlt’ era in su la favola d’Isopo
lo mio pensier per la presente rissa,
dov’ el parlò de la rana e del topo;

My thought turned to Aesop's fableabout the present squabble,where he spoke of the frog and the mouse.

ché più non si pareggia ‘mo’ e ‘issa’
che l’un con l’altro fa, se ben s’accoppia
principio e fine con la mente fissa.

Because "now" and "then" no longer compare,they become one another if you joinbeginning and end with a focused mind.

E come l’un pensier de l’altro scoppia,
così nacque di quello un altro poi,
che la prima paura mi fé doppia.

And as one thought bursts forth from another,so another was born then of that one,which doubled the fear I felt before.

Io pensava così: ‘Questi per noi
sono scherniti con danno e con beffa
sì fatta, ch’assai credo che lor nòi.

I was thinking: 'These souls here are so scorned,mocked with such harm and derision,that, truly, I believe it pains them greatly.'

Se l’ira sovra ’l mal voler s’aggueffa,
ei ne verranno dietro più crudeli
che ’l cane a quella lievre ch’elli acceffa’.

If wrath is added to wicked desire, they will pursue us more cruelly than a dog pursues the hare it catches.

Già mi sentia tutti arricciar li peli
de la paura e stava in dietro intento,
quand’ io dissi: «Maestro, se non celi

I could already feel my hair prickling with fear, and I was intently drawing back when I said: “Master, unless you conceal…”

te e me tostamente, i’ ho pavento
d’i Malebranche. Noi li avem già dietro;
io li ’magino sì, che già li sento».

and I, in terror,of the Malebranche, I am scorched. They are already behind us;I imagine them so, I can already feel them.”

E quei: «S’i’ fossi di piombato vetro,
l’imagine di fuor tua non trarrei
più tosto a me, che quella dentro ’mpetro.

And they said: "If I were made of leaded glass, the image of your outward form would not be impressed upon me as quickly as the one I hold within."

Pur mo venieno i tuo’ pensier tra ’ miei,
con simile atto e con simile faccia,
sì che d’intrambi un sol consiglio fei.

And then your thoughts came among mine, with similar action and with a similar face, so that I made a single counsel of the two.

S’elli è che sì la destra costa giaccia,
che noi possiam ne l’altra bolgia scendere,
noi fuggirem l’imaginata caccia».

If it is true that the right bank yields in such a way, that we can descend into the next pouch, we will flee the imagined hunt.”

Già non compié di tal consiglio rendere,
ch’io li vidi venir con l’ali tese
non molto lungi, per volerne prendere.

He had barely finished giving that advice, when I saw them coming toward us, wings spread wide, not far off, intending to seize us.

Lo duca mio di sùbito mi prese,
come la madre ch’al romore è desta
e vede presso a sé le fiamme accese,

My guide seized me at once, like a mother who is roused by a noise and sees flames near her,

che prende il figlio e fugge e non s’arresta,
avendo più di lui che di sé cura,
tanto che solo una camiscia vesta;

who grabs her child and flees without stopping, caring more for him than for herself, so much so that she is only wearing a shift.

e giù dal collo de la ripa dura
supin si diede a la pendente roccia,
che l’un de’ lati a l’altra bolgia tura.

And down from the hard edge of the bankhe flung himself, face-up, onto the overhanging rockthat closes in one side of the next abyss.

Non corse mai sì tosto acqua per doccia
a volger ruota di molin terragno,
quand’ ella più verso le pale approccia,

As water never raced so swiftly through a sluiceto turn a mill wheel in its earthly course,when it nears the blades with greatest force,

come ’l maestro mio per quel vivagno,
portandosene me sovra ’l suo petto,
come suo figlio, non come compagno.

As my master arrived at that point,carrying me upon his chest,like his own son, not like a companion.

A pena fuoro i piè suoi giunti al letto
del fondo giù, ch’e’ furon in sul colle
sovresso noi; ma non lì era sospetto:

As soon as his feet reached the bedat the very bottom, they were on the hillabove us; but there was no suspicion there:

ché l’alta provedenza che lor volle
porre ministri de la fossa quinta,
poder di partirs’ indi a tutti tolle.

because the high providence that willed themto be ministers of the fifth pit,takes away from all the power to depart thence.

Là giù trovammo una gente dipinta
che giva intorno assai con lenti passi,
piangendo e nel sembiante stanca e vinta.

Down there, we found a painted people,who went about with very slow steps,weeping, their faces weary and defeated.

Elli avean cappe con cappucci bassi
dinanzi a li occhi, fatte de la taglia
che in Clugnì per li monaci fassi.

They had cloaks with low hoods,drawn down over their eyes, made in the styleworn by the monks of Cluny.

Di fuor dorate son, sì ch’elli abbaglia;
ma dentro tutte piombo, e gravi tanto,
che Federigo le mettea di paglia.

Outside, they're gilded, so they dazzle the eye; but inside, they're all lead, and so heavy that Frederick would have put straw on them.

Oh in etterno faticoso manto!
Noi ci volgemmo ancor pur a man manca
con loro insieme, intenti al tristo pianto;

Oh, in the eternally wearisome cloak!We turned again, still to the left,with them together, intent on the sorrowful weeping;

ma per lo peso quella gente stanca
venìa sì pian, che noi eravam nuovi
di compagnia ad ogne mover d’anca.

But by the burden, those weary people moved so slowly that we, with each shift of their hips, found ourselves newly in their company.

Per ch’io al duca mio: «Fa che tu trovi
alcun ch’al fatto o al nome si conosca,
e li occhi, sì andando, intorno movi».

Whereupon I said to my guide: “See if you can findone who is known by deed or name,and as we go, move your eyes around.”

E un che ’ntese la parola tosca,
di retro a noi gridò: «Tenete i piedi,
voi che correte sì per l’aura fosca!

And one who understood the Tuscan words,cried out behind us: "Hold your feet,you who run so swiftly through the dusky air!"

Forse ch’avrai da me quel che tu chiedi».
Onde ’l duca si volse e disse: «Aspetta,
e poi secondo il suo passo procedi».

"Perhaps you shall have from me that which you seek." Whereupon the guide turned and said: "Wait, and then proceed according to his pace."

Ristetti, e vidi due mostrar gran fretta
de l’animo, col viso, d’esser meco;
ma tardavali ’l carco e la via stretta.

They stopped, and I saw two show great eagerness, their faces revealing a desire to be with me; but their burden and the narrow road hindered them.

Quando fuor giunti, assai con l’occhio bieco
mi rimiraron sanza far parola;
poi si volsero in sé, e dicean seco:

When they arrived, they eyed me with suspicion, never uttering a word. Then they turned inward, and spoke amongst themselves:

«Costui par vivo a l’atto de la gola;
e s’e’ son morti, per qual privilegio
vanno scoperti de la grave stola?».

"This one seems alive by the movement of his throat; and if they're dead, by what privilege are they walking around without their heavy cloaks?"

Poi disser me: «O Tosco, ch’al collegio
de l’ipocriti tristi se’ venuto,
dir chi tu se’ non avere in dispregio».

Then they said to me: "Oh Tuscan, you who have come to the collegeof the sorrowful hypocrites, do not disdain to tell us who you are."

E io a loro: «I’ fui nato e cresciuto
sovra ’l bel fiume d’Arno a la gran villa,
e son col corpo ch’i’ ho sempre avuto.

And I to them: "I was born and raisedabove the lovely Arno, in the great city,and I am here still in this body."

Ma voi chi siete, a cui tanto distilla
quant’ i’ veggio dolor giù per le guance?
e che pena è in voi che sì sfavilla?».

But you, who are you, from whom so much sufferingstreams down your cheeks as I can see?And what torment is it that shines so brightly in you?"

E l’un rispuose a me: «Le cappe rance
son di piombo sì grosse, che li pesi
fan così cigolar le lor bilance.

And one replied to me: "These orange cloaksare so heavy with lead, that their weightsmake the scales of their balances groan in this way."

Frati godenti fummo, e bolognesi;
io Catalano e questi Loderingo
nomati, e da tua terra insieme presi

We were Friars of the Jolly Order, and Bolognese; I, Catalano, and this one, Loderingo, both taken together from your land.

come suole esser tolto un uom solingo,
per conservar sua pace; e fummo tali,
ch’ancor si pare intorno dal Gardingo».

as a solitary man is often taken, to keep his peace; and we were such, that it is still visible around Gardingo.”

Io cominciai: «O frati, i vostri mali . . . »;
ma più non dissi, ch’a l’occhio mi corse
un, crucifisso in terra con tre pali.

I began, “O brothers, your suffering . . .”; but I spoke no further, for my eye was caught by a crucified man on the ground with three stakes.

Quando mi vide, tutto si distorse,
soffiando ne la barba con sospiri;
e ’l frate Catalan, ch’a ciò s’accorse,

When he saw me, his face twisted,blowing into his beard with sighs;and Brother Catalan, who noticed this,

mi disse: «Quel confitto che tu miri,
consigliò i Farisei che convenia
porre un uom per lo popolo a’ martìri.

said to me: "That conflicted man whom you observe,advised the Pharisees that it was necessaryto put one man to martyrdom for the people.

Attraversato è, nudo, ne la via,
come tu vedi, ed è mestier ch’el senta
qualunque passa, come pesa, pria.

He has crossed, naked, into the road,as you see, and it is necessary that he feel,before anyone passes, how heavy he is.

E a tal modo il socero si stenta
in questa fossa, e li altri dal concilio
che fu per li Giudei mala sementa».

And in such manner the father-in-law toils in this pit, and the others from the council, which was a bad sowing for the Jews.

Allor vid’ io maravigliar Virgilio
sovra colui ch’era disteso in croce
tanto vilmente ne l’etterno essilio.

Then I saw Virgil marveling over him who was stretched upon the cross so vilely in eternal exile.

Poscia drizzò al frate cotal voce:
«Non vi dispiaccia, se vi lece, dirci
s’a la man destra giace alcuna foce

Then he addressed the friar, saying: "If it pleases you, tell us if there is any passage to the right."

onde noi amendue possiamo uscirci,
sanza costrigner de li angeli neri
che vegnan d’esto fondo a dipartirci».

so that we both might leave here, without forcing those dark angels to come from this pit to separate us.

Rispuose adunque: «Più che tu non speri
s’appressa un sasso che da la gran cerchia
si move e varca tutt’ i vallon feri,

He answered then: "More than you could hope, a stone approaches, moving from the great circle, crossing all the savage valleys."

salvo che ’n questo è rotto e nol coperchia;
montar potrete su per la ruina,
che giace in costa e nel fondo soperchia».

except that it is broken in this spot and it does not cover it; you will be able to climb up the ruin, which lies on the slope and overflows at the bottom.

Lo duca stette un poco a testa china;
poi disse: «Mal contava la bisogna
colui che i peccator di qua uncina».

The guide stood for a moment, head bowed; then he said, "The one who hooks the sinners on this side miscalculated the task."

E ’l frate: «Io udi’ già dire a Bologna
del diavol vizi assai, tra ’ quali udi’
ch’elli è bugiardo, e padre di menzogna».

And the friar said, "I have already heard it said in Bologna that the Devil has many vices, among which I heard that he is a liar and the father of lies."

Appresso il duca a gran passi sen gì,
turbato un poco d’ira nel sembiante;
ond’ io da li ’ncarcati mi parti’

Following this, the duke departed with long strides, his expression somewhat troubled by anger. I then left those who were laden with burdens.

dietro a le poste de le care piante.

Behind the delicate plants.

Purgatorio * Canto XIX

As dawn approaches, Dante dreams of a deformed woman who transforms into a beautiful, singing siren. A holy lady appears, prompting Virgil to rip the siren’s garments, revealing a foul stomach whose stench awakens Dante. An angel then appears, removes the fourth ‘P’ from Dante’s brow with its wing, and directs the poets to the stairway for the fifth terrace. On this new ledge, they find souls lying face-down on the stone floor, bound hand and foot, weeping as they recite a psalm. Dante speaks with one of these figures, who identifies himself as Pope Adrian V. He explains that this is the terrace for the avaricious and the prodigal, who are bound to the earth as they once were by their desires. After briefly recounting his late repentance during his papacy, Adrian V instructs Dante to stop kneeling and to continue his journey, leaving the souls to their penance.

Purgatorio • Canto XIX

Ne l’ora che non può ’l calor dïurno
intepidar più ’l freddo de la luna,
vinto da terra, e talor da Saturno

In the hour when the day's warmth can no longer temper the moon's chill, vanquished by the earth, and sometimes by Saturn,

—quando i geomanti lor Maggior Fortuna
veggiono in orïente, innanzi a l’alba,
surger per via che poco le sta bruna—,

—when geomancers see their Greater Fortunein the east, before the dawn,rising along a path that is only a little dark—,

mi venne in sogno una femmina balba,
ne li occhi guercia, e sovra i piè distorta,
con le man monche, e di colore scialba.

A stammering woman came to me in a dream,cross-eyed, and twisted in her feet,with mutilated hands, and pale of hue.

Io la mirava; e come ’l sol conforta
le fredde membra che la notte aggrava,
così lo sguardo mio le facea scorta

I looked upon her, and just as the sun warms the cold limbs burdened by night, so my gaze brought her comfort.

la lingua, e poscia tutta la drizzava
in poco d’ora, e lo smarrito volto,
com’ amor vuol, così le colorava.

Her tongue, and then her entire frame, she straightenedin just a moment, and her bewildered face,as love desires, it thus colored.

Poi ch’ell’ avea ’l parlar così disciolto,
cominciava a cantar sì, che con pena
da lei avrei mio intento rivolto.

Once she had loosened her words in this way, she began to sing, so that with difficulty I could have turned my attention from her.

«Io son», cantava, «io son dolce serena,
che ’ marinari in mezzo mar dismago;
tanto son di piacere a sentir piena!

"I am," she sang, "I am the sweet siren, who bewitches sailors in the middle of the sea; so full am I of the pleasure of being heard!"

Io volsi Ulisse del suo cammin vago
al canto mio; e qual meco s’ausa,
rado sen parte; sì tutto l’appago!».

I turned Ulysses from his wandering path to my song; and whoever stays with me, rarely departs; I satisfy them so completely!"

Ancor non era sua bocca richiusa,
quand’ una donna apparve santa e presta
lunghesso me per far colei confusa.

Her mouth had not yet closed, when a woman appeared, holy and swift, alongside me, to confound her.

«O Virgilio, Virgilio, chi è questa?»,
fieramente dicea; ed el venìa
con li occhi fitti pur in quella onesta.

"O Virgil, Virgil, who is this?" she said fiercely. And he came, his eyes fixed only on that honest woman.

L’altra prendea, e dinanzi l’apria
fendendo i drappi, e mostravami ’l ventre;
quel mi svegliò col puzzo che n’uscia.

The other one took him, and she opened him up, tearing at his clothes, and showed me his belly; that woke me with the stench that came out of it.

Io mossi li occhi, e ’l buon maestro: «Almen tre
voci t’ho messe!», dicea, «Surgi e vieni;
troviam l’aperta per la qual tu entre».

I moved my eyes, and the good master said, "At least three times I've called to you! Rise and come; let's find the opening through which you must enter."

Sù mi levai, e tutti eran già pieni
de l’alto dì i giron del sacro monte,
e andavam col sol novo a le reni.

Straightaway I rose, and the circles of the holy mountain were already filled with the high day, and we walked with the new sun at our backs.

Seguendo lui, portava la mia fronte
come colui che l’ha di pensier carca,
che fa di sé un mezzo arco di ponte;

Following him, he held my browlike one burdened by thought,who makes of himself the center of an arc, a bridge.

quand’ io udi’ «Venite; qui si varca»
parlare in modo soave e benigno,
qual non si sente in questa mortal marca.

When I heard, "Come; here is the passage,"spoken in a manner so gentle and kind,such as is never heard in this mortal realm.

Con l’ali aperte, che parean di cigno,
volseci in sù colui che sì parlonne
tra due pareti del duro macigno.

With wings spread wide, like a swan's, he turned us upward, he who had spoken so, between the two walls of the hard stone.

Mosse le penne poi e ventilonne,
‘Qui lugent’ affermando esser beati,
ch’avran di consolar l’anime donne.

Then he moved his wings and fanned us, affirming that those who mourn are blessed, for they shall have comfort for their weary souls.

«Che hai che pur inver’ la terra guati?»,
la guida mia incominciò a dirmi,
poco amendue da l’angel sormontati.

"Why do you gaze so intently at the ground?" my guide began, shortly after we had ascended past the angel.

E io: «Con tanta sospeccion fa irmi
novella visïon ch’a sé mi piega,
sì ch’io non posso dal pensar partirmi».

And I said: "A novel vision draws me towards it with such suspicion that I cannot tear myself from the thought of it."

«Vedesti», disse, «quell’antica strega
che sola sovr’ a noi omai si piagne;
vedesti come l’uom da lei si slega.

"You saw," he said, "that ancient sorceress, who now weeps alone above us; you saw how man breaks free from her."

Bastiti, e batti a terra le calcagne;
li occhi rivolgi al logoro che gira
lo rege etterno con le rote magne».

Halt, and strike your heels against the earth;turn your eyes to the lure that circlesthe eternal king with the great wheels."

Quale ’l falcon, che prima a’ pié si mira,
indi si volge al grido e si protende
per lo disio del pasto che là il tira,

As a falcon first gazes down at its feet, then turns to the cry and stretches out, driven by the desire for the food that calls it there,

tal mi fec’ io; e tal, quanto si fende
la roccia per dar via a chi va suso,
n’andai infin dove ’l cerchiar si prende.

So did I; and as the rock splits to make way for those climbing up, I went on until I reached where the circle closes.

Com’ io nel quinto giro fui dischiuso,
vidi gente per esso che piangea,
giacendo a terra tutta volta in giuso.

When I was at last revealed in the fifth circle, I saw people there who wept, lying face down on the ground.

‘Adhaesit pavimento anima mea’
sentia dir lor con sì alti sospiri,
che la parola a pena s’intendea.

“My soul cleaves to the ground.” I heard them wail these harsh words, their sighs so deep that their speech was barely audible.

«O eletti di Dio, li cui soffriri
e giustizia e speranza fa men duri,
drizzate noi verso li alti saliri».

“O you, the chosen of God, whose sufferings, righteousness, and hope make the path less harsh, guide us toward the heights.”

«Se voi venite dal giacer sicuri,
e volete trovar la via più tosto,
le vostre destre sien sempre di fori».

"If you come from a place of safety, and wish to find the path more swiftly, keep your right hands always on the outside."

Così pregò ’l poeta, e sì risposto
poco dinanzi a noi ne fu; per ch’io
nel parlare avvisai l’altro nascosto,

Thus the poet prayed, and we received a response nearby; therefore, while we spoke, I noticed the other one concealed.

e volsi li occhi a li occhi al segnor mio:
ond’ elli m’assentì con lieto cenno
ciò che chiedea la vista del disio.

And I turned my eyes to my lord’s eyes: then he assented with a joyful nod to what my desirous gaze was asking.

Poi ch’io potei di me fare a mio senno,
trassimi sovra quella creatura
le cui parole pria notar mi fenno,

Then, when I could act as I wished, I drew myself towards that creature whose words had made me take note before.

dicendo: «Spirto in cui pianger matura
quel sanza ’l quale a Dio tornar non pòssi,
sosta un poco per me tua maggior cura.

saying: "Spirit, in whom weeping ripensthat without which to God one cannot return,pause a little, for me, your greater care.

Chi fosti e perché vòlti avete i dossi
al sù, mi dì, e se vuo’ ch’io t’impetri
cosa di là ond’ io vivendo mossi».

Who were you, and why do you turn your backsto the height? Tell me, and if you wish I pleadfor you anything from where I moved living.”

Ed elli a me: «Perché i nostri diretri
rivolga il cielo a sé, saprai; ma prima
scias quod ego fui successor Petri.

And they to me: "Why our backs are turned to the heavens, you will know; but first, know that I was the successor of Peter."

Intra Sïestri e Chiaveri s’adima
una fiumana bella, e del suo nome
lo titol del mio sangue fa sua cima.

Between Sestri and Chiavari, a lovely river surges, and of its name, the title of my blood forms its source.

Un mese e poco più prova’ io come
pesa il gran manto a chi dal fango il guarda,
che piuma sembran tutte l’altre some.

A month and a bit, I experienced how heavy the great cloak is to he who guards it from the mud, for all other burdens seem like feathers in comparison.

La mia conversïone, omè!, fu tarda;
ma, come fatto fui roman pastore,
così scopersi la vita bugiarda.

My conversion, alas!, came late;but, once I became a Roman shepherd,thus I unmasked the lying life.

Vidi che lì non s’acquetava il core,
né più salir potiesi in quella vita;
per che di questa in me s’accese amore.

I saw that the heart found no peace there,nor could I climb higher in that life;thus, love for this one ignited within me.

Fino a quel punto misera e partita
da Dio anima fui, del tutto avara;
or, come vedi, qui ne son punita.

Up until then, a wretched soul, estranged from God, I was entirely avaricious; now, as you see, I am punished here.

Quel ch’avarizia fa, qui si dichiara
in purgazion de l’anime converse;
e nulla pena il monte ha più amara.

That which avarice does is revealed here,in the purgation of converted souls;and no torment does the mountain have more bitter.

Sì come l’occhio nostro non s’aderse
in alto, fisso a le cose terrene,
così giustizia qui a terra il merse.

Just as our eye never turned upward, fixated on earthly things, so justice has immersed us here on earth.

Come avarizia spense a ciascun bene
lo nostro amore, onde operar perdési,
così giustizia qui stretti ne tene,

Just as avarice extinguished our love for every good, causing us to lose our ability to act, so justice keeps us bound here.

ne’ piedi e ne le man legati e presi;
e quanto fia piacer del giusto Sire,
tanto staremo immobili e distesi».

In our feet and hands, we are bound and held; and for as long as the just Lord is pleased, we shall remain unmoving and stretched out.”

Io m’era inginocchiato e volea dire;
ma com’ io cominciai ed el s’accorse,
solo ascoltando, del mio reverire,

I was kneeling, ready to speak, but as I began, and he noticed, just by listening, my reverence,

«Qual cagion», disse, «in giù così ti torse?».
E io a lui: «Per vostra dignitate
mia coscïenza dritto mi rimorse».

"What made you kneel?" he asked. "What twisted you downward?"And I to him: "Because of your worth, my conscience justly stung me."

«Drizza le gambe, lèvati sù, frate!»,
rispuose; «non errar: conservo sono
teco e con li altri ad una podestate.

"Straighten your legs, get up, brother!" he responded. "Don't stray. I am here with you, and with the others, under one authority."

Se mai quel santo evangelico suono
che dice ‘Neque nubent’ intendesti,
ben puoi veder perch’ io così ragiono.

If you understood the holy evangelical pronouncement that says 'they neither marry,' then you can well see why I speak this way.

Vattene omai: non vo’ che più t’arresti;
ché la tua stanza mio pianger disagia,
col qual maturo ciò che tu dicesti.

Go now: I do not want you to stay any longer, for your presence disturbs my weeping, with which I ripen what you said.

Nepote ho io di là c’ha nome Alagia,
buona da sé, pur che la nostra casa
non faccia lei per essempro malvagia;

I have a nephew there, his name is Alagia. She is good in herself, so long as our house does not make her wicked by example.

e questa sola di là m’è rimasa».

And she alone remains with me from that place.”

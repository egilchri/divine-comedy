Inferno * Canto XVII

Canto XVII opens with the arrival of the monstrous beast Geryon at the cliff's edge. The creature possesses the face of an honest man, a serpentine body with intricate patterns, two hairy paws, and a scorpion's stinging tail. While Virgil negotiates with Geryon for passage, he sends Dante to observe a group of sinners nearby. Dante walks to the edge of the precipice and finds the usurers sitting on the burning sand, tormented by flakes of fire. Each has a money pouch with a unique coat of arms hanging from their neck, upon which they fix their gaze. After a brief observation, Dante returns to Virgil, who is already seated on Geryon's back. A fearful Dante mounts in front of his guide. The creature then launches from the cliff, beginning a slow, spiraling descent through the air. Geryon deposits the poets at the bottom of the abyss, on the floor of the eighth circle, and immediately disappears.

Inferno • Canto XVII

«Ecco la fiera con la coda aguzza,
che passa i monti e rompe i muri e l’armi!
Ecco colei che tutto ’l mondo appuzza!».

"Behold the beast with the sharpened tail,who passes mountains and shatters walls and weapons!Behold her, who stinks up the whole world!"

Sì cominciò lo mio duca a parlarmi;
e accennolle che venisse a proda,
vicino al fin d’i passeggiati marmi.

Then my guide began to speak to me, and gestured for her to come to the bank, near the end of the traversed marble.

E quella sozza imagine di froda
sen venne, e arrivò la testa e ’l busto,
ma ’n su la riva non trasse la coda.

And that foul image of fraud approached, arriving with its head and chest, but it did not drag its tail onto the bank.

La faccia sua era faccia d’uom giusto,
tanto benigna avea di fuor la pelle,
e d’un serpente tutto l’altro fusto;

His face was the face of a righteous man, so benign did his skin appear from without, and the rest of his body was that of a serpent.

due branche avea pilose insin l’ascelle;
lo dosso e ’l petto e ambedue le coste
dipinti avea di nodi e di rotelle.

Two hairy branches he had up to the armpits; his back and chest and both sides were painted with knots and whorls.

Con più color, sommesse e sovraposte
non fer mai drappi Tartari né Turchi,
né fuor tai tele per Aragne imposte.

With more color, subtle and superimposed, neither Tartar nor Turkish cloths ever bore such, nor were such tapestries spun by Arachne.

Come talvolta stanno a riva i burchi,
che parte sono in acqua e parte in terra,
e come là tra li Tedeschi lurchi

So, sometimes barges rest at the shore,partially in the water and partly on land,and like that among the crude Germans, more

lo bivero s’assetta a far sua guerra,
così la fiera pessima si stava
su l’orlo ch’è di pietra e ’l sabbion serra.

and the beast sits, readying its war,as that worst of beasts was poisedupon the edge of stone where the sand is kept.

Nel vano tutta sua coda guizzava,
torcendo in sù la venenosa forca
ch’a guisa di scorpion la punta armava.

In vain, all his tail was twitching,twisting upwards the venomous forkthat armed its point like a scorpion.

Lo duca disse: «Or convien che si torca
la nostra via un poco insino a quella
bestia malvagia che colà si corca».

The guide said, "Now it is necessary that we turn our path a bit toward that wicked beast that lies there."

Però scendemmo a la destra mammella,
e diece passi femmo in su lo stremo,
per ben cessar la rena e la fiammella.

But we descended to the rightmost ledge, and took ten steps along the edge, to better avoid the sand and the flame.

E quando noi a lei venuti semo,
poco più oltre veggio in su la rena
gente seder propinqua al loco scemo.

And when we came to it, I saw, a little further on the sand, people sitting near the desolate place.

Quivi ’l maestro «Acciò che tutta piena
esperïenza d’esto giron porti»,
mi disse, «va, e vedi la lor mena.

There, the master said to me, "So that you may carry away a complete experience of this circle, go now and observe their way of life."

Li tuoi ragionamenti sian là corti;
mentre che torni, parlerò con questa,
che ne conceda i suoi omeri forti».

Let your words be few there; while you are gone, I shall speak with her, that she grant us her strong shoulders.”

Così ancor su per la strema testa
di quel settimo cerchio tutto solo
andai, dove sedea la gente mesta.

So, I went on, alone, along the final path of that seventh circle, where the grieving people sat.

Per li occhi fora scoppiava lor duolo;
di qua, di là soccorrien con le mani
quando a’ vapori, e quando al caldo suolo:

From their eyes, their torment burst forth; here and there, they sought aid with their hands, now from the vapors, now from the hot ground.

non altrimenti fan di state i cani
or col ceffo or col piè, quando son morsi
o da pulci o da mosche o da tafani.

Not unlike dogs in summer, who,now with their snouts, now with their paws, try to find relieffrom fleas, flies, or gadflies.

Poi che nel viso a certi li occhi porsi,
ne’ quali ’l doloroso foco casca,
non ne conobbi alcun; ma io m’accorsi

Then, once I fixed my eyes on certain faces,in which the sorrowful fire descends,I recognized none; but I perceived

che dal collo a ciascun pendea una tasca
ch’avea certo colore e certo segno,
e quindi par che ’l loro occhio si pasca.

that from each neck hung a pouch,of a certain color and a certain mark,and from these their eyes seemed to feed.

E com’ io riguardando tra lor vegno,
in una borsa gialla vidi azzurro
che d’un leone avea faccia e contegno.

And as I looked among them, I saw in a yellow pouch, blue, that had the face and form of a lion.

Poi, procedendo di mio sguardo il curro,
vidine un’altra come sangue rossa,
mostrando un’oca bianca più che burro.

Then, letting my gaze sweep onward,I saw another, red as blood,displaying a goose whiter than butter.

E un che d’una scrofa azzurra e grossa
segnato avea lo suo sacchetto bianco,
mi disse: «Che fai tu in questa fossa?

And one who had marked his white pouch with a large, blue sow, said to me: “What are you doing in this ditch?”

Or te ne va; e perché se’ vivo anco,
sappi che ’l mio vicin Vitalïano
sederà qui dal mio sinistro fianco.

"Get out of here. And since you're still alive, know that my neighbor, Vitaliano, will sit here at my left side."

Con questi Fiorentin son padoano:
spesse fïate mi ’ntronan li orecchi
gridando: “Vegna ’l cavalier sovrano,

With these Florentines, I am a Paduan. They often deafen my ears, shouting: "Let the sovereign knight come!"

che recherà la tasca con tre becchi!”».
Qui distorse la bocca e di fuor trasse
la lingua, come bue che ’l naso lecchi.

"Who'll bring the purse with three beaks!" Here, he twisted his mouth and stuck out his tongue, like an ox licking its nose.

E io, temendo no ’l più star crucciasse
lui che di poco star m’avea ’mmonito,
torna’mi in dietro da l’anime lasse.

And I, fearing that he might become more angry, he who had warned me to stay only a short while, turned back from those weary souls.

Trova’ il duca mio ch’era salito
già su la groppa del fiero animale,
e disse a me: «Or sie forte e ardito.

I found my guide, who had already mounted the fierce beast's back. He said to me, "Now be strong and brave."

Omai si scende per sì fatte scale;
monta dinanzi, ch’i’ voglio esser mezzo,
sì che la coda non possa far male».

Omai descended these stairs, climbing ahead. I will stay in the middle so its tail cannot harm us.”

Qual è colui che sì presso ha ’l riprezzo
de la quartana, c’ha già l’unghie smorte,
e triema tutto pur guardando ’l rezzo,

Who is that, so close to the graspof the quartan fever, whose nails are already pale,and trembles all over just looking at the shade?

tal divenn’ io a le parole porte;
ma vergogna mi fé le sue minacce,
che innanzi a buon segnor fa servo forte.

At those words, I became as they were spoken; but shame made me heed his threats, which makes a servant bold before a good master.

I’ m’assettai in su quelle spallacce;
sì volli dir, ma la voce non venne
com’ io credetti: ‘Fa che tu m’abbracce’.

I settled myself on those broad shoulders; I wanted to speak, but the voice did not come as I had thought it would: "See that you embrace me."

Ma esso, ch’altra volta mi sovvenne
ad altro forse, tosto ch’i’ montai
con le braccia m’avvinse e mi sostenne;

But he, who had helped me once before, perhaps with something else, as soon as I climbed aboard, embraced me with his arms and held me firm;

e disse: «Gerïon, moviti omai:
le rote larghe, e lo scender sia poco;
pensa la nova soma che tu hai».

And he said: "Gerion, get moving now: let the great wheels turn and the descent be gentle; consider the new weight you bear."

Come la navicella esce di loco
in dietro in dietro, sì quindi si tolse;
e poi ch’al tutto si sentì a gioco,

As a small boat pulls away from shore,backing away, so it departed then;and then, when it felt itself completely free,

là ’v’ era ’l petto, la coda rivolse,
e quella tesa, come anguilla, mosse,
e con le branche l’aere a sé raccolse.

There, where its chest had been, it turned its tail,and with that tautness, like an eel, it moved,and with its claws, it gathered in the air.

Maggior paura non credo che fosse
quando Fetonte abbandonò li freni,
per che ’l ciel, come pare ancor, si cosse;

Greater dread, I think, there was not,when Phaeton abandoned the reins,for which the sky, as still it seems, was scorched;

né quando Icaro misero le reni
sentì spennar per la scaldata cera,
gridando il padre a lui «Mala via tieni!»,

nor when wretched Icarus felt his shoulderslosing feathers, as the wax was scalded,while his father cried to him, "You take the wrong way!"

che fu la mia, quando vidi ch’i’ era
ne l’aere d’ogne parte, e vidi spenta
ogne veduta fuor che de la fera.

was mine, when I saw that I wasin the air on all sides, and saw extinguishedevery sight except for that of the beast.

Ella sen va notando lenta lenta;
rota e discende, ma non me n’accorgo
se non che al viso e di sotto mi venta.

She went, feeling it slowly, slowly;broken, it descends, but I don't notice,except that it fans my face and what's below.

Io sentia già da la man destra il gorgo
far sotto noi un orribile scroscio,
per che con li occhi ’n giù la testa sporgo.

I could already feel the whirlpool on my right handmaking a horrible crash beneath us,so that I bent my head downwards with my eyes.

Allor fu’ io più timido a lo stoscio,
però ch’i’ vidi fuochi e senti’ pianti;
ond’ io tremando tutto mi raccoscio.

Then I grew more fearful at the push and shove,because I saw the flames and heard the cries;so, trembling, I drew myself all together.

E vidi poi, ché nol vedea davanti,
lo scendere e ’l girar per li gran mali
che s’appressavan da diversi canti.

And then I saw, for I hadn't seen it before, the descent and the turning amidst the great sufferings that were drawing near from different directions.

Come ’l falcon ch’è stato assai su l’ali,
che sanza veder logoro o uccello
fa dire al falconiere «Omè, tu cali!»,

Come the falcon, that has been a long time on the wing, that without seeing lure or bird makes the falconer say, "Alas, you descend!"

discende lasso onde si move isnello,
per cento rote, e da lunge si pone
dal suo maestro, disdegnoso e fello;

Descends, weary, from where it swiftly flies,through a hundred turns, and then from afarlands from its master, scornful and sly.

così ne puose al fondo Gerïone
al piè al piè de la stagliata rocca,
e, discarcate le nostre persone,

Gerion placed us at the bottom,at the very foot of the jagged rock,and, having unloaded us,

si dileguò come da corda cocca.

He vanished like an arrow loosed from a bowstring.

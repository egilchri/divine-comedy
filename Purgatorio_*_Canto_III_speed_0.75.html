Purgatorio * Canto III

As the sun rises, Dante notices his shadow on the ground and is alarmed to see Virgil has none. Virgil explains that his physical body is buried in Naples and that as a spirit, he is insubstantial. They reach the base of the mountain of Purgatory but find the cliff before them is sheer and seemingly unscalable. While searching for a path, they encounter a group of souls moving with extreme slowness. These spirits are startled to see Dante cast a shadow, realizing he is alive. They identify themselves as those who died excommunicated from the Church but repented in their final moments. One soul, Manfred of Sicily, steps forward. He shows Dante his fatal wounds and asks him to tell his daughter, Constance, that he is among the saved and to request her prayers. Manfred explains that these souls must wait in Ante-Purgatory for a period thirty times longer than their excommunication, unless their time is shortened by the prayers of the living.

Purgatorio • Canto III

Avvegna che la subitana fuga
dispergesse color per la campagna,
rivolti al monte ove ragion ne fruga,

Although the sudden flight scattered them across the field, we turned toward the mountain where reason gnaws at us.

i’ mi ristrinsi a la fida compagna:
e come sare’ io sanza lui corso?
chi m’avria tratto su per la montagna?

I then drew close to my faithful companion: how could I have continued without him? Who would have guided me up the mountain?

El mi parea da sé stesso rimorso:
o dignitosa coscïenza e netta,
come t’è picciol fallo amaro morso!

And remorse seemed to rise within me:O dignified conscience, pure and bright,How bitter the sting of a tiny fault!

Quando li piedi suoi lasciar la fretta,
che l’onestade ad ogn’ atto dismaga,
la mente mia, che prima era ristretta,

When her feet abandoned the hastethat corrupts honesty in every act,my mind, previously constricted,

lo ’ntento rallargò, sì come vaga,
e diedi ’l viso mio incontr’ al poggio
che ’nverso ’l ciel più alto si dislaga.

widened its focus, as if wandering,and I turned my face toward the slopethat rises highest toward the sky.

Lo sol, che dietro fiammeggiava roggio,
rotto m’era dinanzi a la figura,
ch’avëa in me de’ suoi raggi l’appoggio.

The sun, which blazed red behind me, was broken before my form, which had in me a screen for its rays.

Io mi volsi dallato con paura
d’essere abbandonato, quand’ io vidi
solo dinanzi a me la terra oscura;

I turned aside in fear of being abandoned, when I saw the dark earth alone before me.

e ’l mio conforto: «Perché pur diffidi?»,
a dir mi cominciò tutto rivolto;
«non credi tu me teco e ch’io ti guidi?

And my comfort: "Why do you still doubt?"He began to say, turning completely toward me,"Do you not believe that I am with you and that I guide you?"

Vespero è già colà dov’ è sepolto
lo corpo dentro al quale io facea ombra;
Napoli l’ha, e da Brandizio è tolto.

Evening is already where the body is buried, the body that was once my shadow. Naples holds it, and it was taken from Brindisi.

Ora, se innanzi a me nulla s’aombra,
non ti maravigliar più che d’i cieli
che l’uno a l’altro raggio non ingombra.

Now, if nothing casts a shadow before me, do not be more surprised than you are by the heavens, where one ray does not impede another.

A sofferir tormenti, caldi e geli
simili corpi la Virtù dispone
che, come fa, non vuol ch’a noi si sveli.

To endure torments, heat and cold,Virtue disposes similar bodieswhich, how she does it, she does not wish to reveal to us.

Matto è chi spera che nostra ragione
possa trascorrer la infinita via
che tiene una sustanza in tre persone.

Mad is he who hopes that our reasoncan traverse the infinite paththat holds one substance in three persons.

State contenti, umana gente, al quia;
ché, se potuto aveste veder tutto,
mestier non era parturir Maria;

Be content, you humans, with "why"; for if you had been able to see all things, there would have been no need for Mary to give birth.

e disïar vedeste sanza frutto
tai che sarebbe lor disio quetato,
ch’etternalmente è dato lor per lutto:

and you have seen desire without fruit, such that the longing they have is stilled, which is eternally given to them as sorrow.

io dico d’Aristotile e di Plato
e di molt’ altri»; e qui chinò la fronte,
e più non disse, e rimase turbato.

I speak of Aristotle and Plato, and of many others.” And here he lowered his brow and said no more, remaining troubled.

Noi divenimmo intanto a piè del monte;
quivi trovammo la roccia sì erta,
che ’ndarno vi sarien le gambe pronte.

Meanwhile, we arrived at the foot of the mountain. There we found the rock so steep that our legs would have been useless.

Tra Lerice e Turbìa la più diserta,
la più rotta ruina è una scala,
verso di quella, agevole e aperta.

Between Lerici and Turbia, the most desolate, the most broken ruin is a staircase, compared to that, easy and open.

«Or chi sa da qual man la costa cala»,
disse ’l maestro mio fermando ’l passo,
«sì che possa salir chi va sanz’ ala?».

"Now, who knows from what side the slope descends," my master said, pausing, "so that he who has no wings may ascend?"

E mentre ch’e’ tenendo ’l viso basso
essaminava del cammin la mente,
e io mirava suso intorno al sasso,

And as he kept his face bowed low, examining the path in his mind, and I gazed up around the rock,

da man sinistra m’apparì una gente
d’anime, che movieno i piè ver’ noi,
e non pareva, sì venïan lente.

On the left, a gathering of spirits appeared to me, their feet moving toward us, though they seemed not to move at all, so slow were they.

«Leva», diss’ io, «maestro, li occhi tuoi:
ecco di qua chi ne darà consiglio,
se tu da te medesmo aver nol puoi».

"Look over there, Master," I said, "look with your eyes. There's someone over here who can give us advice, if you can't find it yourself."

Guardò allora, e con libero piglio
rispuose: «Andiamo in là, ch’ei vegnon piano;
e tu ferma la spene, dolce figlio».

He looked then, and with an easy mannerreplied: "Let's go, they approach slowly;and you, my sweet son, keep your hope firm."

Ancora era quel popol di lontano,
i’ dico dopo i nostri mille passi,
quanto un buon gittator trarria con mano,

That crowd was still distant, I say,after we had walked a thousand paces,as far as a good thrower could cast with their hand.

quando si strinser tutti ai duri massi
de l’alta ripa, e stetter fermi e stretti
com’ a guardar, chi va dubbiando, stassi.

when they all pressed themselves against the hard cliffsof the high bank, and stood still and tight,like someone pausing, uncertain, to look.

«O ben finiti, o già spiriti eletti»,
Virgilio incominciò, «per quella pace
ch’i’ credo che per voi tutti s’aspetti,

"O, souls now at rest, or spirits chosen,"Virgil began, "by that peacewhich I believe is awaited by you all,

ditene dove la montagna giace,
sì che possibil sia l’andare in suso;
ché perder tempo a chi più sa più spiace».

Tell us where the mountain lies,so that we can ascend;for losing time displeases the one who knows the most."

Come le pecorelle escon del chiuso
a una, a due, a tre, e l’altre stanno
timidette atterrando l’occhio e ’l muso;

Like little sheep emerging from the fold,one by one, two by two, then three, while those remainingcower, their eyes and muzzles lowered, timid and cold;

e ciò che fa la prima, e l’altre fanno,
addossandosi a lei, s’ella s’arresta,
semplici e quete, e lo ’mperché non sanno;

and what the first does, the others do,pressing against it, if it pauses,simple and still, and they know not the why.

sì vid’ io muovere a venir la testa
di quella mandra fortunata allotta,
pudica in faccia e ne l’andare onesta.

Thus I saw the head of that blessed company begin to move, chaste of face and honest in its gait.

Come color dinanzi vider rotta
la luce in terra dal mio destro canto,
sì che l’ombra era da me a la grotta,

As soon as the light on the ground was broken before me by my right side, so that the shadow stretched from me to the grotto,

restaro, e trasser sé in dietro alquanto,
e tutti li altri che venieno appresso,
non sappiendo ’l perché, fenno altrettanto.

I stopped, and they drew back a little, and all the others who were coming behind did the same, not knowing why.

«Sanza vostra domanda io vi confesso
che questo è corpo uman che voi vedete;
per che ’l lume del sole in terra è fesso.

“Without your asking, I confess to you that this is a human body you see; and that’s why the light of the sun is broken on the earth.”

Non vi maravigliate, ma credete
che non sanza virtù che da ciel vegna
cerchi di soverchiar questa parete».

Do not be amazed, but believe that it is not without a power that comes from the heavens that I seek to surpass this barrier.

Così ’l maestro; e quella gente degna
«Tornate», disse, «intrate innanzi dunque»,
coi dossi de le man faccendo insegna.

So spoke the master; and that worthy crowd then said, "Turn back," and, "Enter in,"using their hands to signal the way.

E un di loro incominciò: «Chiunque
tu se’, così andando, volgi ’l viso:
pon mente se di là mi vedesti unque».

And one of them began: "Whoever you are, as you walk, turn your face: consider whether you ever saw me over there."

Io mi volsi ver’ lui e guardail fiso:
biondo era e bello e di gentile aspetto,
ma l’un de’ cigli un colpo avea diviso.

I turned towards him and looked closely. He was blond and handsome, with a noble appearance, but a wound had split one of his eyebrows.

Quand’ io mi fui umilmente disdetto
d’averlo visto mai, el disse: «Or vedi»;
e mostrommi una piaga a sommo ’l petto.

When I humbly denied having ever seen him, he said, "Now, you see," and he showed me a wound at the top of his chest.

Poi sorridendo disse: «Io son Manfredi,
nepote di Costanza imperadrice;
ond’ io ti priego che, quando tu riedi,

Then, smiling, he said: "I am Manfred, grandson of Empress Constance; therefore, I pray you, when you return,

vadi a mia bella figlia, genitrice
de l’onor di Cicilia e d’Aragona,
e dichi ’l vero a lei, s’altro si dice.

go to my beautiful daughter, motherof the honor of Sicily and Aragon,and tell her the truth, if any other thing is said.

Poscia ch’io ebbi rotta la persona
di due punte mortali, io mi rendei,
piangendo, a quei che volontier perdona.

After I had broken my bodywith two mortal stabs, I gave myself,weeping, to Him who pardons willingly.

Orribil furon li peccati miei;
ma la bontà infinita ha sì gran braccia,
che prende ciò che si rivolge a lei.

Horrific were my sins; but infinite goodness has such great arms, that it takes in all who turn to it.

Se ’l pastor di Cosenza, che a la caccia
di me fu messo per Clemente allora,
avesse in Dio ben letta questa faccia,

If the shepherd of Cosenza, who was then sent after me by Clement, had read this face of mine well in God,

l’ossa del corpo mio sarieno ancora
in co del ponte presso a Benevento,
sotto la guardia de la grave mora.

the bones of my body would still bein the bridge's corner near Benevento,under the watch of the heavy burden.

Or le bagna la pioggia e move il vento
di fuor dal regno, quasi lungo ’l Verde,
dov’ e’ le trasmutò a lume spento.

Now the rain wets them and the wind stirs them,outside the kingdom, almost along the Verde,where he transformed them in extinguished light.

Per lor maladizion sì non si perde,
che non possa tornar, l’etterno amore,
mentre che la speranza ha fior del verde.

Through their curse, it is not so utterly lostthat eternal love cannot return,while hope still bears the flower of green.

Vero è che quale in contumacia more
di Santa Chiesa, ancor ch’al fin si penta,
star li convien da questa ripa in fore,

The truth is, whoever dies under the curseof Holy Church, even if they repent at the end,must remain outside this shore.

per ognun tempo ch’elli è stato, trenta,
in sua presunzïon, se tal decreto
più corto per buon prieghi non diventa.

For every thirty years he persisted in his presumption, unless that decree is shortened by good prayers.

Vedi oggimai se tu mi puoi far lieto,
revelando a la mia buona Costanza
come m’hai visto, e anco esto divieto;

See now if you can make me glad, revealing to my good Costanza how you have seen me, and also this prohibition.

ché qui per quei di là molto s’avanza».

For here, it progresses far beyond those over there.”

Paradiso * Canto XVI

In the Heaven of Mars, Dante continues his conversation with his great-great-grandfather, Cacciaguida. Expressing pride in his noble lineage, Dante addresses his ancestor with the formal pronoun "voi," which prompts a smile from Beatrice. Dante then asks Cacciaguida a series of questions about their family's past and the state of Florence during Cacciaguida’s lifetime. He asks about his ancestors, his birth year, the size of the city's population, and its most prominent families. Cacciaguida answers by describing the old Florence, a smaller city enclosed within its ancient walls and inhabited by a more homogenous citizenry. He proceeds to name numerous noble families who were influential in his day but have since declined or disappeared. He contrasts this earlier state with the Florence of Dante's time, noting the city’s expansion and the influx of new people from the surrounding countryside.

Paradiso • Canto XVI

O poca nostra nobiltà di sangue,
se glorïar di te la gente fai
qua giù dove l’affetto nostro langue,

Oh, what little nobility we possess,that you would cause people to boast of youdown here where our affections languish.

mirabil cosa non mi sarà mai:
ché là dove appetito non si torce,
dico nel cielo, io me ne gloriai.

A marvelous thing will never be,because where desire does not turn,I say, in Heaven, I did glory.

Ben se’ tu manto che tosto raccorce:
sì che, se non s’appon di dì in die,
lo tempo va dintorno con le force.

Because you are a cloak that soon shrinks:so that, if it is not added to day by day,time goes around with its strength.

Dal ‘voi’ che prima a Roma s’offerie,
in che la sua famiglia men persevra,
ricominciaron le parole mie;

From you, who first offered yourself to Rome,in whom your family is less persistent,my words began again.

onde Beatrice, ch’era un poco scevra,
ridendo, parve quella che tossio
al primo fallo scritto di Ginevra.

Whereupon Beatrice, who was a little apart, laughing, appeared like her who coughed at the first fault written of Guinevere.

Io cominciai: «Voi siete il padre mio;
voi mi date a parlar tutta baldezza;
voi mi levate sì, ch’i’ son più ch’io.

I began: "You are my father; you give me all boldness in speaking; you lift me up so that I am more than myself."

Per tanti rivi s’empie d’allegrezza
la mente mia, che di sé fa letizia
perché può sostener che non si spezza.

My mind is so filled with joy by so many streams that it finds happiness in itself, because it can hold fast and not be broken.

Ditemi dunque, cara mia primizia,
quai fuor li vostri antichi e quai fuor li anni
che si segnaro in vostra püerizia;

So, tell me, my dearest first fruits, who were your ancestors, and what were the years marked in your childhood?

ditemi de l’ovil di San Giovanni
quanto era allora, e chi eran le genti
tra esso degne di più alti scanni».

Tell me of the fold of Saint John, what it was like then, and who were the people within it most worthy of high seats.”

Come s’avviva a lo spirar d’i venti
carbone in fiamma, così vid’ io quella
luce risplendere a’ miei blandimenti;

As a charcoal ember blazes to life at the breath of the wind, so did I see that light brighten in response to my entreaties.

e come a li occhi miei si fé più bella,
così con voce più dolce e soave,
ma non con questa moderna favella,

And as she grew more beautiful to my eyes, so did she speak with a voice more sweet and gentle, though not in this modern tongue.

dissemi: «Da quel dì che fu detto ‘Ave’
al parto in che mia madre, ch’è or santa,
s’allevïò di me ond’ era grave,

dissemi: "From that day when 'Hail' was spoken,to the delivery in which my mother, who is now a saint,was relieved of me, by whom she was burdened,

al suo Leon cinquecento cinquanta
e trenta fiate venne questo foco
a rinfiammarsi sotto la sua pianta.

To her Leo, five hundred and fiftyand thirty times this fire cameto reignite beneath her feet.

Li antichi miei e io nacqui nel loco
dove si truova pria l’ultimo sesto
da quei che corre il vostro annüal gioco.

My ancestors and I were born in the placewhere the final sixth is first encounteredby those who run the course of your yearly game.

Basti d’i miei maggiori udirne questo:
chi ei si fosser e onde venner quivi,
più è tacer che ragionare onesto.

It is best to say nothing of my ancestors,to keep silent rather than to speak of them,who they were and whence they came to this place.

Tutti color ch’a quel tempo eran ivi
da poter arme tra Marte e ’l Batista,
eran il quinto di quei ch’or son vivi.

All those who, at that time, were there,capable of bearing arms between Mars and the Baptist,were a fifth of those now living.

Ma la cittadinanza, ch’è or mista
di Campi, di Certaldo e di Fegghine,
pura vediesi ne l’ultimo artista.

But the citizenry, now a mixof Campi, Certaldo, and Fegghine,was seen to be pure in the last craftsman.

Oh quanto fora meglio esser vicine
quelle genti ch’io dico, e al Galluzzo
e a Trespiano aver vostro confine,

Oh, how much better it would be if those people I speak of were neighbors, and you had your borders at Galluzzo and Trespiano,

che averle dentro e sostener lo puzzo
del villan d’Aguglion, di quel da Signa,
che già per barattare ha l’occhio aguzzo!

Than to have them inside and endure the stenchof the peasant from Aguglion, that one from Signa,who already has a sharp eye for a bargain!

Se la gente ch’al mondo più traligna
non fosse stata a Cesare noverca,
ma come madre a suo figlio benigna,

If the people who stray most in this worldhad not been a stepmother to Caesar,but as a mother, kindly to her son,

tal fatto è fiorentino e cambia e merca,
che si sarebbe vòlto a Simifonti,
là dove andava l’avolo a la cerca;

Such a thing is Florentine, ever-changing and trading,that he would have turned to Simifonti,where his ancestor went, seeking.

sariesi Montemurlo ancor de’ Conti;
sarieno i Cerchi nel piovier d’Acone,
e forse in Valdigrieve i Buondelmonti.

The Counts would still hold Montemurlo; the Cerchi would remain in the Acone district, and perhaps the Buondelmonti in Valdigrieve.

Sempre la confusion de le persone
principio fu del mal de la cittade,
come del vostro il cibo che s’appone;

Always the confusion of the peoplewas the beginning of the city’s harm,as your food is of that which is imposed;

e cieco toro più avaccio cade
che cieco agnello; e molte volte taglia
più e meglio una che le cinque spade.

And the blind bull falls more swiftlythan a blind lamb; and many times, one swordcuts more and better than five.

Se tu riguardi Luni e Orbisaglia
come sono ite, e come se ne vanno
di retro ad esse Chiusi e Sinigaglia,

If you consider Luni and Orbisaglia,how they have fallen, and how they are fallingbehind them, Chiusi and Sinigaglia,

udir come le schiatte si disfanno
non ti parrà nova cosa né forte,
poscia che le cittadi termine hanno.

To hear how clans unravelwill seem neither new nor strange to you,once cities have their end.

Le vostre cose tutte hanno lor morte,
sì come voi; ma celasi in alcuna
che dura molto, e le vite son corte.

All your possessions will meet their end,just as you will; but such endings hidein some things that last, while lives are brief.

E come ’l volger del ciel de la luna
cuopre e discuopre i liti sanza posa,
così fa di Fiorenza la Fortuna:

And as the turning of the moon's heavenuncovers and covers the shores without rest,so does Fortune with Florence:

per che non dee parer mirabil cosa
ciò ch’io dirò de li alti Fiorentini
onde è la fama nel tempo nascosa.

Therefore, it should not seem surprisingwhat I will say of the noble Florentines,whose fame is hidden by time.

Io vidi li Ughi e vidi i Catellini,
Filippi, Greci, Ormanni e Alberichi,
già nel calare, illustri cittadini;

I saw the Ughi, and I saw the Catellini,Filippi, Greci, Ormanni, and Alberichi,already in decline, illustrious citizens;

e vidi così grandi come antichi,
con quel de la Sannella, quel de l’Arca,
e Soldanieri e Ardinghi e Bostichi.

And I saw them, great as they were of old,with those of Sannella, those of the Arca,and Soldanieri, Ardinghi, and Bostichi.

Sovra la porta ch’al presente è carca
di nova fellonia di tanto peso
che tosto fia iattura de la barca,

Above the gate, which presently bears the burdenof new treachery, of such weightthat soon it will bring disaster to the ship,

erano i Ravignani, ond’ è disceso
il conte Guido e qualunque del nome
de l’alto Bellincione ha poscia preso.

Were the Ravignani, from whom descendedCount Guido and all who afterward tookthe name of the noble Bellincione.

Quel de la Pressa sapeva già come
regger si vuole, e avea Galigaio
dorata in casa sua già l’elsa e ’l pome.

That one of the Pressa already knew howhe should conduct himself, and Galigaiohad the hilt and the pommel gilded in his house.

Grand’ era già la colonna del Vaio,
Sacchetti, Giuochi, Fifanti e Barucci
e Galli e quei ch’arrossan per lo staio.

The column of the Vaio was already grand,Sacchetti, Giuochi, Fifanti, and Barucci,and Galli, and those who blush about the measure.

Lo ceppo di che nacquero i Calfucci
era già grande, e già eran tratti
a le curule Sizii e Arrigucci.

The stock from which the Calfucci sprangwas already great, and already Sizii and Arrigucciwere drawn to the curule chairs.

Oh quali io vidi quei che son disfatti
per lor superbia! e le palle de l’oro
fiorian Fiorenza in tutt’ i suoi gran fatti.

Oh, how I saw those who are undoneby their pride! And the balls of goldbloomed in Florence in all its great deeds.

Così facieno i padri di coloro
che, sempre che la vostra chiesa vaca,
si fanno grassi stando a consistoro.

Thus did the fathers of those who, whenever your church is vacant, grow fat by sitting in consistory.

L’oltracotata schiatta che s’indraca
dietro a chi fugge, e a chi mostra ’l dente
o ver la borsa, com’ agnel si placa,

The arrogant clan that, like a snake, coilsbehind the fleeing, but to those who bare their teethor open their purse, they become as placid as a lamb.

già venìa sù, ma di picciola gente;
sì che non piacque ad Ubertin Donato
che poï il suocero il fé lor parente.

It was already rising up, but from a small group; so that Ubertino Donato was displeased, who later made them his relatives through marriage.

Già era ’l Caponsacco nel mercato
disceso giù da Fiesole, e già era
buon cittadino Giuda e Infangato.

Caponsacco was already in the marketplace, having descended from Fiesole, and Judas and Infangato were already upstanding citizens.

Io dirò cosa incredibile e vera:
nel picciol cerchio s’entrava per porta
che si nomava da quei de la Pera.

I will tell you something unbelievable and true: one entered the small circle through a gate that was named after those of the Pera.

Ciascun che de la bella insegna porta
del gran barone il cui nome e ’l cui pregio
la festa di Tommaso riconforta,

Each who bears the beautiful emblem of the great baron, whose name and worth comfort the feast of Thomas,

da esso ebbe milizia e privilegio;
avvegna che con popol si rauni
oggi colui che la fascia col fregio.

From him, he received both soldiery and privilege; although he gathers today with the people, he who adorns his sash with a symbol.

Già eran Gualterotti e Importuni;
e ancor saria Borgo più quïeto,
se di novi vicin fosser digiuni.

Already the Gualterotti and the Importuni were at it; and the district would still be more tranquil if it were not hungry for new neighbors.

La casa di che nacque il vostro fleto,
per lo giusto disdegno che v’ha morti
e puose fine al vostro viver lieto,

The house where your weeping began, through the righteous disdain that has killed you and put an end to your happy life,

era onorata, essa e suoi consorti:
o Buondelmonte, quanto mal fuggisti
le nozze süe per li altrui conforti!

was honored, she and her companions:O Buondelmonte, how badly you fledher wedding for the sake of others' comforts!

Molti sarebber lieti, che son tristi,
se Dio t’avesse conceduto ad Ema
la prima volta ch’a città venisti.

Many would be glad, who now are sad,if God had granted you to Emathe first time you came to the city.

Ma conveniesi a quella pietra scema
che guarda ’l ponte, che Fiorenza fesse
vittima ne la sua pace postrema.

But it would have been fitting for that foolish stonethat guards the bridge, that Florence should have madea victim in its final peace.

Con queste genti, e con altre con esse,
vid’ io Fiorenza in sì fatto riposo,
che non avea cagione onde piangesse.

With these people, and others with them, I saw Florence in such peace that it had no cause for tears.

Con queste genti vid’io glorïoso
e giusto il popol suo, tanto che ’l giglio
non era ad asta mai posto a ritroso,

With these people I saw its populace glorious and just, so much so that the lily had never been placed in reverse on a staff.

né per divisïon fatto vermiglio».

Not made red through division.

Inferno * Canto VIII

The canto begins as Dante and Virgil wait by the marsh of Styx. They observe signal fires from a high tower, which are answered from a distance. The ferryman Phlegyas arrives in his skiff, and the two poets embark. As they cross the marsh, a soul covered in mud, Filippo Argenti, rises and confronts Dante. After a hostile exchange, Virgil shoves Argenti back into the mire, where other wrathful souls tear him apart. Upon reaching the far shore, they arrive before the great iron walls of the City of Dis. A multitude of fallen angels guard the gate and refuse to grant them passage. They challenge Virgil’s authority and slam the gate shut in his face. Though momentarily dismayed, Virgil reassures Dante that heavenly aid is on its way to force their entry. The canto ends as they wait outside the city walls.

My guide descended into the boat, and then beckoned me to follow him inside; only when I was in did it seem to be burdened.

Tosto che ’l duca e io nel legno fui,

As soon as the guide and I were in the boat, the ancient prow cut through the water, going deeper than it usually does with others.

Mentre noi corravam la morta gora,
dinanzi mi si fece un pien di fango,
e disse: «Chi se’ tu che vieni anzi ora?».

While we were running along the dead ditch, a figure, covered in mud, appeared before me and said, "Who are you, coming here before your time?"

E io a lui: «S’i’ vegno, non rimango;
ma tu chi se’, che sì se’ fatto brutto?».
Rispuose: «Vedi che son un che piango».

And I to him: "If I come, I do not stay; but who are you, who are so disfigured?" He replied: "You see that I am one who weeps."

E io a lui: «Con piangere e con lutto,
spirito maladetto, ti rimani;
ch’i’ ti conosco, ancor sie lordo tutto».

And I to him: "With weeping and with grief, cursed spirit, remain; for I know you, though you are entirely filthy."

Allor distese al legno ambo le mani;
per che ’l maestro accorto lo sospinse,
dicendo: «Via costà con li altri cani!».

Then he stretched both hands toward the wood; at which my wary master shoved him away, saying: “Be gone from here, with the other dogs!”

Lo collo poi con le braccia mi cinse;
basciommi ’l volto e disse: «Alma sdegnosa,
benedetta colei che ’n te s’incinse!

Then he embraced me with his arms; he kissed my face and said: "Scornful soul, blessed is she who conceived within you!"

Quei fu al mondo persona orgogliosa;
bontà non è che sua memoria fregi:
così s’è l’ombra sua qui furïosa.

That person was proud in the world; no goodness graces their memory: thus their shade is furious here.

Quanti si tegnon or là sù gran regi

How many who now consider themselves great kings up therewill be here like swine in the mud,leaving behind them horrible disgraces!

E io: «Maestro, molto sarei vago

And I said, “Master, I would be very eagerto see him plunged into this mirebefore we leave the lake.”

Ed elli a me: «Avante che la proda
ti si lasci veder, tu sarai sazio:
di tal disïo convien che tu goda».

And he said to me: “Before the shoreis revealed to you, you will be sated:you must find joy in that desire.”

Dopo ciò poco vid’ io quello strazio

che Dio ancor ne lodo e ne ringrazio.

Everyone was shouting, "At Filippo Argenti!"; and the Florentine spirit, in a rage, turned upon itself with its teeth.

Quivi il lasciammo, che più non ne narro;
ma ne l’orecchie mi percosse un duolo,
per ch’io avante l’occhio intento sbarro.

There we left him, and I say no more of him; but a wail struck my ears, and so I fixed my gaze ahead, unblinking.

Lo buon maestro disse: «Omai, figliuolo,

The good master said: "Now, my son, we draw near to the city called Dis, with its grievous citizens and its great host."

I could not tell what he offered them, but he did not remain there long with them, for each of them retreated inside to test it.

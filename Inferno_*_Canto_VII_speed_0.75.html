Inferno * Canto VII

The canto begins as Dante and Virgil confront Plutus, the guardian of the fourth circle. Virgil silences the creature with a rebuke, causing it to collapse. The poets then descend into the circle, where they witness two groups of souls pushing great weights with their chests along a circular path. The groups, identified by Virgil as the avaricious and the prodigal, move in opposite directions, clashing violently where they meet before turning to repeat their labor. Continuing their descent, the poets arrive at the fifth circle, a vast, foul-smelling marsh formed by the river Styx. Here they observe the wrathful, who are naked, covered in mud, and perpetually striking and biting one another. Virgil explains that another group, the sullen, is completely submerged beneath the water; their unseen sighs cause bubbles to rise to the surface. Dante and Virgil then walk along the edge of the swamp, eventually reaching the base of a tall tower.

As swollen sails collapse in folds when the mast breaks, so fell to the earth the cruel beast.

Così scendemmo ne la quarta lacca,
pigliando più de la dolente ripa
che ’l mal de l’universo tutto insacca.

So we descended to the fourth pouch, taking a path along the mournful shore that imprisons all the evil of the universe.

Ahi giustizia di Dio! tante chi stipa
nove travaglie e pene quant’ io viddi?
e perché nostra colpa sì ne scipa?

Ah, divine justice! Who could have piled up so many new torments and pains as I have seen? And why does our fault so strip us bare?

Come fa l’onda là sovra Cariddi,
che si frange con quella in cui s’intoppa,
così convien che qui la gente riddi.

As the wave above Charybdis, which crashes against the one it meets, so must the people here be tossed about.

Qui vid’ i’ gente più ch’altrove troppa,
e d’una parte e d’altra, con grand’ urli,
voltando pesi per forza di poppa.

Here I saw a people more numerous than elsewhere, and on both sides, with great howls, they were turning weights by force of chest.

Percotëansi ’ncontro; e poscia pur lì
si rivolgea ciascun, voltando a retro,
gridando: «Perché tieni?» e «Perché burli?».

I saw them collide, and then right there, each one would spin around, turning back, shouting, "Why do you hold?" and "Why do you mock?"

Così tornavan per lo cerchio tetro
da ogne mano a l’opposito punto,
gridandosi anche loro ontoso metro;

These are clerics, who have no hairy covering on their heads, both popes and cardinals, in whom avarice indulges its excess.

And I: "Master, among these, I should surely recognize some who were stained by these evils."

Inferno * Canto XXX

In the tenth pouch of the eighth circle, Dante and Virgil witness the punishment of the Falsifiers. They first observe two shades, Gianni Schicchi and Myrrha, running in a frenzy. Schicchi attacks another soul, an alchemist named Capocchio, biting his neck and dragging him away. The poets then encounter Master Adamo, a counterfeiter whose body is horribly bloated with dropsy, leaving him immobile and tormented by an unquenchable thirst. Master Adamo identifies himself and points to two other sinners nearby: Potiphar’s wife and Sinon the Greek, both falsifiers of words who suffer from a raging fever. A crude argument erupts between Master Adamo and Sinon. Sinon strikes Adamo’s swollen belly, and the two exchange vicious insults about their respective crimes and punishments. Dante finds himself engrossed by this vulgar quarrel until Virgil sharply rebukes him. Ashamed of his base curiosity, Dante receives Virgil's immediate forgiveness.

Inferno • Canto XXX

per Semelè contra ’l sangue tebano,
come mostrò una e altra fïata,

In the time Juno was enragedat Semele, against Theban blood,as she showed time and again,

e rotollo e percosselo ad un sasso;

He seized the one named Learcus,and dashed him, smashing him against a stone;and she drowned herself, weighted down with the other.

E quando la fortuna volse in basso

sì che ’nsieme col regno il re fu casso,

And when fortune cast downthe height of the Trojans, who dared all,so that with their kingdom, the king was undone,

Ecuba trista, misera e cattiva,
poscia che vide Polissena morta,
e del suo Polidoro in su la riva

Hecuba, sad, wretched, and wicked,after she saw Polyxena dead,and her Polydorus on the shore,

del mar si fu la dolorosa accorta,
forsennata latrò sì come cane;
tanto il dolor le fé la mente torta.

From the sea, the sorrowful woman grew aware,insane, she barked like a dog;so much pain had twisted her mind.

Ma né di Tebe furie né troiane
si vider mäi in alcun tanto crude,
non punger bestie, nonché membra umane,

No Theban Furies, nor Trojan ones, were ever seen so cruel, never to torment beasts, much less human limbs.

quant’ io vidi in due ombre smorte e nude,
che mordendo correvan di quel modo
che ’l porco quando del porcil si schiude.

As I watched two pale, naked shades, they ran, biting in a manner like a hog when it bursts from the pigsty.

L’una giunse a Capocchio, e in sul nodo
del collo l’assannò, sì che, tirando,
grattar li fece il ventre al fondo sodo.

The first shade reached Capocchio, and at the juncture of his neck it bit down, so that, in pulling, it made him scrape his belly along the hard ground.

E l’Aretin che rimase, tremando
mi disse: «Quel folletto è Gianni Schicchi,
e va rabbioso altrui così conciando».

And the Aretine, left behind, trembling, said to me: "That imp is Gianni Schicchi, and he goes about treating others like that in his fury."

«Oh», diss’ io lui, «se l’altro non ti ficchi
li denti a dosso, non ti sia fatica
a dir chi è, pria che di qui si spicchi».

"Oh," I said to him, "if the other one doesn't sink his teeth into your back, it's no trouble to tell me who he is before we leave here."

Ed elli a me: «Quell’ è l’anima antica
di Mirra scellerata, che divenne
al padre, fuor del dritto amore, amica.

And he said to me: "That is the ancient soul of wicked Myrrha, who, outside of proper love, became a lover to her father.

Questa a peccar con esso così venne,
falsificando sé in altrui forma,
come l’altro che là sen va, sostenne,

This soul came to sin with him in this way,disguising herself in a different form,just as the other one going there, sustained.

per guadagnar la donna de la torma,
falsificare in sé Buoso Donati,
testando e dando al testamento norma».

to win the woman of the herd,counterfeiting in himself Buoso Donati,testifying and giving the testament its form.

E poi che i due rabbiosi fuor passati
sovra cu’ io avea l’occhio tenuto,
rivolsilo a guardar li altri mal nati.

And after the two furious ones had passed, upon whom I had kept my eye, I turned it to gaze upon the other ill-born.

Io vidi un, fatto a guisa di lëuto,
pur ch’elli avesse avuta l’anguinaia
tronca da l’altro che l’uomo ha forcuto.

I saw one, shaped like a lute, if only it had had the groin cut off from the other, forked thing that men possess.

La grave idropesì, che sì dispaia
le membra con l’omor che mal converte,
che ’l viso non risponde a la ventraia,

The heavy dropsy, which so distortsthe limbs with the humor that it badly converts,that the face does not correspond to the belly,

faceva lui tener le labbra aperte
come l’etico fa, che per la sete
l’un verso ’l mento e l’altro in sù rinverte.

made him keep his lips apart,as a consumptive does, whose thirsttwists one lip toward the chin, the other up.

«O voi che sanz’ alcuna pena siete,
e non so io perché, nel mondo gramo»,
diss’ elli a noi, «guardate e attendete

"O you who are here without any pain, and I don't know why, in this wretched world," he said to us, "look and listen closely."

a la miseria del maestro Adamo;
io ebbi, vivo, assai di quel ch’i’ volli,
e ora, lasso!, un gocciol d’acqua bramo.

to the misery of Master Adam. While I was alive, I had plenty of what I wanted, and now, alas, I crave a single drop of water.

Li ruscelletti che d’i verdi colli
del Casentin discendon giuso in Arno,
faccendo i lor canali freddi e molli,

The little streams that cascade from the green hills of Casentino down into the Arno, making their channels cool and moist,

sempre mi stanno innanzi, e non indarno,
ché l’imagine lor vie più m’asciuga
che ’l male ond’ io nel volto mi discarno.

They are always before me, and not in vain, for their image dries me out even more than the malady that wastes my face.

La rigida giustizia che mi fruga
tragge cagion del loco ov’ io peccai
a metter più li miei sospiri in fuga.

The rigid justice that probes metakes the cause of the place where I sinnedto further drive my sighs into flight.

Ivi è Romena, là dov’ io falsai
la lega suggellata del Batista;
per ch’io il corpo sù arso lasciai.

There is Romana, where I betrayedthe sealed league of the Baptist;for which I left my body burned.

Ma s’io vedessi qui l’anima trista
di Guido o d’Alessandro o di lor frate,
per Fonte Branda non darei la vista.

But if I saw here the sorrowful soulof Guido or Alessandro, or their brother,I would not give my sight for Fonte Branda.

Dentro c’è l’una già, se l’arrabbiate
ombre che vanno intorno dicon vero;
ma che mi val, c’ho le membra legate?

Within, one is already, if the angered shadesthat wander around tell the truth;but what good is it to me, my limbs are bound?

S’io fossi pur di tanto ancor leggero
ch’i’ potessi in cent’ anni andare un’oncia,
io sarei messo già per lo sentiero,

If I were still so light that I could travel an inch in a hundred years, I would already be placed upon the path.

cercando lui tra questa gente sconcia,
con tutto ch’ella volge undici miglia,
e men d’un mezzo di traverso non ci ha.

Searching for him among these wretched people, though she circles eleven miles, and there is not less than half a mile across.

Io son per lor tra sì fatta famiglia;
e’ m’indussero a batter li fiorini
ch’avevan tre carati di mondiglia».

I am with them, among such a family as this; they induced me to clip the florins, which had three carats of alloy."

E io a lui: «Chi son li due tapini
che fumman come man bagnate ’l verno,
giacendo stretti a’ tuoi destri confini?».

And I to him: "Who are those two wretches there, who smoke like wet hands in winter, lying close beside your right flank?"

«Qui li trovai—e poi volta non dierno—»,
rispuose, «quando piovvi in questo greppo,
e non credo che dieno in sempiterno.

"There they are," he replied, "and they haven't turned away since the day I rained down into this hollow, and I don't believe they ever will."

L’una è la falsa ch’accusò Gioseppo;
l’altr’ è ’l falso Sinon greco di Troia:
per febbre aguta gittan tanto leppo».

One is the deceitful woman who accused Joseph; the other is the false Sinon, Greek of Troy: they spew forth filth from a burning fever.”

E l’un di lor, che si recò a noia
forse d’esser nomato sì oscuro,
col pugno li percosse l’epa croia.

And one of them, who seemed displeasedto be called so obscure,struck his swollen belly with a fist.

Quella sonò come fosse un tamburo;
e mastro Adamo li percosse il volto
col braccio suo, che non parve men duro,

That one sounded like a drum; and Master Adam struck their face with his arm, which seemed no less hard.

dicendo a lui: «Ancor che mi sia tolto
lo muover per le membra che son gravi,
ho io il braccio a tal mestiere sciolto».

saying to him, “Though I'm unableto move because of my heavy limbs,I still have an arm free for this work.”

Ond’ ei rispuose: «Quando tu andavi
al fuoco, non l’avei tu così presto;
ma sì e più l’avei quando coniavi».

And he replied, "When you were at the fire, you didn't have it so quickly; but you had it as much, and more, when you were coining."

E l’idropico: «Tu di’ ver di questo:
ma tu non fosti sì ver testimonio
là ’ve del ver fosti a Troia richesto».

And the dropsical man said, "You speak the truth about this; but you were not such a true witness when you were asked for the truth in Troy."

«S’io dissi falso, e tu falsasti il conio»,
disse Sinon; «e son qui per un fallo,
e tu per più ch’alcun altro demonio!».

"If I spoke falsely, and you debased the currency," Sinon said, "I'm here for a single transgression, and you for more than any other demon!"

«Ricorditi, spergiuro, del cavallo»,
rispuose quel ch’avëa infiata l’epa;
«e sieti reo che tutto il mondo sallo!».

"Remember the horse, you oath-breaker," the one whose belly was swollen replied, "and be ashamed, for the whole world knows your guilt!"

«E te sia rea la sete onde ti crepa»,
disse ’l Greco, «la lingua, e l’acqua marcia
che ’l ventre innanzi a li occhi sì t’assiepa!».

"May you be damned by the thirst that cracks your tongue," the Greek said, "and by the foul water that bloats your belly before your eyes!"

Allora il monetier: «Così si squarcia
la bocca tua per tuo mal come suole;
ché, s’i’ ho sete e omor mi rinfarcia,

Then the money-changer said: "So your mouth tears open,to your detriment, as is its habit;for, though I thirst, and rot swells my insides,"

tu hai l’arsura e ’l capo che ti duole,
e per leccar lo specchio di Narcisso,
non vorresti a ’nvitar molte parole».

you have thirst and a head that aches,and for licking Narcissus' mirror,you wouldn't want to use many words.

Ad ascoltarli er’ io del tutto fisso,
quando ’l maestro mi disse: «Or pur mira,
che per poco che teco non mi risso!».

I was completely fixed on listening to them when the master said to me, "Now look closely, or I'll be forced to scold you!"

Quand’ io ’l senti’ a me parlar con ira,
volsimi verso lui con tal vergogna,
ch’ancor per la memoria mi si gira.

When I heard him speak to me with anger, I turned toward him, so ashamed that the memory of it still shivers through me.

Qual è colui che suo dannaggio sogna,
che sognando desidera sognare,
sì che quel ch’è, come non fosse, agogna,

Like one who dreams of his own suffering, and dreaming, wishes to dream again, so that he yearns for what is, as though it were not,

tal mi fec’ io, non possendo parlare,
che disïava scusarmi, e scusava
me tuttavia, e nol mi credea fare.

Such was I made, unable to speak, that I desired to excuse myself, and excused myself continuously, and did not believe I was doing so.

«Maggior difetto men vergogna lava»,
disse ’l maestro, «che ’l tuo non è stato;
però d’ogne trestizia ti disgrava.

“A greater fault washes away less shame,” the master said, “than what you have done; therefore, free yourself from all sorrow.”

E fa ragion ch’io ti sia sempre allato,
se più avvien che fortuna t’accoglia
dove sien genti in simigliante piato:

And it is right that I should always be near you, if it happens again that fortune brings you where people are in similar strife.

ché voler ciò udire è bassa voglia».

because to want to hear such things is a base desire."

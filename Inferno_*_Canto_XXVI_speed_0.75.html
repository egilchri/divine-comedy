Inferno * Canto XXVI

Dante and Virgil stand on a rock bridge overlooking the eighth pouch of the Eighth Circle. The chasm is filled with innumerable moving flames, each one containing the soul of a fraudulent counselor. Dante’s attention is drawn to a specific flame that is split at the top, appearing as a double tongue of fire. He asks Virgil who is inside. Virgil explains that the twin flame holds Ulysses and Diomedes, punished together for their joint ventures, including the Trojan Horse. Dante expresses a strong desire to speak with them. Virgil agrees but insists on addressing the Greek heroes himself. He calls out to the flame, asking one of the souls to recount the story of his final journey. The larger horn of the flame begins to flicker, and the voice of Ulysses narrates how he urged his aging crew to sail beyond the Pillars of Hercules into the unknown ocean. They sighted a great mountain, but a whirlwind struck their ship, sinking it and drowning all aboard.

Inferno • Canto XXVI

Godi, Fiorenza, poi che se’ sì grande
che per mare e per terra batti l’ali,
e per lo ’nferno tuo nome si spande!

God, Florence, seeing as you are so great, that over sea and land you beat your wings, and through Hell your name spreads!

Tra li ladron trovai cinque cotali
tuoi cittadini onde mi ven vergogna,
e tu in grande orranza non ne sali.

Among the thieves, I found five such men, your citizens, and I am ashamed. Yet, you do not rise in great honor.

Ma se presso al mattin del ver si sogna,
tu sentirai, di qua da picciol tempo,
di quel che Prato, non ch’altri, t’agogna.

But if you dream near the dawn of truth, you will feel, before long, what Prato, and not just others, desires of you.

E se già fosse, non saria per tempo.
Così foss’ ei, da che pur esser dee!
ché più mi graverà, com’ più m’attempo.

And if it were already so, it would be too soon.If it were this way, since it must be!For it will weigh more on me, the more I delay.

Noi ci partimmo, e su per le scalee
che n’avea fatto iborni a scender pria,
rimontò ’l duca mio e trasse mee;

We set off, and up the stairways, which the wardens had made to descend before, my guide climbed again, and pulled me along.

e proseguendo la solinga via,
tra le schegge e tra ’ rocchi de lo scoglio
lo piè sanza la man non si spedia.

And continuing along the solitary path, amidst the shattered stones and the rocky debris, my foot could not advance without my hand.

Allor mi dolsi, e ora mi ridoglio
quando drizzo la mente a ciò ch’io vidi,
e più lo ’ngegno affreno ch’i’ non soglio,

Then I grieved, and now I grieve again, when I direct my mind to what I saw, and I restrain my intellect more than usual.

perché non corra che virtù nol guidi;
sì che, se stella bona o miglior cosa
m’ha dato ’l ben, ch’io stessi nol m’invidi.

Because it shouldn’t proceed unless virtue guides it; thus, if a good star or something better has given me this good, that I would not begrudge myself it.

Quante ’l villan ch’al poggio si riposa,
nel tempo che colui che ’l mondo schiara
la faccia sua a noi tien meno ascosa,

As the peasant rests upon the hillside, during the time when he who illuminates the world holds his face less hidden from us,

come la mosca cede a la zanzara,
vede lucciole giù per la vallea,
forse colà dov’ e’ vendemmia e ara:

as the fly yields to the mosquito,he sees fireflies down in the valley,perhaps where he harvests and plows:

di tante fiamme tutta risplendea
l’ottava bolgia, sì com’ io m’accorsi
tosto che fui là ’ve ’l fondo parea.

The eighth pit blazed so brightly with so many flames, I realized as soon as I arrived where the bottom became visible.

E qual colui che si vengiò con li orsi
vide ’l carro d’Elia al dipartire,
quando i cavalli al cielo erti levorsi,

And like the one who avenged himself with the bears, saw Elijah's chariot depart, when the horses rose, straight up into the sky.

che nol potea sì con li occhi seguire,

tal si move ciascuna per la gola
del fosso, ché nessuna mostra ’l furto,
e ogne fiamma un peccatore invola.

And so each one moves along the ravine's throat, for none reveals their theft, and every flame conceals a sinner.

Io stava sovra ’l ponte a veder surto,
sì che s’io non avessi un ronchion preso,
caduto sarei giù sanz’ esser urto.

I stood upon the bridge, watching them rise, so that had I not grabbed hold of a jutting rock, I would have fallen down without being struck.

E ’l duca che mi vide tanto atteso,
disse: «Dentro dai fuochi son li spirti;
catun si fascia di quel ch’elli è inceso».

And the guide, who saw me so intent, said: "Within these fires are the spirits; each one is cloaked in what it is consumed by."

«Maestro mio», rispuos’ io, «per udirti
son io più certo; ma già m’era avviso
che così fosse, e già voleva dirti:

"My master," I replied, "I am more certain for having heard you, but I already suspected as much, and I was about to say as much:"

chi è ’n quel foco che vien sì diviso
di sopra, che par surger de la pira
dov’ Eteòcle col fratel fu miso?».

"Who is in that fire that is so divided above, which seems to rise from the pyre where Eteocles was placed with his brother?"

He answered me: "Within, Ulysses and Diomedes are tormented, and together they suffer vengeance as they once suffered wrath;

e dentro da la lor fiamma si geme

onde uscì de’ Romani il gentil seme.

And within their flame, the ambush of the horse that made the gate, whence issued forth the noble seed of the Romans, is lamented.

Piangevisi entro l’arte per che, morta,

Within them, you may weep for the cunning that, in death, causes Deidamia still to grieve for Achilles, and for the suffering brought by the Palladium.

"If they can speak within those sparks," I said, "master, I beg you, and I beg again, may this prayer be worth a thousand."

And he to me: "Your prayer merits much praise, and therefore I accept it; but see that you restrain your tongue."

Lascia parlare a me, ch’i’ ho concetto

Let me speak, for I understand what you desire. They would be wary, perhaps, of your words, because they are Greeks.”

Poi che la fiamma fu venuta quivi

When the flame reached the place and time that seemed right to my guide, I heard him speak in this way:

«O you two who are within a single flame, if I deserved of you while I lived, if I deserved of you greatly or little,

When in the world I wrote the lofty verses, do not move; but let one of you say where, for him, he went, lost, to die.

Purgatorio * Canto IV

Dante and Virgil begin the arduous ascent of Mount Purgatory. The path is exceptionally steep, forcing the poets to use both hands and feet to climb the sheer rock face. Exhausted, Dante stops, and Virgil explains that the mountain is most difficult at its base and becomes progressively easier to climb higher up. They find a narrow opening in the rock and follow it to a terrace where they can rest. Dante observes that the sun is on their left, to the north, a phenomenon Virgil explains with a lesson on celestial mechanics and their position in the Southern Hemisphere. Their conversation is interrupted by a voice from behind a large boulder. There, they find a group of souls sitting languidly in the shade. Dante recognizes one, Belacqua, a Florentine friend. Belacqua explains that he and the others are the Late-Repentant, who delayed their repentance until the end of their lives. They must now wait in Ante-Purgatory for a period equal to their lifespan before being allowed to begin their penance. As they speak, Virgil notes it is midday and urges Dante to press on.

Purgatorio • Canto IV

Quando per dilettanze o ver per doglie,
che alcuna virtù nostra comprenda,
l’anima bene ad essa si raccoglie,

quando per diletto o per dolore,che la nostra facoltà percepisca,l’anima a ciò si dedica, completamente.

par ch’a nulla potenza più intenda;
e questo è contra quello error che crede
ch’un’anima sovr’ altra in noi s’accenda.

it seems to focus on no other power; and this goes against the misconception that one soul lights up within another.

E però, quando s’ode cosa o vede
che tegna forte a sé l’anima volta,
vassene ’l tempo e l’uom non se n’avvede;

And yet, when something is heard or seen,that strongly holds the soul's attention,time passes, and the man does not perceive it.

ch’altra potenza è quella che l’ascolta,
e altra è quella c’ha l’anima intera:
questa è quasi legata e quella è sciolta.

For there is another faculty that listens,and another that possesses the whole soul:the former is almost bound, and the latter is free.

Di ciò ebb’ io esperïenza vera,
udendo quello spirto e ammirando;
ché ben cinquanta gradi salito era

Of this I had true experience,hearing that spirit and marveling;for it had ascended fully fifty degrees.

lo sole, e io non m’era accorto, quando
venimmo ove quell’ anime ad una
gridaro a noi: «Qui è vostro dimando».

The sun was setting, and I hadn't noticed, when we arrived where those souls together called out to us: "Here is your request."

Maggiore aperta molte volte impruna
con una forcatella di sue spine
l’uom de la villa quando l’uva imbruna,

Often pierced wide open, it imprisons,with a little fork of its thorns,the man of the village when the grapes turn brown,

che non era la calla onde salìne
lo duca mio, e io appresso, soli,
come da noi la schiera si partìne.

that was not the path by which my guide climbed, and I after him, alone, once we had separated from the company.

Vassi in Sanleo e discendesi in Noli,
montasi su in Bismantova e ’n Cacume
con esso i piè; ma qui convien ch’om voli;

We go to San Leo and descend to Noli, we climb with our feet to Bismantova and Cacume; but here one must fly.

dico con l’ale snelle e con le piume
del gran disio, di retro a quel condotto
che speranza mi dava e facea lume.

I say with nimble wings and with the feathersof great desire, following that conduitwhich gave me hope and made me bright.

Noi salavam per entro ’l sasso rotto,
e d’ogne lato ne stringea lo stremo,
e piedi e man volea il suol di sotto.

We climbed through the broken stone, and the edges pressed in on us from every side, and the ground below required feet and hands.

Poi che noi fummo in su l’orlo suppremo
de l’alta ripa, a la scoperta piaggia,
«Maestro mio», diss’ io, «che via faremo?».

Now that we were upon the uppermost edge of the high bank, on the open shore, I said, "My master, which way shall we go?"

Ed elli a me: «Nessun tuo passo caggia;
pur su al monte dietro a me acquista,
fin che n’appaia alcuna scorta saggia».

And he to me: "Let not your foot falter; ascend the mountain behind me still, until we find some wise guide."

Lo sommo er’ alto che vincea la vista,
e la costa superba più assai
che da mezzo quadrante a centro lista.

Lofty was the summit, surpassing sight, and its proud slope was far steeper than a line drawn from the midpoint of a quadrant to its center.

Io era lasso, quando cominciai:
«O dolce padre, volgiti, e rimira
com’ io rimango sol, se non restai».

I was weary when I began: "Oh, sweet father, turn back and look how alone I remain, if you have not stayed."

«Figliuol mio», disse, «infin quivi ti tira»,
additandomi un balzo poco in sùe
che da quel lato il poggio tutto gira.

"My son," he said, "reach there," pointing me toward a ledge a short way up, which on that side circles the entire hillside.

Sì mi spronaron le parole sue,
ch’i’ mi sforzai carpando appresso lui,
tanto che ’l cinghio sotto i piè mi fue.

Yes, his words spurred me on, so that I strained to follow him, until the ledge was beneath my feet.

A seder ci ponemmo ivi ambedui
vòlti a levante ond’ eravam saliti,
che suole a riguardar giovare altrui.

A little farther on, we both stood, turned toward the east from where we had climbed, which is accustomed to helping others see.

Li occhi prima drizzai ai bassi liti;
poscia li alzai al sole, e ammirava
che da sinistra n’eravam feriti.

First, I turned my eyes to the lower shores; then I raised them to the sun, and I marveled that we were struck from the left.

Ben s’avvide il poeta ch’ïo stava
stupido tutto al carro de la luce,
ove tra noi e Aquilone intrava.

Then the poet noticed that I was staring, completely dumbfounded, at the chariot of light, which entered between us and the North Star.

Ond’ elli a me: «Se Castore e Poluce
fossero in compagnia di quello specchio
che sù e giù del suo lume conduce,

Then he said to me: "If Castor and Pollux were with that mirror that guides its light up and down,

tu vedresti il Zodïaco rubecchio
ancora a l’Orse più stretto rotare,
se non uscisse fuor del cammin vecchio.

you would see the flushed Zodiacstill turning closer to the Bears,if it did not leave its ancient path.

Come ciò sia, se ’l vuoi poter pensare,
dentro raccolto, imagina Sïòn
con questo monte in su la terra stare

As it is, if you wish to consider it, gather yourself within, and imagine Zion standing upon the earth with this mountain.

sì, ch’amendue hanno un solo orizzòn
e diversi emisperi; onde la strada
che mal non seppe carreggiar Fetòn,

Yes, for both share a single horizonand different hemispheres; hence the paththat Phaeton failed to drive.

vedrai come a costui convien che vada
da l’un, quando a colui da l’altro fianco,
se lo ’ntelletto tuo ben chiaro bada».

You will see how it is fitting for him to gofrom one, when to the other, on the other flank,if your intellect is paying good attention.”

«Certo, maestro mio,» diss’ io, «unquanco
non vid’ io chiaro sì com’ io discerno
là dove mio ingegno parea manco,

"Surely, my master," I said, "never before have I seen so clearly as I perceive now, where my intellect seemed lacking."

che ’l mezzo cerchio del moto superno,
che si chiama Equatore in alcun’ arte,
e che sempre riman tra ’l sole e ’l verno,

that the middle circle of the highest motion, which some art calls the Equator, and which always remains between the sun and winter,

per la ragion che di’, quinci si parte
verso settentrïon, quanto li Ebrei
vedevan lui verso la calda parte.

because of the reason I have spoken, from this pointit turns towards the north, as much as the Hebrewssaw it turn towards the warmer part.

Ma se a te piace, volontier saprei
quanto avemo ad andar; ché ’l poggio sale
più che salir non posson li occhi miei».

But if it pleases you, I would gladly know how far we have to go; for the slope rises more than my eyes can manage to climb.”

Ed elli a me: «Questa montagna è tale,
che sempre al cominciar di sotto è grave;
e quant’ om più va sù, e men fa male.

And he to me: "This mountain is such, that always at the beginning, it is heavy; and the more one climbs upward, the less it causes pain.

Però, quand’ ella ti parrà soave
tanto, che sù andar ti fia leggero
com’ a seconda giù andar per nave,

But when it shall seem to you so smooth, that to go up it will be as light as to go down in a ship,

allor sarai al fin d’esto sentiero;
quivi di riposar l’affanno aspetta.
Più non rispondo, e questo so per vero».

Then you will be at the end of this path; there, rest awaits your troubles. I answer no more, and this I know is true."

E com’ elli ebbe sua parola detta,
una voce di presso sonò: «Forse
che di sedere in pria avrai distretta!».

And as he finished speaking,a voice nearby called out, "Perhaps,you'll be more eager to sit first!"

Al suon di lei ciascun di noi si torse,
e vedemmo a mancina un gran petrone,
del qual né io né ei prima s’accorse.

At the sound of her words, each of us turned, and we saw to the left a great boulder, which neither I nor he had noticed before.

Là ci traemmo; e ivi eran persone
che si stavano a l’ombra dietro al sasso
come l’uom per negghienza a star si pone.

We approached that place, and there were people there, who stood in the shadow behind the boulder, much as a man might stand, out of negligence.

E un di lor, che mi sembiava lasso,
sedeva e abbracciava le ginocchia,
tenendo ’l viso giù tra esse basso.

And one of them, who seemed weary to me, sat down, embracing his knees, his face held low between them.

«O dolce segnor mio», diss’ io, «adocchia
colui che mostra sé più negligente
che se pigrizia fosse sua serocchia».

"Oh, my sweet lord," I said, "observe the one who appears the most negligent, as if laziness were his very cloak."

Allor si volse a noi e puose mente,
movendo ’l viso pur su per la coscia,
e disse: «Or va tu sù, che se’ valente!».

Then he turned toward us and paid us mind, raising his face, just at the level of his thigh, and said, "Now go on up, you who are worthy!"

Conobbi allor chi era, e quella angoscia
che m’avacciava un poco ancor la lena,
non m’impedì l’andare a lui; e poscia

I then knew who it was, and the dread that still stole a bit of my breath did not stop me from going toward him; and then

ch’a lui fu’ giunto, alzò la testa a pena,
dicendo: «Hai ben veduto come ’l sole
da l’omero sinistro il carro mena?».

when I reached him, he barely lifted his head, saying: "Have you seen how the sun drives its chariot from the left shoulder?"

Li atti suoi pigri e le corte parole
mosser le labbra mie un poco a riso;
poi cominciai: «Belacqua, a me non dole

His sluggish movements and short words stirred my lips to a slight smile; then I began: "Belacqua, I do not grieve..."

di te omai; ma dimmi: perché assiso
quiritto se’? attendi tu iscorta,
o pur lo modo usato t’ha’ ripriso?».

of you now; but tell me: why are you sittingupright there? Are you awaiting a guide,or have you simply resumed your usual posture?"

Ed elli: «O frate, andar in sù che porta?
ché non mi lascerebbe ire a’ martìri
l’angel di Dio che siede in su la porta.

And he: "Oh brother, what good does going up do? Because the angel of God who sits at the gate would not let me go to the torments."

Prima convien che tanto il ciel m’aggiri
di fuor da essa, quanto fece in vita,
per ch’io ’ndugiai al fine i buon sospiri,

First, it is necessary that Heaven circle me outside of it for as long as it did in life, because I delayed good sighs until the end.

se orazïone in prima non m’aita
che surga sù di cuor che in grazia viva;
l’altra che val, che ’n ciel non è udita?».

If prayer does not first aid me,rising from a heart living in grace,what good is the other, unheard in Heaven?

E già il poeta innanzi mi saliva,
e dicea: «Vienne omai; vedi ch’è tocco
meridïan dal sole e a la riva

And now the poet climbed before me, and said, "Come now; you see that the sun has struck the meridian, and we are at the shore."

Inferno * Canto XXXIII

The canto opens with the pilgrim Dante observing Count Ugolino gnawing on the head of Archbishop Ruggieri in Antenora, the second round of the ninth circle of Hell. At Dante’s request, Ugolino pauses his cannibalistic meal to recount his story. He describes how Ruggieri betrayed him, leading to his imprisonment with his four sons in a Pisan tower. Sealed inside, Ugolino watched helplessly as his sons starved to death one by one before he, too, succumbed to hunger. After speaking, he furiously resumes gnawing the archbishop's skull. Dante and his guide, Virgil, proceed into Ptolomea, the third round of the ninth circle. Here, traitors to guests are frozen on their backs in the ice, with their faces exposed. Their tears freeze in their eye sockets, forming a crystal seal. One sinner, Fra Alberigo, begs Dante to remove this ice. He explains that the souls of those who commit this sin descend to Hell immediately, while a demon inhabits their living body on Earth. After hearing this, Dante breaks his promise and refuses to help.

Inferno • Canto XXXIII

La bocca sollevò dal fiero pasto
quel peccator, forbendola a’ capelli
del capo ch’elli avea di retro guasto.

His mouth he lifted from that savage feast, and wiped it on the hair of the head he’d ruined from behind.

Poi cominciò: «Tu vuo’ ch’io rinovelli
disperato dolor che ’l cor mi preme
già pur pensando, pria ch’io ne favelli.

Then he began: "You want me to renewa desperate grief that weighs upon my heart,just thinking of it, before I even speak."

Ma se le mie parole esser dien seme
che frutti infamia al traditor ch’i’ rodo,
parlar e lagrimar vedrai insieme.

But if my words must serve as seedsthat bring forth infamy for the traitor I gnaw,you will see me speak and weep at once.

Io non so chi tu se’ né per che modo
venuto se’ qua giù; ma fiorentino
mi sembri veramente quand’ io t’odo.

I do not know who you are, nor by what path you have come down here; but you certainly sound Florentine to me, when I hear you.

Tu dei saper ch’i’ fui conte Ugolino,
e questi è l’arcivescovo Ruggieri:
or ti dirò perché i son tal vicino.

You should know that I was Count Ugolino, and this is Archbishop Ruggieri. Now, I will tell you why I am such a neighbor.

Che per l’effetto de’ suo’ mai pensieri,
fidandomi di lui, io fossi preso
e poscia morto, dir non è mestieri;

That, as a result of his evil thoughts, I was captured because I trusted him, and then was killed, it is not necessary to say;

però quel che non puoi avere inteso,
cioè come la morte mia fu cruda,
udirai, e saprai s’e’ m’ha offeso.

But what you could not have understood,that is, how cruel my death was,you will hear, and you will know if he has wronged me.

Breve pertugio dentro da la Muda,
la qual per me ha ’l titol de la fame,
e che conviene ancor ch’altrui si chiuda,

A narrow opening within the Muda,which holds the title of starvation because of me,and which must still be shut to others,

m’avea mostrato per lo suo forame
più lune già, quand’ io feci ’l mal sonno
che del futuro mi squarciò ’l velame.

had shown me, through its opening, many moons already, when I fell into the ill sleep that tore the veil of the future for me.

Questi pareva a me maestro e donno,
cacciando il lupo e ’ lupicini al monte
per che i Pisan veder Lucca non ponno.

This one seemed to me a master and lord, driving the wolf and its cubs toward the mountain, so that the Pisans can't see Lucca.

Con cagne magre, studïose e conte
Gualandi con Sismondi e con Lanfranchi
s’avea messi dinanzi da la fronte.

With emaciated, focused, and well-behaved hounds, Gualandi, with Sismondi and Lanfranchi, had positioned themselves at the forefront.

In picciol corso mi parieno stanchi
lo padre e ’ figli, e con l’agute scane
mi parea lor veder fender li fianchi.

In a short chase, the father and sons seemed weary to me, and I saw their flanks torn open by the sharp-toothed hounds.

Quando fui desto innanzi la dimane,
pianger senti’ fra ’l sonno i miei figliuoli
ch’eran con meco, e dimandar del pane.

When I awoke the next morning, I heard my children, who were with me, crying in their sleep and asking for bread.

Ben se’ crudel, se tu già non ti duoli
pensando ciò che ’l mio cor s’annunziava;
e se non piangi, di che pianger suoli?

Behold how cruel you are, if you do not grieve, thinking of what my heart foretold; and if you do not weep, what are you accustomed to weeping for?

Già eran desti, e l’ora s’appressava
che ’l cibo ne solëa essere addotto,
e per suo sogno ciascun dubitava;

They were already awake, and the hour approachedwhen our food was usually brought to us,and everyone was anxious about his dream.

e io senti’ chiavar l’uscio di sotto
a l’orribile torre; ond’ io guardai
nel viso a’ mie’ figliuoi sanza far motto.

And I heard the door below being locked,at the horrible tower; and so I lookedinto the faces of my sons without a word.

Io non piangëa, sì dentro impetrai:
piangevan elli; e Anselmuccio mio
disse: “Tu guardi sì, padre! che hai?”.

I did not weep, so turned to stone within; they wept; and my little Anselm said: "Father, why do you stare so?".

Perciò non lagrimai né rispuos’ io
tutto quel giorno né la notte appresso,
infin che l’altro sol nel mondo uscìo.

Therefore, I did not weep, nor did I replythat entire day, nor the following night,until the other sun emerged in the world.

Come un poco di raggio si fu messo
nel doloroso carcere, e io scorsi
per quattro visi il mio aspetto stesso,

When a bit of light entered the sorrowful prison, and I saw my own reflection in four faces,

ambo le man per lo dolor mi morsi;
ed ei, pensando ch’io ’l fessi per voglia
di manicar, di sùbito levorsi

I bit both hands in grief; and he, thinking I did it out of a hunger to eat, suddenly rose.

e disser: “Padre, assai ci fia men doglia
se tu mangi di noi: tu ne vestisti
queste misere carni, e tu le spoglia”.

And he said: "Father, it will be less painful for usif you eat of us; you clothed us inthese wretched bodies, and you strip them away."

Queta’mi allor per non farli più tristi;
lo dì e l’altro stemmo tutti muti;
ahi dura terra, perché non t’apristi?

Queta'mi then, so as not to make them more sorrowful;that day and the next we stayed completely mute;ah, harsh earth, why didn’t you open?

Poscia che fummo al quarto dì venuti,
Gaddo mi si gittò disteso a’ piedi,
dicendo: “Padre mio, ché non m’aiuti?”.

After we arrived at the fourth day, Gaddo threw himself at my feet, stretched out, saying, "My father, why don't you help me?"

Quivi morì; e come tu mi vedi,
vid’ io cascar li tre ad uno ad uno
tra ’l quinto dì e ’l sesto; ond’ io mi diedi,

There he died; and as you see me, I saw the three fall one by one between the fifth and sixth days. Then I gave myself,

già cieco, a brancolar sovra ciascuno,
e due dì li chiamai, poi che fur morti.
Poscia, più che ’l dolor, poté ’l digiuno».

already blind, to grope over each, and for two days I called to them, after they were dead. Then, more than grief, hunger prevailed.

Quand’ ebbe detto ciò, con li occhi torti
riprese ’l teschio misero co’ denti,
che furo a l’osso, come d’un can, forti.

When he had said this, with twisted eyes he seized the wretched skull again with his teeth, which were, like a dog's, strong against the bone.

Ahi Pisa, vituperio de le genti
del bel paese là dove ’l sì suona,
poi che i vicini a te punir son lenti,

Ah, Pisa, shame of the peoples,of the fair land where "sì" is spoken,since your neighbors are slow to punish you,

muovasi la Capraia e la Gorgona,
e faccian siepe ad Arno in su la foce,
sì ch’elli annieghi in te ogne persona!

Let Capraia and Gorgona move,and form a hedge at the Arno's mouth,so that it drowns every person in you!

Che se ’l conte Ugolino aveva voce
d’aver tradita te de le castella,
non dovei tu i figliuoi porre a tal croce.

For if Count Ugolino had a reputationof having betrayed you of the castles,you should not have put his sons to such a cross.

Innocenti facea l’età novella,
novella Tebe, Uguiccione e ’l Brigata
e li altri due che ’l canto suso appella.

Innocent was their young age, the new Thebes, Ugoccione and Brigata, and the other two whom the song names above.

Noi passammo oltre, là ’ve la gelata
ruvidamente un’altra gente fascia,
non volta in giù, ma tutta riversata.

We passed beyond, to where the frostHarshly binds another people,Not facing down, but entirely supine.

Lo pianto stesso lì pianger non lascia,
e ’l duol che truova in su li occhi rintoppo,
si volge in entro a far crescer l’ambascia;

The very weeping there permits no tears,and grief, which finds an obstacle upon the eyes,turns inward, to augment the anguish.

ché le lagrime prime fanno groppo,
e sì come visiere di cristallo,
rïempion sotto ’l ciglio tutto il coppo.

For the initial tears create a knot, and like crystal visors, they completely fill the space beneath the brow.

E avvegna che, sì come d’un callo,
per la freddura ciascun sentimento
cessato avesse del mio viso stallo,

And although, as with a callus, the cold had stilled every sensation in the theater of my face,

già mi parea sentire alquanto vento;
per ch’io: «Maestro mio, questo chi move?
non è qua giù ogne vapore spento?».

I already felt some wind, and so I asked, "My master, what moves this? Isn't every vapor extinguished down here?"

Ond’ elli a me: «Avaccio sarai dove
di ciò ti farà l’occhio la risposta,
veggendo la cagion che ’l fiato piove».

And he to me, "Soon you will be where your eye will give you the answer to this, seeing the cause that rains down the wind."

E un de’ tristi de la fredda crosta
gridò a noi: «O anime crudeli
tanto che data v’è l’ultima posta,

And one of the wretched from the frozen crust cried to us: "O souls so cruel that the final fate has been dealt to you,

levatemi dal viso i duri veli,
sì ch’ïo sfoghi ’l duol che ’l cor m’impregna,
un poco, pria che ’l pianto si raggeli».

"Remove from my face these harsh veils, so that I may vent the woe that fills my heart, just a little, before the weeping freezes again."

Per ch’io a lui: «Se vuo’ ch’i’ ti sovvegna,
dimmi chi se’, e s’io non ti disbrigo,
al fondo de la ghiaccia ir mi convegna».

Whereupon I said to him: "If you want me to help you, tell me who you are; and if I don't set you free, may I be forced to go to the bottom of the ice."

Rispuose adunque: «I’ son frate Alberigo;
i’ son quel da le frutta del mal orto,
che qui riprendo dattero per figo».

He replied then: "I am Brother Alberigo; I am he of the fruits of the wicked garden, who here receive a date for a fig."

«Oh», diss’ io lui, «or se’ tu ancor morto?».
Ed elli a me: «Come ’l mio corpo stea
nel mondo sù, nulla scïenza porto.

"Oh," I said to him, "are you still dead, then?"And he to me: "How my body fares in the world above, I have no knowledge."

Cotal vantaggio ha questa Tolomea,
che spesse volte l’anima ci cade
innanzi ch’Atropòs mossa le dea.

This Ptolomea has a special advantage: the soul often falls into it before Atropos takes it.

E perché tu più volentier mi rade
le ’nvetrïate lagrime dal volto,
sappie che, tosto che l’anima trade

And so that you may more willingly remove the frozen tears from my face, know that, as soon as the soul betrays

come fec’ ïo, il corpo suo l’è tolto
da un demonio, che poscia il governa
mentre che ’l tempo suo tutto sia vòlto.

as I did, its body is stolen awayby a demon, who then governs ituntil all its time is spent.

Ella ruina in sì fatta cisterna;
e forse pare ancor lo corpo suso
de l’ombra che di qua dietro mi verna.

She ruins in such a cistern; and perhaps the body still appears above, of the shade that winters behind me.

Tu ’l dei saper, se tu vien pur mo giuso:
elli è ser Branca Doria, e son più anni
poscia passati ch’el fu sì racchiuso».

You ought to know, if you’ve just come down: it’s Ser Branca Doria, and many years have passed since he was shut away.”

«Io credo», diss’ io lui, «che tu m’inganni;
ché Branca Doria non morì unquanche,
e mangia e bee e dorme e veste panni».

"I believe," I said to him, "that you're deceiving me; because Branca Doria never died, and he eats and drinks and sleeps and wears clothes."

«Nel fosso sù», diss’ el, «de’ Malebranche,
là dove bolle la tenace pece,
non era ancora giunto Michel Zanche,

"Up in the ditch," he said, "of the Evilclaws, where the thick pitch boils, Michel Zanche hadn't yet arrived,

che questi lasciò il diavolo in sua vece
nel corpo suo, ed un suo prossimano
che ’l tradimento insieme con lui fece.

that the Devil left in his place,inside his body, and a kinsman of hiswho committed the betrayal together with him.

Ma distendi oggimai in qua la mano;
aprimi li occhi». E io non gliel’ apersi;
e cortesia fu lui esser villano.

But now extend your hand this way;open my eyes." And I did not open them for him;and it was courteous of him to be uncouth.

Ahi Genovesi, uomini diversi
d’ogne costume e pien d’ogne magagna,
perché non siete voi del mondo spersi?

Ah, Genoese, men unlike any other,diverse in custom and filled with every vice,why aren't you scattered from the world entirely?

Ché col peggiore spirto di Romagna
trovai di voi un tal, che per sua opra
in anima in Cocito già si bagna,

Because in the worst spirit of RomagnaI found one of you, who, by his deeds,already bathes his soul in Cocytus.

e in corpo par vivo ancor di sopra.

And still seems alive in body, up above.

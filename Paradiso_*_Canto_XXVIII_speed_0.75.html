Paradiso * Canto XXVIII

In the Primum Mobile, Dante sees a reflection in Beatrice’s eyes. Turning to its source, he beholds an intensely brilliant, dimensionless Point of light. Around this Point spin nine concentric rings of fire. Dante observes that, contrary to the physical universe, the rings closest to the Point spin the fastest, while the outermost rings spin the slowest. He asks Beatrice to explain this paradox. She clarifies that in this spiritual model, speed is determined not by physical size but by proximity to the central Point. The closer a ring is to the Point, the more of its virtue it partakes in, and therefore the faster it moves. Beatrice then identifies each of the nine fiery rings, naming them in order from the center outward as the nine orders of angels: Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones, Dominions, Virtues, Powers, Principalities, Archangels, and Angels.

A point of light appeared, radiating so intensely that the eyes, singed by its blaze, must shut against its piercing sharpness.

e quale stella par quinci più poca,
parrebbe luna, locata con esso
come stella con stella si collòca.

And any star that seems smallest from here would appear a moon, positioned beside it, as a star is positioned beside another star.

Forse cotanto quanto pare appresso
alo cigner la luce che ’l dipigne
quando ’l vapor che ’l porta più è spesso,

Perhaps as much as the halo appears to encircle the light that paints it, when the vapor that carries it is thickest.

distante intorno al punto un cerchio d’igne

quel moto che più tosto il mondo cigne;

far off, around the point, a fiery circle spun, so swiftly that it would have outstripped the movement that most quickly girdles the world.

e questo era d’un altro circumcinto,
e quel dal terzo, e ’l terzo poi dal quarto,
dal quinto il quarto, e poi dal sesto il quinto.

And this one was encircled by another, and that one by a third, and the third then by a fourth, the fourth by the fifth, and then the fifth by the sixth.

Sopra seguiva il settimo sì sparto
già di larghezza, che ’l messo di Iuno
intero a contenerlo sarebbe arto.

Above, the seventh followed, so vast in its expanse that Juno's messenger would have struggled to encompass it whole.

Così l’ottavo e ’l nono; e chiascheduno

in numero distante più da l’uno;

Thus the eighth and the ninth; and each moved more slowly, as it was further away in number from the first.

e quello avea la fiamma più sincera
cui men distava la favilla pura,
credo, però che più di lei s’invera.

And that one held the purest flame, the least distant from the pure spark; I believe it, because it is most enlivened by it.

La donna mia, che mi vedëa in cura
forte sospeso, disse: «Da quel punto
depende il cielo e tutta la natura.

My lady, who saw me deeply concerned, said: "From that point depends the heavens and all of nature."

Mira quel cerchio che più li è congiunto;
e sappi che ’l suo muovere è sì tosto
per l’affocato amore ond’ elli è punto».

Behold that sphere which is most joined to it; and know that its movement is so swift because of the ardent love that spurs it onward.”

E io a lei: «Se ’l mondo fosse posto
con l’ordine ch’io veggio in quelle rote,
sazio m’avrebbe ciò che m’è proposto;

And I to her: "If the world were positionedwith the order that I see in those spheres,that which is proposed to me would satisfy me;

ma nel mondo sensibile si puote
veder le volte tanto più divine,
quant’ elle son dal centro più remote.

but in the sensible world one canobserve the celestial spheres, the more divine they are,the further they are from the center.

Onde, se ’l mio disir dee aver fine
in questo miro e angelico templo

Therefore, if my desire is to find its endin this marvelous and angelic templewhose only boundary is love and light,

udir convienmi ancor come l’essemplo

ché io per me indarno a ciò contemplo».

It would do me good to consider again how the exampleand the exemplar do not proceed in the same manner,for I, in my case, contemplate this in vain.

«Se li tuoi diti non sono a tal nodo
sufficïenti, non è maraviglia:

"If your fingers aren't up to this knot,it's no surprise:it's become so tight from not being attempted!"

Thus, my lady; then she said: "Take holdof what I will tell you, if you want satisfaction;and be meticulous around it."

The physical spheres are broad and diverse,according to the more or less of the virtuethat extends through all their parts.

Greater goodness wants to make greater health; greater health holds a greater body, if it has its parts equally complete.

Therefore, this one who steals the whole of the rest of the universe with him, corresponds to the sphere that loves most and knows most.

you will see the wondrous consequenceof more to less and less to more,in every heaven, in its own intelligence.”

Come rimane splendido e sereno

How splendid and serene remainsthe air's hemisphere, when Boreas blowsfrom that cheek, whence it is most gentle.

For which the filth that first disturbed it is purged and dissolved, so that the sky laughs with the beauties of all its adornment.

Thus I did, after my lady provided me with her clear reply, and the truth became visible like a star in the heavens.

E poi che le parole sue restaro,

And after her words ceased, the circles sparked, no differently than iron does when it boils.

Her inferno followed every spark; and they were so many that their number exceeded the doubling of chess.

Io sentiva osannar di coro in coro

She heard the hymns of choir to choir,to the fixed point that holds them in place,and will hold them always, where they ever were.

E quella che vedëa i pensier dubi

And she who saw the doubtful thoughts within my mind, said: "The first circles have shown you Seraphim and Cherubim.

So swiftly do they follow his commands,striving to resemble him as closely as possible;and they are able, for their sight is sublime.

Quelli altri amori che ’ntorno li vonno,

Those other loves that circle them,are called Thrones of the divine aspect,by which the first triad is completed;

e dei saper che tutti hanno diletto

And know that all find joythe more their sight descendsinto the Truth, wherein all intellect finds rest.

Quinci si può veder come si fonda

Here one can see how the blessed state is foundedin the act of seeing,not in that which loves, which then follows after.

e del vedere è misura mercede,

And from seeing comes measure as reward,which gives birth to grace and good will:so one proceeds from grade to grade.

In this hierarchy are the other goddesses: first, Dominations, then Virtues; the third order is Powers.

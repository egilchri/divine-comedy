Purgatorio * Canto XXI

As Dante and Virgil walk on the fifth terrace, the entire mountain of Purgatory is rocked by a powerful tremor. Simultaneously, all the souls on the mountain shout, "Gloria in excelsis Deo." A shade appears behind the poets and greets them. Virgil explains his and Dante’s situation and asks the newcomer to explain the cause of the earthquake and the shouting. The shade reveals that the mountain shakes not from natural causes but whenever a soul feels it has been purified and is free to ascend to Heaven. The collective shout is the other souls' song of praise for the one who is rising. He then identifies himself as the Roman poet Statius, who has just completed his 500-year penance on this terrace. Statius expresses immense admiration for Virgil's *Aeneid*. When Dante reveals that his guide is Virgil himself, an overjoyed Statius attempts to embrace his feet, but Virgil stops him, reminding him that they are both shades.

Purgatorio • Canto XXI

La sete natural che mai non sazia
se non con l’acqua onde la femminetta
samaritana domandò la grazia,

The natural thirst that is never quenchedexcept by the water for which the Samaritan girlbegged for grace.

mi travagliava, e pungeami la fretta
per la ’mpacciata via dietro al mio duca,
e condoleami a la giusta vendetta.

That tormented me, and I was spurred onward by haste,following my guide down the difficult path,and I felt compassion for the just vengeance.

Ed ecco, sì come ne scrive Luca
che Cristo apparve a’ due ch’erano in via,
già surto fuor de la sepulcral buca,

And behold, just as Luke writes, that Christ appeared to the two who were on the road, having already risen out of the sepulcher's hole.

ci apparve un’ombra, e dietro a noi venìa,
dal piè guardando la turba che giace;
né ci addemmo di lei, sì parlò pria,

Then a shadow appeared, moving behind us, watching the crowd that lay at our feet. We didn't notice it, so it spoke first,

dicendo: «O frati miei, Dio vi dea pace».
Noi ci volgemmo sùbiti, e Virgilio
rendéli ’l cenno ch’a ciò si conface.

saying: "Oh, my brothers, may God grant you peace."We turned quickly, and Virgilreturned the greeting that was fitting.

Poi cominciò: «Nel beato concilio
ti ponga in pace la verace corte
che me rilega ne l’etterno essilio».

Then he began: "May the blessed assembly,the truthful court, place you in peace,as it has banished me to eternal exile."

«Come!», diss’ elli, e parte andavam forte:
«se voi siete ombre che Dio sù non degni,
chi v’ha per la sua scala tanto scorte?».

"Come!" he said, and we walked on quickly. "If you are shades whom God does not deem worthy, who has escorted you so far up his ladder?"

E ’l dottor mio: «Se tu riguardi a’ segni
che questi porta e che l’angel profila,
ben vedrai che coi buon convien ch’e’ regni.

And my teacher said: "If you look at the marks he bears, and at those the angel has drawn, you will see that he must reign with the good."

Ma perché lei che dì e notte fila
non li avea tratta ancora la conocchia
che Cloto impone a ciascuno e compila,

But why did she who spins day and nightnot yet have drawn the spindle from him,which Clotho assigns and compiles for each?

l’anima sua, ch’è tua e mia serocchia,
venendo sù, non potea venir sola,
però ch’al nostro modo non adocchia.

His soul, your sister and mine,as it ascended, could not come alone,because it does not see in our manner.

Ond’ io fui tratto fuor de l’ampia gola
d’inferno per mostrarli, e mosterrolli
oltre, quanto ’l potrà menar mia scola.

And I, in order to show him, led him out of the vast throat of Hell; and beyond, I shall show him as far as my school can take him.

Ma dimmi, se tu sai, perché tai crolli
diè dianzi ’l monte, e perché tutto ad una
parve gridare infino a’ suoi piè molli».

But tell me, if you know, why did the mountain shake just now, and why did everything seem to cry out all at once, down to its soft feet?

Sì mi diè, dimandando, per la cruna
del mio disio, che pur con la speranza
si fece la mia sete men digiuna.

Yes, she gave it to me, asking, through the eyeof my desire, which, with hope,made my thirst less hungry.

Quei cominciò: «Cosa non è che sanza
ordine senta la religïone
de la montagna, o che sia fuor d’usanza.

"Then she began: 'There is nothing that does not have an order, that violates the religion of this mountain, or that is outside of custom.'"

Libero è qui da ogne alterazione:
di quel che ’l ciel da sé in sé riceve
esser ci puote, e non d’altro, cagione.

Here, everything is free from alteration:Only that which Heaven receives into itselfcan be the cause, and nothing else.

Per che non pioggia, non grando, non neve,
non rugiada, non brina più sù cade
che la scaletta di tre gradi breve;

Because no rain, no hail, no snow,no dew, no frost falls any higherthan the short three-step ladder;

nuvole spesse non paion né rade,
né coruscar, né figlia di Taumante,
che di là cangia sovente contrade;

Thick clouds appear neither sparse,nor to flash, nor like Thaumas' daughter,who often changes regions beyond.

secco vapor non surge più avante
ch’al sommo d’i tre gradi ch’io parlai,
dov’ ha ’l vicario di Pietro le piante.

Dry vapor does not rise furtherthan to the summit of the three steps of which I spoke,where Peter's vicar has his footing.

Trema forse più giù poco o assai;
ma per vento che ’n terra si nasconda,
non so come, qua sù non tremò mai.

It might tremble a bit further down; but because of wind hidden in the earth, I don't know how, it has never trembled here above.

Tremaci quando alcuna anima monda
sentesi, sì che surga o che si mova
per salir sù; e tal grido seconda.

It trembles when some pure soul feels it, so that it rises or moves to ascend; and such a cry seconds it.

De la mondizia sol voler fa prova,
che, tutto libero a mutar convento,
l’alma sorprende, e di voler le giova.

From purity, the will itself is tested, which, utterly free to change its dwelling, surprises the soul and benefits it with its willing.

Prima vuol ben, ma non lascia il talento
che divina giustizia, contra voglia,
come fu al peccar, pone al tormento.

She desires good, but does not allow the ability, which divine justice, against her will, as it was in sinning, puts to torment.

E io, che son giaciuto a questa doglia
cinquecent’ anni e più, pur mo sentii
libera volontà di miglior soglia:

And I, who have lain in this suffering for five hundred years and more, have only now felt a free will toward a better threshold.

però sentisti il tremoto e li pii
spiriti per lo monte render lode
a quel Segnor, che tosto sù li ’nvii».

But you felt the tremor, and the pious spirits upon the mountain gave praise to that Lord, who would quickly send them up.

Così ne disse; e però ch’el si gode
tanto del ber quant’ è grande la sete,
non saprei dir quant’ el mi fece prode.

He said this; and because he enjoyed drinking as much as his thirst was great, I cannot say how much good he did me.

E ’l savio duca: «Omai veggio la rete
che qui vi ’mpiglia e come si scalappia,
perché ci trema e di che congaudete.

And the wise leader: "Now I see the netthat entraps you here, and how you escape it,because you tremble and what you rejoice in."

Ora chi fosti, piacciati ch’io sappia,
e perché tanti secoli giaciuto
qui se’, ne le parole tue mi cappia».

"Now tell me who you were, if you please,and why you have lain here for so many ages,that my words may capture it."

«Nel tempo che ’l buon Tito, con l’aiuto
del sommo rege, vendicò le fóra
ond’ uscì ’l sangue per Giuda venduto,

In the era when the good Titus, with the aid of the highest king, avenged the wounds from which the blood sold by Judas flowed,

col nome che più dura e più onora
era io di là», rispuose quello spirto,
«famoso assai, ma non con fede ancora.

"With the name that lasts the longest and brings the most honor, I was there," that spirit replied, "renowned, but not yet with faith."

Tanto fu dolce mio vocale spirto,
che, tolosano, a sé mi trasse Roma,
dove mertai le tempie ornar di mirto.

So sweet was my vocal spirit, that Toulouse drew me to itself, where Rome then merited to adorn my temples with myrtle.

Stazio la gente ancor di là mi noma:
cantai di Tebe, e poi del grande Achille;
ma caddi in via con la seconda soma.

The people there still call me Statius: I sang of Thebes, and then of great Achilles; but I fell on the road with the second burden.

Al mio ardor fuor seme le faville,
che mi scaldar, de la divina fiamma
onde sono allumati più di mille;

My ardor was seeded by the sparksthat warmed me, of the divine flamethat has illuminated more than a thousand;

de l’Eneïda dico, la qual mamma
fummi, e fummi nutrice, poetando:
sanz’ essa non fermai peso di dramma.

Of the Aeneid I speak, which was motherto me, and was my nurse, in the art of poetry:without it, I would not have set the weight of a scruple.

E per esser vivuto di là quando
visse Virgilio, assentirei un sole
più che non deggio al mio uscir di bando».

And because I lived back then, when Virgil lived, I would concede one sun more than I owe for my exile.

Volser Virgilio a me queste parole
con viso che, tacendo, disse ‘Taci’;
ma non può tutto la virtù che vuole;

Volser Virgil these words toward me,with a face that, in its silence, said, “Be still;”but all that virtue wills cannot be done;

ché riso e pianto son tanto seguaci
a la passion di che ciascun si spicca,
che men seguon voler ne’ più veraci.

because laughter and tears so closely attendthe passion that compels each soul to action,that they attend the will less, even when true.

Io pur sorrisi come l’uom ch’ammicca;
per che l’ombra si tacque, e riguardommi
ne li occhi ove ’l sembiante più si ficca;

I, too, smiled like a man who winks;at which the shade fell silent, and regarded mein the eyes, where semblance is most fixed;

e «Se tanto labore in bene assommi»,
disse, «perché la tua faccia testeso
un lampeggiar di riso dimostrommi?».

And he said, "If you gather so much good in your work, then why did your face just now flash with a smile?"

Or son io d’una parte e d’altra preso:
l’una mi fa tacer, l’altra scongiura
ch’io dica; ond’ io sospiro, e sono inteso

Now I am caught between both sides: one makes me silent, the other implores me to speak; thus I sigh, and am understood.

dal mio maestro, e «Non aver paura»,
mi dice, «di parlar; ma parla e digli
quel ch’e’ dimanda con cotanta cura».

From my master, he says, "Have no fear of speaking. But speak and tell him what he asks with such care."

Ond’ io: «Forse che tu ti maravigli,
antico spirto, del rider ch’io fei;
ma più d’ammirazion vo’ che ti pigli.

And I said, “Perhaps you marvel, ancient spirit, at my laughter; but I wish more wonder would seize you.”

Questi che guida in alto li occhi miei,
è quel Virgilio dal qual tu togliesti
forte a cantar de li uomini e d’i dèi.

He who guides my eyes upward is that Virgil from whom you took the strength to sing of men and gods.

Se cagion altra al mio rider credesti,
lasciala per non vera, ed esser credi
quelle parole che di lui dicesti».

If you thought some other cause made me laugh, dismiss it as untrue, and believe the words you spoke about him.”

Già s’inchinava ad abbracciar li piedi
al mio dottor, ma el li disse: «Frate,
non far, ché tu se’ ombra e ombra vedi».

He was already bowing to embrace my teacher’s feet, but he said to him, “Brother, don’t do that, because you are a shade, and you are seeing a shade.”

Ed ei surgendo: «Or puoi la quantitate
comprender de l’amor ch’a te mi scalda,
quand’ io dismento nostra vanitate,

And rising, he said, "Now you can comprehend the measure of the love that warms me for you, when I forget our vanity,

trattando l’ombre come cosa salda».

treating the shadows as if they were solid.

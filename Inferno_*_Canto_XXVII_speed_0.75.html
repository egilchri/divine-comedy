Inferno * Canto XXVII

As the flame of Ulysses departs, another soul concealed within a flame approaches Virgil and Dante. After struggling to form words, it asks for news from Romagna. At Virgil’s prompting, Dante provides a summary of the region's current political state, noting that while no open wars are being fought, the area remains under the control of various tyrants. Dante then asks the soul for its identity. Believing Dante cannot return to the living world, the spirit reveals he is Guido da Montefeltro. He recounts his life as a soldier and later a Franciscan friar. He explains that Pope Boniface VIII solicited his fraudulent counsel to defeat a rival family, offering absolution in advance for the sin. Upon Guido's death, a demon argued against St. Francis that one cannot be absolved for a sin they intend to commit. The demon then brought Guido to Minos for judgment. After telling his story, the flame departs in torment.

Inferno • Canto XXVII

Già era dritta in sù la fiamma e queta
per non dir più, e già da noi sen gia
con la licenza del dolce poeta,

The flame was already upright and still, no longer speaking, and already gone from us, with the permission of the sweet poet.

quand’ un’altra, che dietro a lei venìa,
ne fece volger li occhi a la sua cima
per un confuso suon che fuor n’uscia.

When another, who came behind her, caused us to turn our eyes to her peak with a confused sound that issued forth.

Come ’l bue cicilian che mugghiò prima
col pianto di colui, e ciò fu dritto,
che l’avea temperato con sua lima,

Like the Sicilian bull that first bellowedwith the wails of its tormentor, and this was just,because he had tempered it with his file,

mugghiava con la voce de l’afflitto,
sì che, con tutto che fosse di rame,
pur el pareva dal dolor trafitto;

it roared with the voice of the afflicted,so that, despite being made of bronze,it seemed pierced through by pain;

così, per non aver via né forame
dal principio nel foco, in suo linguaggio
si convertïan le parole grame.

Thus, since it had neither opening nor ventfrom the start in the fire, its woeful wordswere converted into its own language.

Ma poscia ch’ebber colto lor vïaggio
su per la punta, dandole quel guizzo
che dato avea la lingua in lor passaggio,

Then, after they had finished their journeyup the peak, giving it that flickerthat the tongue had given in their passing,

udimmo dire: «O tu a cu’ io drizzo
la voce e che parlavi mo lombardo,
dicendo “Istra ten va, più non t’adizzo”,

we heard him say: "O you to whom I directmy voice, you who just spoke Lombard,saying 'Get away, I don't want you anymore.'"

perch’ io sia giunto forse alquanto tardo,
non t’incresca restare a parlar meco;
vedi che non incresce a me, e ardo!

Because I may have arrived somewhat late, don't begrudge staying to speak with me; see that I don't begrudge it, and I burn!

Se tu pur mo in questo mondo cieco
caduto se’ di quella dolce terra
latina ond’ io mia colpa tutta reco,

If you too have fallen into this blind world, having come from that sweet Latin land from which I trace all my fault,

dimmi se Romagnuoli han pace o guerra;
ch’io fui d’i monti là intra Orbino
e ’l giogo di che Tever si diserra».

Tell me if the Romagnuoli have peace or war, for I was from the mountains there between Urbino and the ridge from which the Tiber unlocks itself."

Io era in giuso ancora attento e chino,
quando il mio duca mi tentò di costa,
dicendo: «Parla tu; questi è latino».

I was still bent low and focused, when my guide nudged my side, saying: "You speak; this one is Latin."

E io, ch’avea già pronta la risposta,
sanza indugio a parlare incominciai:
«O anima che se’ là giù nascosta,

And I, who already had my reply prepared, without delay began to speak: "O soul hidden down below,

Romagna tua non è, e non fu mai,
sanza guerra ne’ cuor de’ suoi tiranni;
ma ’n palese nessuna or vi lasciai.

Your Romagna is not, nor has it ever been, without war in the hearts of its tyrants, but openly, I have left none there now.

Ravenna sta come stata è molt’ anni:
l’aguglia da Polenta la si cova,
sì che Cervia ricuopre co’ suoi vanni.

Ravenna remains as it has been for many years: the eagle of Polenta broods over it, so that it covers Cervia with its wings.

La terra che fé già la lunga prova
e di Franceschi sanguinoso mucchio,
sotto le branche verdi si ritrova.

The land that once endured the long trial, and the bloody heap of Frenchmen, now finds itself beneath the green talons.

E ’l mastin vecchio e ’l nuovo da Verrucchio,
che fecer di Montagna il mal governo,
là dove soglion fan d’i denti succhio.

And the old and new mastiffs from Verrucchio, who made ill government of Montagna, there where they are accustomed to suck the teeth.

Le città di Lamone e di Santerno
conduce il lïoncel dal nido bianco,
che muta parte da la state al verno.

The cities of Lamone and Santernoare led by the cub from the white nest,who changes sides with the seasons.

E quella cu’ il Savio bagna il fianco,
così com’ ella sie’ tra ’l piano e ’l monte,
tra tirannia si vive e stato franco.

And that city whose flank the Savio wets,as it lies between plain and mountain,lives between tyranny and liberty.

Ora chi se’, ti priego che ne conte;
non esser duro più ch’altri sia stato,
se ’l nome tuo nel mondo tegna fronte».

Now, whoever you are, I beg you, tell us. Do not be more harsh than others have been, if you wish your name to endure in the world.”

Poscia che ’l foco alquanto ebbe rugghiato
al modo suo, l’aguta punta mosse
di qua, di là, e poi diè cotal fiato:

After the fire had roared a bitin its own fashion, the sharp tip movedthis way, that way, and then gave forth this breath:

«S’i’ credesse che mia risposta fosse
a persona che mai tornasse al mondo,
questa fiamma staria sanza più scosse;

ma perché giammai di questo fondonon tornò vivo alcun, s’i’odo il vero,sanza tema d’infamia rispondo.»

ma però che già mai di questo fondo
non tornò vivo alcun, s’i’ odo il vero,
sanza tema d’infamia ti rispondo.

But since no one, if I hear correctly,ever returned alive from this depth,I answer you without fear of disgrace.

Io fui uom d’arme, e poi fui cordigliero,
credendomi, sì cinto, fare ammenda;
e certo il creder mio venìa intero,

I was a man of arms, and then I was a friar of the Cordeliers, believing that, so girded, I would atone; and surely my belief came to be fulfilled,

se non fosse il gran prete, a cui mal prenda!,
che mi rimise ne le prime colpe;
e come e quare, voglio che m’intenda.

were it not for the great priest—may ill befall him!—who thrust me back into my former sins;and how and why, I want you to understand.

Mentre ch’io forma fui d’ossa e di polpe
che la madre mi diè, l’opere mie
non furon leonine, ma di volpe.

While I was still made of bone and flesh, which my mother gave to me, my deeds were not those of a lion, but of a fox.

Li accorgimenti e le coperte vie
io seppi tutte, e sì menai lor arte,
ch’al fine de la terra il suono uscie.

I knew all the stratagems and secret paths, and I used their art so well that their sound went out to the ends of the earth.

Quando mi vidi giunto in quella parte
di mia etade ove ciascun dovrebbe
calar le vele e raccoglier le sarte,

When I found myself arrived at that point in my life, where everyone should lower their sails and gather the ropes,

ciò che pria mi piacëa, allor m’increbbe,
e pentuto e confesso mi rendei;
ahi miser lasso! e giovato sarebbe.

What had once pleased me now displeased me, and I gave myself up, remorseful and confessed. Alas, miserable wretch! It would have done some good.

Lo principe d’i novi Farisei,
avendo guerra presso a Laterano,
e non con Saracin né con Giudei,

The prince of the new Pharisees, waging war near the Lateran, but not against Saracens nor Jews,

ché ciascun suo nimico era cristiano,
e nessun era stato a vincer Acri
né mercatante in terra di Soldano,

for every foe of his was Christian, and none had been to conquer Acre nor a merchant in the Soldan’s land,

né sommo officio né ordini sacri
guardò in sé, né in me quel capestro
che solea fare i suoi cinti più macri.

Neither highest office nor holy orders did he hold in esteem, nor in me that rope that used to make his loins more lean.

Ma come Costantin chiese Silvestro
d’entro Siratti a guerir de la lebbre,
così mi chiese questi per maestro

But as Constantine sought Sylvesterto cure him of leprosy from within Soracte,so did this one ask me as his master.

a guerir de la sua superba febbre;
domandommi consiglio, e io tacetti
perché le sue parole parver ebbre.

to cure him of his proud fever;he asked for my counsel, and I kept silentbecause his words seemed intoxicated.

E’ poi ridisse: “Tuo cuor non sospetti;
finor t’assolvo, e tu m’insegna fare
sì come Penestrino in terra getti.

And then he spoke again: "Have no doubt in your heart; I absolve you now, and you must show me how to cast Penestrino down upon the earth.

Lo ciel poss’ io serrare e diserrare,
come tu sai; però son due le chiavi
che ’l mio antecessor non ebbe care”.

I can lock and unlock Heaven, as you know. Therefore, there are two keys that my predecessor did not value.

Allor mi pinser li argomenti gravi
là ’ve ’l tacer mi fu avviso ’l peggio,
e dissi: “Padre, da che tu mi lavi

Then heavy arguments assailed me, where silence seemed the worse course, and I said: “Father, since you cleanse me

di quel peccato ov’ io mo cader deggio,
lunga promessa con l’attender corto
ti farà trïunfar ne l’alto seggio”.

of that sin into which I must now fall, a long promise with a short wait will make you triumph in the high seat.”

Francesco venne poi, com’ io fu’ morto,
per me; ma un d’i neri cherubini
li disse: “Non portar: non mi far torto.

Francesco came later, after I was dead, for me; but one of the black cherubim said to him: "Don't carry him; don't do me wrong."

Venir se ne dee giù tra ’ miei meschini
perché diede ’l consiglio frodolente,
dal quale in qua stato li sono a’ crini;

He must come down among my wretched ones, because he gave the fraudulent counsel, since which time I have been at his hair.

ch’assolver non si può chi non si pente,
né pentere e volere insieme puossi
per la contradizion che nol consente”.

"One cannot be absolved who does not repent, nor can one repent and will at once, because of the contradiction that forbids it."

Oh me dolente! come mi riscossi
quando mi prese dicendomi: “Forse
tu non pensavi ch’io löico fossi!”.

Oh, how pained I was! How I recoiled when he seized me, saying: "Perhaps you didn't think I was a logician!"

A Minòs mi portò; e quelli attorse
otto volte la coda al dosso duro;
e poi che per gran rabbia la si morse,

Minos brought me there; and he wrappedhis tail eight times around his hardened back;and then, in great fury, he bit it.

disse: “Questi è d’i rei del foco furo”;
per ch’io là dove vedi son perduto,
e sì vestito, andando, mi rancuro».

He said, "This is one of the wicked who were consumed by fire;"and so, where you see me, I am lost,and in this garment, as I go, I lament."

Quand’ elli ebbe ’l suo dir così compiuto,
la fiamma dolorando si partio,
torcendo e dibattendo ’l corno aguto.

When he had finished speaking,the flame departed, writhing in pain,twisting and thrashing its pointed horn.

Noi passamm’ oltre, e io e ’l duca mio,
su per lo scoglio infino in su l’altr’ arco
che cuopre ’l fosso in che si paga il fio

We passed beyond, my guide and I, up over the cliff to the next arch, which covers the ditch where payment is made.

a quei che scommettendo acquistan carco.

To those who, wagering, take on burdens.

Paradiso * Canto VII

Following the departure of the spirit of Justinian, who sings "Hosanna" before rejoining a group of rapidly moving souls, Dante is left with a significant doubt. He is hesitant to voice his question, but Beatrice perceives it in his mind. She identifies his confusion: how could a just punishment (the Crucifixion) be justly avenged by a later act (the destruction of Jerusalem)? Beatrice begins a lengthy discourse to resolve his query. She explains that Adam's original sin condemned humanity. She then describes how the human nature assumed by Christ was justly punished on the cross for this sin. However, since this punishment was also inflicted upon a divine person, the act was an offense that required vengeance. This vengeance, she states, was carried out through the subsequent destruction of the city. Beatrice then discerns a second doubt in Dante's mind regarding why this specific method of redemption was chosen and proceeds to address it.

Paradiso • Canto VII

«Osanna, sanctus Deus sabaòth,
superillustrans claritate tua
felices ignes horum malacòth!».

Hosanna, holy God of hosts,shining above with your brilliancethe blessed fires of these realms!

Così, volgendosi a la nota sua,
fu viso a me cantare essa sustanza,
sopra la qual doppio lume s’addua;

So, turning to its own tune, that essence was seen by me to sing, above which a double light is twinned.

ed essa e l’altre mossero a sua danza,
e quasi velocissime faville
mi si velar di sùbita distanza.

And she and the others began their dance, and like the swiftest sparks, they veiled themselves from me in sudden distance.

Io dubitava e dicea ‘Dille, dille!’
fra me, ‘dille’ dicea, ‘a la mia donna
che mi diseta con le dolci stille’.

I doubted, and within myself I said, "Tell her, tell her!" "Tell her," I said, "to my lady, who quenches my thirst with sweet droplets."

Ma quella reverenza che s’indonna
di tutto me, pur per Be e per ice,
mi richinava come l’uom ch’assonna.

But that reverence that masters all of me, just for love of her, and for the coldness of her gaze, bowed me down, like a man in sleep.

Poco sofferse me cotal Beatrice
e cominciò, raggiandomi d’un riso
tal, che nel foco faria l’uom felice:

Beatrice did not endure my state for long,and she began, beaming at me with a smilethat would make a man happy in a fire:

«Secondo mio infallibile avviso,
come giusta vendetta giustamente
punita fosse, t’ha in pensier miso;

"That, in my infallible opinion,how just vengeance was justlymeted out, has put you in thought;

ma io ti solverò tosto la mente;
e tu ascolta, ché le mie parole
di gran sentenza ti faran presente.

But I will quickly free your mind; and you, listen, for my words will make a great truth clear to you.

Per non soffrire a la virtù che vole
freno a suo prode, quell’ uom che non nacque,
dannando sé, dannò tutta sua prole;

So you won’t suffer for the virtue that would serve as a restraint for him, that man who was never born, damning himself, damned all his progeny.

onde l’umana specie inferma giacque
giù per secoli molti in grande errore,
fin ch’al Verbo di Dio discender piacque

Where the infirm human species lay,down for many centuries in great error,until it pleased the Word of God to descend.

u’ la natura, che dal suo fattore
s’era allungata, unì a sé in persona
con l’atto sol del suo etterno amore.

And nature, which had grown distant from its maker, united itself in one person, through the single act of His eternal love.

Or drizza il viso a quel ch’or si ragiona:
questa natura al suo fattore unita,
qual fu creata, fu sincera e buona;

And now direct your gaze to what I speak of: this nature, united to its maker, was, as it was created, pure and good;

ma per sé stessa pur fu ella sbandita
di paradiso, però che si torse
da via di verità e da sua vita.

But by its own doing, it was banished from paradise, because it turned away from the path of truth and from its life.

La pena dunque che la croce porse
s’a la natura assunta si misura,
nulla già mai sì giustamente morse;

The suffering that the cross then offered,measured against the nature it assumed,never before had bitten so justly.

e così nulla fu di tanta ingiura,
guardando a la persona che sofferse,
in che era contratta tal natura.

And thus nothing was so unjust,considering the person who suffered,in whom such a nature was contracted.

Però d’un atto uscir cose diverse:
ch’a Dio e a’ Giudei piacque una morte;
per lei tremò la terra e ’l ciel s’aperse.

But from a single act, diverse things arose:a death that pleased both God and the Jews;because of it, the earth trembled and the heavens opened.

Non ti dee oramai parer più forte,
quando si dice che giusta vendetta
poscia vengiata fu da giusta corte.

You shouldn't find it strange any longer,when it is said that just vengeancewas later avenged by a just court.

Ma io veggi’ or la tua mente ristretta
di pensiero in pensier dentro ad un nodo,
del qual con gran disio solver s’aspetta.

But I see now your mind constrained,bound from thought to thought within a knot,which you await with great desire to loosen.

Tu dici: “Ben discerno ciò ch’i’ odo;
ma perché Dio volesse, m’è occulto,
a nostra redenzion pur questo modo”.

You say: “I understand well what I hear,but why God willed it so is hidden from me,that our redemption should come about in this way.”

Questo decreto, frate, sta sepulto
a li occhi di ciascuno il cui ingegno
ne la fiamma d’amor non è adulto.

Brother, this decree is buried from the eyes of anyone whose intellect is not made mature in the flame of love.

Veramente, però ch’a questo segno
molto si mira e poco si discerne,
dirò perché tal modo fu più degno.

Indeed, because this sign is much observed but little understood, I will tell why this method was more worthy.

La divina bontà, che da sé sperne
ogne livore, ardendo in sé, sfavilla
sì che dispiega le bellezze etterne.

Divine goodness, which casts off all bitterness, burns within itself and scintillates, thus revealing eternal beauty.

Ciò che da lei sanza mezzo distilla
non ha poi fine, perché non si move
la sua imprenta quand’ ella sigilla.

That which she distills without intermediary has no end, because her imprint does not shift when she seals.

Ciò che da essa sanza mezzo piove
libero è tutto, perché non soggiace
a la virtute de le cose nove.

That which rains directly from her is entirely free, because it is not subject to the power of new things.

Più l’è conforme, e però più le piace;
ché l’ardor santo ch’ogne cosa raggia,
ne la più somigliante è più vivace.

The more it conforms, the more it pleases her; for the holy ardor that radiates through all things is most lively in that which is most similar.

Di tutte queste dote s’avvantaggia
l’umana creatura, e s’una manca,
di sua nobilità convien che caggia.

Of all these virtues, the human creature takes advantage, and if one is missing, it must fall from its nobility.

Solo il peccato è quel che la disfranca
e falla dissimìle al sommo bene,
per che del lume suo poco s’imbianca;

Only sin is what sets her free,And makes her unlike the highest good,So that she barely whitens with its light.

e in sua dignità mai non rivene,
se non rïempie, dove colpa vòta,
contra mal dilettar con giuste pene.

And never returns to her dignity,Unless she fills, where guilt has emptied,Against evil delight with just punishments.

Vostra natura, quando peccò tota
nel seme suo, da queste dignitadi,
come di paradiso, fu remota;

Your nature, when it wholly sinnedin its seed, was removed from these dignities,as from paradise.

né ricovrar potiensi, se tu badi
ben sottilmente, per alcuna via,
sanza passar per un di questi guadi:

nor could you be restored, if you considerclosely, by any means,without traversing one of these fords.

o che Dio solo per sua cortesia
dimesso avesse, o che l’uom per sé isso
avesse sodisfatto a sua follia.

Or that God, in His mercy alone, had remitted it, or that man himself, through his own actions, had atoned for his folly.

Ficca mo l’occhio per entro l’abisso
de l’etterno consiglio, quanto puoi
al mio parlar distrettamente fisso.

Now fix your eye into the abyssof the eternal counsel, as much as you can,firmly fixed on my words.

Non potea l’uomo ne’ termini suoi
mai sodisfar, per non potere ir giuso
con umiltate obedïendo poi,

Man could not satisfy himself within his bounds, for he could not descend, with humility, obeying then.

quanto disobediendo intese ir suso;
e questa è la cagion per che l’uom fue
da poter sodisfar per sé dischiuso.

Thus, disobeying, he intended to rise; and this is the cause for which man was shut off from the power to satisfy himself.

Dunque a Dio convenia con le vie sue
riparar l’omo a sua intera vita,
dico con l’una, o ver con amendue.

So, God had to use His waysto restore humanity to its full life,I say with one, or rather, with both.

Ma perché l’ovra tanto è più gradita
da l’operante, quanto più appresenta
de la bontà del core ond’ ell’ è uscita,

Because the work is more pleasing to the one who does it, the more it expresses the goodness of the heart from which it came.

la divina bontà che ’l mondo imprenta,
di proceder per tutte le sue vie,
a rilevarvi suso, fu contenta.

The divine goodness that stamps the world, was pleased to proceed along all its paths, to raise you up.

Né tra l’ultima notte e ’l primo die
sì alto o sì magnifico processo,
o per l’una o per l’altra, fu o fie:

Born between the final night and the first day, so lofty or so magnificent a process, whether through the one or the other, was or will be.

ché più largo fu Dio a dar sé stesso
per far l’uom sufficiente a rilevarsi,
che s’elli avesse sol da sé dimesso;

for God was more generous in giving Himselfto make man capable of redemption,than if He had simply abandoned Himself.

e tutti li altri modi erano scarsi
a la giustizia, se ’l Figliuol di Dio
non fosse umilïato ad incarnarsi.

And all other means were insufficientto justice, had the Son of Godnot humbled Himself to incarnate.

Or per empierti bene ogne disio,
ritorno a dichiararti in alcun loco,
perché tu veggi lì così com’ io.

Or, that every longing may be filled with good,I return to make it clear to you in some place,So that you may see it as I have.

Tu dici: “Io veggio l’acqua, io veggio il foco,
l’aere e la terra e tutte lor misture
venire a corruzione, e durar poco;

You say, "I see the water, I see the fire,the air and earth and all their mixturescoming to corruption, and enduring little;

e queste cose pur furon creature;
per che, se ciò ch’è detto è stato vero,
esser dovrien da corruzion sicure”.

And these things, too, were once creations;Thus, if what has been said is true,they ought to be safe from corruption.”

Li angeli, frate, e ’l paese sincero
nel qual tu se’, dir si posson creati,
sì come sono, in loro essere intero;

The angels, brother, and the pure landin which you dwell can be called creations,as they are, in their complete being.

ma li alimenti che tu hai nomati
e quelle cose che di lor si fanno
da creata virtù sono informati.

But the nourishment you have named,and those things that are made from it,are informed by a created power.

Creata fu la materia ch’elli hanno;
creata fu la virtù informante
in queste stelle che ’ntorno a lor vanno.

The substance they possess was created; the informing virtue in these stars that move around them was created.

L’anima d’ogne bruto e de le piante
di complession potenzïata tira
lo raggio e ’l moto de le luci sante;

The soul of every beast and plant, empowered by its constitution, draws the light and movement of the holy stars.

ma vostra vita sanza mezzo spira
la somma beninanza, e la innamora
di sé sì che poi sempre la disira.

but your life, without intermediary, breathessupreme goodness, and makes it enamoredof itself so that it always desires it.

E quinci puoi argomentare ancora
vostra resurrezion, se tu ripensi
come l’umana carne fessi allora

And from this, you can further deduceyour resurrection, if you considerhow human flesh was made then.

che li primi parenti intrambo fensi».

that the first parents fashioned themselves."

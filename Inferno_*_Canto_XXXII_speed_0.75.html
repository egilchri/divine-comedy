Inferno * Canto XXXII

Dante and Virgil descend into the ninth circle of Hell, a vast frozen lake named Cocytus. In the first ring, Caina, they find the souls of traitors to their kin submerged in ice up to their necks. Dante observes two shades frozen together, butting their heads in rage after their own tears freeze their eyes shut. A nearby sinner, Camiscion de' Pazzi, identifies them and others. The poets proceed into the second ring, Antenora, which holds traitors to their country. Here, Dante accidentally kicks the face of a sinner. When the soul refuses to give his name, Dante grabs his hair. A neighboring sinner then identifies the shade as Bocca degli Abati. Angered by this, Bocca names several other traitors frozen around him. Moving on, Dante sees a final sight: one sinner gnawing on the head of another trapped in the same hole.

Inferno • Canto XXXII

S’ïo avessi le rime aspre e chiocce,
come si converrebbe al tristo buco
sovra ’l qual pontan tutte l’altre rocce,

If I had verses rough and grating,as would befit the wretched holeover which all other cliffs lean,

io premerei di mio concetto il suco
più pienamente; ma perch’ io non l’abbo,
non sanza tema a dicer mi conduco;

I would press the juice of my own thoughtmore fully; but because I do not,I speak, not without fear, as I am brought.

ché non è impresa da pigliare a gabbo
discriver fondo a tutto l’universo,
né da lingua che chiami mamma o babbo.

because it is no task to be mocked,to describe the depths of all the universe,nor for a tongue that calls out "mama" or "papa."

Ma quelle donne aiutino il mio verso
ch’aiutaro Anfïone a chiuder Tebe,
sì che dal fatto il dir non sia diverso.

May those women assist my versewho aided Amphion in sealing Thebes,so that the telling does not stray from the deed.

Oh sovra tutte mal creata plebe
che stai nel loco onde parlare è duro,
mei foste state qui pecore o zebe!

Oh, you wretched rabble, more vile than all,who dwell in the place where speech is pain,better had you been sheep or goats, every one!

Come noi fummo giù nel pozzo scuro
sotto i piè del gigante assai più bassi,
e io mirava ancora a l’alto muro,

When we were down in the dark pit,far below the giant's feet,I was still gazing at the high wall.

dicere udi’mi: «Guarda come passi:
va sì, che tu non calchi con le piante
le teste de’ fratei miseri lassi».

Then he said to me: “Watch how you tread: go carefully, lest you step with your feet on the heads of your wretched, weary brothers.”

Per ch’io mi volsi, e vidimi davante
e sotto i piedi un lago che per gelo
avea di vetro e non d’acqua sembiante.

Because I turned, and I saw before me, and under my feet, a lake, which, by reason of ice, had the appearance of glass, and not of water.

Non fece al corso suo sì grosso velo
di verno la Danoia in Osterlicchi,
né Tanaï là sotto ’l freddo cielo,

Non drew winter's cloak so thick,over its course, the Danube in Austria,nor the Tanais under that cold sky.

com’ era quivi; che se Tambernicchi
vi fosse sù caduto, o Pietrapana,
non avria pur da l’orlo fatto cricchi.

As it was there; for if Mount Tamborniccoor Pietrapana had fallen from above,it would not have made even a crack at the edge.

E come a gracidar si sta la rana
col muso fuor de l’acqua, quando sogna
di spigolar sovente la villana,

And as the frog sits croaking,with its snout above the water, dreamingof the peasant woman's frequent gleaning,

livide, insin là dove appar vergogna
eran l’ombre dolenti ne la ghiaccia,
mettendo i denti in nota di cicogna.

Livid, those shades of torment in the ice, where shame was manifest, gnawed with their teeth, a chattering like storks.

Ognuna in giù tenea volta la faccia;
da bocca il freddo, e da li occhi il cor tristo
tra lor testimonianza si procaccia.

Each held their face downward; from their mouths, the cold, and from their eyes, their sorrow, sought to bear witness among them.

Quand’ io m’ebbi dintorno alquanto visto,
volsimi a’ piedi, e vidi due sì stretti,
che ’l pel del capo avieno insieme misto.

When I had looked about me for a bit, I turned towards my feet and saw two souls so tightly bound that the hair on their heads was all tangled together.

«Ditemi, voi che sì strignete i petti»,
diss’ io, «chi siete?». E quei piegaro i colli;
e poi ch’ebber li visi a me eretti,

"Tell me, you who press yourselves together," I said, "who are you?" And they bent their necks; and after they lifted their faces toward me,

li occhi lor, ch’eran pria pur dentro molli,
gocciar su per le labbra, e ’l gelo strinse
le lagrime tra essi e riserrolli.

Their eyes, which had been only wet within, dripped upon their lips, and the cold gripped the tears between them and sealed them shut.

Con legno legno spranga mai non cinse
forte così; ond’ ei come due becchi
cozzaro insieme, tanta ira li vinse.

Never did wood and wood bind so tightly with a bar; thus, like two beaks, they clashed together, so much anger overcame them.

E un ch’avea perduti ambo li orecchi
per la freddura, pur col viso in giùe,
disse: «Perché cotanto in noi ti specchi?

And one who had lost both his earsbecause of the cold, even with his face down,said: "Why do you stare at us so intently?"

Se vuoi saper chi son cotesti due,
la valle onde Bisenzo si dichina
del padre loro Alberto e di lor fue.

If you wish to know who those two are, the valley where the Bisenzio slopes down belonged to their father, Alberto, and to them.

D’un corpo usciro; e tutta la Caina
potrai cercare, e non troverai ombra
degna più d’esser fitta in gelatina:

From a single body they came forth; and you may search all of Caina, and you will not find a shade more worthy of being fixed in ice.

non quelli a cui fu rotto il petto e l’ombra
con esso un colpo per la man d’Artù;
non Focaccia; non questi che m’ingombra

Not those whose chests were broken, and whose shadows,Shattered with them, by Arthur's hand's harsh blow;Not Focaccia; not these that now encumber.

col capo sì, ch’i’ non veggio oltre più,
e fu nomato Sassol Mascheroni;
se tosco se’, ben sai omai chi fu.

Upon the head, so that I see no further, and he was called Sassol Mascheroni; if you are Tuscan, you surely know who he was.

E perché non mi metti in più sermoni,
sappi ch’i’ fu’ il Camiscion de’ Pazzi;
e aspetto Carlin che mi scagioni».

And so you won't make me ramble further, know that I was Camiscion de' Pazzi; I await Carlin to exonerate me."

Poscia vid’ io mille visi cagnazzi
fatti per freddo; onde mi vien riprezzo,
e verrà sempre, de’ gelati guazzi.

Then I saw a thousand faces contorted, twisted by the cold; this gives me such revulsion, and always will, for the frozen bogs.

E mentre ch’andavamo inver’ lo mezzo
al quale ogne gravezza si rauna,
e io tremava ne l’etterno rezzo;

And while we went towards the center, where all weight gathers, and I trembled in the eternal shade.

se voler fu o destino o fortuna,
non so; ma, passeggiando tra le teste,
forte percossi ’l piè nel viso ad una.

If it was will, or destiny, or fortune, I do not know; but, walking among the heads, I struck my foot hard against one's face.

Piangendo mi sgridò: «Perché mi peste?
se tu non vieni a crescer la vendetta
di Montaperti, perché mi moleste?».

Weeping, it scolded me: "Why do you trample me? If you haven't come to increase the vengeance of Montaperti, why do you torment me?"

E io: «Maestro mio, or qui m’aspetta,
sì ch’io esca d’un dubbio per costui;
poi mi farai, quantunque vorrai, fretta».

And I: "My master, wait here for me, so that I might resolve a doubt concerning him; then you may hurry me as much as you like."

Lo duca stette, e io dissi a colui
che bestemmiava duramente ancora:
«Qual se’ tu che così rampogni altrui?».

The guide stopped, and I said to the shadewho was still cursing violently:"Who are you, to rail so at the others?"

«Or tu chi se’ che vai per l’Antenora,
percotendo», rispuose, «altrui le gote,
sì che, se fossi vivo, troppo fora?».

"And you, who are you, to be going through Antenora, striking the faces of others, so that if you were alive, it would be too much?" he replied.

«Vivo son io, e caro esser ti puote»,
fu mia risposta, «se dimandi fama,
ch’io metta il nome tuo tra l’altre note».

"I am alive, and it may be dear to you," I replied, "if you ask for fame, that I put your name among the other notes."

Ed elli a me: «Del contrario ho io brama.
Lèvati quinci e non mi dar più lagna,
ché mal sai lusingar per questa lama!».

And he to me: "I desire the opposite. Get away from here and don't bother me anymore, for you don't know how to flatter with this blade!"

Allor lo presi per la cuticagna
e dissi: «El converrà che tu ti nomi,
o che capel qui sù non ti rimagna».

Then I grabbed him by the scruff of the neck and said: "You're going to tell me your name, or you won't have a hair left on your head."

Ond’ elli a me: «Perché tu mi dischiomi,
né ti dirò ch’io sia, né mosterrolti,
se mille fiate in sul capo mi tomi».

And he said to me: "Even if you tear me apart, I won't tell you who I am, nor will I reveal myself, even if you strike me on the head a thousand times."

Io avea già i capelli in mano avvolti,
e tratti glien’ avea più d’una ciocca,
latrando lui con li occhi in giù raccolti,

I already had his hair in my hand, twisted, and I had torn out more than one lock, as he howled, eyes downcast.

quando un altro gridò: «Che hai tu, Bocca?
non ti basta sonar con le mascelle,
se tu non latri? qual diavol ti tocca?».

When another one cried, "What's wrong with you, Bocca? Isn't it enough to make noise with your jaws unless you also bark? What devil is possessing you?"

«Omai», diss’ io, «non vo’ che più favelle,
malvagio traditor; ch’a la tua onta
io porterò di te vere novelle».

"Enough," I said, "I don't want you to speak anymore, wicked traitor; I will take true news of you to your shame."

«Va via», rispuose, «e ciò che tu vuoi conta;
ma non tacer, se tu di qua entro eschi,
di quel ch’ebbe or così la lingua pronta.

"Go away," he answered, "and say what you will; but do not be silent, once you leave here, about what had his tongue so ready just now."

El piange qui l’argento de’ Franceschi:
“Io vidi”, potrai dir, “quel da Duera
là dove i peccatori stanno freschi”.

Here, he weeps for the silver of the French:"I saw," you will be able to say, "that man from Duerathere where the sinners stay fresh."

Se fossi domandato “Altri chi v’era?”,
tu hai dallato quel di Beccheria
di cui segò Fiorenza la gorgiera.

If you were asked, "Who else was there?"you would have seen that man from Beccheria,whose throat Florence slit.

Gianni de’ Soldanier credo che sia
più là con Ganellone e Tebaldello,
ch’aprì Faenza quando si dormia».

I believe Gianni de' Soldanieri is there,with Ganelon and Tebaldello,who betrayed Faenza while it slept.

Noi eravam partiti già da ello,
ch’io vidi due ghiacciati in una buca,
sì che l’un capo a l’altro era cappello;

We had already moved away from him, when I saw two frozen in one hole, so that one's head served as a cap for the other.

e come ’l pan per fame si manduca,
così ’l sovran li denti a l’altro pose
là ’ve ’l cervel s’aggiugne con la nuca:

And as bread is devoured by hunger, so the other gnawed at the one's head, where the brain joins the nape of the neck.

non altrimenti Tidëo si rose
le tempie a Menalippo per disdegno,
che quei faceva il teschio e l’altre cose.

No differently did Tydeus gnaw the temples of Menalippus, fueled by outrage, than the other did the skull and the other parts.

«O tu che mostri per sì bestial segno
odio sovra colui che tu ti mangi,
dimmi ’l perché», diss’ io, «per tal convegno,

"O you who display such bestial signs of hate towards the one you devour, tell me why," I said, "in such a circumstance..."

che se tu a ragion di lui ti piangi,
sappiendo chi voi siete e la sua pecca,
nel mondo suso ancora io te ne cangi,

that if you weep for him with good cause, knowing who you are and his sin, I will yet exchange him for you in the upper world.

se quella con ch’io parlo non si secca».

se quella con cui io parlo non si asciuga».

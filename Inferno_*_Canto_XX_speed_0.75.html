Inferno * Canto XX

In the fourth chasm of the eighth circle, Dante Alighieri and Virgil observe a new group of souls. These sinners have their heads twisted completely backward on their necks, forcing them to walk in reverse while their tears fall down their backs. Dante is overcome with pity and begins to weep, but Virgil rebukes him for this emotional response. Virgil then identifies several of the figures. He points to Amphiaraus, Tiresias, and the Etruscan soothsayer Arruns. He provides a detailed account of the "savage virgin" Manto, explaining her travels and how the city of Mantua was founded at the site of her burial. Virgil also identifies the Greek augur Eurypylus, as well as the more contemporary figures of Michael Scot, Guido Bonatti, and Asdente. Noting that the moon is now setting, Virgil tells Dante that they must continue their journey.

Inferno • Canto XX

Di nova pena mi conven far versi
e dar matera al ventesimo canto
de la prima canzon, ch’è d’i sommersi.

Of a fresh torment I must now make verse,and give the matter to the twentieth songof the first song, which is of the submerged.

Io era già disposto tutto quanto
a riguardar ne lo scoperto fondo,
che si bagnava d’angoscioso pianto;

I was already completely prepared to gaze upon the revealed depths, which were soaked with anguished weeping.

e vidi gente per lo vallon tondo
venir, tacendo e lagrimando, al passo
che fanno le letane in questo mondo.

And I saw people coming through the round valley, silent and weeping, at the pace of litanies in this world.

Come ’l viso mi scese in lor più basso,
mirabilmente apparve esser travolto
ciascun tra ’l mento e ’l principio del casso,

As my gaze fell lower upon them, it seemed, remarkably, that each one was twisted between the chin and the start of the chest.

ché da le reni era tornato ’l volto,
e in dietro venir li convenia,
perché ’l veder dinanzi era lor tolto.

For they had turned their faces back from their lower backs, and they were compelled to come that way, since their sight of what lay ahead was stolen from them.

ma io nol vidi, né credo che sia.

Surely I wept, leaning against one of the cragsof the hard stone, so that my guidesaid to me: "Are you still one of the other fools?"

Who feels pity when pity itself is dead? Who is more wicked than one who brings passion to divine judgment?

Behold him, for he has made a breast of his back; because he wished to see too far ahead, he looks behind and makes his way backward.

See Tiresias, who changed his appearancewhen from male, he became female,altering all his limbs completely;

And first, then, he had to beat againthe two snakes entwined, with the staff,so that he could regain his masculine form.

Aronta è quel ch’al ventre li s’atterga,
che ne’ monti di Luni, dove ronca
lo Carrarese che di sotto alberga,

Aronta is he who lies in wait against the belly, who, in the mountains of Luni, where the Carrara man, who dwells below, hoots.

ebbe tra ’ bianchi marmi la spelonca
per sua dimora; onde a guardar le stelle
e ’l mar non li era la veduta tronca.

He had, among the white marbles, a cavern for his dwelling. From there, his view of the stars and the sea was not obstructed.

E quella che ricuopre le mammelle,
che tu non vedi, con le trecce sciolte,
e ha di là ogne pilosa pelle,

And she who covers her breasts,Which you do not see, with unbound tresses,And has all the downy skin beyond,

Manto fu, che cercò per terre molte;
poscia si puose là dove nacqu’ io;
onde un poco mi piace che m’ascolte.

She was a woman who sought across many lands; then she settled where I was born; for that reason, I am pleased that you listen to me.

Poscia che ’l padre suo di vita uscìo
e venne serva la città di Baco,
questa gran tempo per lo mondo gio.

After her father departed from life, and the city of Thebes became a servant of Bacchus, she wandered the world for a long time.

Suso in Italia bella giace un laco,
a piè de l’Alpe che serra Lamagna
sovra Tiralli, c’ha nome Benaco.

In beautiful Italy lies a lake, at the foot of the Alps that enclose Germany, above Tyrol, which is named Benaco.

Per mille fonti, credo, e più si bagna
tra Garda e Val Camonica e Pennino
de l’acqua che nel detto laco stagna.

By a thousand springs, I believe, and more, it is fedbetween Garda and Val Camonica and Penninowith water that stagnates in the said lake.

Loco è nel mezzo là dove ’l trentino
pastore e quel di Brescia e ’l veronese
segnar poria, s’e’ fesse quel cammino.

The place is in the middle, there where the Trentinoshepherd and those of Brescia and Veronacould mark its location, if they took that path.

Siede Peschiera, bello e forte arnese
da fronteggiar Bresciani e Bergamaschi,
ove la riva ’ntorno più discese.

Peschiera sits, a beautiful and strong fortress,ready to face the people of Brescia and Bergamo,where the shore descends the most around it.

Ivi convien che tutto quanto caschi
ciò che ’n grembo a Benaco star non può,
e fassi fiume giù per verdi paschi.

There, it is necessary that all things fallthat cannot remain in Benaco's embrace,and become a river down through green pastures.

Tosto che l’acqua a correr mette co,
non più Benaco, ma Mencio si chiama
fino a Governol, dove cade in Po.

As soon as the water begins to flow, it is no longer called Benaco, but Mincio, as far as Governol, where it empties into the Po.

Non molto ha corso, ch’el trova una lama,
ne la qual si distende e la ’mpaluda;
e suol di state talor essere grama.

It hasn't run much further when it finds a stream, into which it spreads and turns into a marsh; and it is sometimes troublesome in summer.

Quindi passando la vergine cruda
vide terra, nel mezzo del pantano,
sanza coltura e d’abitanti nuda.

Then passing, the crude virginsaw land, in the middle of the marsh,uncultivated and devoid of inhabitants.

Lì, per fuggire ogne consorzio umano,
ristette con suoi servi a far sue arti,
e visse, e vi lasciò suo corpo vano.

There, to flee all human company, he stayed with his servants to practice his arts, and lived, and left his vain body there.

Li uomini poi che ’ntorno erano sparti
s’accolsero a quel loco, ch’era forte
per lo pantan ch’avea da tutte parti.

The men, once scattered about, gathered at that place, which was fortified by the swamp that was on all sides.

Fer la città sovra quell’ ossa morte;
e per colei che ’l loco prima elesse,
Mantüa l’appellar sanz’ altra sorte.

To build the city upon those dead bones; and by her who first chose the place, they named it Mantua, without another lot.

Già fuor le genti sue dentro più spesse,
prima che la mattia da Casalodi
da Pinamonte inganno ricevesse.

Her people were once more numerous within, before Casalodi's madness received deceit from Pinamonte.

Però t’assenno che, se tu mai odi
originar la mia terra altrimenti,
la verità nulla menzogna frodi».

But I warn you, if ever you hear my homeland spoken of otherwise, let truth not be deceived by any falsehood.

E io: «Maestro, i tuoi ragionamenti
mi son sì certi e prendon sì mia fede,
che li altri mi sarien carboni spenti.

And I: "Master, your reasonings are so certain and take such hold of my faith that all others would be extinguished coals to me."

Ma dimmi, de la gente che procede,
se tu ne vedi alcun degno di nota;
ché solo a ciò la mia mente rifiede».

And tell me, of the people who proceed, if you see any worthy of note; for my mind concerns itself with only this."

Allor mi disse: «Quel che da la gota
porge la barba in su le spalle brune,
fu—quando Grecia fu di maschi vòta,

«That one,» he said to me, «whose beard hangs down to his dark shoulders, was—when Greece was emptied of men,

sì ch’a pena rimaser per le cune—
augure, e diede ’l punto con Calcanta
in Aulide a tagliar la prima fune.

so that scarcely any were left in their cradles—a soothsayer, and with Calchas he marked the time in Aulis to sever the first rope.

Euripilo ebbe nome, e così ’l canta
l’alta mia tragedìa in alcun loco:
ben lo sai tu che la sai tutta quanta.

Eurypylus was his name, and so my lofty tragedy sings of him in a certain place: you know this well, as you know the whole of it.

Quell’ altro che ne’ fianchi è così poco,
Michele Scotto fu, che veramente
de le magiche frode seppe ’l gioco.

The other one, whose flanks are so meager, was Michele Scotto, who truly knew the game of magical fraud.

Vedi Guido Bonatti; vedi Asdente,
ch’avere inteso al cuoio e a lo spago
ora vorrebbe, ma tardi si pente.

Behold Guido Bonatti; behold Asdente, who now wishes he had stuck to leather and thread, but it's too late, he repents.

Vedi le triste che lasciaron l’ago,
la spuola e ’l fuso, e fecersi ’ndivine;
fecer malie con erbe e con imago.

Observe the wretched women who forsook the needle,the shuttle, and the spindle, and became diviners;they worked their spells with herbs and with effigies.

Ma vienne omai, ché già tiene ’l confine
d’amendue li emisperi e tocca l’onda
sotto Sobilia Caino e le spine;

Now may it come, for it already holds the borderof both hemispheres and touches the wavebeneath Seville Cain and the thorns;

e già iernotte fu la luna tonda:
ben ten de’ ricordar, ché non ti nocque
alcuna volta per la selva fonda».

And last night the moon was round already:you should remember well, for it did not harm youany time in the deep forest.

Sì mi parlava, e andavamo introcque.

Yes, he spoke to me, and we went on then.

Purgatorio * Canto XXVII

As the sun sets, an angel on the final terrace of Purgatory informs Dante, Virgil, and Statius that they must pass through a wall of fire to proceed. Dante is overcome with terror and refuses to move. Virgil attempts to reason with him, but Dante only agrees to enter the flames after Virgil tells him that Beatrice is on the other side. Virgil leads the way into the fire, with Dante following and Statius behind. On the other side, a second angel directs them to a stairway as night falls. The ascent is halted by the darkness, so the three poets lie down on the steps to rest. While Virgil and Statius sleep, Dante dreams of a young woman named Leah, who is gathering flowers in a field. At dawn, Dante awakens. Virgil informs him that his journey under guidance is complete, declaring Dante's will to be free and making him lord of himself.

Purgatorio • Canto XXVII

Sì come quando i primi raggi vibra
là dove il suo fattor lo sangue sparse,
cadendo Ibero sotto l’alta Libra,

Just as when the first rays vibrate,where his maker spilled his blood,falling, Ibero, beneath the high Scales,

e l’onde in Gange da nona rïarse,
sì stava il sole; onde ’l giorno sen giva,
come l’angel di Dio lieto ci apparse.

and the waves in the Ganges were not yet reddened by noon, so stood the sun; whence the day was departing, when the angel of God appeared to us, happy.

Fuor de la fiamma stava in su la riva,
e cantava ‘Beati mundo corde!’
in voce assai più che la nostra viva.

Outside the flame, he stood upon the shore, and sang, "Blessed are the pure of heart!" in a voice far more vibrant than our own.

Poscia «Più non si va, se pria non morde,
anime sante, il foco: intrate in esso,
e al cantar di là non siate sorde»,

Then, "No further passage is granted, unless the fire first bites, holy souls: enter into it, and do not be deaf to the song beyond."

ci disse come noi li fummo presso;
per ch’io divenni tal, quando lo ’ntesi,
qual è colui che ne la fossa è messo.

He told us how we came near them; at which, when I understood, I became like one who is placed in the tomb.

In su le man commesse mi protesi,
guardando il foco e imaginando forte
umani corpi già veduti accesi.

Upon my clasped hands I leaned, gazing at the fire, and vividly imagining human bodies consumed by flame.

Volsersi verso me le buone scorte;
e Virgilio mi disse: «Figliuol mio,
qui può esser tormento, ma non morte.

Turning toward me, the good spirits approached; and Virgil said, “My son, here may be torment, but not death.”

Ricorditi, ricorditi! E se io
sovresso Gerïon ti guidai salvo,
che farò ora presso più a Dio?

Remember, remember! And if I guided you safely above Geryon, what will I do now, closer to God?

Credi per certo che se dentro a l’alvo
di questa fiamma stessi ben mille anni,
non ti potrebbe far d’un capel calvo.

Believe for certain that if, inside the belly of this flame, you remained a thousand years, it could not make a single hair on your head turn white.

E se tu forse credi ch’io t’inganni,
fatti ver’ lei, e fatti far credenza
con le tue mani al lembo d’i tuoi panni.

And if perhaps you believe I deceive you, go to her, and with your own hands, feel the edge of your clothes, and see.

Pon giù omai, pon giù ogne temenza;
volgiti in qua e vieni: entra sicuro!».
E io pur fermo e contra coscïenza.

Lay aside now, lay aside all fear; turn this way and come: enter without worry!” And yet I stood firm, against my conscience.

Quando mi vide star pur fermo e duro,
turbato un poco disse: «Or vedi, figlio:
tra Bëatrice e te è questo muro».

When he saw me standing there, still firm and unyielding, he said, somewhat troubled: "Now see, my son: between Beatrice and you is this wall."

Come al nome di Tisbe aperse il ciglio
Piramo in su la morte, e riguardolla,
allor che ’l gelso diventò vermiglio;

As Pyramus opened his eyes at the name of Thisbe, at the moment of his death, and looked upon her, when the mulberry tree turned crimson;

così, la mia durezza fatta solla,
mi volsi al savio duca, udendo il nome
che ne la mente sempre mi rampolla.

Thus, my hardness softened, I turned toward the wise leader, hearing the name that always springs up in my mind.

Ond’ ei crollò la fronte e disse: «Come!
volenci star di qua?»; indi sorrise
come al fanciul si fa ch’è vinto al pome.

Then he shook his head and said, "What, you wish to stay here?" Then he smiled, as one does to a child who has been bested by an apple.

Poi dentro al foco innanzi mi si mise,
pregando Stazio che venisse retro,
che pria per lunga strada ci divise.

Then he stepped into the fire before me, pleading with Statius to follow, for the long road had separated us before.

Sì com’ fui dentro, in un bogliente vetro
gittato mi sarei per rinfrescarmi,
tant’ era ivi lo ’ncendio sanza metro.

As soon as I was in, I would have thrown myself into a boiling glass, to cool off, so immeasurable was the burning there.

Lo dolce padre mio, per confortarmi,
pur di Beatrice ragionando andava,
dicendo: «Li occhi suoi già veder parmi».

My sweet father, to comfort me, continued to speak only of Beatrice, saying, "I seem to see her eyes already."

Guidavaci una voce che cantava
di là; e noi, attenti pur a lei,
venimmo fuor là ove si montava.

A voice singing from the other side guided us; and we, intent on it, came out to where the ascent began.

‘Venite, benedicti Patris mei’,
sonò dentro a un lume che lì era,
tal che mi vinse e guardar nol potei.

"Come, blessed of my Father,"sounded from within a light that was there,so bright that it overcame me, and I could not look upon it.

«Lo sol sen va», soggiunse, «e vien la sera;
non v’arrestate, ma studiate il passo,
mentre che l’occidente non si annera».

"The sun descends," he continued, "and evening approaches. Do not linger, but quicken your pace before the west grows dark."

Dritta salia la via per entro ’l sasso
verso tal parte ch’io toglieva i raggi
dinanzi a me del sol ch’era già basso.

Directly upwards went the path, into the stone, towards a direction that took the sun's rays from me, as it was already low.

E di pochi scaglion levammo i saggi,
che ’l sol corcar, per l’ombra che si spense,
sentimmo dietro e io e li miei saggi.

And from a few steps, we raised the wise ones,that the sun was setting, due to the shadow that extinguished,we felt behind, both I and my wise ones.

E pria che ’n tutte le sue parti immense
fosse orizzonte fatto d’uno aspetto,
e notte avesse tutte sue dispense,

And before the horizon, in all its vast expanse, had taken on a single appearance, and before the night had dispensed all of its shadows.

ciascun di noi d’un grado fece letto;
ché la natura del monte ci affranse
la possa del salir più e ’l diletto.

Each of us made a bed of a single degree; for the nature of the mountain took from us the strength and the pleasure of climbing further.

Quali si stanno ruminando manse
le capre, state rapide e proterve
sovra le cime avante che sien pranse,

Like the docile goats that chew,Once swift and obstinate,atop the heights before they dine.

tacite a l’ombra, mentre che ’l sol ferve,
guardate dal pastor, che ’n su la verga
poggiato s’è e lor di posa serve;

Silent in the shade, while the sun blazes, watched over by the shepherd, who leans upon his staff and allows them rest;

e quale il mandrïan che fori alberga,
lungo il pecuglio suo queto pernotta,
guardando perché fiera non lo sperga;

And like the shepherd who camps out,keeping watch through the quiet night over his flock,lest a beast scatter them.

tali eravamo tutti e tre allotta,
io come capra, ed ei come pastori,
fasciati quinci e quindi d’alta grotta.

Such were the three of us then,I like a goat, and they like shepherds,enfolded here and there by a high grotto.

Poco parer potea lì del di fori;
ma, per quel poco, vedea io le stelle
di lor solere e più chiare e maggiori.

Little could be seen there of the outside day; but, through that little, I saw the stars in their usual state, both clearer and larger.

Sì ruminando e sì mirando in quelle,
mi prese il sonno; il sonno che sovente,
anzi che ’l fatto sia, sa le novelle.

Thus ruminating, and gazing upon them, sleep overcame me; sleep that often knows the news before the deed is done.

Ne l’ora, credo, che de l’orïente
prima raggiò nel monte Citerea,
che di foco d’amor par sempre ardente,

Nor at the hour, I believe, when from the eastFirst shone the sun on Cytherea's mount,Which seems forever burning with love's fire,

giovane e bella in sogno mi parea
donna vedere andar per una landa
cogliendo fiori; e cantando dicea:

Young and beautiful, it seemed to me in a dream,I saw a woman walking through a field,gathering flowers; and singing, she said:

«Sappia qualunque il mio nome dimanda
ch’i’ mi son Lia, e vo movendo intorno
le belle mani a farmi una ghirlanda.

Let any who ask my name know that I am Leah, and I go about moving my fair hands to make myself a garland.

Per piacermi a lo specchio, qui m’addorno;
ma mia suora Rachel mai non si smaga
dal suo miraglio, e siede tutto giorno.

To please myself in the mirror, here I adorn myself; but my sister Rachel never moves from hers, and sits there all day.

Ell’ è d’i suoi belli occhi veder vaga
com’ io de l’addornarmi con le mani;
lei lo vedere, e me l’ovrare appaga».

She takes pleasure in gazing at her beautiful eyes, as I do in adorning myself with my hands; her pleasure is in seeing, and mine in the doing.

E già per li splendori antelucani,
che tanto a’ pellegrin surgon più grati,
quanto, tornando, albergan men lontani,

And already, by the pre-dawn splendors, which rise more gratefully to pilgrims, the closer they are, returning, to their home.

le tenebre fuggian da tutti lati,
e ’l sonno mio con esse; ond’ io leva’mi,
veggendo i gran maestri già levati.

The shadows fled from every side, and with them, my sleep. Thus I roused myself, seeing the great masters already risen.

«Quel dolce pome che per tanti rami
cercando va la cura de’ mortali,
oggi porrà in pace le tue fami».

"That sweet fruit, which mortals' care seeks through so many branches, will today appease your hunger."

Virgilio inverso me queste cotali
parole usò; e mai non furo strenne
che fosser di piacere a queste iguali.

Virgil turned to me and spoke these words. Never have gifts been as pleasing as these.

Tanto voler sopra voler mi venne
de l’esser sù, ch’ad ogne passo poi
al volo mi sentia crescer le penne.

So great a will to climb upon the willI had to rise, that with each step I took,I felt my wings grow stronger for the flight.

Come la scala tutta sotto noi
fu corsa e fummo in su ’l grado superno,
in me ficcò Virgilio li occhi suoi,

Once the entire stairway lay beneath us,and we had reached the topmost step,Virgil fixed his eyes upon me.

e disse: «Il temporal foco e l’etterno
veduto hai, figlio; e se’ venuto in parte
dov’ io per me più oltre non discerno.

He said: "You have seen the fire, both temporal and eternal, my son, and you have come to a place where I, by myself, can no longer perceive."

Tratto t’ho qui con ingegno e con arte;
lo tuo piacere omai prendi per duce;
fuor se’ de l’erte vie, fuor se’ de l’arte.

I have brought you here with intellect and skill. Now take your own pleasure as your guide. You are out of the steep paths, out of the art.

Vedi lo sol che ’n fronte ti riluce;
vedi l’erbette, i fiori e li arbuscelli
che qui la terra sol da sé produce.

See the sun that shines on your face; see the grasses, the flowers, and the bushes that the earth here brings forth on its own.

Mentre che vegnan lieti li occhi belli
che, lagrimando, a te venir mi fenno,
seder ti puoi e puoi andar tra elli.

While the lovely eyes that, weeping, brought me to you, appear joyful, you may sit and walk among them.

Non aspettar mio dir più né mio cenno;
libero, dritto e sano è tuo arbitrio,
e fallo fora non fare a suo senno:

Do not await my further words or gestures; your will is free, upright, and sound, and it would be wrong not to act on its counsel.

per ch’io te sovra te corono e mitrio».

for I crown and mitre you over yourself.

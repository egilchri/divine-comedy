Inferno * Canto XXXI

As Dante and Virgil depart the eighth circle, a loud horn blast sounds. Dante initially mistakes a group of giants for the towers of a city. Virgil corrects him, explaining they are seeing the upper bodies of giants who stand in the central pit leading to the ninth circle. They first encounter Nimrod, who speaks a nonsensical language and carries a great horn. Moving along the pit's edge, they see Ephialtes, who is chained for his rebellion against the ancient gods and shakes in rage. Virgil then addresses a third, unchained giant named Antaeus. By praising his past deeds and strength, Virgil persuades Antaeus to aid their descent. The giant complies, taking both poets in his massive hand and carefully lowering them from the precipice down to the frozen surface of Cocytus, the final circle of Hell. After placing them on the ice, Antaeus straightens back to his position.

Inferno • Canto XXXI

Una medesma lingua pria mi morse,
sì che mi tinse l’una e l’altra guancia,
e poi la medicina mi riporse;

A single language first stung me, staining both my cheeks, and then it offered me the cure.

così od’ io che solea far la lancia
d’Achille e del suo padre esser cagione
prima di trista e poi di buona mancia.

So I understand how the spear once used by Achilles, and by his father, could be the cause of woe before it was of fortunate reward.

Noi demmo il dosso al misero vallone
su per la ripa che ’l cinge dintorno,
attraversando sanza alcun sermone.

We turned our backs upon the wretched valley, ascending the bank that enclosed it round, proceeding without exchanging any words.

Quiv’ era men che notte e men che giorno,
sì che ’l viso m’andava innanzi poco;
ma io senti’ sonare un alto corno,

It was less than night, less than day, so that my vision extended only a little ahead; but I heard a loud horn sound.

tanto ch’avrebbe ogne tuon fatto fioco,
che, contra sé la sua via seguitando,
dirizzò li occhi miei tutti ad un loco.

so loud it would have made any thunder seem faint, and, following its own course, it directed my eyes all towards a single place.

Dopo la dolorosa rotta, quando
Carlo Magno perdé la santa gesta,
non sonò sì terribilmente Orlando.

After the grievous rout, whenCharlemagne lost his sacred host,Roland did not sound so terribly.

Poco portäi in là volta la testa,
che me parve veder molte alte torri;
ond’ io: «Maestro, dì, che terra è questa?».

I had barely turned my head in that direction when I seemed to see many tall towers. And so I asked, "Master, tell me, what land is this?"

Ed elli a me: «Però che tu trascorri
per le tenebre troppo da la lungi,
avvien che poi nel maginare abborri.

And he to me: "Because you wander far through the shadows, it happens that you later stumble in your imagining.

Tu vedrai ben, se tu là ti congiungi,
quanto ’l senso s’inganna di lontano;
però alquanto più te stesso pungi».

You will see clearly, if you join them there, how much the senses deceive from a distance; therefore, spur yourself on a bit more."

Poi caramente mi prese per mano
e disse: «Pria che noi siam più avanti,
acciò che ’l fatto men ti paia strano,

Then lovingly he took my hand, and said: "Before we go any further, so that the event may seem less strange to you,

sappi che non son torri, ma giganti,
e son nel pozzo intorno da la ripa
da l’umbilico in giuso tutti quanti».

Know, then, that these are not towers, but giants, and they are in the well around the bank, from the navel down, all of them.

Come quando la nebbia si dissipa,
lo sguardo a poco a poco raffigura
ciò che cela ’l vapor che l’aere stipa,

As when the fog dissipates, the gaze little by little makes out what the vapor, which thickens the air, conceals,

così forando l’aura grossa e scura,
più e più appressando ver’ la sponda,
fuggiemi errore e cresciemi paura;

So piercing the heavy, dark air, drawing ever closer to the shore, error fled, and fear grew in me.

però che, come su la cerchia tonda
Montereggion di torri si corona,
così la proda che ’l pozzo circonda

but as Monteriggioni is crowned with towers upon its round wall, so was the shore that encircled the well.

torreggiavan di mezza la persona
li orribili giganti, cui minaccia
Giove del cielo ancora quando tuona.

The horrible giants towered, from the waist up, and even now, when Jupiter thunders from the sky, they still threaten.

E io scorgeva già d’alcun la faccia,
le spalle e ’l petto e del ventre gran parte,
e per le coste giù ambo le braccia.

And I already saw the face of one,the shoulders and chest and a large part of the belly,and both arms down along the sides.

Natura certo, quando lasciò l’arte
di sì fatti animali, assai fé bene
per tòrre tali essecutori a Marte.

Nature, surely, when she abandoned the artof making such creatures, acted wellto take such executors from Mars.

E s’ella d’elefanti e di balene
non si pente, chi guarda sottilmente,
più giusta e più discreta la ne tene;

And if she doesn't regret the elephants and whales,for those who look closely, she is held to bemore just and more discreet.

ché dove l’argomento de la mente
s’aggiugne al mal volere e a la possa,
nessun riparo vi può far la gente.

Because where the mind's intentjoins with malicious will and might,no defense can humankind provide.

La faccia sua mi parea lunga e grossa
come la pina di San Pietro a Roma,
e a sua proporzione eran l’altre ossa;

His face appeared to me long and large, like the pinecone of Saint Peter's in Rome, and the other bones were in proportion to it.

sì che la ripa, ch’era perizoma
dal mezzo in giù, ne mostrava ben tanto
di sovra, che di giugnere a la chioma

that the bank, which was a loincloth from the waist down, showed so much of what was above that it would have reached the hair

tre Frison s’averien dato mal vanto;
però ch’i’ ne vedea trenta gran palmi
dal loco in giù dov’ omo affibbia ’l manto.

Three Friesians had given themselves over to boast; for I saw them, thirty great palms in measure, from the place down where a man fastens his cloak.

«Raphèl maì amècche zabì almi»,
cominciò a gridar la fiera bocca,
cui non si convenia più dolci salmi.

"Raphèl maì amècche zabì almi," the beastly maw began to cry, to which sweet psalms were no longer fitting.

E ’l duca mio ver’ lui: «Anima sciocca,
tienti col corno, e con quel ti disfoga
quand’ ira o altra passïon ti tocca!

And my guide to him: "Foolish soul, keep to your horn, and vent yourself with that when anger or any other passion touches you!"

Cércati al collo, e troverai la soga
che ’l tien legato, o anima confusa,
e vedi lui che ’l gran petto ti doga».

Seek your neck, and you will find the ropethat binds it fast, o confused soul,and see him who wounds your great breast.

Poi disse a me: «Elli stessi s’accusa;
questi è Nembrotto per lo cui mal coto
pur un linguaggio nel mondo non s’usa.

Then he said to me, "He accuses himself; this is Nimrod, because of whose wicked attempt the world no longer shares a single language."

Lasciànlo stare e non parliamo a vòto;
ché così è a lui ciascun linguaggio
come ’l suo ad altrui, ch’a nullo è noto».

Let him be. Let us not speak in vain, for to him, every language is as his own is to another, which is unknown to all.

Facemmo adunque più lungo vïaggio,
vòlti a sinistra; e al trar d’un balestro
trovammo l’altro assai più fero e maggio.

We therefore journeyed further, turning left. And at the distance of a crossbow shot, we found the next, much more ferocious and imposing.

A cigner lui qual che fosse ’l maestro,
non so io dir, ma el tenea soccinto
dinanzi l’altro e dietro il braccio destro

I cannot say who his teacher was, but he held the other close, his right arm behind him.

d’una catena che ’l tenea avvinto
dal collo in giù, sì che ’n su lo scoperto
si ravvolgëa infino al giro quinto.

...of a chain that bound him,from the neck down, so that it wrapped aroundhis exposed flesh, reaching the fifth circuit.

«Questo superbo volle esser esperto
di sua potenza contra ’l sommo Giove»,
disse ’l mio duca, «ond’ elli ha cotal merto.

"This arrogant man sought to test the limits of his power against the highest Jove," my guide said, "which is why he has earned such a fate."

Fïalte ha nome, e fece le gran prove
quando i giganti fer paura a’ dèi;
le braccia ch’el menò, già mai non move».

His name is Fialte, and he performed great featswhen the giants terrified the gods;the arms he wielded, he no longer moves.

E io a lui: «S’esser puote, io vorrei
che de lo smisurato Brïareo
esperïenza avesser li occhi mei».

And I to him: “If it were possible, I would wishthat my eyes might experiencethe immeasurable Briareus.”

Ond’ ei rispuose: «Tu vedrai Anteo
presso di qui che parla ed è disciolto,
che ne porrà nel fondo d’ogne reo.

And he replied: "You will see Antaeusclose by, who speaks and is unbound,who will place us at the bottom of all wrongdoers."

Quel che tu vuo’ veder, più là è molto
ed è legato e fatto come questo,
salvo che più feroce par nel volto».

What you wish to see is much further on,bound, and made just like this one,except he seems more ferocious in his face.

Non fu tremoto già tanto rubesto,
che scotesse una torre così forte,
come Fïalte a scuotersi fu presto.

Never was there an earthquake so violent,that it shook a tower so strongly,as quickly as Fialte shook himself.

Allor temett’ io più che mai la morte,
e non v’era mestier più che la dotta,
s’io non avessi viste le ritorte.

Then I feared death more than ever, and there was no need to fear it more than the learned, if I had not seen the contortions.

Noi procedemmo più avante allotta,
e venimmo ad Anteo, che ben cinque alle,
sanza la testa, uscia fuor de la grotta.

We proceeded onward then, and came to Antaeus, who, with no head, stuck out from the cave a full five ells.

«O tu che ne la fortunata valle
che fece Scipïon di gloria reda,
quand’ Anibàl co’ suoi diede le spalle,

«O you who in the blessed valley,that made Scipio heir to glory,when Hannibal turned his back with his men,

recasti già mille leon per preda,
e che, se fossi stato a l’alta guerra
de’ tuoi fratelli, ancor par che si creda

you have already sent a thousand lions to prey,and that, if you had been at the high warof your brothers, it still seems that it is believed

ch’avrebber vinto i figli de la terra:
mettine giù, e non ten vegna schifo,
dove Cocito la freddura serra.

that the sons of the earth would have won:place them down, and let it not disgust you,where Cocytus locks in the cold.

Non ci fare ire a Tizio né a Tifo:
questi può dar di quel che qui si brama;
però ti china e non torcer lo grifo.

Do not make Titius or Typhus angry:he can provide what is desired here;so bow down, and do not twist your snout.

Ancor ti può nel mondo render fama,
ch’el vive, e lunga vita ancor aspetta
se ’nnanzi tempo grazia a sé nol chiama».

And still, he can grant you renown in the world, for he lives, and expects a long life, if grace does not call him before his time.

Così disse ’l maestro; e quelli in fretta
le man distese, e prese ’l duca mio,
ond’ Ercule sentì già grande stretta.

So spoke the master; and he quickly extended his hands and grasped my guide, a grip which Hercules himself had once felt acutely.

Virgilio, quando prender si sentio,
disse a me: «Fatti qua, sì ch’io ti prenda»;
poi fece sì ch’un fascio era elli e io.

Virgil, when he felt himself grasped, said to me: "Come here, so that I may take you"; then he acted in such a way that he and I were a single bundle.

Qual pare a riguardar la Carisenda
sotto ’l chinato, quando un nuvol vada
sovr’ essa sì, ched ella incontro penda:

As one sees the Carisenda,leaning, when a cloud passesabove it, so that it seems to hang:

tal parve Antëo a me che stava a bada
di vederlo chinare, e fu tal ora
ch’i’ avrei voluto ir per altra strada.

As Antaeus appeared to me, who was watching him,ready to see him stoop, and it was such a momentthat I would have preferred to take another path.

Ma lievemente al fondo che divora
Lucifero con Giuda, ci sposò;
né, sì chinato, lì fece dimora,

He joined us gently at the bottom that consumesLucifer with Judas, and then departed;nor, once he had bowed, did he linger there.

e come albero in nave si levò.

And he rose like a mast in a ship.

Purgatorio * Canto XX

Continuing on the fifth terrace, Dante and Virgil observe souls lying prone on the stone floor, bound at their hands and feet. The penitents are weeping and reciting the psalm, ‘Adhaesit pavimento anima mea.’ At Virgil’s prompting, Dante addresses one spirit, who identifies himself as Hugh Capet. The soul laments the avarice of his royal descendants in France, recounting their historical betrayals and violent acts. He explains that during the day, the souls on this terrace praise examples of poverty and generosity, while at night they shout out examples of greed. As Dante moves away from Hugh Capet, the entire mountain of Purgatory is seized by a violent tremor. In unison, every soul on the mountain shouts, “Gloria in excelsis Deo.” Dante is left frightened and confused by the event, but Virgil urges him forward as they continue their journey.

Purgatorio • Canto XX

Contra miglior voler voler mal pugna;
onde contra ’l piacer mio, per piacerli,
trassi de l’acqua non sazia la spugna.

Against a better will, ill will struggles; wherefore against my own pleasure, to please her, I drew the sponge, not sated, from the water.

Mossimi; e ’l duca mio si mosse per li
luoghi spediti pur lungo la roccia,
come si va per muro stretto a’ merli;

And we moved on; my guide proceeded through those fast passages along the rock, as one walks along a narrow wall near the battlements.

ché la gente che fonde a goccia a goccia
per li occhi il mal che tutto ’l mondo occupa,
da l’altra parte in fuor troppo s’approccia.

because the people who weep drop by dropthrough their eyes the evil that the whole world holds,press too close to the other side.

Maladetta sie tu, antica lupa,
che più che tutte l’altre bestie hai preda
per la tua fame sanza fine cupa!

Cursed are you, ancient she-wolf,who, more than any other beast, has preybecause of your endless, dark hunger!

O ciel, nel cui girar par che si creda
le condizion di qua giù trasmutarsi,
quando verrà per cui questa disceda?

Oh, heavens, in whose turning it seems one believesthat the conditions here below are transformed,when will he come for whom this descends?

Noi andavam con passi lenti e scarsi,
e io attento a l’ombre, ch’i’ sentia
pietosamente piangere e lagnarsi;

We moved with slow, meager steps, and I, attentive to the shades, felt them pitifully weeping and lamenting.

e per ventura udi’ «Dolce Maria!»
dinanzi a noi chiamar così nel pianto
come fa donna che in parturir sia;

And by chance I heard, “Sweet Mary!”called out before us in the weeping,like a woman in the throes of birth.

e seguitar: «Povera fosti tanto,
quanto veder si può per quello ospizio
dove sponesti il tuo portato santo».

And she continued, "You were as poor as one can see, at that hospice where you gave birth to your holy child."

Seguentemente intesi: «O buon Fabrizio,
con povertà volesti anzi virtute
che gran ricchezza posseder con vizio».

Next I heard: "Oh, good Fabrizio, you preferred virtue in poverty to possessing great wealth with vice."

Queste parole m’eran sì piaciute,
ch’io mi trassi oltre per aver contezza
di quello spirto onde parean venute.

These words had pleased me so much that I went forward to learn the identity of the spirit from which they seemed to have come.

Esso parlava ancor de la larghezza
che fece Niccolò a le pulcelle,
per condurre ad onor lor giovinezza.

He was still speaking of the generosity Niccolò showed the young women, in order to guide their youth toward honor.

«O anima che tanto ben favelle,
dimmi chi fosti», dissi, «e perché sola
tu queste degne lode rinovelle.

"O soul that speaks so well," I said, "tell me who you were, and why you alone renew these worthy praises."

Non fia sanza mercé la tua parola,
s’io ritorno a compiér lo cammin corto
di quella vita ch’al termine vola».

Your words shall not be without reward,if I return to complete the short pathof that life which flies toward its end.”

Ed elli: «Io ti dirò, non per conforto
ch’io attenda di là, ma perché tanta
grazia in te luce prima che sie morto.

And he said, "I will tell you, not for comfort,because I expect none in the afterlife, but becausesuch grace shines in you before your death."

Io fui radice de la mala pianta
che la terra cristiana tutta aduggia,
sì che buon frutto rado se ne schianta.

I was the root of the wicked plantthat darkens all of Christian lands,so that good fruit is rarely harvested.

Ma se Doagio, Lilla, Guanto e Bruggia
potesser, tosto ne saria vendetta;
e io la cheggio a lui che tutto giuggia.

But were Doagio, Lille, Ghent, and Bruges to rise, revenge would come swiftly; and I ask it of him who judges all.

Chiamato fui di là Ugo Ciappetta;
di me son nati i Filippi e i Luigi
per cui novellamente è Francia retta.

I was called Hugh Capet on that side; from me were born the Philips and the Louises who newly govern France.

Figliuol fu’ io d’un beccaio di Parigi:
quando li regi antichi venner meno
tutti, fuor ch’un renduto in panni bigi,

I was the son of a Parisian butcher: when the ancient kings all failed, save one dressed in gray robes,

trova’mi stretto ne le mani il freno
del governo del regno, e tanta possa
di nuovo acquisto, e sì d’amici pieno,

found in my hands the reinsof governing the kingdom, and so much powerof new acquisition, and so full of friends,

ch’a la corona vedova promossa
la testa di mio figlio fu, dal quale
cominciar di costor le sacrate ossa.

that, to the widowed crown, was advancedthe head of my son, from whom beganthe sacred bones of these.

Mentre che la gran dota provenzale
al sangue mio non tolse la vergogna,
poco valea, ma pur non facea male.

While the great Provençal dowry did not strip my blood of shame, it was of little worth, but still did no harm.

Lì cominciò con forza e con menzogna
la sua rapina; e poscia, per ammenda,
Pontì e Normandia prese e Guascogna.

He began his plunder with force and lies; then, as amends, he seized Poitou, Normandy, and Gascony.

Carlo venne in Italia e, per ammenda,
vittima fé di Curradino; e poi
ripinse al ciel Tommaso, per ammenda.

Charles came to Italy and, as amends, made Conradin a victim; then, as amends, he sent Thomas back to Heaven.

Tempo vegg’ io, non molto dopo ancoi,
che tragge un altro Carlo fuor di Francia,
per far conoscer meglio e sé e ’ suoi.

I see a time, not long from now, that will bring forth another Charles from France, so that he may better display himself and his kin.

Sanz’ arme n’esce e solo con la lancia
con la qual giostrò Giuda, e quella ponta
sì, ch’a Fiorenza fa scoppiar la pancia.

Without a weapon he will come forth, only with the lanceJudas jousted with, and he will thrust it sothat he’ll burst Florence’s belly open.

Quindi non terra, ma peccato e onta
guadagnerà, per sé tanto più grave,
quanto più lieve simil danno conta.

Therefore, he will gain not land, but sin and shame,all the more burdensome for himself,the less he reckons such a loss to be.

L’altro, che già uscì preso di nave,
veggio vender sua figlia e patteggiarne
come fanno i corsar de l’altre schiave.

The other, who was already taken from his ship, I see selling his daughter and bartering over her, like corsairs do with other slaves.

O avarizia, che puoi tu più farne,
poscia c’ha’ il mio sangue a te sì tratto,
che non si cura de la propria carne?

O greed, what more can you do now, since you've drawn my blood to you so completely that it no longer cares about its own flesh?

Perché men paia il mal futuro e ’l fatto,
veggio in Alagna intrar lo fiordaliso,
e nel vicario suo Cristo esser catto.

So the evil yet to come and what has already happened seem less dire, I see the fleur-de-lis enter Alagna, and Christ captured in his vicar.

Veggiolo un’altra volta esser deriso;
veggio rinovellar l’aceto e ’l fiele,
e tra vivi ladroni esser anciso.

I see him mocked again; I see the vinegar and gall renewed, and Christ slain amidst living thieves.

Veggio il novo Pilato sì crudele,
che ciò nol sazia, ma sanza decreto
portar nel Tempio le cupide vele.

I see the new Pilate, so cruel, that he is not satisfied, but without a decree, he carries covetous sails into the Temple.

O Segnor mio, quando sarò io lieto
a veder la vendetta che, nascosa,
fa dolce l’ira tua nel tuo secreto?

O my Lord, when will I be gladto see the vengeance that, hidden,makes your wrath sweet in your secret place?

Ciò ch’io dicea di quell’ unica sposa
de lo Spirito Santo e che ti fece
verso me volger per alcuna chiosa,

is what I was saying about that unique brideof the Holy Spirit, which caused you to turntowards me, needing some explanation.

tanto è risposto a tutte nostre prece
quanto ’l dì dura; ma com’ el s’annotta,
contrario suon prendemo in quella vece.

As long as the day lasts, all our prayers receive a reply; but when night falls, we take up a different tune.

Noi repetiam Pigmalïon allotta,
cui traditore e ladro e paricida
fece la voglia sua de l’oro ghiotta;

Then we repeated Pygmalion,whom his greedy craving for gold made a traitor, a thief, and a parricide.

e la miseria de l’avaro Mida,
che seguì a la sua dimanda gorda,
per la qual sempre convien che si rida.

And the misery of the miser Midas, which followed his greedy request, because of which one must always laugh.

Del folle Acàn ciascun poi si ricorda,
come furò le spoglie, sì che l’ira
di Iosüè qui par ch’ancor lo morda.

Of mad Achan, each then remembers,how he stole the spoils, so that the wrathof Joshua seems even now to bite him here.

Indi accusiam col marito Saffira;
lodiam i calci ch’ebbe Elïodoro;
e in infamia tutto ’l monte gira

They recall the accusations Saphira made to her husband;they praise the kicks that Heliodorus took;and infamy swirls over the whole mountain.

Polinestòr ch’ancise Polidoro;
ultimamente ci si grida: “Crasso,
dilci, che ’l sai: di che sapore è l’oro?”.

Polynestor, who killed Polydorus;at last, people shout at us: "Crassus,tell us, since you know: what does gold taste like?"

Talor parla l’uno alto e l’altro basso,
secondo l’affezion ch’ad ir ci sprona
ora a maggiore e ora a minor passo:

One speaks in a high voice, the other in a low,according to the passion that spurs us to anger,now at a greater pace, now a lesser.

però al ben che ’l dì ci si ragiona,
dianzi non era io sol; ma qui da presso
non alzava la voce altra persona».

But regarding the good that this day discusses with us,I was not alone just now; but nearby,no other person raised their voice."

Noi eravam partiti già da esso,
e brigavam di soverchiar la strada
tanto quanto al poder n’era permesso,

We had already departed from it, and we hastened to overcome the road as far as our power allowed us,

quand’ io senti’, come cosa che cada,
tremar lo monte; onde mi prese un gelo
qual prender suol colui ch’a morte vada.

When I felt, like something falling, the mountain tremble; whereat a chill took me, such as seizes the man who goes to die.

Certo non si scoteo sì forte Delo,
pria che Latona in lei facesse ’l nido
a parturir li due occhi del cielo.

Surely Delos never shook so violently, before Latona made it the nest to give birth to the two eyes of heaven.

Poi cominciò da tutte parti un grido
tal, che ’l maestro inverso me si feo,
dicendo: «Non dubbiar, mentr’ io ti guido».

Then from all sides a cry arose,so loud that my master turned to me,saying: “Have no fear, while I guide you.”

‘Glorïa in excelsis’ tutti ‘Deo’
dicean, per quel ch’io da’ vicin compresi,
onde intender lo grido si poteo.

"Glory to God in the highest," all were saying, as far as I could understand from those nearby, so that the cry could be heard distinctly.

No’ istavamo immobili e sospesi
come i pastor che prima udir quel canto,
fin che ’l tremar cessò ed el compiési.

We remained still and suspended, like the shepherds who first heard that song, until the trembling ceased and it concluded.

Poi ripigliammo nostro cammin santo,
guardando l’ombre che giacean per terra,
tornate già in su l’usato pianto.

Then we resumed our holy journey, watching the shadows lying on the ground, returned now to their accustomed weeping.

Nulla ignoranza mai con tanta guerra
mi fé desideroso di sapere,
se la memoria mia in ciò non erra,

No ignorance ever waged such war on me,making me so eager to know,if my memory does not fail me now,

quanta pareami allor, pensando, avere;
né per la fretta dimandare er’ oso,
né per me lì potea cosa vedere:

as I then seemed to have, in my thoughts; nor, in my haste, dared I ask, nor could I see anything for myself there:

così m’andava timido e pensoso.

Thus, I proceeded, timid and thoughtful.

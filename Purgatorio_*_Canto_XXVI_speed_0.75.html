Purgatorio * Canto XXVI

On the seventh terrace, Dante, Virgil, and Statius continue their journey alongside a wall of fire. Dante’s physical body casts a shadow on the flames, which attracts the attention of the souls within. One spirit emerges to question Dante, who confirms that he is still alive. The pilgrims then observe two distinct groups of souls moving in opposite directions. When the two lines of souls meet, they exchange a brief, chaste kiss, cry out examples of their former sins ("Sodom and Gomorrah" and "Pasiphaë enters the cow"), and then hurry apart. The soul who first spoke to Dante identifies himself as the poet Guido Guinizelli. He then points to another spirit in the fire, calling him a superior poet. This second figure is Arnaut Daniel, who speaks to Dante in his native Provençal. After introducing himself and asking to be remembered, Arnaut Daniel vanishes back into the flames.

Purgatorio • Canto XXVI

Mentre che sì per l’orlo, uno innanzi altro,
ce n’andavamo, e spesso il buon maestro
diceami: «Guarda: giovi ch’io ti scaltro»;

While we went along the edge, one before the other, and often the good master said to me: "Look: it will be of use to you that I be shrewd."

feriami il sole in su l’omero destro,
che già, raggiando, tutto l’occidente
mutava in bianco aspetto di cilestro;

The sun struck my right shoulder, and already, radiating, all the west was changing to a white, celestial aspect;

e io facea con l’ombra più rovente
parer la fiamma; e pur a tanto indizio
vidi molt’ ombre, andando, poner mente.

and I made the flame appear more burning with the shadow; and even with so much indication, I saw many shades, as I went, take note.

Questa fu la cagion che diede inizio
loro a parlar di me; e cominciarsi
a dir: «Colui non par corpo fittizio»;

This was the reason they began to speak of me; and they began to say: "He doesn't appear to be a phantom body."

poi verso me, quanto potëan farsi,
certi si fero, sempre con riguardo
di non uscir dove non fosser arsi.

then toward me, as close as they could get, certain ones came, always mindful not to step beyond where they would not be burned.

«O tu che vai, non per esser più tardo,
ma forse reverente, a li altri dopo,
rispondi a me che ’n sete e ’n foco ardo.

"O you who go, not to be slower, but perhaps reverent, behind the others, answer me, I who burn in thirst and fire."

Né solo a me la tua risposta è uopo;
ché tutti questi n’hanno maggior sete
che d’acqua fredda Indo o Etïopo.

Not just for me is your answer needed, for all these have a greater thirst than that of cold water, the Indian or the Ethiopian.

Dinne com’ è che fai di te parete
al sol, pur come tu non fossi ancora
di morte intrato dentro da la rete».

Then why do you make yourself a wallagainst the sun, as if you were not yetentered the net of death?

Sì mi parlava un d’essi; e io mi fora
già manifesto, s’io non fossi atteso
ad altra novità ch’apparve allora;

Yes, one of them spoke to me; and I would have already revealed myself, had I not been occupied by another novelty that then appeared.

ché per lo mezzo del cammino acceso
venne gente col viso incontro a questa,
la qual mi fece a rimirar sospeso.

For, halfway along the burning path, a group of people appeared, facing these others, and it made me pause, staring at them in wonder.

Lì veggio d’ogne parte farsi presta
ciascun’ ombra e basciarsi una con una
sanza restar, contente a brieve festa;

Now I see shadows on every side, quickening,each one embracing anotherwithout pause, content with the briefest of celebrations.

così per entro loro schiera bruna
s’ammusa l’una con l’altra formica,
forse a spïar lor via e lor fortuna.

Thus, within their somber ranks,each ant nudges another,perhaps to scout their path and destiny.

Tosto che parton l’accoglienza amica,
prima che ’l primo passo lì trascorra,
sopragridar ciascuna s’affatica:

As soon as they depart from the friendly welcome,before their first step takes them forward,each one strains to shout the loudest.

la nova gente: «Soddoma e Gomorra»;
e l’altra: «Ne la vacca entra Pasife,
perché ’l torello a sua lussuria corra».

The new souls cry, "Sodom and Gomorrah," while the others shout, "Pasiphae in the cow, so the bull may run to her lust."

Poi, come grue ch’a le montagne Rife
volasser parte, e parte inver’ l’arene,
queste del gel, quelle del sole schife,

Then, as cranes, some flying to the Riphean mountains, others toward the sands, these shunning the cold, those the sun,

l’una gente sen va, l’altra sen vene;
e tornan, lagrimando, a’ primi canti
e al gridar che più lor si convene;

One group departs, another arrives; and they return, weeping, to their first songs and to the cries that suit them best.

e raccostansi a me, come davanti,
essi medesmi che m’avean pregato,
attenti ad ascoltar ne’ lor sembianti.

And they gather near me, as they did before, they themselves who had entreated me, their expressions intent on listening.

Io, che due volte avea visto lor grato,
incominciai: «O anime sicure
d’aver, quando che sia, di pace stato,

I, who had twice seen them pleased, began: "O souls assuredof having, whenever it may be, a state of peace,

non son rimase acerbe né mature
le membra mie di là, ma son qui meco
col sangue suo e con le sue giunture.

My limbs, on the other side, were left neither unripe nor mature, but are here with me, with their own blood and with their joints.

Quinci sù vo per non esser più cieco;
donna è di sopra che m’acquista grazia,
per che ’l mortal per vostro mondo reco.

Here, I go up so that I will no longer be blind; a woman is above, who wins grace for me, for which I bring the mortal through your world.

Ma se la vostra maggior voglia sazia
tosto divegna, sì che ’l ciel v’alberghi
ch’è pien d’amore e più ampio si spazia,

But if your greatest longing is soon sated, may it be that Heaven welcomes you, which is full of love and expands the more,

ditemi, acciò ch’ancor carte ne verghi,
chi siete voi, e chi è quella turba
che se ne va di retro a’ vostri terghi».

Tell me, so that I may still write down your words, who are you, and who are those who go behind your backs?

Non altrimenti stupido si turba
lo montanaro, e rimirando ammuta,
quando rozzo e salvatico s’inurba,

No differently is a mountain man bewildered, and staring, falls silent, when, crude and wild, he comes to town.

che ciascun’ ombra fece in sua paruta;
ma poi che furon di stupore scarche,
lo qual ne li alti cuor tosto s’attuta,

that each shade did in its appearance; but after they were discharged of their wonder, which quickly fades in lofty hearts,

«Beato te, che de le nostre marche»,
ricominciò colei che pria m’inchiese,
«per morir meglio, esperïenza imbarche!

"Blessed are you, who from our lands,"she began again, the one who first questioned me,"to embark on experience, so you might die better!"

La gente che non vien con noi, offese
di ciò per che già Cesar, trïunfando,
“Regina” contra sé chiamar s’intese:

The people who do not come with us, offendedby that for which Caesar, already triumphant,heard himself called "Queen" against himself:

però si parton “Soddoma” gridando,
rimproverando a sé com’ hai udito,
e aiutan l’arsura vergognando.

But they depart, crying out “Sodom,”reproaching themselves, as you have heard,and aiding the burning, ashamed.

Nostro peccato fu ermafrodito;
ma perché non servammo umana legge,
seguendo come bestie l’appetito,

Our sin was hermaphroditic;but because we did not serve human law,following appetite like beasts,

in obbrobrio di noi, per noi si legge,
quando partinci, il nome di colei
che s’imbestiò ne le ’mbestiate schegge.

In our disgrace, the tale is told for us, when we departed, of the woman who, driven by lust, became a beast among the bestial ruins.

Or sai nostri atti e di che fummo rei:
se forse a nome vuo’ saper chi semo,
tempo non è di dire, e non saprei.

Or tell of our deeds and of what we were guilty: if perhaps you wish to know our names, there is no time to speak, nor would I know how.

Farotti ben di me volere scemo:
son Guido Guinizzelli, e già mi purgo
per ben dolermi prima ch’a lo stremo».

My desire to speak of myself, Farotti, is diminished; I am Guido Guinizzelli, and already I cleanse myself by sorrowing well before my final breath.

Quali ne la tristizia di Ligurgo
si fer due figli a riveder la madre,
tal mi fec’ io, ma non a tanto insurgo,

As in the sorrow of Lycurgus, his two sons beheld their mother, such was I, but did not rise so high.

quand’ io odo nomar sé stesso il padre
mio e de li altri miei miglior che mai
rime d’amore usar dolci e leggiadre;

when I hear my father name himself,and those others, my betters, who used,more than any, sweet and lovely love-rhymes;

e sanza udire e dir pensoso andai
lunga fïata rimirando lui,
né, per lo foco, in là più m’appressai.

And without speaking or hearing, lost in thought, I wentfor a long time gazing at him,nor, because of the fire, did I draw closer.

Poi che di riguardar pasciuto fui,
tutto m’offersi pronto al suo servigio
con l’affermar che fa credere altrui.

After I was sated with looking at him,I offered myself entirely, ready for his service,with the affirmation that makes others believe.

Ed elli a me: «Tu lasci tal vestigio,
per quel ch’i’ odo, in me, e tanto chiaro,
che Letè nol può tòrre né far bigio.

And he said to me: “You leave such an imprint, from what I hear, within me, and so clear, that Lethe cannot erase it, nor make it dim.

Ma se le tue parole or ver giuraro,
dimmi che è cagion per che dimostri
nel dire e nel guardar d’avermi caro».

But if your words just now were sworn true, tell me what is the reason you show in your speech and your gaze that you hold me dear.

E io a lui: «Li dolci detti vostri,
che, quanto durerà l’uso moderno,
faranno cari ancora i loro incostri».

And I to him: "Your sweet words, which, as long as modern usage endures, will make their traces cherished still."

«O frate», disse, «questi ch’io ti cerno
col dito», e additò un spirto innanzi,
«fu miglior fabbro del parlar materno.

"Oh brother," he said, "this one whom I point out to you with my finger," and he indicated a spirit ahead, "was a better craftsman of the mother tongue."

Versi d’amore e prose di romanzi
soverchiò tutti; e lascia dir li stolti
che quel di Lemosì credon ch’avanzi.

Verses of love and prose of romanceSurpassed all others; let the foolish sayThat man from Limoges is still the best.

A voce più ch’al ver drizzan li volti,
e così ferman sua oppinïone
prima ch’arte o ragion per lor s’ascolti.

Faces turn toward a voice rather than truth, and thus they solidify their opinion before art or reason has a chance to be heard.

Così fer molti antichi di Guittone,
di grido in grido pur lui dando pregio,
fin che l’ha vinto il ver con più persone.

Thus many of Guittone's contemporaries did,giving him praise from voice to voice,until truth, with more people, conquered him.

Or se tu hai sì ampio privilegio,
che licito ti sia l’andare al chiostro
nel quale è Cristo abate del collegio,

Or if you hold such ample privilege, that it is permissible for you to go to the cloister, in which Christ is the abbot of the college,

falli per me un dir d’un paternostro,
quanto bisogna a noi di questo mondo,
dove poter peccar non è più nostro».

to be deceived by my saying of a Pater Noster, as much as we need of this world, where we can no longer sin.”

Poi, forse per dar luogo altrui secondo
che presso avea, disparve per lo foco,
come per l’acqua il pesce andando al fondo.

Then, perhaps to give way to another who was near, he vanished into the fire, like a fish descending through water.

Io mi fei al mostrato innanzi un poco,
e dissi ch’al suo nome il mio disire
apparecchiava grazïoso loco.

I moved forward a bit toward the one who had been pointed out, and said that my desire was preparing a gracious place for his name.

El cominciò liberamente a dire:
«Tan m’abellis vostre cortes deman,
qu’ieu no me puesc ni voill a vos cobrire.

He began to speak freely:"Your courteous request pleases me so muchthat I can neither conceal myself from you nor wish to."

Ieu sui Arnaut, que plor e vau cantan;
consiros vei la passada folor,
e vei jausen lo joi qu’esper, denan.

I am Arnaut, who weeps and goes singing;I see with sorrow the folly of the past,and I see joyfully the joy that I await, ahead.

Ara vos prec, per aquella valor
que vos guida al som de l’escalina,
sovenha vos a temps de ma dolor!».

Now I beg you, by that virtuewhich guides you to the top of the ladder,remember my suffering in time!

Poi s’ascose nel foco che li affina.

Then he hid himself in the fire that refines them.

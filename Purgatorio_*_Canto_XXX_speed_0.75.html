Purgatorio * Canto XXX

The procession in the Earthly Paradise comes to a halt, and the chariot stops. One of the twenty-four elders sings, and the others join. Angels appear, singing and scattering flowers. Amidst the flowers, a woman emerges, veiled in white, crowned with olive, and wearing a green mantle over a flame-colored dress. Dante, feeling her presence, turns to speak to Virgil but discovers that his guide has disappeared. As Dante weeps for Virgil’s absence, the woman, Beatrice, calls him by name. She sternly tells him not to weep for Virgil, as he must weep for another reason. Filled with shame, Dante lowers his gaze. Beatrice then addresses the angels, recounting how Dante strayed from the righteous path after her death. She explains that he had fallen so far that she journeyed to the gate of Hell to entreat Virgil to guide him on this penitential journey.

Purgatorio • Canto XXX

Quando il settentrïon del primo cielo,
che né occaso mai seppe né orto
né d’altra nebbia che di colpa velo,

When the north of the first heaven, which never knew setting or rising, nor any other fog than that of sin,

e che faceva lì ciascun accorto
di suo dover, come ’l più basso face
qual temon gira per venire a porto,

and where each was mindful of their duty,like the lowest, who turns, fearing, to reach port,

fermo s’affisse: la gente verace,
venuta prima tra ’l grifone ed esso,
al carro volse sé come a sua pace;

firmly remained. The truthful folk,who had come before between the griffin and it,turned toward the chariot as toward their haven;

e un di loro, quasi da ciel messo,
‘Veni, sponsa, de Libano’ cantando
gridò tre volte, e tutti li altri appresso.

And one of them, as if sent from heaven, singing, "Come, bride, from Lebanon," cried out three times, and all the others followed.

Quali i beati al novissimo bando
surgeran presti ognun di sua caverna,
la revestita voce alleluiando,

As the blessed will rise at the final summons, each from their grave, with voices renewed, singing hallelujah,

cotali in su la divina basterna
si levar cento, ad vocem tanti senis,
ministri e messaggier di vita etterna.

A hundred spirits rose upon the divine litter, at the voice of the aged man, ministers and messengers of eternal life.

Tutti dicean: ‘Benedictus qui venis!’,
e fior gittando e di sopra e dintorno,
‘Manibus, oh, date lilïa plenis!’.

All were saying, "Blessed is he who comes!" throwing flowers above and around, "Give lilies with full hands!"

Io vidi già nel cominciar del giorno
la parte orïental tutta rosata,
e l’altro ciel di bel sereno addorno;

I had already seen, at the start of the day,the eastern sky entirely rose-colored,and the other heavens adorned with lovely clarity.

e la faccia del sol nascere ombrata,
sì che per temperanza di vapori
l’occhio la sostenea lunga fïata:

and the face of the rising sun shadowed,so that through the moderation of vaporsthe eye could sustain it for a long while:

così dentro una nuvola di fiori
che da le mani angeliche saliva
e ricadeva in giù dentro e di fori,

Within a cloud of blossoms, it was so,ascending from angelic hands,then falling back, within and all about.

sovra candido vel cinta d’uliva
donna m’apparve, sotto verde manto
vestita di color di fiamma viva.

Above a white veil, wreathed in olive, a woman appeared to me, beneath a green cloak, clothed in the color of living flame.

E lo spirito mio, che già cotanto
tempo era stato ch’a la sua presenza
non era di stupor, tremando, affranto,

And my spirit, which for so longhad not been in her presence, trembled,broken by amazement.

sanza de li occhi aver più conoscenza,
per occulta virtù che da lei mosse,
d’antico amor sentì la gran potenza.

without my eyes knowing her any longer,by a hidden power that moved from her,I felt the great potency of an ancient love.

Tosto che ne la vista mi percosse
l’alta virtù che già m’avea trafitto
prima ch’io fuor di püerizia fosse,

As soon as the high virtue that had already wounded me before I was out of childhood struck my sight,

volsimi a la sinistra col respitto
col quale il fantolin corre a la mamma
quando ha paura o quando elli è afflitto,

I turned left with the same relief a child feels when running to its mother, whether in fear or in sorrow.

per dicere a Virgilio: ‘Men che dramma
di sangue m’è rimaso che non tremi:
conosco i segni de l’antica fiamma’.

to quote Virgil: "Less than a drop of blood remains in me that doesn't tremble: I recognize the signs of the ancient flame."

Ma Virgilio n’avea lasciati scemi
di sé, Virgilio dolcissimo patre,
Virgilio a cui per mia salute die’mi;

But Virgil had left us bereft of himself, Virgil, sweetest father, Virgil, to whom, for my salvation, he gave me.

né quantunque perdeo l’antica matre,
valse a le guance nette di rugiada,
che, lagrimando, non tornasser atre.

Nor did all that the ancient mother lost avail those cheeks, clean with dew, that, weeping, would not become darkened again.

«Dante, perché Virgilio se ne vada,
non pianger anco, non piangere ancora;
ché pianger ti conven per altra spada».

"Dante, do not weep yet, do not weep now, because Virgil must leave you; you must weep for a different blade."

Quasi ammiraglio che in poppa e in prora
viene a veder la gente che ministra
per li altri legni, e a ben far l’incora;

Like an admiral who goes through the stern and the prowto see the crew working on the other ships,and encourages them to do well;

in su la sponda del carro sinistra,
quando mi volsi al suon del nome mio,
che di necessità qui si registra,

upon the left side of the cart,when I turned at the sound of my name,which is recorded here by necessity,

vidi la donna che pria m’appario
velata sotto l’angelica festa,
drizzar li occhi ver’ me di qua dal rio.

I saw the woman who first appeared to me, veiled beneath the angelic festival, turn her eyes toward me from this side of the river.

Tutto che ’l vel che le scendea di testa,
cerchiato de le fronde di Minerva,
non la lasciasse parer manifesta,

Although the veil that cascaded from her head, encircled by Minerva's leaves, did not allow her to appear fully revealed,

regalmente ne l’atto ancor proterva
continüò come colui che dice
e ’l più caldo parlar dietro reserva:

Regally, even in that act of insolence,she continued as one who speaks,and reserves the warmest words for last.

«Guardaci ben! Ben son, ben son Beatrice.
Come degnasti d’accedere al monte?
non sapei tu che qui è l’uom felice?».

"Look closely! Truly, I am Beatrice. How did you deign to come to the mountain? Did you not know that here is where man finds happiness?"

Li occhi mi cadder giù nel chiaro fonte;
ma veggendomi in esso, i trassi a l’erba,
tanta vergogna mi gravò la fronte.

My eyes fell to the clear fountain. But seeing myself reflected there, I lifted them to the grass, so great was the shame that weighed on my brow.

Così la madre al figlio par superba,
com’ ella parve a me; perché d’amaro
sente il sapor de la pietade acerba.

Thus the mother seems to her son proud, as she seemed to me; because she tastes the bitter flavor of harsh pity.

Ella si tacque; e li angeli cantaro
di sùbito ‘In te, Domine, speravi’;
ma oltre ‘pedes meos’ non passaro.

She fell silent; and the angels immediately sang from above, "In te, Domine, speravi," but did not go beyond "pedes meos."

Sì come neve tra le vive travi
per lo dosso d’Italia si congela,
soffiata e stretta da li venti schiavi,

Just as snow, caught between the living beams,across Italy's spine does congeal,blown and compacted by the slave winds,

poi, liquefatta, in sé stessa trapela,
pur che la terra che perde ombra spiri,
sì che par foco fonder la candela;

then, liquified, it seeps into itself,so long as the earth, losing shadow, breathes,such that it seems fire is melting the candle;

così fui sanza lagrime e sospiri
anzi ’l cantar di quei che notan sempre
dietro a le note de li etterni giri;

Thus I was without tears or sighs, before the song of those who always sing behind the notes of the eternal spheres.

ma poi che ’ntesi ne le dolci tempre
lor compatire a me, par che se detto
avesser: ‘Donna, perché sì lo stempre?’,

But when I understood that they, in their sweet music, felt compassion for me, it was as though they had said: "Lady, why do you so dissolve?"

lo gel che m’era intorno al cor ristretto,
spirito e acqua fessi, e con angoscia
de la bocca e de li occhi uscì del petto.

The ice that had bound my heart began to break, becoming spirit and water. With anguish, it fled my breast through my mouth and eyes.

Ella, pur ferma in su la detta coscia
del carro stando, a le sustanze pie
volse le sue parole così poscia:

She, fixed upon the chariot's flank, then turned her words to the pious essences:

«Voi vigilate ne l’etterno die,
sì che notte né sonno a voi non fura
passo che faccia il secol per sue vie;

"You watch in the eternal day, so that neither night nor sleep steals from you the steps the world takes along its path;

onde la mia risposta è con più cura
che m’intenda colui che di là piagne,
perché sia colpa e duol d’una misura.

Wherefore my answer is more carefully made, that he who weeps on the other side may understand me, so that guilt and grief may be of equal measure.

Non pur per ovra de le rote magne,
che drizzan ciascun seme ad alcun fine
secondo che le stelle son compagne,

Not merely through the work of the great spheres, which direct each seed toward its purpose, according to the stars' companionship,

ma per larghezza di grazie divine,
che sì alti vapori hanno a lor piova,
che nostre viste là non van vicine,

but through the abundance of divine graces, which possess such high vapors as their rain, that our sight cannot reach them.

questi fu tal ne la sua vita nova
virtüalmente, ch’ogne abito destro
fatto averebbe in lui mirabil prova.

This man, in his youthful life, was so full of potential that every virtuous habit would have found in him a marvelous test.

Ma tanto più maligno e più silvestro
si fa ’l terren col mal seme e non cólto,
quant’ elli ha più di buon vigor terrestro.

But the more the land is left untended, the more savage and wild it becomes with bad seed, the more fertile the earth's own strength.

Alcun tempo il sostenni col mio volto:
mostrando li occhi giovanetti a lui,
meco il menava in dritta parte vòlto.

For a time I sustained him with my face:showing to him my youthful eyes,with me I led him, turned towards the right path.

Sì tosto come in su la soglia fui
di mia seconda etade e mutai vita,
questi si tolse a me, e diessi altrui.

As soon as I reached the thresholdof my second age and changed my life,he took himself from me, and gave himself to another.

Quando di carne a spirto era salita,
e bellezza e virtù cresciuta m’era,
fu’ io a lui men cara e men gradita;

When my flesh had ascended to spirit,and beauty and virtue had grown in me,I was less dear to him, and less pleasing.

e volse i passi suoi per via non vera,
imagini di ben seguendo false,
che nulla promession rendono intera.

And he turned his steps down a false path, following false images of good, which render no promise whole.

Né l’impetrare ispirazion mi valse,
con le quali e in sogno e altrimenti
lo rivocai: sì poco a lui ne calse!

Nor did entreating inspiration avail me, by which means, in dream and otherwise, I recalled him: so little did he heed it!

Tanto giù cadde, che tutti argomenti
a la salute sua eran già corti,
fuor che mostrarli le perdute genti.

So far down did he fall that every argument for his salvation was already short, save to show him the lost people.

Per questo visitai l’uscio d’i morti,
e a colui che l’ha qua sù condotto,
li prieghi miei, piangendo, furon porti.

Therefore, I visited the gate of the dead, and to the one who led him here, my prayers, weeping, were brought.

Alto fato di Dio sarebbe rotto,
se Letè si passasse e tal vivanda
fosse gustata sanza alcuno scotto

A decree of God's destiny would be shattered,if Lethe were traversed and such nourishmentwere tasted without penalty.

di pentimento che lagrime spanda».

of repentance, that it may shed tears."

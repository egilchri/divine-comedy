Inferno * Canto XV

Dante and Virgil walk along one of the stone dikes containing the river Phlegethon. This elevated path shields them from the rain of fire falling on the sterile, sandy plain below. A group of souls running on the plain peers up at the travelers. One shade reaches out, grabs the hem of Dante's garment, and is recognized as Ser Brunetto Latini, Dante’s former teacher. Because the souls are condemned to run without pause, Brunetto walks alongside the dike beneath Dante as they talk. He predicts that Dante will face persecution from the Florentines but will ultimately achieve great fame. Before departing, Brunetto identifies several of his companions, all of whom were renowned clerics and men of letters. Seeing another group approach, he commends his written work, the *Tesoro*, to Dante’s memory and then runs swiftly to rejoin his own band.

Inferno • Canto XV

Ora cen porta l’un de’ duri margini;
e ’l fummo del ruscel di sopra aduggia,
sì che dal foco salva l’acqua e li argini.

Now one of the hard banks carries us; and the smoke shadows the stream above, so that it protects the water and the banks from the fire.

Quali Fiamminghi tra Guizzante e Bruggia,
temendo ’l fiotto che ’nver’ lor s’avventa,
fanno lo schermo perché ’l mar si fuggia;

As the Flemish once did, between Wissant and Bruges, fearing the surge that comes toward them, build barriers to push back the sea.

e quali Padoan lungo la Brenta,
per difender lor ville e lor castelli,
anzi che Carentana il caldo senta:

And like the Paduans along the Brenta, to defend their towns and castles, before the heat of summer touches Carantana:

a tale imagine eran fatti quelli,
tutto che né sì alti né sì grossi,
qual che si fosse, lo maestro félli.

And like the tale they are made,though neither as high nor as large,whatever they were, the master made them.

Già eravam da la selva rimossi
tanto, ch’i’ non avrei visto dov’ era,
perch’ io in dietro rivolto mi fossi,

Already we had moved away from the woodso much that I wouldn’t have seen where it was,had I turned back to look.

quando incontrammo d’anime una schiera
che venian lungo l’argine, e ciascuna
ci riguardava come suol da sera

When we encountered a gathering of soulswho came along the bank, and each oneregarded us as one does at twilight.

guardare uno altro sotto nuova luna;
e sì ver’ noi aguzzavan le ciglia
come ’l vecchio sartor fa ne la cruna.

looking at one another under a new moon;and so they strained their eyes toward us,like an old tailor at his needle's eye.

Così adocchiato da cotal famiglia,
fui conosciuto da un, che mi prese
per lo lembo e gridò: «Qual maraviglia!».

Thus, watched by that gathering, I was recognized by one, who grabbed my cloak and cried: "What wonder!"

E io, quando ’l suo braccio a me distese,
ficcaï li occhi per lo cotto aspetto,
sì che ’l viso abbrusciato non difese

And I, when he reached his arm out to me, fixed my eyes upon his cooked appearance, so that his scorched face did not hinder me.

la conoscenza süa al mio ’ntelletto;
e chinando la mano a la sua faccia,
rispuosi: «Siete voi qui, ser Brunetto?».

His knowledge became clear to my intellect; and lowering my hand to his face, I replied: "Are you here, Ser Brunetto?"

E quelli: «O figliuol mio, non ti dispiaccia
se Brunetto Latino un poco teco
ritorna ’n dietro e lascia andar la traccia».

And he said: "Oh, my son, do not be displeased if Brunetto Latino walks a little way with you and leaves the others behind."

I’ dissi lui: «Quanto posso, ven preco;
e se volete che con voi m’asseggia,
faròl, se piace a costui che vo seco».

I told him: "As much as I can, I will. And if you wish me to sit with you, I will do so, if it pleases the one who goes with me."

«O figliuol», disse, «qual di questa greggia
s’arresta punto, giace poi cent’ anni
sanz’ arrostarsi quando ’l foco il feggia.

"Oh, son," he said, "whichever of these is halted, will lie for a hundred years without rising, even when the fire strikes its liver."

Però va oltre: i’ ti verrò a’ panni;
e poi rigiugnerò la mia masnada,
che va piangendo i suoi etterni danni».

But this goes further: I will come for your garments; and then I will rejoin my company, who weep for their eternal suffering.

Io non osava scender de la strada
per andar par di lui; ma ’l capo chino
tenea com’ uom che reverente vada.

I did not dare to leave the roadto walk beside him; but I kept my head bowedlike a man who walks in reverence.

El cominciò: «Qual fortuna o destino
anzi l’ultimo dì qua giù ti mena?
e chi è questi che mostra ’l cammino?».

He began, "What fortune or destiny,before your final day, brings you here below?And who is this who shows the way?"

«Là sù di sopra, in la vita serena»,
rispuos’ io lui, «mi smarri’ in una valle,
avanti che l’età mia fosse piena.

“Up there, in the bright world,” I answered him, “I went astray in a valley, before my life had reached its fullness.”

Pur ier mattina le volsi le spalle:
questi m’apparve, tornand’ ïo in quella,
e reducemi a ca per questo calle».

"As morning broke, I turned my back on it: this one appeared to me, as I was returning to that place, and brought me back here by this path."

Ed elli a me: «Se tu segui tua stella,
non puoi fallire a glorïoso porto,
se ben m’accorsi ne la vita bella;

And he said to me: "If you follow your star, you cannot fail to reach a glorious harbor, as I understood in my own beautiful life;

e s’io non fossi sì per tempo morto,
veggendo il cielo a te così benigno,
dato t’avrei a l’opera conforto.

And if I had not died so prematurely, seeing the heavens so benevolent to you, I would have given you encouragement in your work.

Ma quello ingrato popolo maligno
che discese di Fiesole ab antico,
e tiene ancor del monte e del macigno,

But that ungrateful, malicious peopledescended long ago from Fiesole,and still holds to the mountain and the stone,

ti si farà, per tuo ben far, nimico;
ed è ragion, ché tra li lazzi sorbi
si disconvien fruttare al dolce fico.

He will make an enemy of you, for your own good; and it is right, because among the sour medlars, it does not befit the sweet fig to bear fruit.

Vecchia fama nel mondo li chiama orbi;
gent’ è avara, invidiosa e superba:
dai lor costumi fa che tu ti forbi.

The world knows them as blind, an old story; they are a greedy, envious, and proud people. Beware of their ways.

La tua fortuna tanto onor ti serba,
che l’una parte e l’altra avranno fame
di te; ma lungi fia dal becco l’erba.

Your fortune holds you in such high regard, that both factions will hunger for you; but the grass will be far from their beaks.

Faccian le bestie fiesolane strame
di lor medesme, e non tocchin la pianta,
s’alcuna surge ancora in lor letame,

Let the beasts of Fiesole make fodder of themselves, and not touch the plant, if any still rises in their dung.

in cui riviva la sementa santa
di que’ Roman che vi rimaser quando
fu fatto il nido di malizia tanta».

in which the holy seed might reviveof those Romans who remained there whenthe nest of so much malice was made.”

«Se fosse tutto pieno il mio dimando»,
rispuos’ io lui, «voi non sareste ancora
de l’umana natura posto in bando;

"If all that I desire were granted," I replied to him, "you would not yet be exiled from human nature;

ché ’n la mente m’è fitta, e or m’accora,
la cara e buona imagine paterna
di voi quando nel mondo ad ora ad ora

for in my mind, and it grieves me now, is fixedthe dear and good paternal imageof you when in the world, now and again,

m’insegnavate come l’uom s’etterna:
e quant’ io l’abbia in grado, mentr’ io vivo
convien che ne la mia lingua si scerna.

you taught me how man makes himself eternal;and how much I am in your debt, as long as I live,must be plain to see in my words.

Ciò che narrate di mio corso scrivo,
e serbolo a chiosar con altro testo
a donna che saprà, s’a lei arrivo.

I write down what you say of my journey, and I keep it to annotate with another text, for a woman who will understand, if I reach her.

Tanto vogl’ io che vi sia manifesto,
pur che mia coscïenza non mi garra,
ch’a la Fortuna, come vuol, son presto.

I desire so much for this to be clear, as long as my conscience does not rebuke me, that I am ready for Fortune, as she wills.

Non è nuova a li orecchi miei tal arra:
però giri Fortuna la sua rota
come le piace, e ’l villan la sua marra».

Such a pledge is not new to my ears; therefore, let Fortune turn her wheel as she desires, and the peasant his hoe.

Lo mio maestro allora in su la gota
destra si volse in dietro e riguardommi;
poi disse: «Bene ascolta chi la nota».

My master then turned back to the right side of his cheek and looked at me. Then he said: "He who takes note hears well."

Né per tanto di men parlando vommi
con ser Brunetto, e dimando chi sono
li suoi compagni più noti e più sommi.

But even while speaking thus, I did not cease to walk with Ser Brunetto, and asked who his most notable and eminent companions were.

Ed elli a me: «Saper d’alcuno è buono;
de li altri fia laudabile tacerci,
ché ’l tempo saria corto a tanto suono.

And he to me: "To know of some is good; to be silent about the others is laudable, for time would be short for such a multitude."

In somma sappi che tutti fur cherci
e litterati grandi e di gran fama,
d’un peccato medesmo al mondo lerci.

In short, know that they were all clerics, great scholars, and of great renown, stained by the same foul sin in the world.

Priscian sen va con quella turba grama,
e Francesco d’Accorso anche; e vedervi,
s’avessi avuto di tal tigna brama,

Priscian goes with that wretched crowd, and Francesco d'Accorso too; and to see them, if I had desired such a blight,

colui potei che dal servo de’ servi
fu trasmutato d’Arno in Bacchiglione,
dove lasciò li mal protesi nervi.

He whom the servant of servants had moved from the Arno to the Bacchiglione, could have done it; where he left behind his poorly-stretched nerves.

Di più direi; ma ’l venire e ’l sermone
più lungo esser non può, però ch’i’ veggio
là surger nuovo fummo del sabbione.

I would say more, but my arrival and speechcannot be any longer, because I seenew smoke rising from the sand.

Gente vien con la quale esser non deggio.
Sieti raccomandato il mio Tesoro,
nel qual io vivo ancora, e più non cheggio».

People come with whom I should not be. Commend to you my Treasure, in which I still live, and I ask for nothing more.

Poi si rivolse, e parve di coloro
che corrono a Verona il drappo verde
per la campagna; e parve di costoro

Then he turned, and appeared like one of thosewho race in Verona for the green clothacross the countryside; and he seemed like one of these.

quelli che vince, non colui che perde.

The one who wins, not the one who loses.

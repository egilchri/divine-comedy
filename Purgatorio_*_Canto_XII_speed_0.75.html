Purgatorio * Canto XII

Dante and Virgil walk side-by-side on the First Terrace. Virgil encourages the hunched-over Dante to straighten up and quicken his pace. As they proceed, Dante looks down and observes the pavement, which is covered in masterful carvings. He describes thirteen distinct reliefs depicting figures from mythology and scripture who exemplify punished pride, including Lucifer, Briareus, Nimrod, Niobe, and Saul. At the end of this pathway of art, they are met by the Angel of Humility. The angel’s face is too bright for Dante to look at directly. It strikes its wings against Dante’s forehead, erasing the first "P" branded there, and directs the poets to the stairs leading to the next terrace. As they begin to climb, Dante feels a new lightness in his body and finds the ascent surprisingly easy. Virgil explains that this ease is due to the removal of the "P" for pride and that the journey will become even less difficult as the remaining letters are cleansed.

Purgatorio • Canto XII

Di pari, come buoi che vanno a giogo,
m’andava io con quell’ anima carca,
fin che ’l sofferse il dolce pedagogo.

Likewise, like oxen yoked together, I moved along with that burdened soul, until the sweet teacher allowed it.

Ma quando disse: «Lascia lui e varca;
ché qui è buono con l’ali e coi remi,
quantunque può, ciascun pinger sua barca»;

But when he said: "Leave him now, and proceed;for here, with wings and oars, as best he can,each one must push his boat along with speed";

dritto sì come andar vuolsi rife’mi
con la persona, avvegna che i pensieri
mi rimanessero e chinati e scemi.

Straight as one must go, he made me,with his person, although my thoughtsremained both bowed and diminished.

Io m’era mosso, e seguia volontieri
del mio maestro i passi, e amendue
già mostravam com’ eravam leggeri;

I had set forth, and willingly followedmy master's steps, and both of usalready showed how nimble we were.

ed el mi disse: «Volgi li occhi in giùe:
buon ti sarà, per tranquillar la via,
veder lo letto de le piante tue».

And then he said to me: "Cast your eyes downward: it will serve you well, to steady your path, to see the ground beneath your feet."

Come, perché di lor memoria sia,
sovra i sepolti le tombe terragne
portan segnato quel ch’elli eran pria,

Come, so that their memory might endure, earthen tombs above the buried bear markings of what they once were.

onde lì molte volte si ripiagne
per la puntura de la rimembranza,
che solo a’ pïi dà de le calcagne;

where there many times one weeps againfor memory's sting, which only to the piousoffers its spurs.

sì vid’ io lì, ma di miglior sembianza
secondo l’artificio, figurato
quanto per via di fuor del monte avanza.

Yes, I saw them there, but of a better appearance,shaped with more artistry,as far as the outward path ascends the mountain.

Vedea colui che fu nobil creato
più ch’altra creatura, giù dal cielo
folgoreggiando scender, da l’un lato.

I saw him who was nobly created, more than any other creature, flash down from the sky, on one side.

Vedëa Brïareo fitto dal telo
celestïal giacer, da l’altra parte,
grave a la terra per lo mortal gelo.

I saw Briareus, struck by the celestial sphere, lie still, on the other side, heavy upon the earth with the chill of death.

Vedea Timbreo, vedea Pallade e Marte,
armati ancora, intorno al padre loro,
mirar le membra d’i Giganti sparte.

Timbreo saw, Pallas saw, and Mars saw, still armed, around their father, the scattered limbs of the Giants.

Vedea Nembròt a piè del gran lavoro
quasi smarrito, e riguardar le genti
che ’n Sennaàr con lui superbi fuoro.

Nimrod saw, at the foot of the great work, almost lost, and he watched the people who with him were proud in Sennaar.

O Nïobè, con che occhi dolenti
vedea io te segnata in su la strada,
tra sette e sette tuoi figliuoli spenti!

O Niobe, with what sorrowful eyes did I see you marked on the road, among your seven dead sons and seven daughters!

O Saùl, come in su la propria spada
quivi parevi morto in Gelboè,
che poi non sentì pioggia né rugiada!

O Saul, how you appeared there, upon your own sword, dead on Gilboa, a place that never felt rain or dew!

O folle Aragne, sì vedea io te
già mezza ragna, trista in su li stracci
de l’opera che mal per te si fé.

O foolish Arachne, I saw you there, already half-spider, grieving upon the rags of the work that, ill-fated, you did.

O Roboàm, già non par che minacci
quivi ’l tuo segno; ma pien di spavento
nel porta un carro, sanza ch’altri il cacci.

Oh, Rehoboam, your insignia no longer seems to threaten there; but full of terror, a chariot bears it away, without anyone driving it.

Mostrava ancor lo duro pavimento
come Almeon a sua madre fé caro
parer lo sventurato addornamento.

It still showed the hard pavement, how Alcmaeon made his mother's adornment appear precious, that wretched thing.

Mostrava come i figli si gittaro
sovra Sennacherìb dentro dal tempio,
e come, morto lui, quivi il lasciaro.

It showed how his sons threw themselves upon Sennacherib inside the temple, and how, once he was dead, they left him there.

Mostrava la ruina e ’l crudo scempio
che fé Tamiri, quando disse a Ciro:
«Sangue sitisti, e io di sangue t’empio».

It depicted the ruin and the cruel butchery that Tamiris inflicted when she said to Cyrus, "You thirsted for blood, and I will fill you with blood."

Mostrava come in rotta si fuggiro
li Assiri, poi che fu morto Oloferne,
e anche le reliquie del martiro.

It showed how the Assyrians fled in disarray after Holofernes was killed, and also the remnants of the martyrdom.

Vedeva Troia in cenere e in caverne;
o Ilïón, come te basso e vile
mostrava il segno che lì si discerne!

He saw Troy in ash and in caverns;O Ilion, how base and vilethe mark that was discerned there showed you to be!

Qual di pennel fu maestro o di stile
che ritraesse l’ombre e ’ tratti ch’ivi
mirar farieno uno ingegno sottile?

What brush or style could ever have captured the shadows and strokes found there, which would have challenged a subtle intellect to behold?

Morti li morti e i vivi parean vivi:
non vide mei di me chi vide il vero,
quant’ io calcai, fin che chinato givi.

The dead seemed dead, the living seemed alive: no one ever saw the truth as I did, while I walked there with my head bowed.

Or superbite, e via col viso altero,
figliuoli d’Eva, e non chinate il volto
sì che veggiate il vostro mal sentero!

Or, step forward, with your heads held high, children of Eve, and do not bow your faces so that you might see your miserable path!

Più era già per noi del monte vòlto
e del cammin del sole assai più speso
che non stimava l’animo non sciolto,

More of the mountain was already turned for us, and of the sun's path more time had been spent than our untroubled minds had reckoned.

quando colui che sempre innanzi atteso
andava, cominciò: «Drizza la testa;
non è più tempo di gir sì sospeso.

When the one who was always waited for ahead began, "Lift up your head; there's no more time for wandering in doubt."

Vedi colà un angel che s’appresta
per venir verso noi; vedi che torna
dal servigio del dì l’ancella sesta.

Behold there, an angel hastens, coming toward us. See how the sixth handmaiden of the day returns from her service.

Di reverenza il viso e li atti addorna,
sì che i diletti lo ’nvïarci in suso;
pensa che questo dì mai non raggiorna!».

She adorns her face and gestures with reverence, so that the delights may send us upward. Consider that this day will never dawn again!

Io era ben del suo ammonir uso
pur di non perder tempo, sì che ’n quella
materia non potea parlarmi chiuso.

I was already accustomed to her admonishment, specifically regarding the non-importance of lost time, so that she could not speak to me opaquely on that subject.

A noi venìa la creatura bella,
biancovestito e ne la faccia quale
par tremolando mattutina stella.

Toward us came the beautiful creature, robed in white, and in the face like a star trembling at dawn.

Le braccia aperse, e indi aperse l’ale;
disse: «Venite: qui son presso i gradi,
e agevolemente omai si sale.

With arms outstretched, and then her wings unfurled, she said: "Come: the steps are near, and now it is easy to ascend."

A questo invito vegnon molto radi:
o gente umana, per volar sù nata,
perché a poco vento così cadi?».

At this invitation, very few come. Oh, human race, born to fly upward, why do you fall so easily at the slightest breeze?

Menocci ove la roccia era tagliata;
quivi mi batté l’ali per la fronte;
poi mi promise sicura l’andata.

I entered where the rock was cut away;there he beat his wings upon my brow;then he promised that my journey would be safe.

Come a man destra, per salire al monte
dove siede la chiesa che soggioga
la ben guidata sopra Rubaconte,

Come a man's right, to ascend the mountainwhere sits the church that reigns overthe well-guided, above Rubaconte,

si rompe del montar l’ardita foga
per le scalee che si fero ad etade
ch’era sicuro il quaderno e la doga;

the mountain's bold surge now breaks,along the stairs that were made in an agewhen the ledger and the cask were safe.

così s’allenta la ripa che cade
quivi ben ratta da l’altro girone;
ma quinci e quindi l’alta pietra rade.

Thus the bank slopes downward, collapsingthere quite swiftly from the other circle;but here and there the high stone scrapes.

Noi volgendo ivi le nostre persone,
‘Beati pauperes spiritu!’ voci
cantaron sì, che nol diria sermone.

Turning our faces there,"Blessed are the poor in spirit!" voicessang so sweetly that words cannot convey it.

Ahi quanto son diverse quelle foci
da l’infernali! ché quivi per canti
s’entra, e là giù per lamenti feroci.

Ah, how different those entrances arefrom the infernal ones! Here, one enters with songs,and down there, with ferocious laments.

Già montavam su per li scaglion santi,
ed esser mi parea troppo più lieve
che per lo pian non mi parea davanti.

Already we ascended the holy steps, and I felt much lighter than I had before on the plain.

Ond’ io: «Maestro, dì, qual cosa greve
levata s’è da me, che nulla quasi
per me fatica, andando, si riceve?».

And I said: "Master, tell me, what heavy thing has been lifted from me, that walking now, I feel almost no fatigue?"

Rispuose: «Quando i P che son rimasi
ancor nel volto tuo presso che stinti,
saranno, com’ è l’un, del tutto rasi,

He replied, "When the P's that remain,still almost faded on your face,are entirely erased, like the first,

fier li tuoi piè dal buon voler sì vinti,
che non pur non fatica sentiranno,
ma fia diletto loro esser sù pinti».

Lift then your feet, so conquered by good will,that they will feel not only no fatigue,but it will be their pleasure to be raised.

Allor fec’ io come color che vanno
con cosa in capo non da lor saputa,
se non che ’ cenni altrui sospecciar fanno;

Then I did as those who walkwith something on their head they don't understand,except that the gestures of others make them suspect it is there.

per che la mano ad accertar s’aiuta,
e cerca e truova e quello officio adempie
che non si può fornir per la veduta;

Because the hand helps to make sure,and searches and finds and fulfills that taskwhich cannot be completed through sight;

e con le dita de la destra scempie
trovai pur sei le lettere che ’ncise
quel da le chiavi a me sovra le tempie:

and with the simple fingers of my right handI found the six letters etched there stillby the one with the keys, upon my temples.

a che guardando, il mio duca sorrise.

And upon seeing this, my guide smiled.

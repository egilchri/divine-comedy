Purgatorio * Canto IX

At dawn in the Valley of the Rulers, Dante falls asleep and dreams he is seized by a golden eagle that carries him into the sphere of fire. The dream's intense heat awakens him, and he finds himself disoriented and in a new location, much farther up the mountain. Virgil explains that while he slept, a lady named Lucia descended and carried him to their current position before the entrance to Purgatory proper. Reassured, Dante follows Virgil to a gate guarded by an angel seated on the highest of three steps. After Virgil explains their divine mission, the angel uses his sword to inscribe the letter 'P' seven times on Dante's forehead. The angel then unlocks the gate with a silver and a gold key, warning Dante not to look back after passing through. The gate opens with a loud, grating sound, and the two poets enter.

Purgatorio • Canto IX

La concubina di Titone antico
già s’imbiancava al balco d’orïente,
fuor de le braccia del suo dolce amico;

Tithonus's ancient concubinewas already whitening at the balcony of the orient,outside the arms of her sweet friend;

di gemme la sua fronte era lucente,
poste in figura del freddo animale
che con la coda percuote la gente;

Her brow was bright with gems,arranged in the shape of the cold beastthat strikes the people with its tail;

e la notte, de’ passi con che sale,
fatti avea due nel loco ov’ eravamo,
e ’l terzo già chinava in giuso l’ale;

and night, with the steps by which it climbs,had taken two in the place where we were,and the third was already lowering its wings;

quand’ io, che meco avea di quel d’Adamo,
vinto dal sonno, in su l’erba inchinai
là ’ve già tutti e cinque sedavamo.

When I, who bore within me a spark of Adam, overcome by sleep, bent down upon the grass where we five already sat.

Ne l’ora che comincia i tristi lai
la rondinella presso a la mattina,
forse a memoria de’ suo’ primi guai,

At the hour when the swallow begins its mournful lament, close to the morning, perhaps in remembrance of its first sorrows.

e che la mente nostra, peregrina
più da la carne e men da’ pensier presa,
a le sue visïon quasi è divina,

And that our minds, wandering,more distant from the flesh and less occupied by thought,are almost divine in their visions.

in sogno mi parea veder sospesa
un’aguglia nel ciel con penne d’oro,
con l’ali aperte e a calare intesa;

In a dream, I seemed to see suspendedan eagle in the sky with golden feathers,its wings spread wide, poised to descend;

ed esser mi parea là dove fuoro
abbandonati i suoi da Ganimede,
quando fu ratto al sommo consistoro.

And it seemed to me I was where his ownwere abandoned by Ganymede,when he was snatched to the highest consistory.

Fra me pensava: ‘Forse questa fiede
pur qui per uso, e forse d’altro loco
disdegna di portarne suso in piede’.

Within myself, I thought: ‘Perhaps she strikes here simply out of habit, and perhaps she disdains to carry others up on foot from another place.’

Poi mi parea che, poi rotata un poco,
terribil come folgor discendesse,
e me rapisse suso infino al foco.

Then it seemed to me that, having spun a bit, she descended, terrible as lightning, and snatched me upward toward the fire.

Ivi parea che ella e io ardesse;
e sì lo ’ncendio imaginato cosse,
che convenne che ’l sonno si rompesse.

Here, it seemed that she and I were burning, and the imagined fire burned so intensely that sleep had to break.

Non altrimenti Achille si riscosse,
li occhi svegliati rivolgendo in giro
e non sappiendo là dove si fosse,

No differently did Achilles rouse himself, turning his awakened eyes around, not knowing where he was.

quando la madre da Chirón a Schiro
trafuggò lui dormendo in le sue braccia,
là onde poi li Greci il dipartiro;

When his mother, from Chiron to Scyros, spirited him away, sleeping in her arms, from whence the Greeks later tore him.

che mi scoss’ io, sì come da la faccia
mi fuggì ’l sonno, e diventa’ ismorto,
come fa l’uom che, spaventato, agghiaccia.

that I started, as from my face sleep fled, and I became pale, as a man does who, frightened, grows cold.

Dallato m’era solo il mio conforto,
e ’l sole er’ alto già più che due ore,
e ’l viso m’era a la marina torto.

My only comfort now was the sea's embrace, and the sun was already more than two hours high, and my face was turned towards the shore.

«Non aver tema», disse il mio segnore;
«fatti sicur, ché noi semo a buon punto;
non stringer, ma rallarga ogne vigore.

"Have no fear," my guide said,"Take heart, for we are in a good place;Don't strain, but release all your strength."

Tu se’ omai al purgatorio giunto:
vedi là il balzo che ’l chiude dintorno;
vedi l’entrata là ’ve par digiunto.

You have now reached Purgatory: see there the cliff that encloses it round about; see the entrance where it seems disjoined.

Dianzi, ne l’alba che procede al giorno,
quando l’anima tua dentro dormia,
sovra li fiori ond’ è là giù addorno

Drawn by dawn's light, that precedes the day,when your soul still slept within,above the flowers that bloom below the way.

venne una donna, e disse: “I’ son Lucia;
lasciatemi pigliar costui che dorme;
sì l’agevolerò per la sua via”.

Then came a woman, and she said, “I am Lucia;let me take him who sleeps;I shall ease his path.”

Sordel rimase e l’altre genti forme;
ella ti tolse, e come ’l dì fu chiaro,
sen venne suso; e io per le sue orme.

Sordello remained, and the other shades as well. She took you, and when the day grew bright, she went upwards; and I followed in her footsteps.

Qui ti posò, ma pria mi dimostraro
li occhi suoi belli quella intrata aperta;
poi ella e ’l sonno ad una se n’andaro».

Here, she set you down, but first, her lovely eyes showed me that open entrance; then she and sleep departed together.

A guisa d’uom che ’n dubbio si raccerta
e che muta in conforto sua paura,
poi che la verità li è discoperta,

In the manner of a man who reassures himself in doubt, who turns his fear to comfort once truth has been revealed to him,

mi cambia’ io; e come sanza cura
vide me ’l duca mio, su per lo balzo
si mosse, e io di rietro inver’ l’altura.

I changed, and when my guide saw me thus, without a care, he moved up the ridge, and I followed him towards the height.

Lettor, tu vedi ben com’ io innalzo
la mia matera, e però con più arte
non ti maravigliar s’io la rincalzo.

Reader, you see how I raise my subject, and therefore do not be surprised if I reinforce it with more skill.

Noi ci appressammo, ed eravamo in parte
che là dove pareami prima rotto,
pur come un fesso che muro diparte,

We drew closer, and we were at a placethat seemed broken before,just like a fissure that cleaves a wall.

vidi una porta, e tre gradi di sotto
per gire ad essa, di color diversi,
e un portier ch’ancor non facea motto.

I saw a door, and three steps below,to reach it, of different hues,and a gatekeeper who still didn't speak.

E come l’occhio più e più v’apersi,
vidil seder sovra ’l grado sovrano,
tal ne la faccia ch’io non lo soffersi;

And as my eye opened wider and wider, I saw him seated above the highest step, his face such that I could not bear it;

e una spada nuda avëa in mano,
che reflettëa i raggi sì ver’ noi,
ch’io drizzava spesso il viso in vano.

and he held a naked sword in his hand, which reflected light towards us so intensely that I often raised my face in vain.

«Dite costinci: che volete voi?»,
cominciò elli a dire, «ov’ è la scorta?
Guardate che ’l venir sù non vi nòi».

"Tell me, you there: what do you want?" he began to say. "Where is your guide? Be careful that coming up here doesn't harm you."

«Donna del ciel, di queste cose accorta»,
rispuose ’l mio maestro a lui, «pur dianzi
ne disse: “Andate là: quivi è la porta”».

"Lady of Heaven, wise in these matters," my master answered him, "she just now told us: 'Go there: there is the gate.'"

«Ed ella i passi vostri in bene avanzi»,
ricominciò il cortese portinaio:
«Venite dunque a’ nostri gradi innanzi».

"And may she further your steps," the courteous gatekeeper began again, "then come forward, to our steps."

Là ne venimmo; e lo scaglion primaio
bianco marmo era sì pulito e terso,
ch’io mi specchiai in esso qual io paio.

There we arrived; the first step was white marble, so polished and clear that I saw myself reflected in it.

Era il secondo tinto più che perso,
d’una petrina ruvida e arsiccia,
crepata per lo lungo e per traverso.

It was the second step, stained rather than worn, of a rough, scorched stone, cracked lengthwise and across.

Lo terzo, che di sopra s’ammassiccia,
porfido mi parea, sì fiammeggiante
come sangue che fuor di vena spiccia.

The third, which was piled above, seemed to me porphyry, so fiery as blood spurting from a vein.

Sovra questo tenëa ambo le piante
l’angel di Dio sedendo in su la soglia
che mi sembiava pietra di diamante.

Above these, both feet held,God’s angel sat upon the threshold,seeming to me a stone of diamond.

Per li tre gradi sù di buona voglia
mi trasse il duca mio, dicendo: «Chiedi
umilemente che ’l serrame scioglia».

Up the three steps, he drew me with good will,my guide, who said: "Ask humbly that he unbar the gate."

Divoto mi gittai a’ santi piedi;
misericordia chiesi e ch’el m’aprisse,
ma tre volte nel petto pria mi diedi.

Devoutly, I knelt at his holy feet; I begged for mercy and that he would open for me, but first I struck my chest three times.

Sette P ne la fronte mi descrisse
col punton de la spada, e «Fa che lavi,
quando se’ dentro, queste piaghe» disse.

Seven P's he inscribed on my browwith the point of his sword, and said, "See that you wash these wounds when you are within."

Cenere, o terra che secca si cavi,
d’un color fora col suo vestimento;
e di sotto da quel trasse due chiavi.

Ashes, or dry earth that one might carve,of a single hue was its raiment's color;and from beneath that he drew two keys.

L’una era d’oro e l’altra era d’argento;
pria con la bianca e poscia con la gialla
fece a la porta sì, ch’i’ fu’ contento.

One was of gold and the other of silver; first with the white one, and then with the yellow, he did to the door such that I was content.

«Quandunque l’una d’este chiavi falla,
che non si volga dritta per la toppa»,
diss’ elli a noi, «non s’apre questa calla.

"Whenever one of these keys fails, so that it does not turn straight in the lock," he said to us, "this door will not open."

Più cara è l’una; ma l’altra vuol troppa
d’arte e d’ingegno avanti che diserri,
perch’ ella è quella che ’l nodo digroppa.

The first one is more precious; but the other one requires too much art and ingenuity before it can be unlocked, because it is the one that unravels the knot.

Da Pier le tegno; e dissemi ch’i’ erri
anzi ad aprir ch’a tenerla serrata,
pur che la gente a’ piedi mi s’atterri».

"I keep it from Pier," and he told me that I errmore in opening it than in keeping it locked,as long as the people fall at my feet."

Poi pinse l’uscio a la porta sacrata,
dicendo: «Intrate; ma facciovi accorti
che di fuor torna chi ’n dietro si guata».

Then he pushed the door to the sacred gate, saying: "Enter; but I make you aware that whoever looks back returns outside."

E quando fuor ne’ cardini distorti
li spigoli di quella regge sacra,
che di metallo son sonanti e forti,

And when, on hinges warped, the corners of that sacred gate, of sounding, solid metal, yielded.

non rugghiò sì né si mostrò sì acra
Tarpëa, come tolto le fu il buono
Metello, per che poi rimase macra.

Nor did Tarpeia roar so loud, nor show herself so bitter, as when the good Metellus was taken from her, because of which she later remained emaciated.

Io mi rivolsi attento al primo tuono,
e ‘Te Deum laudamus’ mi parea
udire in voce mista al dolce suono.

I turned my attention to the first sound, and "Te Deum laudamus" I seemed to hear in a voice mixed with the sweet sound.

Tale imagine a punto mi rendea
ciò ch’io udiva, qual prender si suole
quando a cantar con organi si stea;

This image, at that point, made me understandwhat I was hearing, as one usually doeswhen singing with organs.

ch’or sì or no s’intendon le parole.

Now yes, now no, the words are understood.

Inferno * Canto XXII

Escorted by a squad of ten Malebranche demons, Dante and Virgil walk along the bank of the fifth bolgia, a ditch filled with boiling pitch. The demons use their hooks to snag a sinner, Ciampolo of Navarre, pulling him from the boiling lake. At Virgil’s prompting, the sinner identifies himself and names other grafters hidden in the pitch, including Friar Gomita and Michel Zanche. To engineer his escape, Ciampolo offers to trick his fellow sinners into surfacing. He persuades the demons to stand back, and as soon as they do, he dives back into the pitch. Enraged by the deception, two demons, Alichino and Calcabrina, begin to fight, tumbling into the pitch themselves and becoming stuck. While the other demons are preoccupied with rescuing their ensnared comrades, Dante and Virgil use the distraction to slip away and continue their journey.

Inferno • Canto XXII

Io vidi già cavalier muover campo,
e cominciare stormo e far lor mostra,
e talvolta partir per loro scampo;

I have seen knights break camp, begin a skirmish, and put on a show of force, sometimes retreating to save themselves.

corridor vidi per la terra vostra,
o Aretini, e vidi gir gualdane,
fedir torneamenti e correr giostra;

I have seen the countryside of your land, oh citizens of Arezzo, and I have seen troops moving, tournaments fought, and jousts run.

quando con trombe, e quando con campane,
con tamburi e con cenni di castella,
e con cose nostrali e con istrane;

sometimes with trumpets, and sometimes with bells,with drums and with signals from castles,and with things of our land and with foreign ones;

né già con sì diversa cennamella
cavalier vidi muover né pedoni,
né nave a segno di terra o di stella.

Never before, with such varied instruments, have I seen knights or foot soldiers move, nor a ship toward a point on land or a star.

Noi andavam con li diece demoni.
Ahi fiera compagnia! ma ne la chiesa
coi santi, e in taverna coi ghiottoni.

We were going with the ten demons.Ah, a fierce company! But in church with the saints, and in taverns with gluttons.

Pur a la pegola era la mia ’ntesa,
per veder de la bolgia ogne contegno
e de la gente ch’entro v’era incesa.


Come i dalfini, quando fanno segno
a’ marinar con l’arco de la schiena
che s’argomentin di campar lor legno,

Like dolphins, when they signal to sailors with the arch of their backs, urging them to save their vessel,

talor così, ad alleggiar la pena,
mostrav’ alcun de’ peccatori ’l dosso
e nascondea in men che non balena.

In this way, to lighten their torment, one of the sinners would show his back, only to disappear faster than a flash of lightning.

E come a l’orlo de l’acqua d’un fosso
stanno i ranocchi pur col muso fuori,
sì che celano i piedi e l’altro grosso,

And as frogs at the edge of a ditch stand,with only their snouts poking out,hiding their feet and the rest of their bulk,

sì stavan d’ogne parte i peccatori;
ma come s’appressava Barbariccia,
così si ritraén sotto i bollori.

So stood the sinners on every side; but as Barbariccia drew near, they retreated beneath the boiling pitch.

I’ vidi, e anco il cor me n’accapriccia,
uno aspettar così, com’ elli ’ncontra
ch’una rana rimane e l’altra spiccia;

I saw it, and my heart still shudders, one waiting there, just as it happens when one frog remains and the other leaps away.

e Graffiacan, che li era più di contra,
li arruncigliò le ’mpegolate chiome
e trassel sù, che mi parve una lontra.

And Graffiacane, who was positioned directly opposite him, raked his claws through his matted hair and dragged him up, so that he seemed like an otter to me.

I’ sapea già di tutti quanti ’l nome,
sì li notai quando fuorono eletti,
e poi ch’e’ si chiamaro, attesi come.

I already knew all their names, I had taken note of them when they were chosen, and then I listened to how they were called.

«O Rubicante, fa che tu li metti
li unghioni a dosso, sì che tu lo scuoi!»,
gridavan tutti insieme i maladetti.

"Oh Rubicante, use your claws on him, peel off his skin!" the damned ones all cried together.

E io: «Maestro mio, fa, se tu puoi,
che tu sappi chi è lo sciagurato
venuto a man de li avversari suoi».

And I: "My master, see, if you are able, that you discover who this wretched man is, come into the hands of his adversaries."

Lo duca mio li s’accostò allato;
domandollo ond’ ei fosse, e quei rispuose:
«I’ fui del regno di Navarra nato.

My guide then drew near to him,and asked him who he was. He answered:"I was born in the kingdom of Navarre."

Mia madre a servo d’un segnor mi puose,
che m’avea generato d’un ribaldo,
distruggitor di sé e di sue cose.

My mother placed me as a servant to a lord, who had fathered me with a rogue, a waster of himself and his belongings.

Poi fui famiglia del buon re Tebaldo;
quivi mi misi a far baratteria,
di ch’io rendo ragione in questo caldo».

Then I served good King Thibault; there I took to petty thievery, for which I now account in this heat.

E Cirïatto, a cui di bocca uscia
d’ogne parte una sanna come a porco,
li fé sentir come l’una sdruscia.

And Cirïatto, whose mouth bore a tusk on each side like a boar, made him feel how one of them ripped.

Tra male gatte era venuto ’l sorco;
ma Barbariccia il chiuse con le braccia
e disse: «State in là, mentr’ io lo ’nforco».

The mouse had come among the wicked cats; but Barbariccia grabbed him with his arms and said: "Stay back while I impale him."

E al maestro mio volse la faccia;
«Domanda», disse, «ancor, se più disii
saper da lui, prima ch’altri ’l disfaccia».

And he turned his face toward my master. "Ask him again," he said, "if you desire to learn more from him, before another tears him apart."

Lo duca dunque: «Or dì: de li altri rii
conosci tu alcun che sia latino
sotto la pece?». E quelli: «I’ mi partii,

The guide then said: "Now tell me: do you know any other Italians here among the sinners under the pitch?" And he replied: "I departed..."

poco è, da un che fu di là vicino.
Così foss’ io ancor con lui coperto,
ch’i’ non temerei unghia né uncino!».

"Not long ago, he was there, nearby.""If I were still covered with him, I wouldn't fear claw or hook!"

E Libicocco «Troppo avem sofferto»,
disse; e preseli ’l braccio col runciglio,
sì che, stracciando, ne portò un lacerto.

And Libicocco said, "We've suffered too much," and with his grappling hook, he seized him by the arm, and tearing, he carried off a piece.

Draghignazzo anco i volle dar di piglio
giuso a le gambe; onde ’l decurio loro
si volse intorno intorno con mal piglio.

Draghignazzo also wanted to grab hold of him, down by the legs. Whereupon, the captain turned round and round, looking angry.

Quand’ elli un poco rappaciati fuoro,
a lui, ch’ancor mirava sua ferita,
domandò ’l duca mio sanza dimoro:

When they had calmed down a bit, my leader, without delay, asked him, who was still staring at his wound:

«Chi fu colui da cui mala partita
di’ che facesti per venire a proda?».
Ed ei rispuose: «Fu frate Gomita,

"Who was the one from whom you suffered such a wretched fate, that brought you to this shore?" And he replied: "It was Friar Gomita,

quel di Gallura, vasel d’ogne froda,
ch’ebbe i nemici di suo donno in mano,
e fé sì lor, che ciascun se ne loda.

that Gomita of Gallura, a vessel of all fraud, who held the enemies of his lord in his grasp, and did with them such things that each one of them praises him.

Danar si tolse e lasciolli di piano,
sì com’ e’ dice; e ne li altri offici anche
barattier fu non picciol, ma sovrano.

Danar took himself away and left them on the level, as he himself says; and in the other offices too, he was not a small-time grafter, but a sovereign one.

Usa con esso donno Michel Zanche
di Logodoro; e a dir di Sardigna
le lingue lor non si sentono stanche.

With him, Don Michel Zanche of Logodoro, and of Sardinia, their tongues feel no weariness in speaking.

Omè, vedete l’altro che digrigna;
i’ direi anche, ma i’ temo ch’ello
non s’apparecchi a grattarmi la tigna».

Alas, see the other one, he's baring his teeth; I'd speak as well, but I fear he'll get ready to scratch my head.

E ’l gran proposto, vòlto a Farfarello
che stralunava li occhi per fedire,
disse: «Fatti ’n costà, malvagio uccello!».

And the great one, turning to Farfarello, who was rolling his eyes as if to strike, said: "Get back there, wicked bird!"

«Se voi volete vedere o udire»,
ricominciò lo spaürato appresso,
«Toschi o Lombardi, io ne farò venire;

"If you want to see or hear,"the frightened one began again,"Tuscans or Lombards, I will bring them forth;

ma stieno i Malebranche un poco in cesso,
sì ch’ei non teman de le lor vendette;
e io, seggendo in questo loco stesso,

but let the Malebranche hold back a little,so they won't fear their revenges;and I, sitting in this very place,

per un ch’io son, ne farò venir sette
quand’ io suffolerò, com’ è nostro uso
di fare allor che fori alcun si mette».

because of one I am, I will bring forth sevenwhen I whistle, as is our customto do when someone steps outside.

Cagnazzo a cotal motto levò ’l muso,
crollando ’l capo, e disse: «Odi malizia
ch’elli ha pensata per gittarsi giuso!».

Cagnazzo lifted his snout at these words, shaking his head, and said: "Listen to the trick he has thought up to throw himself down!"

Ond’ ei, ch’avea lacciuoli a gran divizia,
rispuose: «Malizioso son io troppo,
quand’ io procuro a’ mia maggior trestizia».

And he, who had a great abundance of snares, replied: "I am far too malicious, when I seek out greater misery for myself."

Alichin non si tenne e, di rintoppo
a li altri, disse a lui: «Se tu ti cali,
io non ti verrò dietro di gualoppo,

Alichino didn't hold back, and, interrupting the others, said to him: "If you go down, I won't come after you at a gallop,

ma batterò sovra la pece l’ali.
Lascisi ’l collo, e sia la ripa scudo,
a veder se tu sol più di noi vali».

but I will beat my wings above the pitch. Let's leave, and let the bank be a shield, to see if you alone are worth more than us."

O tu che leggi, udirai nuovo ludo:
ciascun da l’altra costa li occhi volse,
quel prima, ch’a ciò fare era più crudo.

O you who read, you will hear a new game: each turned their eyes to the opposite bank, that one first, who was the most cruel in doing so.

Lo Navarrese ben suo tempo colse;
fermò le piante a terra, e in un punto
saltò e dal proposto lor si sciolse.

Then the Navarrese seized his moment; he planted his feet on the ground and, in an instant, leaped and broke free from their plan.

Di che ciascun di colpa fu compunto,
ma quei più che cagion fu del difetto;
però si mosse e gridò: «Tu se’ giunto!».

Of which each one was stung with guilt, but that one most who was the cause of the fault; therefore he moved and cried out: "You have arrived!"

Ma poco i valse: ché l’ali al sospetto
non potero avanzar; quelli andò sotto,
e quei drizzò volando suso il petto:

But little it helped him, for the wings of suspicion could not make headway. The first plunged under, and the second straightened, soaring upward on his chest.

non altrimenti l’anitra di botto,
quando ’l falcon s’appressa, giù s’attuffa,
ed ei ritorna sù crucciato e rotto.

Just like a duck, when a falcon nears, dives suddenly under, and he returns up, frustrated and defeated.

Irato Calcabrina de la buffa,
volando dietro li tenne, invaghito
che quei campasse per aver la zuffa;

Enraged, Calcabrina, of the wicked face, followed them, flying, obsessed with the idea that they might survive, so he could continue the fight.

e come ’l barattier fu disparito,
così volse li artigli al suo compagno,
e fu con lui sopra ’l fosso ghermito.

And when the grafter disappeared, he turned his claws on his companion, and grappled with him over the ditch.

Ma l’altro fu bene sparvier grifagno
ad artigliar ben lui, e amendue
cadder nel mezzo del bogliente stagno.

But the other, a falcon of the griffon breed, was quickto clutch at him with his talons, and bothfell into the midst of the boiling swamp.

Lo caldo sghermitor sùbito fue;
ma però di levarsi era neente,
sì avieno inviscate l’ali sue.

Quick to clutch was the heat,but to rise was in vain,so mired were his wings.

Barbariccia, con li altri suoi dolente,
quattro ne fé volar da l’altra costa
con tutt’ i raffi, e assai prestamente

Barbariccia, with his companions in grief, sent four of them flying from the other bank, with all their hooks, and very quickly.

di qua, di là discesero a la posta;
porser li uncini verso li ’mpaniati,
ch’eran già cotti dentro da la crosta.

On this side and that, they descended to their station; they thrust their hooks toward the sinners, who were already cooked inside their crust.

E noi lasciammo lor così ’mpacciati.

And we left them thus entangled.

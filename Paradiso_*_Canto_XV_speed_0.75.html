Paradiso * Canto XV

In the Fifth Heaven of Mars, Dante and Beatrice stand before a vast cross formed by the shining lights of souls. A hymn emanates from the cross, but Dante cannot distinguish the individual words. A single, radiant soul detaches itself from the cross’s right arm and descends to its base, moving with the speed of a meteor. This spirit addresses Dante in Latin, but the speech is too profound for Dante to comprehend. At Beatrice's prompting, the soul agrees to speak more plainly. The spirit then reveals his identity in Italian: he is Cacciaguida, Dante’s great-great-grandfather. He joyfully greets his descendant, referring to himself as Dante’s “root” and stating that he has been waiting for him in this heaven for over five hundred years. He expresses his gratitude to Beatrice for granting Dante this vision.

Paradiso • Canto XV

Benigna volontade in che si liqua
sempre l’amor che drittamente spira,
come cupidità fa ne la iniqua,

Benign will, in which love, that breathes rightly, always liquefies itself, as cupidity does in the unjust.

silenzio puose a quella dolce lira,
e fece quïetar le sante corde
che la destra del cielo allenta e tira.

Silence it placed upon that sweet lyre,and quieted the holy chordsthat the right hand of heaven loosens and draws taut.

Come saranno a’ giusti preghi sorde
quelle sustanze che, per darmi voglia
ch’io le pregassi, a tacer fur concorde?

How will those substances be deaf to righteous prayers, which, to make me desire to pray to them, agreed to be silent?

Bene è che sanza termine si doglia
chi, per amor di cosa che non duri
etternalmente, quello amor si spoglia.

It is well that he grieve without end,who, for love of something that does not last eternally,abandons that love.

Quale per li seren tranquilli e puri
discorre ad ora ad or sùbito foco,
movendo li occhi che stavan sicuri,

As a sudden fire darts across serene, tranquil, and clear skies, stirring the eyes that had been at rest,

e pare stella che tramuti loco,
se non che da la parte ond’ e’ s’accende
nulla sen perde, ed esso dura poco:

like a star that shifts its place,except that from the part where it ignites,nothing is lost, and it itself endures but briefly.

tale dal corno che ’n destro si stende
a piè di quella croce corse un astro
de la costellazion che lì resplende;

then from the horn that stretches to the right,at the base of that cross, a star sped forthfrom the constellation that shines there bright;

né si partì la gemma dal suo nastro,
ma per la lista radïal trascorse,
che parve foco dietro ad alabastro.

Nor did the gem depart from its ribbon,but sped along the radiant stripe,appearing as fire behind alabaster.

Sì pïa l’ombra d’Anchise si porse,
se fede merta nostra maggior musa,
quando in Eliso del figlio s’accorse.

Thus, Anchises' shade inclined itself, if our greatest muse merits faith, when in Elysium he recognized his son.

«O sanguis meus, o superinfusa
gratïa Deï, sicut tibi cui
bis unquam celi ianüa reclusa?».

"Oh, my blood, oh grace poured down so abundantlyby God, to you, of all people, to whomhas the gate of heaven ever been twice opened?"

Così quel lume: ond’ io m’attesi a lui;
poscia rivolsi a la mia donna il viso,
e quinci e quindi stupefatto fui;

Thus spoke that light; and I turned to him. Then I turned my face to my lady, and was astonished both ways.

ché dentro a li occhi suoi ardeva un riso
tal, ch’io pensai co’ miei toccar lo fondo
de la mia gloria e del mio paradiso.

For within her eyes, a smile burned so bright, I believed that with my own, I had touched the depths of my glory and my paradise.

Indi, a udire e a veder giocondo,
giunse lo spirto al suo principio cose,
ch’io non lo ’ntesi, sì parlò profondo;

Indeed, in hearing and seeing joy, the spirit arrived at its original things, which I did not understand, so profoundly did it speak.

né per elezïon mi si nascose,
ma per necessità, ché ’l suo concetto
al segno d’i mortal si soprapuose.

Nor for election did it hide itself from me, but by necessity, for its conception surpassed the measure of mortals.

E quando l’arco de l’ardente affetto
fu sì sfogato, che ’l parlar discese
inver’ lo segno del nostro intelletto,

And when the arc of ardent affection was so discharged that speech descended toward the target of our intellect,

la prima cosa che per me s’intese,
«Benedetto sia tu», fu, «trino e uno,
che nel mio seme se’ tanto cortese!».

The first thing I understood was, "Blessed be you, one and three, who are so gracious to my seed!"

E seguì: «Grato e lontano digiuno,
tratto leggendo del magno volume
du’ non si muta mai bianco né bruno,

And he continued: "Grateful and distant fast, I understood,drawn forth by reading from the great volumethat never changes white nor brown,

solvuto hai, figlio, dentro a questo lume
in ch’io ti parlo, mercè di colei
ch’a l’alto volo ti vestì le piume.

You are now freed, my son, within this lightwhere I speak to you, through the grace of shewho clothed you with wings for your high flight.

Tu credi che a me tuo pensier mei
da quel ch’è primo, così come raia
da l’un, se si conosce, il cinque e ’l sei;

You believe that my thought comes to me, my son,from the First Cause, just as the five and sixdescend from one, if one understands it well.

e però ch’io mi sia e perch’ io paia
più gaudïoso a te, non mi domandi,
che alcun altro in questa turba gaia.

And though I may appear, and indeed am, more joyful to you than anyone else in this merry throng, you do not ask me why.

Tu credi ’l vero; ché i minori e ’ grandi
di questa vita miran ne lo speglio
in che, prima che pensi, il pensier pandi;

You believe the truth; for the small and the great of this life look into the mirror where, before you think, you reveal your thought.

ma perché ’l sacro amore in che io veglio
con perpetüa vista e che m’asseta
di dolce disïar, s’adempia meglio,

so that the sacred love in which I watch, with perpetual gaze, and that which quenches me with sweet longing, might be better fulfilled.

la voce tua sicura, balda e lieta
suoni la volontà, suoni ’l disio,
a che la mia risposta è già decreta!».

Your voice, confident, bold, and joyful, should voice your will, voice your desire, to which my response is already decreed!

Io mi volsi a Beatrice, e quella udio
pria ch’io parlassi, e arrisemi un cenno
che fece crescer l’ali al voler mio.

I turned to Beatrice, and she heard me before I spoke, and she gave me a nod that caused the wings of my will to grow.

Poi cominciai così: «L’affetto e ’l senno,
come la prima equalità v’apparse,
d’un peso per ciascun di voi si fenno,

Then I began: "Affection and intellect, when the first equality became apparent to you, were made of a single weight for each of you."

però che ’l sol che v’allumò e arse,
col caldo e con la luce è sì iguali,
che tutte simiglianze sono scarse.

but that the sun which illumined and burned you, with its heat and its light, is so perfectly matched that all comparisons fall short.

Ma voglia e argomento ne’ mortali,
per la cagion ch’a voi è manifesta,
diversamente son pennuti in ali;

But mortal will and reasoning, for the reason that is plain to you, have wings of different feathers.

ond’ io, che son mortal, mi sento in questa
disagguaglianza, e però non ringrazio
se non col core a la paterna festa.

And I, who am mortal, feel myself in this disparity, and therefore offer thanks only within my heart to the paternal feast.

Ben supplico io a te, vivo topazio
che questa gioia prezïosa ingemmi,
perché mi facci del tuo nome sazio».

Then I beseech you, living topaz, to gem this precious joy, that you may fill me with your name.

«O fronda mia in che io compiacemmi
pur aspettando, io fui la tua radice»:
cotal principio, rispondendo, femmi.

"O, my frond, in which I took delight, even while awaiting, I was your root": such a beginning he made in reply.

Poscia mi disse: «Quel da cui si dice
tua cognazione e che cent’ anni e piùe
girato ha ’l monte in la prima cornice,

Poi mi disse: "Colui da cui si diceche sei suo parente, e che più di cento anniha girato il monte nella prima cornice,

mio figlio fu e tuo bisavol fue:
ben si convien che la lunga fatica
tu li raccorci con l’opere tue.

"My son was he, and your great-great-grandfather; it is fitting that you, with your deeds, shorten his long labor."

Fiorenza dentro da la cerchia antica,
ond’ ella toglie ancora e terza e nona,
si stava in pace, sobria e pudica.

Florence, within the ancient walls, from which she still calls terce and nones, remained in peace, sober, and modest.

Non avea catenella, non corona,
non gonne contigiate, non cintura
che fosse a veder più che la persona.

She wore no chain, no crown, no patterned dresses, no belt that drew attention away from her person.

Non faceva, nascendo, ancor paura
la figlia al padre, che ’l tempo e la dote
non fuggien quinci e quindi la misura.

Born, the daughter still caused no fear in her father, for neither time nor dowry escaped their proper measure.

Non avea case di famiglia vòte;
non v’era giunto ancor Sardanapalo
a mostrar ciò che ’n camera si puote.

She had no empty family homes; Sardanapalus had not yet arrived to display what was possible in the chambers.

Non era vinto ancora Montemalo
dal vostro Uccellatoio, che, com’ è vinto
nel montar sù, così sarà nel calo.

Montemalo was not yet overcomeby your Uccellatoio, which, as it yieldsin the ascent, will similarly yield in descent.

Bellincion Berti vid’ io andar cinto
di cuoio e d’osso, e venir da lo specchio
la donna sua sanza ’l viso dipinto;

I saw Bellincion Berti, girded in leather and bone, and his lady come from her mirror without painted face.

e vidi quel d’i Nerli e quel del Vecchio
esser contenti a la pelle scoperta,
e le sue donne al fuso e al pennecchio.

And I saw those of the Nerli and those of the Vecchio content in their plain skin, and their ladies at the spindle and distaff.

Oh fortunate! ciascuna era certa
de la sua sepultura, e ancor nulla
era per Francia nel letto diserta.

Oh, how fortunate! Each person was certain of their burial place, and no bed was yet deserted in France.

L’una vegghiava a studio de la culla,
e, consolando, usava l’idïoma
che prima i padri e le madri trastulla;

One kept watch, intent on the cradle, and, offering comfort, used the language that first soothes fathers and mothers.

l’altra, traendo a la rocca la chioma,
favoleggiava con la sua famiglia
d’i Troiani, di Fiesole e di Roma.

The other, drawing out her hair with a spinning wheel, gossiped with her family about the Trojans, Fiesole, and Rome.

Saria tenuta allor tal maraviglia
una Cianghella, un Lapo Salterello,
qual or saria Cincinnato e Corniglia.

Such wonder would then be held for a Cianghella, a Lapo Salterello, as is now held for Cincinnatus and Cornelia.

A così riposato, a così bello
viver di cittadini, a così fida
cittadinanza, a così dolce ostello,

To such a tranquil, such a beautiful life of citizens, to such a loyal citizenry, to such a sweet dwelling,

Maria mi diè, chiamata in alte grida;
e ne l’antico vostro Batisteo
insieme fui cristiano e Cacciaguida.

Maria gave me to be, and I was called with loud cries; and in your ancient Baptistery together I was Christian and Cacciaguida.

Moronto fu mio frate ed Eliseo;
mia donna venne a me di val di Pado,
e quindi il sopranome tuo si feo.

Moronto was my brother, as was Eliseo; my lady came to me from the valley of the Po, and from her came your surname.

Poi seguitai lo ’mperador Currado;
ed el mi cinse de la sua milizia,
tanto per bene ovrar li venni in grado.

Then I followed the Emperor Conrad; and he girded me with his military order, so well did I serve that I earned his favor.

Dietro li andai incontro a la nequizia
di quella legge il cui popolo usurpa,
per colpa d’i pastor, vostra giustizia.

Behind those I then came to meet the wickedness of that law, whose people usurp your justice because of the shepherds.

Quivi fu’ io da quella gente turpa
disviluppato dal mondo fallace,
lo cui amor molt’ anime deturpa;

There I was, freed from that filthy people, removed from the deceitful world, whose love corrupts so many souls.

e venni dal martiro a questa pace».

And I came from torment to this peace."

Inferno * Canto III

Dante and Virgil arrive at the Gate of Hell and read its inscription. Passing through, they enter the Vestibule, a region for the souls of the uncommitted—those who lived without choosing good or evil—and the neutral angels. Here, on a dark plain, Dante observes these naked souls in a constant, frantic chase after a blank, shifting banner. They are perpetually stung by wasps and hornets, and their blood and tears fall to the ground where worms consume it. The poets proceed to the bank of the river Acheron, where they see a crowd of souls waiting to cross. The ferryman, Charon, challenges Dante’s presence as a living being. Virgil silences him by stating that the journey is divinely willed. As the despairing souls board the boat, the ground trembles violently and a flash of red light strikes the air, causing Dante to faint.

Inferno • Canto III

‘Per me si va ne la città dolente,
per me si va ne l’etterno dolore,
per me si va tra la perduta gente.

Through me you enter into the city of woe,through me you enter into eternal pain,through me you enter among the lost race.

Giustizia mosse il mio alto fattore;
fecemi la divina podestate,
la somma sapïenza e ’l primo amore.

Justice moved my high maker;divine power made me,highest wisdom, and primal love.

Dinanzi a me non fuor cose create
se non etterne, e io etterno duro.
Lasciate ogne speranza, voi ch’intrate’.

Before me, nothing was created save eternal things, and I endure eternally. Abandon all hope, ye who enter.

Queste parole di colore oscuro
vid’ ïo scritte al sommo d’una porta;
per ch’io: «Maestro, il senso lor m’è duro».

These words of a somber hueI saw inscribed above a gate;Whereat I: "Master, their meaning is obscure to me."

Ed elli a me, come persona accorta:
«Qui si convien lasciare ogne sospetto;
ogne viltà convien che qui sia morta.

And he to me, as one who understood: "Here it is fitting to abandon all mistrust; here all cowardice must die."

Noi siam venuti al loco ov’ i’ t’ho detto
che tu vedrai le genti dolorose
c’hanno perduto il ben de l’intelletto».

We have arrived at the place where I told youthat you would see the sorrowful peoplewho have lost the good of the intellect.”

E poi che la sua mano a la mia puose
con lieto volto, ond’ io mi confortai,
mi mise dentro a le segrete cose.

And after he placed his hand on minewith a joyful expression, by which I was comforted,he led me inside to the hidden things.

Quivi sospiri, pianti e alti guai
risonavan per l’aere sanza stelle,
per ch’io al cominciar ne lagrimai.

Here sighs, laments, and loud wailsechoed through the starless air,so that I began to weep.

Diverse lingue, orribili favelle,
parole di dolore, accenti d’ira,
voci alte e fioche, e suon di man con elle

Various languages, dreadful utterances,words of pain, accents of rage,high and faint voices, and the sound of hands with them.

facevano un tumulto, il qual s’aggira
sempre in quell’ aura sanza tempo tinta,
come la rena quando turbo spira.

They made a tumult, which whirls itselfalways in that timeless, colored air,like sand when a whirlwind breathes upon it.

E io ch’avea d’error la testa cinta,
dissi: «Maestro, che è quel ch’i’ odo?
e che gent’ è che par nel duol sì vinta?».

And I, whose head was encircled by error, said: "Master, what is it that I hear? And what people are these that seem so overcome by grief?"

Ed elli a me: «Questo misero modo
tegnon l’anime triste di coloro
che visser sanza ’nfamia e sanza lodo.

And he said to me: "This miserable state is held by the wretched souls of those who lived without infamy and without praise.

Mischiate sono a quel cattivo coro
de li angeli che non furon ribelli
né fur fedeli a Dio, ma per sé fuoro.

Mixed with them is that wretched choir of the angels who were not rebels, nor were they faithful to God, but were for themselves.

Caccianli i ciel per non esser men belli,
né lo profondo inferno li riceve,
ch’alcuna gloria i rei avrebber d’elli».

The heavens cast them out, so as not to be less beautiful, nor does deep Hell receive them, for the damned would have some glory from them.

E io: «Maestro, che è tanto greve
a lor che lamentar li fa sì forte?».
Rispuose: «Dicerolti molto breve.

E io: "Maestro, what weighs on them so heavilythat it makes them lament so loudly?"He replied: "I will tell you very briefly."

Questi non hanno speranza di morte,
e la lor cieca vita è tanto bassa,
che ’nvidïosi son d’ogne altra sorte.

These have no hope of death, and their blind life is so wretched that they are envious of every other fate.

Fama di loro il mondo esser non lassa;
misericordia e giustizia li sdegna:
non ragioniam di lor, ma guarda e passa».

The world will not let their fame exist; mercy and justice scorn them: let us not speak of them, but look and pass on."

E io, che riguardai, vidi una ’nsegna
che girando correva tanto ratta,
che d’ogne posa mi parea indegna;

And I, who looked on, saw a banner that, circling, ran so swiftly it seemed unworthy of any rest.

e dietro le venìa sì lunga tratta
di gente, ch’i’ non averei creduto
che morte tanta n’avesse disfatta.

And behind it came so long a line of people that I would not have believed death had undone so many.

Poscia ch’io v’ebbi alcun riconosciuto,
vidi e conobbi l’ombra di colui
che fece per viltade il gran rifiuto.

After I had recognized some, I saw and knew the shade of the one who made the great refusal out of cowardice.

Incontanente intesi e certo fui
che questa era la setta d’i cattivi,
a Dio spiacenti e a’ nemici sui.

Immediately, I understood, and I was certain that this was the faction of the wicked, displeasing to God and to His enemies.

Questi sciaurati, che mai non fur vivi,
erano ignudi e stimolati molto
da mosconi e da vespe ch’eran ivi.

These wretched souls, who had never truly lived, were naked and tormented by many flies and wasps that swarmed about them.

Elle rigavan lor di sangue il volto,
che, mischiato di lagrime, a’ lor piedi
da fastidiosi vermi era ricolto.

Flies and wasps, maddened by the blood that streamed down their faces, tormented them, and the blood, mingled with tears, was collected at their feet by vile worms.

E poi ch’a riguardar oltre mi diedi,
vidi genti a la riva d’un gran fiume;
per ch’io dissi: «Maestro, or mi concedi

And then, when I turned my gaze beyond, I saw people on the banks of a great river; at which point I said: "Master, now allow me..."

ch’i’ sappia quali sono, e qual costume
le fa di trapassar parer sì pronte,
com’ i’ discerno per lo fioco lume».

that I might know who they are, and what practicesmake them seem so eager to cross,as I make out in the dim light."

Ed elli a me: «Le cose ti fier conte
quando noi fermerem li nostri passi
su la trista riviera d’Acheronte».

And he to me: “The things will be made known to youwhen we halt our stepsupon the wretched river Acheron.”

Allor con li occhi vergognosi e bassi,
temendo no ’l mio dir li fosse grave,
infino al fiume del parlar mi trassi.

Then, with eyes ashamed and lowered, fearing my words might weigh heavily upon him, I spoke no more until we reached the river.

Ed ecco verso noi venir per nave
un vecchio, bianco per antico pelo,
gridando: «Guai a voi, anime prave!

And then, toward us, coming in a boat, appeared an old man, his hair white with age, shouting: "Woe to you, wicked souls!"

Non isperate mai veder lo cielo:
i’ vegno per menarvi a l’altra riva
ne le tenebre etterne, in caldo e ’n gelo.

Abandon all hope of ever seeing Heaven; I come to lead you to the other shore, into the eternal darkness, into fire and ice.

E tu che se’ costì, anima viva,
pàrtiti da cotesti che son morti».
Ma poi che vide ch’io non mi partiva,

And you there, still breathing, depart from these who are dead." But when he saw that I did not move,

disse: «Per altra via, per altri porti
verrai a piaggia, non qui, per passare:
più lieve legno convien che ti porti».

he said, "By another path, by other shoresyou'll come to land, not here, to pass across:a lighter craft must carry you instead."

E ’l duca lui: «Caron, non ti crucciare:
vuolsi così colà dove si puote
ciò che si vuole, e più non dimandare».

And the guide said to him: "Charon, do not rage:so it is willed where power is joined with will,and ask no more."

Quinci fuor quete le lanose gote
al nocchier de la livida palude,
che ’ntorno a li occhi avea di fiamme rote.

Then quieted the woolly cheeksof the ferryman of the livid swamp,whose eyes were circled by flames.

Ma quell’ anime, ch’eran lasse e nude,
cangiar colore e dibattero i denti,
ratto che ’nteser le parole crude.

But those souls, weary and naked, changed color and gnashed their teeth, as soon as they heard the cruel words.

Bestemmiavano Dio e lor parenti,
l’umana spezie e ’l loco e ’l tempo e ’l seme
di lor semenza e di lor nascimenti.

They blasphemed God and their parents, the human race, the place, the time, and the seed of their conception and birth.

Poi si ritrasser tutte quante insieme,
forte piangendo, a la riva malvagia
ch’attende ciascun uom che Dio non teme.

Then they all drew back together,weeping loudly, towards the wretched shorethat awaits every man who does not fear God.

Caron dimonio, con occhi di bragia
loro accennando, tutte le raccoglie;
batte col remo qualunque s’adagia.

Caron, the demon, with eyes like coals, beckons them all,collecting them; he strikes with his oar anyone who delays.

Come d’autunno si levan le foglie
l’una appresso de l’altra, fin che ’l ramo
vede a la terra tutte le sue spoglie,

As in autumn the leaves lift,one after another, until the branchsees all its spoils upon the earth,

similemente il mal seme d’Adamo
gittansi di quel lito ad una ad una,
per cenni come augel per suo richiamo.

Likewise, the evil seed of Adamcast themselves from that shore, one by one,at signals, as a bird to its lure.

Così sen vanno su per l’onda bruna,
e avanti che sien di là discese,
anche di qua nuova schiera s’auna.

Thus they journey on the dark wave, and before they have disembarked on the other side, a new throng gathers on this one.

«Figliuol mio», disse ’l maestro cortese,
«quelli che muoion ne l’ira di Dio
tutti convegnon qui d’ogne paese;

"My son," the courteous master said, "all those who die in God's wrath converge here from every land;

e pronti sono a trapassar lo rio,
ché la divina giustizia li sprona,
sì che la tema si volve in disio.

and they are eager to cross the river, for divine justice spurs them on, so that their fear turns to desire.

Quinci non passa mai anima buona;
e però, se Caron di te si lagna,
ben puoi sapere omai che ’l suo dir suona».

Here, no soul that is good ever passes; and therefore, if Charon complains of you, you can well know now what his words mean.

Finito questo, la buia campagna
tremò sì forte, che de lo spavento
la mente di sudore ancor mi bagna.

This said, the gloomy land trembled so violently that my mind still sweats with the terror.

La terra lagrimosa diede vento,
che balenò una luce vermiglia
la qual mi vinse ciascun sentimento;

The weeping earth unleashed a wind,that flashed a crimson light,which overwhelmed all my senses;

e caddi come l’uom cui sonno piglia.

And I fell, like a man overtaken by sleep.

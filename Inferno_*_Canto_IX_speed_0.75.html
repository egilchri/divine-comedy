Inferno * Canto IX

At the gates of the City of Dis, Virgil reassures a fearful Dante while they await entry. Suddenly, the three blood-stained Furies—Megaera, Alecto, and Tisiphone—appear on a high tower. They threaten the poets and summon Medusa to turn Dante to stone. Virgil immediately commands Dante to cover his eyes, placing his own hands over them for protection. A great blast of sound announces a heavenly messenger who walks across the Styx, parting the foul air before him. He arrives at the gate, rebukes the demons for their insolence, and touches the gate with a small wand, causing it to spring open. The messenger then departs without speaking to the poets. Dante and Virgil enter the city and discover a vast plain filled with open, fiery tombs from which laments can be heard. Virgil explains that these sepulchers contain the heretics.

Inferno • Canto IX

Quel color che viltà di fuor mi pinse
veggendo il duca mio tornare in volta,
più tosto dentro il suo novo ristrinse.

The color that cowardice had painted on my face, seeing my guide turn back, quickly tightened within me.

Attento si fermò com’ uom ch’ascolta;
ché l’occhio nol potea menare a lunga
per l’aere nero e per la nebbia folta.

Attentive, he stopped like a man who listens; for his eye could not see far through the black air and the thick fog.

«Pur a noi converrà vincer la punga»,
cominciò el, «se non . . . Tal ne s’offerse.
Oh quanto tarda a me ch’altri qui giunga!».

"We must win this fight," he began, "if not... Such a thing has presented itself to us. Oh, how it pains me that another arrives here!"

I’ vidi ben sì com’ ei ricoperse
lo cominciar con l’altro che poi venne,
che fur parole a le prime diverse;

I saw quite well how he overlaid the beginning with what came after, since those later words were different from the first.

ma nondimen paura il suo dir dienne,
perch’ io traeva la parola tronca
forse a peggior sentenzia che non tenne.

But fear still colored his words,because I was interpreting his broken phrase,perhaps drawing a worse meaning than he intended.

«In questo fondo de la trista conca
discende mai alcun del primo grado,
che sol per pena ha la speranza cionca?».

"Does anyone ever descend into this pit of the mournful abyss from the first circle, where hope is the only suffering?"

Questa question fec’ io; e quei «Di rado
incontra», mi rispuose, «che di noi
faccia il cammino alcun per qual io vado.

This question I put; and he replied, "Rarely does one of us find the path I tread."

Ver è ch’altra fïata qua giù fui,
congiurato da quella Eritón cruda
che richiamava l’ombre a’ corpi sui.

It is true that I was once below, conjured there by that cruel Erichtho, who called the shades back to their bodies.

Di poco era di me la carne nuda,
ch’ella mi fece intrar dentr’ a quel muro,
per trarne un spirto del cerchio di Giuda.

My bare flesh had nearly disappeared when she forced me to enter that wall, to draw out a spirit from the circle of Judas.

Quell’ è ’l più basso loco e ’l più oscuro,
e ’l più lontan dal ciel che tutto gira:
ben so ’l cammin; però ti fa sicuro.

That is the lowest place, the darkest, and the farthest from the heaven that turns all things. I know the path well; therefore, be assured.

Questa palude che ’l gran puzzo spira
cigne dintorno la città dolente,
u’ non potemo intrare omai sanz’ ira».

This swamp, which exhales a great stench, encircles the sorrowful city, where we cannot now enter without anger.”

E altro disse, ma non l’ho a mente;
però che l’occhio m’avea tutto tratto
ver’ l’alta torre a la cima rovente,

And he said more, though I do not recall it; for my eye was entirely drawn towards the tall tower whose top was aflame.

dove in un punto furon dritte ratto
tre furïe infernal di sangue tinte,
che membra feminine avieno e atto,

There, at a certain point, three infernal furies, stained with blood, shot forward, with feminine limbs and bearing.

e con idre verdissime eran cinte;
serpentelli e ceraste avien per crine,
onde le fiere tempie erano avvinte.

And they were girded with verdant hydras; little serpents and horned asps served as their hair, with which their savage temples were bound.

E quei, che ben conobbe le meschine
de la regina de l’etterno pianto,
«Guarda», mi disse, «le feroci Erine.

And he, who well knew the wretched ones of the queen of eternal weeping, said to me, "Behold the fierce Erinyes."

Quest’ è Megera dal sinistro canto;
quella che piange dal destro è Aletto;
Tesifón è nel mezzo»; e tacque a tanto.

"This is Megaera, who sings with a mournful voice; the one wailing on the right is Alecto; Tisiphone is in the middle," and then he fell silent.

Con l’unghie si fendea ciascuna il petto;
battiensi a palme e gridavan sì alto,
ch’i’ mi strinsi al poeta per sospetto.

With their nails, each tore at her breast; they beat themselves with their palms and cried so loudly that I, in fear, drew close to the poet.

«Vegna Medusa: sì ’l farem di smalto»,
dicevan tutte riguardando in giuso;
«mal non vengiammo in Tesëo l’assalto».

"By Medusa's name: we shall glaze him," they all said, staring down. "We did not take vengeance in Theseus's assault."

«Volgiti ’n dietro e tien lo viso chiuso;
ché se ’l Gorgón si mostra e tu ’l vedessi,
nulla sarebbe di tornar mai suso».

"Turn back and keep your eyes shut tight;for if you see the Gorgon and look upon her,there would be no return to the world above."

Così disse ’l maestro; ed elli stessi
mi volse, e non si tenne a le mie mani,
che con le sue ancor non mi chiudessi.

So spoke the master; and he himselfturned me, and did not rely on my hands,so that he didn't close my eyes with his own.

O voi ch’avete li ’ntelletti sani,
mirate la dottrina che s’asconde
sotto ’l velame de li versi strani.

O you who have sound intellects, consider the doctrine that hides itself beneath the veil of these strange verses.

E già venìa su per le torbide onde
un fracasso d’un suon, pien di spavento,
per cui tremavano amendue le sponde,

And already there came up from the murky waves a crashing sound, full of terror, because of which both shores trembled.

non altrimenti fatto che d’un vento
impetüoso per li avversi ardori,
che fier la selva e sanz’ alcun rattento

than a wind,impetuous because of opposing heat,that strikes the forest, without any pause.

li rami schianta, abbatte e porta fori;
dinanzi polveroso va superbo,
e fa fuggir le fiere e li pastori.

The branches it shatters, fells, and carries away;before it, dust-filled, it strides in its pride,and puts to flight the beasts and the shepherds.

Li occhi mi sciolse e disse: «Or drizza il nerbo
del viso su per quella schiuma antica
per indi ove quel fummo è più acerbo».

He loosened my eyes and said: "Now direct the strength of your gaze toward that ancient foam, from there where the smoke is most acrid."

Come le rane innanzi a la nimica
biscia per l’acqua si dileguan tutte,
fin ch’a la terra ciascuna s’abbica,

As frogs before their enemy,the serpent, scatter across the water,until each one huddles at the shore,

vid’ io più di mille anime distrutte
fuggir così dinanzi ad un ch’al passo
passava Stige con le piante asciutte.

I saw more than a thousand ruined soulsflee in this way before one who crossedthe Styx with dry feet.

Dal volto rimovea quell’ aere grasso,
menando la sinistra innanzi spesso;
e sol di quell’ angoscia parea lasso.

From his face, he brushed away that heavy air, often moving his left hand forward, and he seemed wearied only by that anguish.

Ben m’accorsi ch’elli era da ciel messo,
e volsimi al maestro; e quei fé segno
ch’i’ stessi queto ed inchinassi ad esso.

Then I realized he was sent from heaven, and I turned to my master, who signaled that I should be still and bow before him.

Ahi quanto mi parea pien di disdegno!
Venne a la porta e con una verghetta
l’aperse, che non v’ebbe alcun ritegno.

Alas, how full of disdain he seemed! He came to the gate and with a small rod opened it, and there was no resistance.

«O cacciati del ciel, gente dispetta»,
cominciò elli in su l’orribil soglia,
«ond’ esta oltracotanza in voi s’alletta?

"O you cast from Heaven, spiteful people," he began on the horrible threshold, "where does this arrogance tempt you?"

Perché recalcitrate a quella voglia
a cui non puote il fin mai esser mozzo,
e che più volte v’ha cresciuta doglia?

Why do you struggle against that desirewhich cannot be cut short,and which has increased your suffering many times?

Che giova ne le fata dar di cozzo?
Cerbero vostro, se ben vi ricorda,
ne porta ancor pelato il mento e ’l gozzo».

What good does it do to kick against fate?Your Cerberus, if you recall correctly,still bears the marks, his chin and throat all shaved.

Poi si rivolse per la strada lorda,
e non fé motto a noi, ma fé sembiante
d’omo cui altra cura stringa e morda

Then he turned back along the filthy road, and he said not a word to us, but wore the expression of a man whom some other worry grips and gnaws.

che quella di colui che li è davante;
e noi movemmo i piedi inver’ la terra,
sicuri appresso le parole sante.

than that of the one before him;and we moved our feet toward the earth,trusting in the holy words.

Dentro li ’ntrammo sanz’ alcuna guerra;
e io, ch’avea di riguardar disio
la condizion che tal fortezza serra,

Inside we entered without any conflict; and I, who desired to observe the condition that enclosed such a fortress,

com’ io fui dentro, l’occhio intorno invio:
e veggio ad ogne man grande campagna,
piena di duolo e di tormento rio.

as soon as I was inside, I sent my eye around: and I saw on every side a great expanse, filled with pain and wicked torment.

Sì come ad Arli, ove Rodano stagna,
sì com’ a Pola, presso del Carnaro
ch’Italia chiude e suoi termini bagna,

As at Arles, where the Rhône stagnates, as at Pola, near the Quarnero, which confines Italy and bathes its borders,

fanno i sepulcri tutt’ il loco varo,
così facevan quivi d’ogne parte,
salvo che ’l modo v’era più amaro;

The tombs make every place grim, as they did there on every side, except the manner was more bitter.

ché tra li avelli fiamme erano sparte,
per le quali eran sì del tutto accesi,
che ferro più non chiede verun’ arte.

For among the tombs, flames were scattered, by which they were so utterly ignited that no art requires more heat than this.

Tutti li lor coperchi eran sospesi,
e fuor n’uscivan sì duri lamenti,
che ben parean di miseri e d’offesi.

All their lids were suspended, and from them issued such harsh laments that they clearly seemed to belong to the wretched and the offended.

E io: «Maestro, quai son quelle genti
che, seppellite dentro da quell’ arche,
si fan sentir coi sospiri dolenti?».

And I: "Master, who are those people who, buried within those tombs, make themselves heard with sorrowful sighs?"

E quelli a me: «Qui son li eresïarche
con lor seguaci, d’ogne setta, e molto
più che non credi son le tombe carche.

And they to me: "Here are the heresiarchs and their followers, from every sect, and the tombs are far more laden than you believe.

Simile qui con simile è sepolto,
e i monimenti son più e men caldi».
E poi ch’a la man destra si fu vòlto,

"Like is buried with like," and the monuments are more and less warm." And after he turned to the right,

passammo tra i martìri e li alti spaldi.

We passed between the tortures and the high ramparts.

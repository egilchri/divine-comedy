Inferno * Canto II

As evening falls, Dante expresses fear and doubt to Virgil, declaring himself unworthy of the impending journey. He compares his situation unfavorably to the underworld journeys of Aeneas and Saint Paul. In response, Virgil rebukes Dante’s cowardice and explains the reason for his mission. Virgil recounts that he was in Limbo when a blessed lady, Beatrice, descended from Heaven to summon him. Beatrice, moved by love, explained that she had learned of Dante’s struggle from Saint Lucia. Lucia, in turn, had been sent by the Virgin Mary, who had taken pity on Dante’s plight in the dark wood. Upon hearing that three such blessed ladies in Heaven are concerned for his well-being, Dante’s courage is restored. He compares his renewed spirit to flowers reviving in the morning sun. He then affirms his resolve, and the two poets proceed together onto the "deep and savage path."

Inferno • Canto II

Lo giorno se n’andava, e l’aere bruno
toglieva li animai che sono in terra
da le fatiche loro; e io sol uno

The day was departing, and the darkening air was freeing the creatures of the earth from their labors; and I alone

m’apparecchiava a sostener la guerra
sì del cammino e sì de la pietate,
che ritrarrà la mente che non erra.

was preparing myself to endure the war, both of the journey and of pity, which will recount the mind that errs not.

O muse, o alto ingegno, or m’aiutate;
o mente che scrivesti ciò ch’io vidi,
qui si parrà la tua nobilitate.

O Muse, O lofty intellect, now assist me; O mind that wrote down what I saw, here will your nobility be revealed.

Io cominciai: «Poeta che mi guidi,
guarda la mia virtù s’ell’ è possente,
prima ch’a l’alto passo tu mi fidi.

I began: "Poet, you who guide me, consider my strength, see if it is sufficient before you trust me to the high passage."

Tu dici che di Silvïo il parente,
corruttibile ancora, ad immortale
secolo andò, e fu sensibilmente.

You say that the ancestor of Silvio, still subject to decay, went into the immortal world, and did so perceptibly.

Però, se l’avversario d’ogne male
cortese i fu, pensando l’alto effetto
ch’uscir dovea di lui, e ’l chi e ’l quale

But if the adversary of all evil was kind to him, considering the lofty outcome that would come from him, and who and what he was.

non pare indegno ad omo d’intelletto;
ch’e’ fu de l’alma Roma e di suo impero
ne l’empireo ciel per padre eletto:

It does not seem unworthy of an intelligent man, for he was chosen in the Empyrean heaven as father of noble Rome and of its empire.

la quale e ’l quale, a voler dir lo vero,
fu stabilita per lo loco santo
u’ siede il successor del maggior Piero.

Which and who, to tell the truth, were established for the holy place where sits the successor of the great Peter.

Per quest’ andata onde li dai tu vanto,
intese cose che furon cagione
di sua vittoria e del papale ammanto.

Through this journey, of which you boast, he understood things that were the cause of his victory and the papal mantle.

Andovvi poi lo Vas d’elezïone,
per recarne conforto a quella fede
ch’è principio a la via di salvazione.

Then went the vessel of election, to bring comfort to that faith which is the beginning of the path to salvation.

Ma io, perché venirvi? o chi ’l concede?
Io non Enëa, io non Paulo sono;
me degno a ciò né io né altri ’l crede.

But I, why should I come? Or who allows it? I am not Aeneas, I am not Paul; neither I nor anyone else considers me worthy.

Per che, se del venire io m’abbandono,
temo che la venuta non sia folle.
Se’ savio; intendi me’ ch’i’ non ragiono».

For if I give myself to the idea of coming, I fear that my arrival will be foolish. You are wise; you understand better than I can explain.

E qual è quei che disvuol ciò che volle
e per novi pensier cangia proposta,
sì che dal cominciar tutto si tolle,

And like one who renounces what he once desired, and with new thoughts changes his intent, so that he abandons the whole endeavor from its start,

tal mi fec’ ïo ’n quella oscura costa,
perché, pensando, consumai la ’mpresa
che fu nel cominciar cotanto tosta.

Such did I become on that shadowy slope, because, in thinking, I consumed the undertaking that had been so swift in its beginning.

«S’i’ ho ben la parola tua intesa»,
rispuose del magnanimo quell’ ombra,
«l’anima tua è da viltade offesa;

"If I have rightly understood your words," responded that magnanimous shade, "your soul is afflicted by cowardice;

la qual molte fïate l’omo ingombra
sì che d’onrata impresa lo rivolve,
come falso veder bestia quand’ ombra.

which often impedes a man, turning him back from a noble undertaking, as a false vision does a beast when it sees a shadow.

Da questa tema acciò che tu ti solve,
dirotti perch’ io venni e quel ch’io ’ntesi
nel primo punto che di te mi dolve.

From this fear, that you may be freed, I will tell you why I came and what I understood at the first moment that I felt grief for you.

Io era tra color che son sospesi,
e donna mi chiamò beata e bella,
tal che di comandare io la richiesi.

I was among those in limbo, and a woman, blessed and beautiful, called to me, so that I asked her to give me orders.

Lucevan li occhi suoi più che la stella;
e cominciommi a dir soave e piana,
con angelica voce, in sua favella:

Her eyes shone brighter than any star, and she began to speak, gently and softly, with an angelic voice, in her own language:

“O anima cortese mantoana,
di cui la fama ancor nel mondo dura,
e durerà quanto ’l mondo lontana,

"O gentle soul of Mantua, whose renown still lives in the world, and shall endure as long as the world is distant,"

l’amico mio, e non de la ventura,
ne la diserta piaggia è impedito
sì nel cammin, che vòlt’ è per paura;

my friend, and not of Fortune's whim,is hindered on that desolate shore,so in his journey, he has turned for fear;

e temo che non sia già sì smarrito,
ch’io mi sia tardi al soccorso levata,
per quel ch’i’ ho di lui nel cielo udito.

And I fear he is not so lost alreadythat my aid has arrived too late,because of what I have heard of him in Heaven.

Or movi, e con la tua parola ornata
e con ciò c’ha mestieri al suo campare,
l’aiuta sì ch’i’ ne sia consolata.

Now move, and with your eloquent wordand with what is needed for his survival,assist him so that I may be consoled.

I’ son Beatrice che ti faccio andare;
vegno del loco ove tornar disio;
amor mi mosse, che mi fa parlare.

I am Beatrice, and I come to you;I come from the place where I long to return;love moved me, which makes me speak.

Quando sarò dinanzi al segnor mio,
di te mi loderò sovente a lui”.
Tacette allora, e poi comincia’ io:

"When I am before my Lord, I will often praise you to Him." Then she was silent, and I began:

“O donna di virtù sola per cui
l’umana spezie eccede ogne contento
di quel ciel c’ha minor li cerchi sui,

“O woman, virtue’s sole embodiment, through whom the human race surpasses all contentment found within that heaven whose circles are the least,

tanto m’aggrada il tuo comandamento,
che l’ubidir, se già fosse, m’è tardi;
più non t’è uo’ ch’aprirmi il tuo talento.

So much does your command delight me, that I am tardy in obeying it, if that were already done. It remains for you only to reveal your will to me.

Ma dimmi la cagion che non ti guardi
de lo scender qua giuso in questo centro
de l’ampio loco ove tornar tu ardi”.

But tell me the reason you don't hesitateto descend here into the depths of this center,this vast place from which you are eager to return."

“Da che tu vuo’ saver cotanto a dentro,
dirotti brievemente”, mi rispuose,
“perch’ i’ non temo di venir qua entro.

“Since you are so keen to know the details, I will tell you briefly," he answered, "why I do not fear coming here."

Temer si dee di sole quelle cose
c’hanno potenza di fare altrui male;
de l’altre no, ché non son paurose.

One should only fear those things with the power to harm others; the rest are not fearsome.

I’ son fatta da Dio, sua mercé, tale,
che la vostra miseria non mi tange,
né fiamma d’esto ’ncendio non m’assale.

I am made by God, and by His grace, such that your misery does not touch me, nor does the flame of this fire assault me.

Donna è gentil nel ciel che si compiange
di questo ’mpedimento ov’ io ti mando,
sì che duro giudicio là sù frange.

A lady in Heaven takes pity on you,and she grieves for the obstruction that sends me to you,so that she breaks a harsh judgment above.

Questa chiese Lucia in suo dimando
e disse:—Or ha bisogno il tuo fedele
di te, e io a te lo raccomando—.

Then Lucia made this request,and said: "Now your faithful one has need of you,and I commend him to you."

Lucia, nimica di ciascun crudele,
si mosse, e venne al loco dov’ i’ era,
che mi sedea con l’antica Rachele.

Lucia, enemy of all the cruel, moved, and came to the place where I was, who sat with ancient Rachel.

Disse:—Beatrice, loda di Dio vera,
ché non soccorri quei che t’amò tanto,
ch’uscì per te de la volgare schiera?

She said: "Beatrice, true praise of God, why do you not help the one who loved you so much, who left the vulgar throng for your sake?"

Non odi tu la pieta del suo pianto,
non vedi tu la morte che ’l combatte
su la fiumana ove ’l mar non ha vanto?—.

Do you not feel pity for his weeping, do you not see the death that battles him on the river where the sea has no dominion?

Al mondo non fur mai persone ratte
a far lor pro o a fuggir lor danno,
com’ io, dopo cotai parole fatte,

In the world, there were never people so swiftat achieving their gain or fleeing their harmas I, after such words were spoken.

venni qua giù del mio beato scanno,
fidandomi del tuo parlare onesto,
ch’onora te e quei ch’udito l’hanno”.

I came down here from my blessed seat, trusting in your honest words, which honor you and those who have heard them.”

Poscia che m’ebbe ragionato questo,
li occhi lucenti lagrimando volse,
per che mi fece del venir più presto.

After he had spoken these words, he turned his shining eyes, weeping, which made me more eager to come.

E venni a te così com’ ella volse:
d’inanzi a quella fiera ti levai
che del bel monte il corto andar ti tolse.

And I came to you as she desired: I rescued you from that beast who prevented you from ascending the beautiful mountain.

Dunque: che è? perché, perché restai,
perché tanta viltà nel core allette,
perché ardire e franchezza non hai,

So, what is it? Why, why did you stay? Why do you harbor so much cowardice in your heart? Why do you lack courage and frankness?

poscia che tai tre donne benedette
curan di te ne la corte del cielo,
e ’l mio parlar tanto ben ti promette?».

After such three blessed women care for you in the court of Heaven, and my words promise you so much good?

Quali fioretti dal notturno gelo
chinati e chiusi, poi che ’l sol li ’mbianca,
si drizzan tutti aperti in loro stelo,

Like little flowers from the nightly frost,bent and closed, then when the sun whitens them,they straighten, all open on their stem.

tal mi fec’ io di mia virtude stanca,
e tanto buono ardire al cor mi corse,
ch’i’ cominciai come persona franca:

Such did I with my weary strength,and such good courage ran into my heart,that I began like a free person:

«Oh pietosa colei che mi soccorse!
e te cortese ch’ubidisti tosto
a le vere parole che ti porse!

"Oh, compassionate one, who aided me! And you, kind soul, who swiftly heeded the truthful words she offered you!"

Tu m’hai con disiderio il cor disposto
sì al venir con le parole tue,
ch’i’ son tornato nel primo proposto.

You have so awakened my heart with desirethrough your words, that I have returned to my first intent.

Or va, ch’un sol volere è d’ambedue:
tu duca, tu segnore e tu maestro».
Così li dissi; e poi che mosso fue,

"Now go, since we both have but a single will: you, guide, you, lord, and you, master." I said to him; and after he had moved,

intrai per lo cammino alto e silvestro.

I entered upon the high and wild path.

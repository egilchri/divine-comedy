Purgatorio * Canto I

Dante and Virgil emerge from Hell onto the shores of an island at the base of Mount Purgatory. The pre-dawn sky is calm, and Dante sees a constellation of four bright stars he has never witnessed before. Their presence is challenged by a stern, white-bearded old man, Cato of Utica, the island's guardian. He assumes they are damned souls who have escaped their punishment. Virgil explains that Dante is alive and their journey is willed by a lady from Heaven. Convinced by the mention of a divine command, Cato allows them to proceed. He instructs Virgil to first wash the grime of Hell from Dante's face with dew and then to gird Dante's waist with a smooth reed from the shore. After Cato departs, Virgil cleanses Dante's face as instructed. He then plucks a reed, and another immediately grows in its place.

Purgatorio • Canto I

Per correr miglior acque alza le vele
omai la navicella del mio ingegno,
che lascia dietro a sé mar sì crudele;

To sail on better waters, now my ship of the intellect hoists its sails, leaving behind a sea so cruel.

e canterò di quel secondo regno
dove l’umano spirito si purga
e di salire al ciel diventa degno.

And I will sing of that second realm, where the human spirit purges itself and becomes worthy to ascend to Heaven.

Ma qui la morta poesì resurga,
o sante Muse, poi che vostro sono;
e qui Calïopè alquanto surga,

But here let dead poetry rise again, O holy Muses, since I am yours; and here let Calliope somewhat ascend,

seguitando il mio canto con quel suono
di cui le Piche misere sentiro
lo colpo tal, che disperar perdono.

following my song with that sound,of which the wretched Magpies feltthe blow so, that they despaired of pardon.

Dolce color d’orïental zaffiro,
che s’accoglieva nel sereno aspetto
del mezzo, puro infino al primo giro,

Sweet color of oriental sapphire, which was gathering in the serene aspect of the air, pure even to the first circle,

a li occhi miei ricominciò diletto,
tosto ch’io usci’ fuor de l’aura morta
che m’avea contristati li occhi e ’l petto.

A delight returned to my eyes,as soon as I emerged from the dead airthat had saddened my eyes and chest.

Lo bel pianeto che d’amar conforta
faceva tutto rider l’orïente,
velando i Pesci ch’erano in sua scorta.

The lovely planet that comforts loversmade all the east laugh,veiling Pisces, who were in its company.

I’ mi volsi a man destra, e puosi mente
a l’altro polo, e vidi quattro stelle
non viste mai fuor ch’a la prima gente.

I turned to the right, and I fixed my gazeupon the other pole, and I saw four starsnever seen except by the first people.

Goder pareva ’l ciel di lor fiammelle:
oh settentrïonal vedovo sito,
poi che privato se’ di mirar quelle!

God seemed to rejoice at their flames in the sky: oh northern site, widowed, since you are deprived of seeing them!

Com’ io da loro sguardo fui partito,
un poco me volgendo a l ’altro polo,
là onde ’l Carro già era sparito,

As I turned away from their gaze, I shifted slightly towards the other pole, from where the Wagon had already vanished.

vidi presso di me un veglio solo,
degno di tanta reverenza in vista,
che più non dee a padre alcun figliuolo.

I saw near me an old man, alone, worthy of such reverence in appearance that no son owes more to a father.

Lunga la barba e di pel bianco mista
portava, a’ suoi capelli simigliante,
de’ quai cadeva al petto doppia lista.

Long was the beard that he wore, mixed with white hair, much like his own, which fell in a double row down to his chest.

Li raggi de le quattro luci sante
fregiavan sì la sua faccia di lume,
ch’i’ ’l vedea come ’l sol fosse davante.

The rays of the four holy lights adorned his face with such radiance that I saw him as if the sun were before me.

«Chi siete voi che contro al cieco fiume
fuggita avete la pregione etterna?»,
diss’ el, movendo quelle oneste piume.

“Who are you, who have fled against the blind river, escaping the eternal prison?” he asked, moving those honest feathers.

«Chi v’ha guidati, o che vi fu lucerna,
uscendo fuor de la profonda notte
che sempre nera fa la valle inferna?

"Who has guided you, or what served as your lantern, emerging from the deep night that perpetually darkens the infernal valley?"

Son le leggi d’abisso così rotte?
o è mutato in ciel novo consiglio,
che, dannati, venite a le mie grotte?».

Are the laws of the abyss then broken? Or has a new counsel been formed in Heaven, that you, the damned, come to my caverns?"

Lo duca mio allor mi diè di piglio,
e con parole e con mani e con cenni
reverenti mi fé le gambe e ’l ciglio.

Then my guide seized me, and with words, hands, and gestures, he respectfully calmed my legs and my brow.

Poscia rispuose lui: «Da me non venni:
donna scese del ciel, per li cui prieghi
de la mia compagnia costui sovvenni.

Then he replied: "I did not come on my own; a woman descended from Heaven, and at her request, I came to the aid of this man."

Ma da ch’è tuo voler che più si spieghi
di nostra condizion com’ ell’ è vera,
esser non puote il mio che a te si nieghi.

But since you wish that I explain further the truth of our state, my will cannot be denied to yours.

Questi non vide mai l’ultima sera;
ma per la sua follia le fu sì presso,
che molto poco tempo a volger era.

This one never saw the final evening;but due to his madness, it was so near to him,that only a short time remained to turn around.

Sì com’ io dissi, fui mandato ad esso
per lui campare; e non lì era altra via
che questa per la quale i’ mi son messo.

As I have said, I was sent to himto save him; there was no other waythan this one, by which I have set myself.

Mostrata ho lui tutta la gente ria;
e ora intendo mostrar quelli spirti
che purgan sé sotto la tua balìa.

I have shown him all the wicked people; and now I intend to show him those spirits who purify themselves under your command.

Com’ io l’ho tratto, saria lungo a dirti;
de l’alto scende virtù che m’aiuta
conducerlo a vederti e a udirti.

How I brought him here would take too long to tell; a power descends from above, which aids me in guiding him to see and hear you.

Or ti piaccia gradir la sua venuta:
libertà va cercando, ch’è sì cara,
come sa chi per lei vita rifiuta.

Now, may it please you to welcome him: he seeks freedom, which is so dear, as one knows who gives up life for her.

Tu ’l sai, ché non ti fu per lei amara
in Utica la morte, ove lasciasti
la vesta ch’al gran dì sarà sì chiara.

You know it, for death in Utica was not bitter to you because of her, where you left the garment that will be so bright on the great day.

Non son li editti etterni per noi guasti,
ché questi vive e Minòs me non lega;
ma son del cerchio ove son li occhi casti

The eternal edicts are not spoiled for us, since this one lives, and Minos does not bind me; rather, I am of the circle where chaste eyes reside.

di Marzia tua, che ’n vista ancor ti priega,
o santo petto, che per tua la tegni:
per lo suo amore adunque a noi ti piega.

Of your Marcia, who still prays to you in sight, O holy breast, which holds her as your own: therefore, for her love, yield to us.

Lasciane andar per li tuoi sette regni;
grazie riporterò di te a lei,
se d’esser mentovato là giù degni».

Let her descend through your seven realms; I will bring word of you to her, if you deign to be mentioned down there.”

«Marzïa piacque tanto a li occhi miei
mentre ch’i’ fu’ di là», diss’ elli allora,
«che quante grazie volse da me, fei.

"Marzia pleased my eyes so muchwhen I was over there," he said then,"that I granted her every favor she wished of me."

Or che di là dal mal fiume dimora,
più muover non mi può, per quella legge
che fatta fu quando me n’usci’ fora.

Now that she dwells beyond the evil river,she can no longer move me, because of that lawthat was made when I departed from it.

Ma se donna del ciel ti move e regge,
come tu di’, non c’è mestier lusinghe:
bastisi ben che per lei mi richegge.

But if a lady from Heaven guides and governs you,as you say, there is no need for flattery:it should be enough that she requests me.

Va dunque, e fa che tu costui ricinghe
d’un giunco schietto e che li lavi ’l viso,
sì ch’ogne sucidume quindi stinghe;

Go, then, and have him girded with a smooth reed, and wash his face so that all the grime is wiped away;

ché non si converria, l’occhio sorpriso
d’alcuna nebbia, andar dinanzi al primo
ministro, ch’è di quei di paradiso.

For if his eye were veiled by any mist, he would not be fit to stand before the first minister, who is of those in Paradise.

Questa isoletta intorno ad imo ad imo,
là giù colà dove la batte l’onda,
porta di giunchi sovra ’l molle limo:

This little island around the lowest depths, down there where the wave strikes, bears reeds upon the soft mud:

null’ altra pianta che facesse fronda
o indurasse, vi puote aver vita,
però ch’a le percosse non seconda.

No other plant could put forth leavesor harden, could live there,because it does not yield to the blows.

Poscia non sia di qua vostra reddita;
lo sol vi mosterrà, che surge omai,
prendere il monte a più lieve salita».

"Then, let your return not be from this side; the sun, which is now rising, will show you the way to take the mountain by a gentler ascent."

Così sparì; e io sù mi levai
sanza parlare, e tutto mi ritrassi
al duca mio, e li occhi a lui drizzai.

Thus he vanished; and I rose up,without a word, and drew myself entirelyto my guide, and fixed my eyes on him.

El cominciò: «Figliuol, segui i miei passi:
volgianci in dietro, ché di qua dichina
questa pianura a’ suoi termini bassi».

He began, "Son, follow my steps: let us turn back, for from this direction, this plain slopes down toward its lower bounds."

L’alba vinceva l’ora mattutina
che fuggia innanzi, sì che di lontano
conobbi il tremolar de la marina.

Dawn was overcoming the morning hour, which fled before it, so that from afar I recognized the trembling of the sea.

Noi andavam per lo solingo piano
com’ om che torna a la perduta strada,
che ’nfino ad essa li pare ire in vano.

We walked across the solitary plain like a man returning to a lost road, who until that point feels he has wandered in vain.

Quando noi fummo là ’ve la rugiada
pugna col sole, per essere in parte
dove, ad orezza, poco si dirada,

When we were where the dewFought with the sun, in order to be in a placeWhere, at the breeze, it thins out little,

ambo le mani in su l’erbetta sparte
soavemente ’l mio maestro pose:
ond’ io, che fui accorto di sua arte,

Both hands upon the scattered grassmy master gently laid:whereupon I, aware of his skill,

porsi ver’ lui le guance lagrimose;
ivi mi fece tutto discoverto
quel color che l’inferno mi nascose.

I turned my tear-streaked face to him; then,there, he revealed me fully,the color that Hell had hidden from me.

Venimmo poi in sul lito diserto,
che mai non vide navicar sue acque
omo, che di tornar sia poscia esperto.

We came then to the deserted shore, which had never seen a man sail its waters, one who might later know how to return.

Quivi mi cinse sì com’ altrui piacque:
oh maraviglia! ché qual elli scelse
l’umile pianta, cotal si rinacque

There, he girded me as another had wished: Oh, wonder! for just as the humble plant he chose, I was reborn.

subitamente là onde l’avelse.

Suddenly, from where he had taken me away.

Inferno * Canto X

Continuing through the Sixth Circle, Dante and Virgil walk among open, fiery tombs containing the souls of heretics. A voice from one sepulcher, recognizing Dante’s Florentine accent, calls out to him. The shade is Farinata degli Uberti, a political leader from a rival faction, who rises from the flames to engage Dante in a tense discussion about their families' political history in Florence. Their exchange is interrupted by another soul, Cavalcante de’ Cavalcanti, who appears beside Farinata and asks why his son, Guido, is not accompanying Dante. A misunderstanding in Dante’s response leads Cavalcante to believe his son is dead, and he collapses back into the tomb in grief. Farinata, ignoring the interruption, resumes his conversation and prophesies Dante’s future exile. Dante then asks how the damned can know the future but not the present. Farinata explains their prophetic sight is limited to distant events. Before leaving, Dante asks Farinata to tell Cavalcante his son is still alive.

Inferno • Canto X

Ora sen va per un secreto calle,
tra ’l muro de la terra e li martìri,
lo mio maestro, e io dopo le spalle.

Now he goes by a secret path, between the wall of the earth and the torments, my master, and I behind his back.

«O virtù somma, che per li empi giri
mi volvi», cominciai, «com’ a te piace,
parlami, e sodisfammi a’ miei disiri.

"O supreme virtue, which turns me through the impious circles," I began, "as it pleases you, speak to me, and satisfy my desires."

La gente che per li sepolcri giace
potrebbesi veder? già son levati
tutt’ i coperchi, e nessun guardia face».

"Can the people within the tombs be seen? The lids are all raised, and no one stands guard."

E quelli a me: «Tutti saran serrati
quando di Iosafàt qui torneranno
coi corpi che là sù hanno lasciati.

And they said to me: "All will be sealed away when they return here from Josaphat with the bodies they left above."

Suo cimitero da questa parte hanno
con Epicuro tutti suoi seguaci,
che l’anima col corpo morta fanno.

Their cemetery lies on this side,with Epicurus and all his followers,who claim the soul dies with the body.

Però a la dimanda che mi faci
quinc’ entro satisfatto sarà tosto,
e al disio ancor che tu mi taci».

But to the question you put to mewithin these walls, you shall soon be satisfied,and to the desire that you do not speak.

E io: «Buon duca, non tegno riposto
a te mio cuor se non per dicer poco,
e tu m’hai non pur mo a ciò disposto».

And I: "Good guide, I do not keep my heart hidden from you, except to speak a little, and you have just now prepared me for this."

«O Tosco che per la città del foco
vivo ten vai così parlando onesto,
piacciati di restare in questo loco.

"Oh Tuscan, you who go alive through the city of fire, speaking so honestly, please stay in this place."

La tua loquela ti fa manifesto
di quella nobil patrïa natio,
a la qual forse fui troppo molesto».

Your speech makes you known as a nativeof that noble homeland,which perhaps I was too harsh toward."

Subitamente questo suono uscìo
d’una de l’arche; però m’accostai,
temendo, un poco più al duca mio.

Suddenly this sound emerged from one of the tombs; thus, I drew near, fearing, a little closer to my guide.

Ed el mi disse: «Volgiti! Che fai?
Vedi là Farinata che s’è dritto:
da la cintola in sù tutto ’l vedrai».

And he said to me: "Turn around! What are you doing? See Farinata there, he has risen: you will see all of him from the waist up."

Io avea già il mio viso nel suo fitto;
ed el s’ergea col petto e con la fronte
com’ avesse l’inferno a gran dispitto.

I had already fixed my gaze upon his, and he stood erect, his chest and brow held high, as if he held all of Hell in utter disdain.

E l’animose man del duca e pronte
mi pinser tra le sepulture a lui,
dicendo: «Le parole tue sien conte».

And the spirited hands of the guide, swift and sure, pushed me amongst the tombs toward him, saying: "Let your words be measured."

Com’ io al piè de la sua tomba fui,
guardommi un poco, e poi, quasi sdegnoso,
mi dimandò: «Chi fuor li maggior tui?».

When I was at the foot of his tomb, he looked at me for a moment and then, as if in disdain, asked, "Who were your ancestors?"

Io ch’era d’ubidir disideroso,
non gliel celai, ma tutto gliel’ apersi;
ond’ ei levò le ciglia un poco in suso;

I, eager to obey, did not conceal it from him, but laid it all bare; at which he raised his eyebrows a little.

poi disse: «Fieramente furo avversi
a me e a miei primi e a mia parte,
sì che per due fïate li dispersi».

Then he said: "They were fiercely opposedto me, my ancestors, and my faction,so that I scattered them twice."

«S’ei fur cacciati, ei tornar d’ogne parte»,
rispuos’ io lui, «l’una e l’altra fïata;
ma i vostri non appreser ben quell’ arte».

"If they were banished, they returned from every direction," I replied to him, "both times; but yours never mastered that art."

Allor surse a la vista scoperchiata
un’ombra, lungo questa, infino al mento:
credo che s’era in ginocchie levata.

Then, at the uncovered view, a shade rose up, alongside this one, as far as the chin: I believe it had risen up on its knees.

Dintorno mi guardò, come talento
avesse di veder s’altri era meco;
e poi che ’l sospecciar fu tutto spento,

He looked around me, as if with the desireto see if another was there with me;and then, when his suspicion was entirely quenched,

piangendo disse: «Se per questo cieco
carcere vai per altezza d’ingegno,
mio figlio ov’ è? e perché non è teco?».

weeping, he said, "If through this blind prison you travel because of your great intellect, where is my son? And why isn't he with you?"

E io a lui: «Da me stesso non vegno:
colui ch’attende là, per qui mi mena
forse cui Guido vostro ebbe a disdegno».

And I to him: "I come not of myself; he who awaits there guides me, perhaps he whom your Guido held in disdain."

Le sue parole e ’l modo de la pena
m’avean di costui già letto il nome;
però fu la risposta così piena.

His words and the manner of his suffering had already revealed his name to me; thus my reply was so direct.

Di sùbito drizzato gridò: «Come?
dicesti “elli ebbe”? non viv’ elli ancora?
non fiere li occhi suoi lo dolce lume?».

Straightaway, he straightened and cried: "What? Did you say 'he had'? Isn't he still alive? Doesn't the sweet light strike his eyes?"

Quando s’accorse d’alcuna dimora
ch’io facëa dinanzi a la risposta,
supin ricadde e più non parve fora.

When he realized I hesitated before answering, he fell backward and vanished from sight.

Ma quell’ altro magnanimo, a cui posta
restato m’era, non mutò aspetto,
né mosse collo, né piegò sua costa;

But that other magnanimous spirit, near whom I had remained, did not change his expression, nor move his neck, nor turn his torso.

e sé continüando al primo detto,
«S’elli han quell’ arte», disse, «male appresa,
ciò mi tormenta più che questo letto.

And continuing with his first words, "If they have learned that art poorly," he said, "that torments me more than this bed."

Ma non cinquanta volte fia raccesa
la faccia de la donna che qui regge,
che tu saprai quanto quell’ arte pesa.

But not fifty times will the face of the woman who reigns here be rekindled before you will know how heavy that art is.

E se tu mai nel dolce mondo regge,
dimmi: perché quel popolo è sì empio
incontr’ a’ miei in ciascuna sua legge?».

And if you ever rule in the sweet world, tell me: why is that people so wicked against mine in every law they make?

Ond’ io a lui: «Lo strazio e ’l grande scempio
che fece l’Arbia colorata in rosso,
tal orazion fa far nel nostro tempio».

And I to him: "The torment and the great slaughter that colored the Arbia red, makes such prayers rise in our temple."

Poi ch’ebbe sospirando il capo mosso,
«A ciò non fu’ io sol», disse, «né certo
sanza cagion con li altri sarei mosso.

Then, sighing, he shook his head, "I wasn't alone in this," he said, "and certainly I wouldn't have been moved with the others without cause."

Ma fu’ io solo, là dove sofferto
fu per ciascun di tòrre via Fiorenza,
colui che la difesi a viso aperto».

But I alone, where the sufferingwas for each to take Florence away,was the one who defended her openly.

«Deh, se riposi mai vostra semenza»,
prega’ io lui, «solvetemi quel nodo
che qui ha ’nviluppata mia sentenza.

"If you ever find rest for your descendants," I prayed to him, "untie for me that knot that has entangled my judgment here."

El par che voi veggiate, se ben odo,
dinanzi quel che ’l tempo seco adduce,
e nel presente tenete altro modo».

The way it seems to me, if I understand correctly, is that you see ahead of time what time brings with it, and in the present you possess another way of knowing.”

«Noi veggiam, come quei c’ha mala luce,
le cose», disse, «che ne son lontano;
cotanto ancor ne splende il sommo duce.

“We see, as those with failing sight, the things that are far off,” he said, “for that much still shines upon us from the highest leader.”

Quando s’appressano o son, tutto è vano
nostro intelletto; e s’altri non ci apporta,
nulla sapem di vostro stato umano.

When they draw near or are present, our intellect is entirely useless; and if others do not inform us, we know nothing of your human condition.

Però comprender puoi che tutta morta
fia nostra conoscenza da quel punto
che del futuro fia chiusa la porta».

But you can understand that all our knowledge will be dead from that moment the door to the future is closed.”

Allor, come di mia colpa compunto,
dissi: «Or direte dunque a quel caduto
che ’l suo nato è co’ vivi ancor congiunto;

Then, as if consumed with guilt for my own failings, I said: "You will tell the fallen one, then, that his son is still joined with the living;

e s’i’ fui, dianzi, a la risposta muto,
fate i saper che ’l fei perché pensava
già ne l’error che m’avete soluto».

And if I was silent just now when you questioned me, let him know I did so because I was still thinking of the error you absolved me of.

E già ’l maestro mio mi richiamava;
per ch’i’ pregai lo spirto più avaccio
che mi dicesse chi con lu’ istava.

And already my master was calling me back; because of that, I asked the spirit more quickly to tell me who was with him.

Dissemi: «Qui con più di mille giaccio:
qua dentro è ’l secondo Federico
e ’l Cardinale; e de li altri mi taccio».

Dissemi: "Here lie more than a thousand. Within is the second Frederick, and the Cardinal; of the others, I say no more."

Indi s’ascose; e io inver’ l’antico
poeta volsi i passi, ripensando
a quel parlar che mi parea nemico.

Indi vanished, and I turned my steps toward the ancient poet, reconsidering that speech that seemed hostile to me.

Elli si mosse; e poi, così andando,
mi disse: «Perché se’ tu sì smarrito?».
E io li sodisfeci al suo dimando.

Elli moved; and then, walking along, she said to me: "Why are you so lost?" And I answered her question.

«La mente tua conservi quel ch’udito
hai contra te», mi comandò quel saggio;
«e ora attendi qui», e drizzò ’l dito:

"Keep in mind what you've heard against yourself," the wise man commanded me. "And now wait here," he said, pointing his finger.

«quando sarai dinanzi al dolce raggio
di quella il cui bell’ occhio tutto vede,
da lei saprai di tua vita il vïaggio».

"When you stand before the sweet ray of light of she whose beautiful eye sees all, you will know the journey of your life."

Appresso mosse a man sinistra il piede:
lasciammo il muro e gimmo inver’ lo mezzo
per un sentier ch’a una valle fiede,

Then he moved his foot to the left: we left the wall and went toward the center along a path that cut into a valley.

che ’nfin là sù facea spiacer suo lezzo.

that up there made his stench displeasing.

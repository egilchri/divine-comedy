Inferno * Canto XIII

Dante and Virgil enter a dense, dark wood with gnarled, thorny trees and no discernible path. Strange cries of lament echo through the forest, where Harpies are perched among the branches. On Virgil’s instruction, Dante snaps a twig from a large tree, which immediately bleeds and speaks, crying out in pain. The soul within the tree identifies himself as Pier della Vigna, a courtier who took his own life. He explains that all the trees in this wood are the souls of those who committed suicide. Their conversation is interrupted by the appearance of two naked souls running through the undergrowth, pursued by a pack of black hounds. The dogs catch one of the running figures and tear him to pieces. The second soul hides within a bush, which the hounds then viciously attack. After the dogs depart, the damaged bush speaks, identifying itself as the soul of a Florentine who hanged himself.

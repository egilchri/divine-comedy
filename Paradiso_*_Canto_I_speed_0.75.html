Paradiso * Canto I

The canto begins in the Earthly Paradise where Dante, alongside Beatrice, invokes the god Apollo for inspiration. It is noon, and Beatrice fixes her gaze directly upon the sun. Following her example, Dante is also able to stare at the sun, which appears to double in brightness. When Dante turns his gaze from the sun back to Beatrice, he feels himself undergoing a profound change and begins to ascend heavenward. As they rise, he is enveloped in brilliant light and hears the harmonious music of the celestial spheres. Confused by the speed of their travel and the surrounding phenomena, he asks Beatrice for an explanation. She informs him that they have left the mortal world and are traveling through the heavens. She clarifies that his purified soul is now following its natural inclination, rising back to its divine source with a speed greater than lightning.

Paradiso • Canto I

La gloria di colui che tutto move
per l’universo penetra, e risplende
in una parte più e meno altrove.

The glory of the one who moves all thingspermeates the universe, and shines,more in one place, and less in others.

Nel ciel che più de la sua luce prende
fu’ io, e vidi cose che ridire
né sa né può chi di là sù discende;

In the heaven that receives most of His light, I was, and I saw things that neither knows how nor can recount he who descends from there.

perché appressando sé al suo disire,
nostro intelletto si profonda tanto,
che dietro la memoria non può ire.

Because, drawing near to its desire, our intellect plunges so deep that it cannot follow memory.

Veramente quant’ io del regno santo
ne la mia mente potei far tesoro,
sarà ora materia del mio canto.

Truly, as much of the holy kingdom as I was able to store in my mind will now be the matter of my song.

O buono Appollo, a l’ultimo lavoro
fammi del tuo valor sì fatto vaso,
come dimandi a dar l’amato alloro.

O good Apollo, for this final labor, make me such a vessel of your valor as you demand to bestow the beloved laurel.

Infino a qui l’un giogo di Parnaso
assai mi fu; ma or con amendue
m’è uopo intrar ne l’aringo rimaso.

Hitherto, a single summit of Parnassus sufficed me; but now, with both, I must enter the remaining arena.

Entra nel petto mio, e spira tue
sì come quando Marsïa traesti
de la vagina de le membra sue.

Enter into my breast, and breathe your song,as once you drew Marsyasfrom the sheath of his limbs.

O divina virtù, se mi ti presti
tanto che l’ombra del beato regno
segnata nel mio capo io manifesti,

O divine power, if you lend yourself to meso much that the shadow of the blessed kingdommarked in my head I might reveal,

vedra’mi al piè del tuo diletto legno
venire, e coronarmi de le foglie
che la materia e tu mi farai degno.

I shall see myself at the foot of your beloved tree come, and crown myself with the leaves that you and the matter will make me worthy of.

Sì rade volte, padre, se ne coglie
per trïunfare o cesare o poeta,
colpa e vergogna de l’umane voglie,

So rarely, Father, does one gather itto triumph, be it Caesar or poet,a fault and shame of human desires.

che parturir letizia in su la lieta
delfica deïtà dovria la fronda
peneia, quando alcun di sé asseta.

For joy to come to birth on the happyDelphic deity, the laurel boughshould then be craved, when thirsting for itself.

Poca favilla gran fiamma seconda:
forse di retro a me con miglior voci
si pregherà perché Cirra risponda.

A little spark is followed by a great flame:perhaps behind me, with better voices,they will pray that Cirra responds.

Surge ai mortali per diverse foci
la lucerna del mondo; ma da quella
che quattro cerchi giugne con tre croci,

The world's lamp rises to mortals through various openings, but from the one that joins four circles with three crosses,

con miglior corso e con migliore stella
esce congiunta, e la mondana cera
più a suo modo tempera e suggella.

with better path and better starit rises joined, and the worldly waxmore in its way tempers and seals.

Fatto avea di là mane e di qua sera
tal foce, e quasi tutto era là bianco
quello emisperio, e l’altra parte nera,

Such a strait had made on this side morning, and on that evening, and almost all of that hemisphere was white, and the other part black.

quando Beatrice in sul sinistro fianco
vidi rivolta e riguardar nel sole:
aguglia sì non li s’affisse unquanco.

When I saw Beatrice turned toward the left side, gazing at the sun: never did an eagle so fix itself upon it.

E sì come secondo raggio suole
uscir del primo e risalire in suso,
pur come pelegrin che tornar vuole,

And as a second ray is wont to emerge from the first and rise again, just as a pilgrim who wishes to return,

così de l’atto suo, per li occhi infuso
ne l’imagine mia, il mio si fece,
e fissi li occhi al sole oltre nostr’ uso.

So from his act, infused through my eyes into my own image, mine was made, and I fixed my eyes upon the sun, beyond our wont.

Molto è licito là, che qui non lece
a le nostre virtù, mercé del loco
fatto per proprio de l’umana spece.

Much is permitted there, which here is notto our virtues, thanks to that placemade the exclusive domain of humankind.

Io nol soffersi molto, né sì poco,
ch’io nol vedessi sfavillar dintorno,
com’ ferro che bogliente esce del foco;

I endured it not for long, nor yet so briefly,that I did not see it sparking all around,like iron that, boiling hot, comes from the fire.

e di sùbito parve giorno a giorno
essere aggiunto, come quei che puote
avesse il ciel d’un altro sole addorno.

and suddenly day seemed to add itself to day,as if the heavens, in their power,had adorned themselves with another sun.

Beatrice tutta ne l’etterne rote
fissa con li occhi stava; e io in lei
le luci fissi, di là sù rimote.

Beatrice, entirely absorbed in the eternal spheres, remained fixed in gaze; and I fixed my eyes upon her, removed from earthly concerns.

Nel suo aspetto tal dentro mi fei,
qual si fé Glauco nel gustar de l’erba
che ’l fé consorto in mar de li altri dèi.

In her appearance, I became so transformed within, just as Glaucus was when he tasted the herb that made him a companion in the sea to the other gods.

Trasumanar significar per verba
non si poria; però l’essemplo basti
a cui esperïenza grazia serba.

To pass beyond the human cannot be told in words; thus, let the example suffice for those to whom grace offers the experience.

S’i’ era sol di me quel che creasti
novellamente, amor che ’l ciel governi,
tu ’l sai, che col tuo lume mi levasti.

If I was only that which you, love, who governs Heaven, newly created, you know it, for you raised me with your light.

Quando la rota che tu sempiterni
desiderato, a sé mi fece atteso
con l’armonia che temperi e discerni,

When the wheel that you, eternal one, desired, made me attentive to itself, with the harmony that you temper and discern,

parvemi tanto allor del cielo acceso
de la fiamma del sol, che pioggia o fiume
lago non fece alcun tanto disteso.

I was so far removed from the ignited sky, from the sun's flame, that no rain, river, or lake ever spread so wide.

La novità del suono e ’l grande lume
di lor cagion m’accesero un disio
mai non sentito di cotanto acume.

The novelty of the sound and the great light of their cause ignited in me a desire never before felt, of such sharpness.

Ond’ ella, che vedea me sì com’ io,
a quïetarmi l’animo commosso,
pria ch’io a dimandar, la bocca aprio

And she, who saw me as I was, opened her mouth to calm my agitated soul before I could even ask.

e cominciò: «Tu stesso ti fai grosso
col falso imaginar, sì che non vedi
ciò che vedresti se l’avessi scosso.

And she began: "You yourself become consumedwith false imaginings, so that you do not seewhat you would see, had you cast them off.

Tu non se’ in terra, sì come tu credi;
ma folgore, fuggendo il proprio sito,
non corse come tu ch’ad esso riedi».

You are not on earth, as you believe, but like lightning fleeing its own place, you did not run as you do now, returning to it.

S’io fui del primo dubbio disvestito
per le sorrise parolette brevi,
dentro ad un nuovo più fu’ inretito

If I was stripped of the first doubt,by the brief, smiling words,I was caught inside a newer one.

e dissi: «Già contento requïevi
di grande ammirazion; ma ora ammiro
com’ io trascenda questi corpi levi».

And I said: "I am already content, restingin great admiration; but now I marvelat how I surpass these weightless bodies."

Ond’ ella, appresso d’un pïo sospiro,
li occhi drizzò ver’ me con quel sembiante
che madre fa sovra figlio deliro,

And she, after a pious sigh, directed her eyes toward me, with the look a mother gives her delirious son,

e cominciò: «Le cose tutte quante
hanno ordine tra loro, e questo è forma
che l’universo a Dio fa simigliante.

And she began: "All things whatsoeverhave order among themselves, and this is formthat makes the universe resemble God.

Qui veggion l’alte creature l’orma
de l’etterno valore, il qual è fine
al quale è fatta la toccata norma.

Who gaze upon the high creations, the traceof the eternal worth, which is the endfor which the touched norm is made.

Ne l’ordine ch’io dico sono accline
tutte nature, per diverse sorti,
più al principio loro e men vicine;

And to this order all natures lean, through diverse destinies, some closer to their origin, some less.

onde si muovono a diversi porti
per lo gran mar de l’essere, e ciascuna
con istinto a lei dato che la porti.

where they move toward different harborsacross the great sea of existence, each onewith an instinct given to it that guides.

Questi ne porta il foco inver’ la luna;
questi ne’ cor mortali è permotore;
questi la terra in sé stringe e aduna;

This one carries the fire toward the moon;this one is the mover in mortal hearts;this one gathers and binds the earth to itself;

né pur le creature che son fore
d’intelligenza quest’ arco saetta,
ma quelle c’hanno intelletto e amore.

Not only do creatures without intellect feel the pull of this bow, but also those that possess intellect and love.

La provedenza, che cotanto assetta,
del suo lume fa ’l ciel sempre quïeto
nel qual si volge quel c’ha maggior fretta;

The providence that so ordains,with its light, keeps the heavens ever still,wherein revolves that which has the greatest haste.

e ora lì, come a sito decreto,
cen porta la virtù di quella corda
che ciò che scocca drizza in segno lieto.

And now there, as if to a set place, the power of that string carries us, which directs what it shoots toward a joyous target.

Vero è che, come forma non s’accorda
molte fïate a l’intenzion de l’arte,
perch’ a risponder la materia è sorda,

It's true that, like form that doesn't often align with the artist's intent because the material is deaf to its call,

così da questo corso si diparte
talor la creatura, c’ha podere
di piegar, così pinta, in altra parte;

so that the creature, which has the power to bend, thus impelled, in another direction, sometimes departs from this course;

e sì come veder si può cadere
foco di nube, sì l’impeto primo
l’atterra torto da falso piacere.

And just as one can see fire fall from a cloud, so is the initial impetus brought low, twisted by false pleasure.

Non dei più ammirar, se bene stimo,
lo tuo salir, se non come d’un rivo
se d’alto monte scende giuso ad imo.

I can no longer marvel at your ascent, if I understand correctly, except as one marvels at a stream that descends from a high mountain to the valley below.

Maraviglia sarebbe in te se, privo
d’impedimento, giù ti fossi assiso,
com’ a terra quïete in foco vivo».

Wonder would be in you if, withoutimpediment, you had sat down,as fire rests quietly on the ground.”

Quinci rivolse inver’ lo cielo il viso.

Then he turned his face toward the sky.

Inferno * Canto XXXIV

Virgil leads Dante into Judecca, the final zone of the ninth circle of Hell. Here, sinners are completely encased in the frozen lake of Cocytus. In the center stands the emperor of Hell, Lucifer, a massive, three-faced giant frozen from the chest down. The constant beating of his six bat-like wings generates the icy wind that freezes the circle. Each of Lucifer’s three mouths chews on a sinner: Judas Iscariot is in the central mouth, while Brutus and Cassius are in the mouths on either side. To escape Hell, Virgil instructs Dante to hold on to him. He then grasps Lucifer's hairy flank and begins to climb down his body. When they reach the center of the Earth at Lucifer’s waist, Virgil turns himself and Dante completely around and starts climbing upward. They proceed through a dark natural cavern, eventually emerging onto the surface of the Southern Hemisphere to see the stars again.

Inferno • Canto XXXIV

«Vexilla regis prodeunt inferni
verso di noi; però dinanzi mira»,
disse ’l maestro mio, «se tu ’l discerni».

"The banners of Hell advance toward us,"my master said, "Look ahead, then, if you can make him out."

Come quando una grossa nebbia spira,
o quando l’emisperio nostro annotta,
par di lungi un molin che ’l vento gira,

As when a thick fog blows, or when our hemisphere darkens, a mill turning in the wind appears from afar.

veder mi parve un tal dificio allotta;
poi per lo vento mi ristrinsi retro
al duca mio, ché non lì era altra grotta.

I seemed to see such a structure then; then because of the wind, I drew back behind my guide, for there was no other shelter there.

Già era, e con paura il metto in metro,
là dove l’ombre tutte eran coperte,
e trasparien come festuca in vetro.

It was now, and with fear I put myself in motion, there where all the shadows were covered, and they appeared like straw in glass.

Altre sono a giacere; altre stanno erte,
quella col capo e quella con le piante;
altra, com’ arco, il volto a’ piè rinverte.

Some are lying down; others stand upright, one with her head and another with her feet; another, like a bow, reverses her face toward her feet.

Quando noi fummo fatti tanto avante,
ch’al mio maestro piacque di mostrarmi
la creatura ch’ebbe il bel sembiante,

When we had gone so far ahead that it pleased my master to show me the creature who had the beautiful face,

d’innanzi mi si tolse e fé restarmi,
«Ecco Dite», dicendo, «ed ecco il loco
ove convien che di fortezza t’armi».

He stepped in front of me, and made me stop. "Behold Dis," he said, "and behold the place where you must arm yourself with strength."

Com’ io divenni allor gelato e fioco,
nol dimandar, lettor, ch’i’ non lo scrivo,
però ch’ogne parlar sarebbe poco.

How I then became frozen and faint, do not ask, reader, for I do not write it, because any words would be insufficient.

Io non mori’ e non rimasi vivo;
pensa oggimai per te, s’hai fior d’ingegno,
qual io divenni, d’uno e d’altro privo.

I did not die, nor did I remain alive; now consider for yourself, if you have a spark of intellect, what I became, devoid of both.

Lo ’mperador del doloroso regno
da mezzo ’l petto uscia fuor de la ghiaccia;
e più con un gigante io mi convegno,

The emperor of the grievous kingdom, emerged from the ice at the middle of his chest; and with a giant I more closely align myself,

che i giganti non fan con le sue braccia:
vedi oggimai quant’ esser dee quel tutto
ch’a così fatta parte si confaccia.

that giants do not make with their arms: now see how much of the whole suits such a part.

S’el fu sì bel com’ elli è ora brutto,
e contra ’l suo fattore alzò le ciglia,
ben dee da lui procedere ogne lutto.

If he was once as beautiful as he is now ugly, and raised his eyebrows against his maker, all sorrow must rightly proceed from him.

Oh quanto parve a me gran maraviglia
quand’ io vidi tre facce a la sua testa!
L’una dinanzi, e quella era vermiglia;

Oh, how great a marvel it seemed to mewhen I saw three faces on his head!One in front, and that one was red;

l’altr’ eran due, che s’aggiugnieno a questa
sovresso ’l mezzo di ciascuna spalla,
e sé giugnieno al loco de la cresta:

and the other two, joining with the first,rose above the midpoint of each shoulder,and met at the crown of the head.

e la destra parea tra bianca e gialla;
la sinistra a vedere era tal, quali
vegnon di là onde ’l Nilo s’avvalla.

And the right side appeared between white and yellow; the left, to look upon it, was like that which comes from where the Nile descends.

Sotto ciascuna uscivan due grand’ ali,
quanto si convenia a tanto uccello:
vele di mar non vid’ io mai cotali.

Beneath each, two great wings emerged, as befitted so massive a bird; I never saw such sails upon the sea.

Non avean penne, ma di vispistrello
era lor modo; e quelle svolazzava,
sì che tre venti si movean da ello:

They had no feathers, but in the manner of a battheir wings were made; and those wings they beat,so that three winds came forth from them in a sudden squall.

quindi Cocito tutto s’aggelava.
Con sei occhi piangëa, e per tre menti
gocciava ’l pianto e sanguinosa bava.

Then Cocytus was entirely frozen. With six eyes, he wept, and down three chins, tears and bloody slaver dripped.

Da ogne bocca dirompea co’ denti
un peccatore, a guisa di maciulla,
sì che tre ne facea così dolenti.

From every mouth, a sinner, like a claw, was ripped apart by teeth, making three of them so anguished.

A quel dinanzi il mordere era nulla
verso ’l graffiar, che talvolta la schiena
rimanea de la pelle tutta brulla.

Compared to the clawing, the biting was nothing; sometimes, the skin on their backs was entirely razed.

«Quell’ anima là sù c’ha maggior pena»,
disse ’l maestro, «è Giuda Scarïotto,
che ’l capo ha dentro e fuor le gambe mena.

"That soul up there that suffers the most," said the master, "is Judas Iscariot, who has his head inside and thrashes his legs outside."

De li altri due c’hanno il capo di sotto,
quel che pende dal nero ceffo è Bruto:
vedi come si storce, e non fa motto!;

Of the other two who have their heads beneath, the one who hangs from the black face is Brutus: see how he writhes, and makes no sound!

e l’altro è Cassio, che par sì membruto.
Ma la notte risurge, e oramai
è da partir, ché tutto avem veduto».

And the other is Cassius, who seems so strong. But night returns, and now it is time to depart, for we have seen it all.”

Com’ a lui piacque, il collo li avvinghiai;
ed el prese di tempo e loco poste,
e quando l’ali fuoro aperte assai,

As he wished, I embraced his neck; and he chose time and place, and when his wings were fully spread,

appigliò sé a le vellute coste;
di vello in vello giù discese poscia
tra ’l folto pelo e le gelate croste.

he clung to the velvety slopes; then he descended from fleece to fleece, between the thick fur and the frozen crusts.

Quando noi fummo là dove la coscia
si volge, a punto in sul grosso de l’anche,
lo duca, con fatica e con angoscia,

When we were at the point where the thigh turns, precisely at the base of the haunches, the guide, with effort and anguish,

volse la testa ov’ elli avea le zanche,
e aggrappossi al pel com’ om che sale,
sì che ’n inferno i’ credea tornar anche.

turned his head toward where he had his legs, and gripped the fur like a man climbing, so that I thought we would return to Hell as well.

«Attienti ben, ché per cotali scale»,
disse ’l maestro, ansando com’ uom lasso,
«conviensi dipartir da tanto male».

"Hold tight, for by such stairs," the master said, panting like a weary man, "one must depart from such suffering."

Poi uscì fuor per lo fóro d’un sasso
e puose me in su l’orlo a sedere;
appresso porse a me l’accorto passo.

Then he went out through the opening of a stone, and placed me on the edge to sit. Afterward, he offered me his careful footing.

Io levai li occhi e credetti vedere
Lucifero com’ io l’avea lasciato,
e vidili le gambe in sù tenere;

I raised my eyes and thought I saw Lucifer as I had left him, and saw his legs held upwards.

e s’io divenni allora travagliato,
la gente grossa il pensi, che non vede
qual è quel punto ch’io avea passato.

And if I was troubled then, let the crude masses consider it, for they cannot see the point I had traversed.

«Lèvati sù», disse ’l maestro, «in piede:
la via è lunga e ’l cammino è malvagio,
e già il sole a mezza terza riede».

"Get up," the master said, "on your feet: the road is long and the path is treacherous, and already the sun returns to the middle of the third."

Non era camminata di palagio
là ’v’ eravam, ma natural burella
ch’avea mal suolo e di lume disagio.

It was no palace hallwaywhere we were, but a natural burrow,with a rough floor and a lack of light.

«Prima ch’io de l’abisso mi divella,
maestro mio», diss’ io quando fui dritto,
«a trarmi d’erro un poco mi favella:

"Before I depart from the abyss, my master," I said, once I had stood, "speak to me for a moment, to draw me from my error."

ov’ è la ghiaccia? e questi com’ è fitto
sì sottosopra? e come, in sì poc’ ora,
da sera a mane ha fatto il sol tragitto?».

"Where is the ice? And why is this one stuck so deep upside down? And how, in so little time, has the sun traveled from evening to morning?"

Ed elli a me: «Tu imagini ancora
d’esser di là dal centro, ov’ io mi presi
al pel del vermo reo che ’l mondo fóra.

And he said to me, "You still imagine yourself to be on the other side of the center, where I took hold of the hair of the evil worm that pierces the world.

Di là fosti cotanto quant’ io scesi;
quand’ io mi volsi, tu passasti ’l punto
al qual si traggon d’ogne parte i pesi.

Beyond that point, you remained as long as I descended. When I turned, you passed the place to which all weights are drawn.

E se’ or sotto l’emisperio giunto
ch’è contraposto a quel che la gran secca
coverchia, e sotto ’l cui colmo consunto

And now you are beneath the hemisphere opposite the one the great dry land covers, and beneath whose zenith the sun is consumed.

fu l’uom che nacque e visse sanza pecca;
tu haï i piedi in su picciola spera
che l’altra faccia fa de la Giudecca.

It was the man who was born and lived without sin; you have your feet upon a small sphere that makes up the other face of Judecca.

Qui è da man, quando di là è sera;
e questi, che ne fé scala col pelo,
fitto è ancora sì come prim’ era.

Here it is daytime, where it is evening over there; and this one, who made a ladder with his fur, is still fixed in place as he was before.

Da questa parte cadde giù dal cielo;
e la terra, che pria di qua si sporse,
per paura di lui fé del mar velo,

On this side, he fell from the heavens; and the earth, which had previously extended outward on this side, fearing him, made a veil of the sea.

e venne a l’emisperio nostro; e forse
per fuggir lui lasciò qui loco vòto
quella ch’appar di qua, e sù ricorse».

And he arrived in our hemisphere; and perhaps, to flee from him, the one that appears on this side left this place empty and retreated upward."

Luogo è là giù da Belzebù remoto
tanto quanto la tomba si distende,
che non per vista, ma per suono è noto

The place is down there, far from Beelzebub,as distant as the tomb extends itself,and it is known not by sight, but by sound.

d’un ruscelletto che quivi discende
per la buca d’un sasso, ch’elli ha roso,
col corso ch’elli avvolge, e poco pende.

of a small stream that descends herethrough a hole in a stone, which it has eroded,with the course that it winds, and slopes slightly.

Lo duca e io per quel cammino ascoso
intrammo a ritornar nel chiaro mondo;
e sanza cura aver d’alcun riposo,

The guide and I, along that hidden path,entered to return to the bright world;and without caring to rest,

salimmo sù, el primo e io secondo,
tanto ch’i’ vidi de le cose belle
che porta ’l ciel, per un pertugio tondo.

We climbed, he first and I behind, until through a circular opening, I saw some of the beautiful things the heavens hold.

E quindi uscimmo a riveder le stelle.

And so we went forth, to see again the stars.

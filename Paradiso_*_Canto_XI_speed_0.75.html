Paradiso * Canto XI

In the Sphere of the Sun, the soul of Thomas Aquinas speaks to Dante to resolve a doubt from the previous canto. To explain his earlier statement, Thomas narrates the life of Francis of Assisi. He describes Francis’s birthplace, his devotion to a figure he calls "Lady Poverty," and the gathering of his first followers, such as Bernard and Sylvester. The narrative recounts key events, including Francis seeking and receiving approval for his order from Pope Innocent III, his journey to preach before the Sultan in Egypt, and his receiving the stigmata on the mountain of La Verna. After describing Francis's death, Thomas concludes his speech by criticizing his own order. He states they have strayed from the path of their founder, seeking worldly goods instead of spiritual knowledge. Having clarified his meaning, Thomas falls silent and the circle of bright souls resumes its movement.

Paradiso • Canto XI

O insensata cura de’ mortali,
quanto son difettivi silogismi
quei che ti fanno in basso batter l’ali!

Oh, foolish care of mortals, how defective are the syllogisms that make you beat your wings low!

Chi dietro a iura e chi ad amforismi
sen giva, e chi seguendo sacerdozio,
e chi regnar per forza o per sofismi,

Some went after laws, others after aphorisms, and some followed priesthood, while others sought to rule by force or sophistry.

e chi rubare e chi civil negozio,
chi nel diletto de la carne involto
s’affaticava e chi si dava a l’ozio,

and some to robbery, some to civic duty, some to the pleasures of the flesh, some to toil, and some to idleness.

quando, da tutte queste cose sciolto,
con Bëatrice m’era suso in cielo
cotanto glorïosamente accolto.

When, freed from all these things, I had ascended to heaven with Beatrice, so gloriously welcomed.

Poi che ciascuno fu tornato ne lo
punto del cerchio in che avanti s’era,
fermossi, come a candellier candelo.

After each one had returned to the point of the circle where they had been before, he stopped, like a candle in a candlestick.

E io senti’ dentro a quella lumera
che pria m’avea parlato, sorridendo
incominciar, faccendosi più mera:

And I heard within that light, which had spoken to me before, beginning to smile, becoming brighter:

«Così com’ io del suo raggio resplendo,
sì, riguardando ne la luce etterna,
li tuoi pensieri onde cagioni apprendo.

“Just as I shine from its ray, so, gazing into the eternal light, I understand the causes of your thoughts.”

Tu dubbi, e hai voler che si ricerna
in sì aperta e ’n sì distesa lingua
lo dicer mio, ch’al tuo sentir si sterna,

You doubt, and you want my words to be re-spunin such a plain and ample languagethat they may be accessible to your understanding,

ove dinanzi dissi: “U’ ben s’impingua”,
e là u’ dissi: “Non nacque il secondo”;
e qui è uopo che ben si distingua.

Where I previously said, "Where one thrives,"and where I said, "The second was not born";here it is necessary to make a clear distinction.

La provedenza, che governa il mondo
con quel consiglio nel quale ogne aspetto
creato è vinto pria che vada al fondo,

The providence that governs the worldwith that counsel wherein every created thingis vanquished before it reaches the end,

però che andasse ver’ lo suo diletto
la sposa di colui ch’ad alte grida
disposò lei col sangue benedetto,

so that she might go toward her beloved,the bride of him who, with loud cries,espoused her with blessed blood,

in sé sicura e anche a lui più fida,
due principi ordinò in suo favore,
che quinci e quindi le fosser per guida.

in herself secure and even more faithful to him, she ordained two princes in her favor, that they might be her guide on either side.

L’un fu tutto serafico in ardore;
l’altro per sapïenza in terra fue
di cherubica luce uno splendore.

The first was wholly seraphic in ardor; the other, through wisdom, on earth was a splendor of cherubic light.

De l’un dirò, però che d’amendue
si dice l’un pregiando, qual ch’om prende,
perch’ ad un fine fur l’opere sue.

Of the one I will speak, for of bothpraise is given, no matter which is chosen,since their works had the same end in sight.

Intra Tupino e l’acqua che discende
del colle eletto dal beato Ubaldo,
fertile costa d’alto monte pende,

Between Tupino and the water that flows downfrom the hill chosen by blessed Ubaldo,a fertile slope hangs from a high mountain.

onde Perugia sente freddo e caldo
da Porta Sole; e di rietro le piange
per grave giogo Nocera con Gualdo.

Where Perugia feels cold and warmthfrom Porta Sole; and behind it weepsunder a heavy yoke Nocera with Gualdo.

Di questa costa, là dov’ ella frange
più sua rattezza, nacque al mondo un sole,
come fa questo talvolta di Gange.

Of this slope, where it most sharply breaks,a sun was born into the world,as this one sometimes does from the Ganges.

Però chi d’esso loco fa parole,
non dica Ascesi, ché direbbe corto,
ma Orïente, se proprio dir vuole.

But whoever speaks of that place,shouldn't say Assisi, as that would be too brief,but the Orient, if they wish to speak truly.

Non era ancor molto lontan da l’orto,
ch’el cominciò a far sentir la terra
de la sua gran virtute alcun conforto;

Not far from the garden,he began to allow the earth to feelsome comfort from his great virtue.

ché per tal donna, giovinetto, in guerra
del padre corse, a cui, come a la morte,
la porta del piacer nessun diserra;

for such a woman, young man, he went to war against her father, who, like death itself, never unlocks the gate of pleasure;

e dinanzi a la sua spirital corte
et coram patre le si fece unito;
poscia di dì in dì l’amò più forte.

And before her spiritual court, he made himself united with her in the presence of her father; then, day by day, he loved her more and more.

Questa, privata del primo marito,
millecent’ anni e più dispetta e scura
fino a costui si stette sanza invito;

This one, deprived of her first husband,resentful and in darkness for a thousand one hundred years or more,remained without a suitor until this man.

né valse udir che la trovò sicura
con Amiclate, al suon de la sua voce,
colui ch’a tutto ’l mondo fé paura;

nor did she hear a waltz that found her safewith Amyclas, at the sound of his voice,he who brought fear to all the world.

né valse esser costante né feroce,
sì che, dove Maria rimase giuso,
ella con Cristo pianse in su la croce.

Neither a waltz to be constant nor fierce, so that, where Mary remained below, she wept with Christ upon the cross.

Ma perch’ io non proceda troppo chiuso,
Francesco e Povertà per questi amanti
prendi oramai nel mio parlar diffuso.

But so that I don't proceed too obscurely, take Francis and Poverty, as lovers, now in my extended speech.

La lor concordia e i lor lieti sembianti,
amore e maraviglia e dolce sguardo
facieno esser cagion di pensier santi;

Their concord and their joyous faces, love and wonder and sweet gaze caused holy thoughts to arise.

tanto che ’l venerabile Bernardo
si scalzò prima, e dietro a tanta pace
corse e, correndo, li parve esser tardo.

So much so that the venerable Bernard first removed his shoes, and after such peace he ran, and as he ran, it seemed to him he was late.

Oh ignota ricchezza! oh ben ferace!
Scalzasi Egidio, scalzasi Silvestro
dietro a lo sposo, sì la sposa piace.

Oh, unknown riches! Oh, fertile goodness!Egidio removes his shoes, Silvestro removes his shoes,following the groom, so pleased is the bride.

Indi sen va quel padre e quel maestro
con la sua donna e con quella famiglia
che già legava l’umile capestro.

Then goes that father and that master, with his woman, and with that family who had already bound the humble halter.

Né li gravò viltà di cuor le ciglia
per esser fi’ di Pietro Bernardone,
né per parer dispetto a maraviglia;

Nor did his humble heart weigh down his browFor being son to Peter Bernardone,Nor cause him wonder's scorn to undergo.

ma regalmente sua dura intenzione
ad Innocenzio aperse, e da lui ebbe
primo sigillo a sua religïone.

but royally, his firm intenthe opened to Innocent, and from him receivedthe first seal for his order.

Poi che la gente poverella crebbe
dietro a costui, la cui mirabil vita
meglio in gloria del ciel si canterebbe,

When the poor folk grew in numberfollowing this one, whose wondrous lifewould be better sung in Heaven's glory,

di seconda corona redimita
fu per Onorio da l’Etterno Spiro
la santa voglia d’esto archimandrita.

Of a second crown, redeemed,by Honorius, from the Eternal Breath,was the holy will of this archimandrite.

E poi che, per la sete del martiro,
ne la presenza del Soldan superba
predicò Cristo e li altri che ’l seguiro,

And after, due to a thirst for martyrdom, in the presence of the haughty Sultan, he preached Christ and the others who followed Him,

e per trovare a conversione acerba
troppo la gente e per non stare indarno,
redissi al frutto de l’italica erba,

and to find the conversion unripe,the people too much so, and not to linger in vain,I returned to the fruit of the Italian herb,

nel crudo sasso intra Tevero e Arno
da Cristo prese l’ultimo sigillo,
che le sue membra due anni portarno.

in the harsh stone between the Tiber and Arno,Christ received his final mark,which his limbs bore for two years.

Quando a colui ch’a tanto ben sortillo
piacque di trarlo suso a la mercede
ch’el meritò nel suo farsi pusillo,

When He who had assigned him such great gracechose to draw him upward to the rewardthat he earned by making himself small,

a’ frati suoi, sì com’ a giuste rede,
raccomandò la donna sua più cara,
e comandò che l’amassero a fede;

to his brothers, as to just heirs,he commended his most precious lady,and he commanded that they love her faithfully;

e del suo grembo l’anima preclara
mover si volle, tornando al suo regno,
e al suo corpo non volle altra bara.

And from her womb, the illustrious souldesired to depart, returning to its kingdom,and for her body, she wanted no other tomb.

Pensa oramai qual fu colui che degno
collega fu a mantener la barca
di Pietro in alto mar per dritto segno;

Think now of who was the man worthyof being a colleague in maintaining the barqueof Peter on the high sea, with a true course;

e questo fu il nostro patrïarca;
per che qual segue lui, com’ el comanda,
discerner puoi che buone merce carca.

And this was our patriarch; therefore, whoever follows him as he commands, you can discern that he carries good merchandise.

Ma ’l suo pecuglio di nova vivanda
è fatto ghiotto, sì ch’esser non puote
che per diversi salti non si spanda;

But his flock, made greedy for new fodder,cannot help but scatter through various leaps.

e quanto le sue pecore remote
e vagabunde più da esso vanno,
più tornano a l’ovil di latte vòte.

And the farther his scattered, wandering sheep stray, the emptier of milk they return to the fold.

Ben son di quelle che temono ’l danno
e stringonsi al pastor; ma son sì poche,
che le cappe fornisce poco panno.

They are of those that fear harmand cling to the shepherd; but they are so few,that little cloth provides the cloaks.

Or, se le mie parole non son fioche,
se la tua audïenza è stata attenta,
se ciò ch’è detto a la mente revoche,

Or, if my words haven't grown faint, if you've been listening closely, if you recall what's been said,

in parte fia la tua voglia contenta,
perché vedrai la pianta onde si scheggia,
e vedra’ il corrègger che argomenta

part of your desire will be satisfied, because you will see the plant from which it splinters, and you will see the one who corrects it, who argues.

“U’ ben s’impingua, se non si vaneggia”».

"And it thrives, if one doesn't delude oneself."

Inferno * Canto V

Upon entering the Second Circle of Hell, Dante and Virgil encounter Minos, the demonic judge who assigns each soul to its proper circle of punishment by coiling his tail around himself. After Virgil commands Minos to let them pass, they enter a dark place where a violent, incessant whirlwind buffets the souls of the lustful. Virgil points out famous figures such as Semiramis, Dido, Cleopatra, and Achilles within the storm. Dante, feeling pity, calls out to two souls, Francesca da Rimini and Paolo Malatesta, who are swept along together. Francesca tells Dante their story: they fell in love while reading a book about Lancelot and Guinevere. This led to a kiss, after which they were discovered and killed by Francesca’s husband. Overwhelmed by compassion upon hearing her tale, Dante faints and falls to the ground.

Così discesi del cerchio primaio
giù nel secondo, che men loco cinghia
e tanto più dolor, che punge a guaio.

Thus I descended from the first circle down into the second, which girdles less space, and so much more pain that it provokes wailing.

Minos stands there, monstrous and he growls:he examines the sins at the entrance;he judges and consigns as he enfolds.

Always before him many stand: they go in turn to judgment, speak and listen, and then are sent down.

Or the mournful notes beginto make themselves felt; now I have cometo where much weeping strikes me.

Io venni in loco d’ogne luce muto,

I came into a place bereft of all light, which roars like the sea in a tempest when assailed by opposing winds.

La bufera infernal, che mai non resta,
mena li spirti con la sua rapina;
voltando e percotendo li molesta.

The infernal tempest, which never ceases, drives the spirits with its plunder; whirling and beating, it torments them.

Quando giungon davanti a la ruina,
quivi le strida, il compianto, il lamento;
bestemmian quivi la virtù divina.

When they arrive before the ruin, there is screaming, weeping, and wailing; there they blaspheme against divine virtue.

Intesi ch’a così fatto tormento
enno dannati i peccator carnali,
che la ragion sommettono al talento.

I understood that such torment is the fate of the carnal sinners, those who subject reason to their desire.

E come li stornei ne portan l’ali

E come starlings are borne on their wingsin the cold season, in a wide and full flock,so that breath carries the wicked spirits

di qua, di là, di giù, di sù li mena;
nulla speranza li conforta mai,
non che di posa, ma di minor pena.

Here, there, below, above, it drives them;no hope ever comforts them,not of rest, but of lesser pain.

faccendo in aere di sé lunga riga,
così vid’ io venir, traendo guai,

And as cranes go chanting their cries, making a long line of themselves in the air, so I saw them come, drawing out their woes.

I began: "Poet, I would gladly speak to those two who go together, and seem so light upon the wind."

Ed elli a me: «Vedrai quando saranno

And he to me: "You will see when they arecloser to us; and then you entreat themby that love that drives them, and they will come."

Sì tosto come il vento a noi li piega,
mossi la voce: «O anime affannate,
venite a noi parlar, s’altri nol niega!».

As soon as the wind bends them toward us, I raised my voice: "O weary souls, come speak with us, if no one else forbids it!"

Quali colombe dal disio chiamate

Like doves, summoned by desire,with wings raised and steady, toward their sweet nest,they come through the air, borne by their will;

So great was the loving cry that those spirits emerged from the company where Dido resides, coming toward us through the tainted air.

«O animal grazïoso e benigno

"O gracious and kind creature,you who are journeying through the tainted airto visit us who stained the world crimson,"

se fosse amico il re de l’universo,

If the King of the Universe were our friend,we would pray to him for your peace,since you have pity for our wicked plight.

Di quel che udire e che parlar vi piace,

Regarding what pleases you to hear and to speak, we will listen and speak to you, as long as the wind, as it is doing now, is silent for us.

Siede la terra dove nata fui
su la marina dove ’l Po discende

The land where I was born lies there,on the shore where the Po descends,seeking peace with its followers.

Amor, ch’al cor gentil ratto s’apprende,
prese costui de la bella persona
che mi fu tolta; e ’l modo ancor m’offende.

Love, which quickly takes hold in a gentle heart, seized this man because of the beautiful body that was taken from me; and the manner still offends me.

Amor, ch’a nullo amato amar perdona,
mi prese del costui piacer sì forte,
che, come vedi, ancor non m’abbandona.

Love, which excuses no one beloved from loving, seized me with such a strong delight in him, that, as you see, it still does not abandon me.

Amor condusse noi ad una morte.
Caina attende chi a vita ci spense».
Queste parole da lor ci fuor porte.

Love led us to one death. Cain awaits the one who extinguished our lives." These words were brought forth from them.

Quand’ io intesi quell’ anime offense,
china’ il viso, e tanto il tenni basso,
fin che ’l poeta mi disse: «Che pense?».

When I understood those offended souls, I lowered my face, and held it so, until the poet said to me: "What are you thinking?"

Quando rispuosi, cominciai: «Oh lasso,
quanti dolci pensier, quanto disio
menò costoro al doloroso passo!».

When I replied, I began: "Alas, how many sweet thoughts, how much desire led these souls to this grievous pass!"

Poi mi rivolsi a loro e parla’ io,
e cominciai: «Francesca, i tuoi martìri
a lagrimar mi fanno tristo e pio.

Then I turned to them and spoke,and began: "Francesca, your tormentscause me to weep with sorrow and pity."

Ma dimmi: al tempo d’i dolci sospiri,
a che e come concedette amore
che conosceste i dubbiosi disiri?».

But tell me: in the time of sweet sighs, by what means and how did Love allow you to recognize those uncertain desires?

E quella a me: «Nessun maggior dolore

And she to me: "There is no greater pain than to remember a happy time in misery; and your teacher knows this.

But if you have so great a longing to know the first root of our love, I will speak like one who weeps and tells.

One day, for pleasure, we read of Lancelot, of how love constrained him. We were alone, and unsuspecting.

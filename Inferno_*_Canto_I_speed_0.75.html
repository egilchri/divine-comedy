Inferno * Canto I

The narrator finds himself lost within a dark, forbidding forest. Upon reaching the end of the valley, he sees a sunlit hill and begins to ascend. His path is soon blocked by three successive beasts: a leopard, a lion, and a she-wolf. The she-wolf is the most terrifying, and its presence drives the narrator back down the slope toward the dark woods. As he retreats, the figure of a man appears. The narrator calls out for help, and the figure identifies himself as the spirit of the Roman poet Virgil. After the narrator pleads for aid against the she-wolf, Virgil explains that this beast prevents passage along the current route. He proposes an alternate journey, offering to guide the narrator through a realm of eternal punishment and then a place of purification. Virgil states another guide will be required for the final ascent. The narrator accepts, and Virgil sets off, with the narrator following him.

Inferno • Canto I

Nel mezzo del cammin di nostra vita
mi ritrovai per una selva oscura,
ché la diritta via era smarrita.
Midway upon the journey of our life, I found myself within a dark wood, for the straightforward path had been lost.

Ahi quanto a dir qual era è cosa dura
esta selva selvaggia e aspra e forte
che nel pensier rinova la paura!

Ah, how hard it is to describe what that wood was like, that wild, harsh, and formidable place, which, in thought alone, renews my fear!

Tant’ è amara che poco è più morte;
ma per trattar del ben ch’i’ vi trovai,
dirò de l’altre cose ch’i’ v’ho scorte.

So bitter is it, death is scarcely more so; but to speak of the good that I found there, I will tell of the other things I saw.

Io non so ben ridir com’ i’ v’intrai,
tant’ era pien di sonno a quel punto
che la verace via abbandonai.

I can’t rightly say how I entered it, so full of sleep was I at that point that I abandoned the true path.

Ma poi ch’i’ fui al piè d’un colle giunto,
là dove terminava quella valle
che m’avea di paura il cor compunto,

And once I reached the foot of a hill, there, at the point where that valley ended, the valley that had pierced my heart with fear,

guardai in alto e vidi le sue spalle
vestite già de’ raggi del pianeta
che mena dritto altrui per ogne calle.

I looked up and saw its shoulders, already dressed in the rays of the planet that guides people directly on every path.

Allor fu la paura un poco queta,
che nel lago del cor m’era durata
la notte ch’i’ passai con tanta pieta.

Then fear subsided somewhat, which had endured within the lake of my heart, throughout the night I passed in such sorrow.

E come quei che con lena affannata,
uscito fuor del pelago a la riva,
si volge a l’acqua perigliosa e guata,

And as one who, with breath labored, having emerged from the sea to the shore, turns to the perilous water and stares.

così l’animo mio, ch’ancor fuggiva,
si volse a retro a rimirar lo passo
che non lasciò già mai persona viva.

And so my soul, which was still fleeing, turned back to look again at the passage that never left anyone alive.

Poi ch’èi posato un poco il corpo lasso,
ripresi via per la piaggia diserta,
sì che ’l piè fermo sempre era ’l più basso.

After I had rested my weary body a while, I took up the journey once more along the desolate shore, keeping my foot always on the lower path.

Ed ecco, quasi al cominciar de l’erta,
una lonza leggera e presta molto,
che di pel macolato era coverta;

And then, almost at the start of the ascent, a leopard, sleek and swift, its coat covered in spots.

e non mi si partia dinanzi al volto,
anzi ’mpediva tanto il mio cammino,
ch’i’ fui per ritornar più volte vòlto.

And it did not move away from my sight, instead, it so blocked my path that I turned to go back several times.

Temp’ era dal principio del mattino,
e ’l sol montava ’n sù con quelle stelle
ch’eran con lui quando l’amor divino

It was then at the start of morning, and the sun was rising with those stars that were with it when divine love

mosse di prima quelle cose belle;
sì ch’a bene sperar m’era cagione
di quella fiera a la gaetta pelle

began to move those beautiful things; thus giving me good reason to hope, regarding that beast with the swift hide.

l’ora del tempo e la dolce stagione;
ma non sì che paura non mi desse
la vista che m’apparve d’un leone.

the hour of time and the gentle season; yet not so that fear did not seize me at the sight of a lion.

Questi parea che contra me venisse
con la test’ alta e con rabbiosa fame,
sì che parea che l’aere ne tremesse.

This beast seemed to advance against me,head held high, consumed by furious hunger,so that the very air itself seemed to tremble.

Ed una lupa, che di tutte brame
sembiava carca ne la sua magrezza,
e molte genti fé già viver grame,

And a she-wolf, that with every longingseemed laden in her gauntness,and many people had caused to live in misery,

questa mi porse tanto di gravezza
con la paura ch’uscia di sua vista,
ch’io perdei la speranza de l’altezza.

This beast offered me such a weight,with the terror that emanated from its gaze,that I lost all hope of the summit.

E qual è quei che volontieri acquista,
e giugne ’l tempo che perder lo face,
che ’n tutti suoi pensier piange e s’attrista;

And like one who eagerly makes a purchase, only to have the time arrive when they must relinquish it, who weeps and sorrows in every thought they have;

tal mi fece la bestia sanza pace,
che, venendomi ’ncontro, a poco a poco
mi ripigneva là dove ’l sol tace.

Such was the beast without peace to me,that, as it came toward me, little by little,it pushed me back to where the sun is silent.

Mentre ch’i’ rovinava in basso loco,
dinanzi a li occhi mi si fu offerto
chi per lungo silenzio parea fioco.

While I was descending into a lower place,before my eyes was offered to meone who seemed faint from a long silence.

Quando vidi costui nel gran diserto,
«Miserere di me», gridai a lui,
«qual che tu sii, od ombra od omo certo!».

When I saw him in the great wasteland, I cried to him, "Have mercy on me, whoever you are, shade or true man!"

Rispuosemi: «Non omo, omo già fui,
e li parenti miei furon lombardi,
mantoani per patrïa ambedui.

He answered me: "I am not a man, though once I was one, and my parents were Lombards, both from Mantua as their homeland."

Nacqui sub Iulio, ancor che fosse tardi,
e vissi a Roma sotto ’l buono Augusto
nel tempo de li dèi falsi e bugiardi.

I was born under Julius, though it was late, and I lived in Rome under the good Augustus, in the time of the false and lying gods.

Poeta fui, e cantai di quel giusto
figliuol d’Anchise che venne di Troia,
poi che ’l superbo Ilïón fu combusto.

I was a poet, and I sang of that just son of Anchises, who came from Troy, after proud Ilium was burned.

Ma tu perché ritorni a tanta noia?
perché non sali il dilettoso monte
ch’è principio e cagion di tutta gioia?».

But why do you return to such tedium? Why do you not ascend the delightful mountain, which is the beginning and cause of all joy?”.

«Or se’ tu quel Virgilio e quella fonte
che spandi di parlar sì largo fiume?»,
rispuos’ io lui con vergognosa fronte.

“So, are you Virgil, and that spring which pours forth such a wide river of speech?” I responded to him, my face ashamed.

«O de li altri poeti onore e lume,
vagliami ’l lungo studio e ’l grande amore
che m’ha fatto cercar lo tuo volume.

"O glory and light of the other poets, may my long study and great love, which have made me search your volume, avail me."

Tu se’ lo mio maestro e ’l mio autore,
tu se’ solo colui da cu’ io tolsi
lo bello stilo che m’ha fatto onore.

You are my master and my author; you alone are the one from whom I took the beautiful style that has brought me honor.

Vedi la bestia per cu’ io mi volsi;
aiutami da lei, famoso saggio,
ch’ella mi fa tremar le vene e i polsi».

See the beast for which I turned; help me against her, wise one, for she makes my veins and pulses tremble.”

«A te convien tenere altro vïaggio»,
rispuose, poi che lagrimar mi vide,
«se vuo’ campar d’esto loco selvaggio;

“You must take another path,” he replied, after seeing me weep, “if you wish to escape this savage place;

ché questa bestia, per la qual tu gride,
non lascia altrui passar per la sua via,
ma tanto lo ’mpedisce che l’uccide;

because this beast, the cause of your cries, does not allow anyone to pass along its path, but impedes them so much that it kills them.

e ha natura sì malvagia e ria,
che mai non empie la bramosa voglia,
e dopo ’l pasto ha più fame che pria.

And it has such a wicked and evil nature that it never satisfies its greedy desire, and after eating it is hungrier than before.

Molti son li animali a cui s’ammoglia,
e più saranno ancora, infin che ’l veltro
verrà, che la farà morir con doglia.

Many are the beasts with whom she couples, and more will be, until the greyhound arrives, who will slay her with suffering.

Questi non ciberà terra né peltro,
ma sapïenza, amore e virtute,
e sua nazion sarà tra feltro e feltro.

This one will nourish not on earth nor pewter, but on wisdom, love, and virtue, and his nation will be between felt and felt.

Di quella umile Italia fia salute
per cui morì la vergine Cammilla,
Eurialo e Turno e Niso di ferute.

From that humble Italy will come salvation, for which the virgin Camilla died, and Euryalus, Turnus, and Nisus of wounds.

Questi la caccerà per ogne villa,
fin che l’avrà rimessa ne lo ’nferno,
là onde ’nvidia prima dipartilla.

This one will chase it through every village, until he has returned it to Hell, from whence envy first sent it forth.

Ond’ io per lo tuo me’ penso e discerno
che tu mi segui, e io sarò tua guida,
e trarrotti di qui per loco etterno;

And so I think and understand for your own good that you should follow me, and I will be your guide, and I will lead you from here to an eternal place.

ove udirai le disperate strida,
vedrai li antichi spiriti dolenti,
ch’a la seconda morte ciascun grida;

Here you will hear desperate shrieks,You will see the ancient, grieving spirits,Who each cry out against the second death;

e vederai color che son contenti
nel foco, perché speran di venire
quando che sia a le beate genti.

And you will see those who are contentin the fire, because they hope to arrivewhenever they may among the blessed people.

A le quai poi se tu vorrai salire,
anima fia a ciò più di me degna:
con lei ti lascerò nel mio partire;

And to the dock, if you wish to ascend,a soul will be there, worthier than I:with her I shall leave you in my departing.

ché quello imperador che là sù regna,
perch’ i’ fu’ ribellante a la sua legge,
non vuol che ’n sua città per me si vegna.

For that emperor who reigns above, because I was rebellious against his law, does not wish for me to come into his city.

In tutte parti impera e quivi regge;
quivi è la sua città e l’alto seggio:
oh felice colui cu’ ivi elegge!».

He rules in all places and reigns here; here is his city and his high seat: oh, happy is he whom he chooses there!

E io a lui: «Poeta, io ti richeggio
per quello Dio che tu non conoscesti,
acciò ch’io fugga questo male e peggio,

And I to him: “Poet, I beg you,by that God whom you did not know,so that I may flee this evil and worse,

che tu mi meni là dov’ or dicesti,
sì ch’io veggia la porta di san Pietro
e color cui tu fai cotanto mesti».

that you lead me there where you just spoke of, so that I may see the gate of Saint Peter and those whom you make so sad.”

Allor si mosse, e io li tenni dietro.

Then he moved, and I followed him.

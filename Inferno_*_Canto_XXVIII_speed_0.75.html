Inferno * Canto XXVIII

Dante and Virgil enter the ninth ditch of the eighth circle, where they observe sinners who are perpetually mutilated by a sword-wielding devil. The souls walk in a circuit; as their wounds heal, the devil cleaves them open again. The first shade Dante encounters is Mohammed, who is split from his chin to his anus. He points out his kinsman Ali, whose head is cloven. Mohammed asks Dante to warn a living man, Fra Dolcino. Next, Pier da Medicina, with a slit throat and severed nose, requests that Dante deliver a message. He then points to Curio, who cannot speak because his tongue is cut out. Dante also sees Mosca de' Lamberti, who holds up the stumps of his amputated hands. The final figure Dante witnesses is Bertran de Born, a headless torso carrying its own severed head like a lantern. The head speaks to Dante, identifying itself before Dante and Virgil move on.

Inferno • Canto XXVIII

Chi poria mai pur con parole sciolte
dicer del sangue e de le piaghe a pieno
ch’i’ ora vidi, per narrar più volte?

Who could ever, even with unchained words, fully describe the blood and the wounds that I now saw, even if telling the tale many times over?

Ogne lingua per certo verria meno
per lo nostro sermone e per la mente
c’hanno a tanto comprender poco seno.

Truly, any tongue would fail,along with our language and the mindsthat lack the capacity to grasp so much.

S’el s’aunasse ancor tutta la gente
che già, in su la fortunata terra
di Puglia, fu del suo sangue dolente

If all the people were to gather again,who once, on Apulia’s fortunate land,were pained by their own blood.

per li Troiani e per la lunga guerra
che de l’anella fé sì alte spoglie,
come Livïo scrive, che non erra,

for the Trojans and the long warthat brought such high spoils from the rings,as Livy writes, who does not err,

con quella che sentio di colpi doglie
per contastare a Ruberto Guiscardo;
e l’altra il cui ossame ancor s’accoglie

with her who felt the pains of blowsto contend against Robert Guiscard;and the other whose bones are still gathered

a Ceperan, là dove fu bugiardo
ciascun Pugliese, e là da Tagliacozzo,
dove sanz’ arme vinse il vecchio Alardo;

at Ceprano, where every Apulian was a liar, and at Tagliacozzo, where old Alardo conquered without arms.

e qual forato suo membro e qual mozzo
mostrasse, d’aequar sarebbe nulla
il modo de la nona bolgia sozzo.

And one showed a pierced limb, another a missing one; it would be nothing to try to equal the filthy state of the ninth pit.

Già veggia, per mezzul perdere o lulla,
com’ io vidi un, così non si pertugia,
rotto dal mento infin dove si trulla.

Already I saw, in the act of losing its middle or its lower parts, as I saw one, there’s no hole like it, split from the chin to where one breaks wind.

Tra le gambe pendevan le minugia;
la corata pareva e ’l tristo sacco
che merda fa di quel che si trangugia.

Between the legs hung the guts; the belly seemed to show, as did the wretched sack that turns to waste whatever is swallowed.

Mentre che tutto in lui veder m’attacco,
guardommi e con le man s’aperse il petto,
dicendo: «Or vedi com’ io mi dilacco!

While I was occupied with gazing at every part of him, he looked at me and with his hands tore open his chest, saying: "Now see how I rip myself!"

vedi come storpiato è Mäometto!
Dinanzi a me sen va piangendo Alì,
fesso nel volto dal mento al ciuffetto.

See how Muhammad is mangled! Ahead of me, Ali walks, weeping, his face split from chin to the forelock.

E tutti li altri che tu vedi qui,
seminator di scandalo e di scisma
fuor vivi, e però son fessi così.

And all the others whom you see here, were sowers of scandal and schism while alive, and therefore they are so rent.

Un diavolo è qua dietro che n’accisma
sì crudelmente, al taglio de la spada
rimettendo ciascun di questa risma,

A devil is here behind us, tormenting us so cruelly, slashing each of this lot again and again with his sword.

quand’ avem volta la dolente strada;
però che le ferite son richiuse
prima ch’altri dinanzi li rivada.

When we had turned along the grievous road; for their wounds close up before another arrives to slash them again.

Ma tu chi se’ che ’n su lo scoglio muse,
forse per indugiar d’ire a la pena
ch’è giudicata in su le tue accuse?».

But you, who are you, that you muse upon the rock, perhaps to delay the anger of the punishment that has been judged against your accusations?

«Né morte ’l giunse ancor, né colpa ’l mena»,
rispuose ’l mio maestro, «a tormentarlo;
ma per dar lui esperïenza piena,

“Neither death has touched him yet, nor any sin,” my master replied, “to bring him to this torment; but to give him full experience,

a me, che morto son, convien menarlo
per lo ’nferno qua giù di giro in giro;
e quest’ è ver così com’ io ti parlo».

“He, who is alive, has to go through it with me, for I am dead. We must go round and round through hell, and this is as true as I am speaking to you.”

Più fuor di cento che, quando l’udiro,
s’arrestaron nel fosso a riguardarmi
per maraviglia, oblïando il martiro.

More than a hundred, who, when they heard, stopped in the ditch to look at me, astonished, forgetting their torment.

«Or dì a fra Dolcin dunque che s’armi,
tu che forse vedra’ il sole in breve,
s’ello non vuol qui tosto seguitarmi,

"Now tell Fra Dolcino, then, to arm himself, you who perhaps will see the sun soon, if he does not wish to follow me here swiftly."

sì di vivanda, che stretta di neve
non rechi la vittoria al Noarese,
ch’altrimenti acquistar non saria leve».

yes, of provisions, that the grip of snowdoesn't bring victory to the Novarese,for otherwise they would not have strength to conquer.”

Poi che l’un piè per girsene sospese,
Mäometto mi disse esta parola;
indi a partirsi in terra lo distese.

After he paused, lifting one foot to leave, Muhammad spoke these words to me; then he set it down on the ground to depart.

Un altro, che forata avea la gola
e tronco ’l naso infin sotto le ciglia,
e non avea mai ch’una orecchia sola,

Another, whose throat had been pierced,and whose nose was cut off beneath the eyebrows,and who had only one ear,

ristato a riguardar per maraviglia
con li altri, innanzi a li altri aprì la canna,
ch’era di fuor d’ogne parte vermiglia,

stood still to gaze in wonder,with the others, and opened his throat first,which was red on the outside.

e disse: «O tu cui colpa non condanna
e cu’ io vidi su in terra latina,
se troppa simiglianza non m’inganna,

and said: "O you who blame does not condemn,and whom I saw upon the Latin earth,if too much likeness does not trick me,

rimembriti di Pier da Medicina,
se mai torni a veder lo dolce piano
che da Vercelli a Marcabò dichina.

Remember Pier da Medicina, if ever you return to see the sweet plain that slopes down from Vercelli to Marcabò.

E fa saper a’ due miglior da Fano,
a messer Guido e anco ad Angiolello,
che, se l’antiveder qui non è vano,

And tell the two best men of Fano, Sir Guido and also Angiolello, that, if foresight here is not in vain,

gittati saran fuor di lor vasello
e mazzerati presso a la Cattolica
per tradimento d’un tiranno fello.

they will be thrown out of their vesselsand murdered near Cattolicathrough the treachery of a cruel tyrant.

Tra l’isola di Cipri e di Maiolica
non vide mai sì gran fallo Nettuno,
non da pirate, non da gente argolica.

Between the island of Cyprus and Majorca,Neptune never witnessed such a great wrong,not by pirates, nor by Argive people.

Quel traditor che vede pur con l’uno,
e tien la terra che tale qui meco
vorrebbe di vedere esser digiuno,

that traitor who sees even with one eye,and holds the land that would want to see him,just as he is with me, deprived of it, wanting to starve.

farà venirli a parlamento seco;
poi farà sì, ch’al vento di Focara
non sarà lor mestier voto né preco».

He will bring them to speak with him in parliament; then he will arrange things so that they will not need vows or prayers against the Focara wind."

E io a lui: «Dimostrami e dichiara,
se vuo’ ch’i’ porti sù di te novella,
chi è colui da la veduta amara».

And I to him: "Show me and tell me, if you wish that I carry back tidings of you, who is that one of bitter aspect."

Allor puose la mano a la mascella
d’un suo compagno e la bocca li aperse,
gridando: «Questi è desso, e non favella.

Then he put his hand to the jawof a companion, and opened his mouth,crying: "This is he, and he does not speak."

Questi, scacciato, il dubitar sommerse
in Cesare, affermando che ’l fornito
sempre con danno l’attender sofferse».

This one, banished, drowned all doubtin Caesar, declaring that to waitalways brought harm to what was provided."

Oh quanto mi pareva sbigottito
con la lingua tagliata ne la strozza
Curïo, ch’a dir fu così ardito!

Oh, how frightened he seemed to me,with his tongue cut in his throat,Curio, who was so bold in speech!

E un ch’avea l’una e l’altra man mozza,
levando i moncherin per l’aura fosca,
sì che ’l sangue facea la faccia sozza,

And one who had both hands cut off, raising his stumps through the dim air, so that the blood made his face filthy.

gridò: «Ricordera’ti anche del Mosca,
che disse, lasso!, “Capo ha cosa fatta”,
che fu mal seme per la gente tosca».

He cried: "Remember Mosca too, who, alas, said, 'Deed done,' which was a bad seed for the Tuscan people."

E io li aggiunsi: «E morte di tua schiatta»;
per ch’elli, accumulando duol con duolo,
sen gio come persona trista e matta.

And I added to him: "And death to your lineage"; at which, piling grief upon grief, he went away like a sorrowful and mad person.

Ma io rimasi a riguardar lo stuolo,
e vidi cosa ch’io avrei paura,
sanza più prova, di contarla solo;

But I remained to observe the throng, and I saw something that I would fear, without further proof, to describe.

se non che coscïenza m’assicura,
la buona compagnia che l’uom francheggia
sotto l’asbergo del sentirsi pura.

Except that conscience reassures me, and the good company that makes a person free beneath the breastplate of feeling pure.

Io vidi certo, e ancor par ch’io ’l veggia,
un busto sanza capo andar sì come
andavan li altri de la trista greggia;

I truly saw, and still it seems I see it, a body without a head walking among the other souls of that wretched flock.

e ’l capo tronco tenea per le chiome,
pesol con mano a guisa di lanterna:
e quel mirava noi e dicea: «Oh me!».

And he held his severed head by the hair, swinging it like a lantern in his hand. It stared at us and said, "Alas!"

Di sé facea a sé stesso lucerna,
ed eran due in uno e uno in due;
com’ esser può, quei sa che sì governa.

He made a lantern of himself,and they were two in one, and one in two;how this can be, he who governs thus knows.

Quando diritto al piè del ponte fue,
levò ’l braccio alto con tutta la testa
per appressarne le parole sue,

When he was directly at the bridge's foot, he raised his arm high, with his whole head, to bring his words closer.

che fuoro: «Or vedi la pena molesta,
tu che, spirando, vai veggendo i morti:
vedi s’alcuna è grande come questa.

who were there: "Now see the grievous pain, you who, breathing, go about seeing the dead: see if any is as great as this."

E perché tu di me novella porti,
sappi ch’i’ son Bertram dal Bornio, quelli
che diedi al re giovane i ma’ conforti.

And so that you may bring news of me, know that I am Bertram de Born, the one who gave the young king evil counsel.

Io feci il padre e ’l figlio in sé ribelli;
Achitofèl non fé più d’Absalone
e di Davìd coi malvagi punzelli.

I set father and son against each other; Achitophel did no worse with Absalom and David, using wicked goads.

Perch’ io parti’ così giunte persone,
partito porto il mio cerebro, lasso!,
dal suo principio ch’è in questo troncone.

Because I divided such united persons, I carry my brain, alas!, divided from its beginning, which is in this trunk.

Così s’osserva in me lo contrapasso».

Thus the law of requital is observed in me."
